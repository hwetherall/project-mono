Title: Entering MedTech Substrate Supply: Buying Process, Budget Dynamics, and Pricing Landscape for Advanced Flexible Substrates and Electrode Modules in CGM, Neurostimulation, and BCI

Abstract
This report analyzes the enterprise B2B buying process, budget structure, and pricing/selection dynamics for advanced medical device substrates, flexible circuit components, and electrode modules used in wearable and implantable devices across Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and brain–computer interface (BCI) segments in North America and Europe (with a global expansion lens). It triangulates vendor white papers, regulatory/reimbursement documentation, market research synopses, and engineering literature to identify the decision-making unit (DMU), approval chains, budget holders, procurement cycles, selection criteria, pricing norms, and commercial realities. The report further assesses where a high-density interconnect substrate leader like LG Innotek (LGIT)—with 10 µm-class chip‑on‑film and flexible PCB capability in IT/auto but without medical certifications or biosignal IP—can defensibly enter the medical supply chain, using companies such as DYCONEX (Micro Systems Technologies), CorTec, NeuroNexus, and leading EMS/CDMOs as benchmarks. The analysis highlights concrete near-term entry points (non-implant external CGM transmitter flex/interposers, patch interconnects, antennas) and a staged path toward implantable electrode substrates, detailing required quality, biocompatibility, and regulatory steps (ISO 13485, IPC Class III, traceability, sterilization compatibility, soak/accelerated life testing, noble‑metal electrode finishes, and protective coatings). Evidence is documented using APA in-text citations with source links and a consolidated reference list. Introduction: Why Flexible Substrates and Electrode Modules Matter in CGM, Neurostimulation, and BCI
Across CGM, neurostimulation, and BCI, the shift toward thin, conformal, high‑density flexible electronics and electrode arrays is central to device miniaturization, comfort, signal integrity, and biocompatibility. On the implantable side, established substrates (polyimide, LCP) with noble-metal thin films and specialized electrode finishes (Pt, PtIr, IrO2) underpin chronic stability; on the wearable side, flexible circuit integration enables low-profile sensing, amplification, and telemetry. Vendors like DYCONEX (Micro Systems Technologies) explicitly position biostable LCP/PI thin-film interconnects with recessed/raised electrode options and ISO 13485/IPC Class III manufacturing for long‑term implants in neural and cardiac applications, setting an industry benchmark for substrate suppliers aspiring to medical-grade participation ([Medical Device Network/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/); [MedDeviceOnline/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/biocompatible-flexible-substrates-for-directly-implantable-applications-0002)). In parallel, academic and translational work continues to validate flexible PCB platforms for glucose monitoring, highlighting integration paths for flexible circuits into biosensing front ends and potentiostats in textile/wearable formats ([BioRxiv flexible PCB potentiostat](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.24.707657v1.full.pdf)).

Reimbursement and clinical adoption indicators underscore demand durability. For example, U.S. CMS rulemaking codifies ongoing procedural reimbursement for neurostimulator analysis and programming (e.g., CPT 95970, 95976, 95977), including attention to evolving hypoglossal nerve stimulation cost inputs, which signals an active and economically supported therapy ecosystem drawing sustained component demand ([Federal Register – CPT 95970/95976/95977 excerpts](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/16/2025-13271/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cy-2026-payment-policies-under-the-physician-fee-schedule-and-other)).

CGM’s industrial structure is likewise consolidated yet partnership‑oriented. Market reports cite Abbott, Dexcom, Medtronic, and GlySens as leading players, with notable integrations like Dexcom–Ypsomed (closed-loop system) and Abbott–Insulet (sensor–pump AID integration) illustrating a buying preference for proven modules and interoperable components, which creates defined entry points for certified flex substrate and electrode module suppliers who can slot into OEM product architectures ([PR Newswire – CGM competitive insights and partnerships](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/global-continuous-glucose-monitoring-cgm-market-report-2021-2026-featuring-dexcom-abbott-laboratories-medtronic-and-glysens-301454481.html)).

On the neurotechnology supplier roster, specialized electrode companies (CorTec, NeuroNexus, Microprobes) and precision substrate houses (DYCONEX) serve as canonical incumbents. NeuroNexus’s NIH BRAIN exhibits show configurable high‑definition arrays with multi‑channel stimulation/recording platforms for investigational clinical use—emblematic of the performance and documentation expectations for electrode vendors in clinical/near‑clinical programs ([NeuroNexus BRAIN platform exhibits](https://braininitiative.nih.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ppp_neuronexus_high_def_electrodes_508c.pdf)). Market compendia further identify broader competitive sets for flexible implantable electrodes, including Abbott, Blackrock Neurotech, Inomed, CorTec, Plexon, Ripple Neuro, Synapse Biomedical, Neuralink, and others, mapping a fragmented yet credential‑heavy landscape for implantable interconnect and electrode supply ([ResearchAndMarkets – flexible implantable electrode market, companies](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6226271/flexible-implantable-electrode-market-report?srsltid=AfmBOorsUWfpkV4twVdQwfGzbEpp_aechZZ-87xVUw5Xd99CPyuiPh2p)).

A. Buying Process and Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

A1. Typical buying cycle length by segment
- Neurostimulation/BCI implantables: For implantable electrode substrates and interconnects, buying cycles are prolonged due to design assurance, biocompatibility verification, sterilization compatibility, process validation, and clinical/regulatory dependencies. Vendor documentation emphasizes accelerated life testing, IPC Class III/IIIA compliance, ISO 13485 traceability, and sterilization compatibility as prerequisites—each requiring qualification time, audits, and engineering iterations. Practically, this translates to multi‑quarter to multi‑year evaluation/procurement cycles tied to device program gates (feasibility → design verification → design validation → clinical builds → commercialization). DYCONEX highlights Class III requirements, accelerated lifetime testing, and full traceability—signals of long validation paths before release to serial supply ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Device Network/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)). Medical Design Briefs advises that ECMs/EMS should be engaged early for DFM reviews, audits, and process capability assessments, reinforcing that supplier selection is embedded in upstream design cycles rather than late-stage tactical sourcing ([Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices)).

- CGM/wearables (external/transdermal subsystems): External transmitter flexes, adhesive patch interconnects, and flexible antennas are still regulated but avoid chronic implantation requirements; supplier selection can align to device refresh cycles and platform extensions. However, given CGM OEM partnership models (e.g., Dexcom–Ypsomed, Abbott–Insulet) and emphasis on reliability and cost, evaluation remains rigorous, with supplier QMS and process audits common prior to AVL approval. Buying cycles are typically shorter than implantables but still multi-quarter as they tie into NPI windows and coordinated product/partner timelines ([PR Newswire – CGM partnerships](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/global-continuous-glucose-monitoring-cgm-market-report-2021-2026-featuring-dexcom-abbott-laboratories-medtronic-and-glysens-301454481.html)).

- Research/clinical-stage BCI: Clinical/research BCI projects often source investigational arrays from specialists like NeuroNexus under investigational-use contexts; cycles are driven by study timelines and sponsor approvals, with bespoke designs configured per protocol—again implying quarters to years rather than weeks. NeuroNexus’s BRAIN platform underscores investigational status and sponsor approvals, consistent with extended evaluation paths ([NeuroNexus BRAIN exhibits](https://braininitiative.nih.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ppp_neuronexus_high_def_electrodes_508c.pdf)).

Evidence indicates there is no “fast” enterprise cycle for implantables; even for external wearables, supplier onboarding, design transfers, and QMS audits introduce multi-quarter timelines. The sources do not provide numeric medians; thus, time-to-close ranges are presented qualitatively. ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices); [PR Newswire – CGM partnerships](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/global-continuous-glucose-monitoring-cgm-market-report-2021-2026-featuring-dexcom-abbott-laboratories-medtronic-and-glysens-301454481.html); [NeuroNexus BRAIN exhibits](https://braininitiative.nih.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ppp_neuronexus_high_def_electrodes_508c.pdf))

A2. DMU size and role composition
The DMU for substrate/electrode module sourcing in regulated medtech is cross‑functional and formal. Recurring roles include:

- R&D/Design Engineering (Electrical, Materials, Packaging)
- Manufacturing Engineering/Process Development
- Quality Assurance/Quality Engineering (QA/QE)
- Regulatory Affairs/Clinical (esp. for implantables)
- Sourcing/Strategic Procurement
- Supplier Quality/Incoming Inspection
- Operations/Planning
- Executive Program Management; occasionally Business Unit GM/VP for final signoff

Medical Design Briefs emphasizes early engineering collaboration (DFM), production/testing capability checks, and ongoing reviews of yields/field data, implying involvement from engineering, quality, and supply chain leadership. EMS/CDMOs marketing (EMSG, CO‑AX, GMI) stress ISO 13485, FDA registration (where applicable), process validation, and traceability, indicating that Supplier Quality and Regulatory interface deeply with vendor qualification. Implantables (Class III-type) add sterilization/materials/biocompatibility experts. - For external CGM subsystems: Electrical/Hardware Engineering Managers, Platform Leads, and Sourcing Category Managers often initiate supplier scouting for flex interposers, antennas, and patch connectors to meet cost/miniaturization targets within the next platform cycle. A4. Typical evaluators/blockers
- Supplier Quality Engineering (SQE) and Quality Assurance (QA) are primary technical/compliance evaluators and can block vendors over missing ISO 13485 certification, inadequate IPC Class III/IIIA evidence, lack of biocompatibility/sterilization compatibility documentation, or insufficient traceability/capability validation. DYCONEX’s marketing directly addresses these criteria (Class III standards, validated processes, traceability), underscoring their blocker status in the DMU ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Device Network/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

- Regulatory Affairs can block implant‑adjacent or implant‑direct components if supplier materials/coatings lack biocompatibility records (e.g., ISO 10993 testing via the OEM or supplier dossiers) or if sterilization methods compromise material integrity. Market Prospects notes medical PCBs require protective coatings (e.g., Parylene) and must pass environmental durability testing to mitigate patient risk—a regulatory concern that can halt qualification ([Market Prospects – medical PCBs: coatings/testing](https://www.market-prospects.com/articles/the-new-role-of-pcbs-in-smart-medical-devices)).

- Program Management/Finance can block for schedule risk if vendor cannot align to NPI/clinical build windows; Medical Design Briefs stresses throughput/capability alignment to program needs, implying schedule-based blocks are common ([Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices)).

A5. Budget-holder and executive-approver titles
- Budget holders typically include BU Program Directors and Operations/Supply Chain leadership for tooling/NRE and COGS line items; executive approvers often include the BU VP/GM and VP Operations/Sourcing for entering new strategic suppliers (especially for implant-class components). EMS/CDMO providers highlight full product/box build and traceable assembly services under ISO 13485/FDA registration, suggesting budget interfaces with Operations and Supply Chain leaders who own make/buy and supplier awards ([CO‑AX medical electronics](https://coaxinc.com/markets/medical-electronics/); [EMSG medical PCB](https://emsginc.com/industries-we-serve/medical-industry/); [GMI medical CDMO](https://www.gmisolutions.com/industries-served/focus-healthcare)).

A6. Approval chain stages and common failure points
- Stages: (1) Technical discovery/DFM reviews; (2) QMS and process audits (ISO 13485, IPC Class III, traceability, sterilization compatibility); (3) Prototype/Pilot qualification (FAI/PPAP-like documentation, reliability testing); (4) Commercial/legal review; (5) AVL/award and ramp; (6) Ongoing quarterly business/quality reviews. Medical Design Briefs describes ongoing manufacturer–OEM collaboration, including biweekly production yield reviews and field data monitoring, consistent with a structured approval and maintenance cycle ([Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices)).

- Failure points: (a) Insufficient documentation/traceability; (b) inability to meet Class III/IIIA or sterilization/material requirements; (c) weak accelerated life data for implants; (d) late demonstration of throughput/capability; (e) misalignment to NPI timelines; (f) cost non-competitiveness vs. certified incumbents. DYCONEX’s positioning on accelerated life testing, traceability and Class III standards reflects these historical failure modes and the need to pre‑empt them with robust evidence ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Device Network/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

B. Budget Structure and Commercial Reality

B7. Budget structure norms (OPEX vs CAPEX; centralized vs distributed; recurring vs project)
- For component supply and CDMO services, spend is predominantly project-based and COGS‑linked (OPEX) once in production, preceded by CAPEX/NRE (tooling, fixtures, test development, validation) during development. EMS/CDMOs advertise turnkey support including prototyping to full production and box-build, suggesting mixed NRE plus per‑unit commercial structures that roll into production OPEX/COGS post‑validation ([EMSG medical PCB](https://emsginc.com/industries-we-serve/medical-industry/); [GMI medical CDMO](https://www.gmisolutions.com/industries-served/focus-healthcare)).

- Budget centralization: For implantables and regulated wearables, budget authority is typically centralized within the BU program’s capital plan and Operations/Supply Chain COGS governance, with Engineering controlling NRE releases and Operations/Procurement controlling recurring spend awards—consistent with the EMS guidance that the ECM should improve buying performance and collaborate on throughput and costs ([Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices)).

B8. Budget ownership norms by function
- NRE/CAPEX: R&D Engineering and Program Management typically own NRE release for DFM/proto/validation; Operations/Manufacturing Engineering may own fixtures and test‑equipment CAPEX. EMS/CDMOs’ NPI and engineering collaboration messaging implies engineering/program ownership of early spend ([Medical Design Briefs – evaluating ECMs](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/29100-evaluating-electronics-contract-manufacturers-for-medical-devices)).

- Recurring COGS/OPEX: Operations/Supply Chain and Strategic Sourcing own supplier awards and recurring unit pricing, with Supplier Quality accountable for ongoing quality cost/PPM targets. EMS/CDMOs emphasize scalable production and logistics, aligning with Operations budget governance ([GMI medical CDMO](https://www.gmisolutions.com/industries-served/focus-healthcare)).

C. Use Cases, Pricing, and Selection Dynamics

Strategic Implications and Entry Pathways for LG Innotek (LGIT)

Foundational insight: What wins in medical substrate/electrode supply
The most compelling through-line across sources is that medical‑grade substrate/electrode suppliers differentiate on regulated process maturity (ISO 13485; IPC Class III/IIIA), documented material systems (LCP/PI with noble metals and electrode finishes), sterilization compatibility, and rigorous reliability validation (accelerated lifetime/soak, bend/flex endurance), backed by full traceability. DYCONEX’s positioning codifies the benchmark. Engineering literature reinforces material choices (Pt, Au, Ti; PEDOT:PSS; polyimide, PDMS, silk) and the feasibility of ultrathin multilayer LCP structures via standard PCB processes, offering a technology path that aligns well with LGIT’s 10 µm‑class HDI sophistication—provided medical QMS and validation investments are made ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Device Network/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/); [MedDeviceOnline/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/biocompatible-flexible-substrates-for-directly-implantable-applications-0002); [Medical Design Briefs LCP substrates](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/32810-using-lcp-based-flexible-substrates-for-medical-smart-sensor-applications); [Frontiers – biocompatible materials](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nanotechnology/articles/10.3389/fnano.2025.1686599/full)).

Where LGIT can defensibly participate first (near-term)
- External CGM subsystems and wearables: Supply high‑density flexible interposers, transmitter flexes, rigid‑flex interconnects, and flexible antennas for CGM transmitters and skin‑patch connectors. These components are safety‑critical but not chronically implanted, reducing the immediate biocompatibility/sterilization bar relative to implant leads. The wearable glucose potentiostat case and CGM partnership ecosystem suggest robust demand for reliable, thin, high‑volume flex circuits around sensors/transmitters. LGIT’s existing 10 µm HDI capability and mass‑manufacturing heritage fit these jobs, provided it establishes ISO 13485 QMS, IPC Class III builds, and medical-grade documentation/traceability workflows akin to InsightPCB/FlexiPCB’s certifications—steps that are verifiable in supplier marketing and are baseline for medtech AVL inclusion ([BioRxiv flexible PCB potentiostat](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.24.707657v1.full.pdf); [PR Newswire – CGM partnerships](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/global-continuous-glucose-monitoring-cgm-market-report-2021-2026-featuring-dexcom-abbott-laboratories-medtronic-and-glysens-301454481.html); [FlexiPCB – ISO 13485/IPC Class 3](https://flexipcb.com); [InsightPCB – IPC-6013/ISO](https://www.insightpcb.com/flexible-rigid-flex-pcbs)).

- Investigational/clinical‑stage arrays with established sponsors: Partner as a build‑to‑print HDI substrate provider to clinical‑stage BCI or peripheral neurostimulation developers under the system integrator’s regulatory umbrella (e.g., supplying noble‑metal thin‑film substrates without owning electrode electrochemistry IP). NeuroNexus’s BRAIN platform and market rosters show multiple players sourcing investigational electrodes; a co‑development path with a credentialed electrode OEM can accelerate LGIT’s learning while avoiding immediate direct implant responsibility. This requires LCP/PI thin‑film process adaptation, electrode finish plating stack capabilities, and joint validation protocols with the prime contractor ([NeuroNexus BRAIN exhibits](https://braininitiative.nih.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ppp_neuronexus_high_def_electrodes_508c.pdf); [ResearchAndMarkets – flexible implantable electrode companies](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6226271/flexible-implantable-electrode-market-report?srsltid=AfmBOorsUWfpkV4twVdQwfGzbEpp_aechZZ-87xVUw5Xd99CPyuiPh2p)).

Medium‑term (staged toward implantables)
- Transition to implant‑adjacent subassemblies: After ISO 13485 and medical IPC Class III are established and audited, expand into implant‑adjacent flex interconnects used within IPG (implantable pulse generator) enclosures or as replaceable subassemblies subject to sterilization but not chronic tissue contact. Build sterilization compatibility evidence and reliability dossiers progressively, guided by DYCONEX’s documented practices (accelerated life, Class IIIA where applicable), and by Medical Design Briefs’ LCP process guidance ([DYCONEX Medtech Implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [Medical Design Briefs LCP substrates](https://www.medicaldesignbriefs.com/component/content/article/32810-using-lcp-based-flexible-substrates-for-medical-smart-sensor-applications)).

This mirrors DYCONEX’s offerings and requires robust materials science plus Class III documentation. Protective coatings (e.g., Parylene) and electrochemical surface preparation (e.g., roughened Pt, IrOx) should be validated per target therapy. Only after pilot clinical supply under a prime contractor should LGIT consider direct implant OEM contracting ([MedDeviceOnline/DYCONEX whitepaper](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/biocompatible-flexible-substrates-for-directly-implantable-applications-0002); [Market Prospects – Parylene and testing](https://www.market-prospects.com/articles/the-new-role-of-pcbs-in-smart-medical-devices)).

Conclusion
For LG Innotek, the opportunity in CGM, neurostimulation, and BCI flexible substrates and electrode modules is sizable, but success depends on entering where its HDI capabilities translate into immediate OEM value under medtech process constraints.
