Competitive intelligence analysis for advanced medical device substrates, flexible circuit components, and electrode modules (CGM, neurostimulation, BCI)

Date: March 19, 2026

Executive summary

LG Innotek (LGIT) brings world-scale expertise in high-density substrates (e.g., FC‑BGA) and flexible interconnect technologies originally deployed in smartphones/IT and automotive sensing. The company is openly pivoting from “parts” to integrated “solutions,” reorganized around optics (sensing), package (substrates), and mobility (control) — with the package solution business singled out as LGIT’s most profitable growth engine ([Korea Times, 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/tech/2026/01/133_373653.html)). At the same time, medical OEMs and their CDMO partners are moving toward turnkey, miniaturized, long‑life implantables and connected wearables, which depend on thin, flexible, high‑density electrode and substrate technologies — the exact intersection of LGIT’s core strengths. However, entry into these regulated markets requires ISO 13485‑aligned quality systems under the FDA’s newly harmonized Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR), credible biocompatible material systems, and partnerships to fill gaps in biosignal IP and finished‑device experience ([FDA QMSR, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)).

This report synthesizes market demands and the competitive field across component suppliers and CDMOs serving three target device classes — continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and brain-computer interfaces (BCI). It analyzes competitors’ capabilities, geographic footprint, partnerships, and regulatory posture using only the provided sources, then maps defensible entry points for LGIT. Key findings:

- Demand drivers: The CGM market is expanding (approx. $5.15B in 2023 to $7.7B by 2030, per GlobalData cited by ClinicalTrialsArena), with implantable approaches (e.g., GlucoTrack CBGM) moving toward long‑life, minimally obtrusive sensors where zero‑lag intravascular sensing requires robust micro‑interconnects and electrodes ([ClinicalTrialsArena, 2026](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/glucotrack-eyes-human-trials-for-implantable-blood-glucose-monitor)). Neurostimulation and BCI leaders (e.g., Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Nevro, Neuralink, Synchron) increasingly rely on ultra‑reliable micro‑interconnects and flexible arrays for long‑term implantation and high channel density ([The World of Implantable Devices – Pain category, 2012–2026 index](https://www.implantable-device.com/category/conditions/pain/)).
- Supply chain and CDMO consolidation: Active implant system CDMOs (e.g., Cirtec Medical) are securing upstream partnerships (e.g., Ilika’s solid‑state battery electrodes to Cirtec) and downstream OEM manufacturing mandates (e.g., GlucoTrack selected Cirtec to manufacture its implantable CBGM), signaling a preference for integrated suppliers with implantable‑grade microelectronics, power, and assembly capabilities ([ProactiveInvestors, 2026](https://www.proactiveinvestors.com/companies/news/1048513/ilika-ships-first-commercial-electrode-batch-to-cirtec-medical-1048513.html); [BioSpace, 2024](https://www.biospace.com/glucotrack-selects-cirtec-medical-as-manufacturer-for-its-implantable-continuous-blood-glucose-monitor)).
- Technology fit: LGIT’s 10‑µm‑class patterning, thin flexible packaging, BLE modules for medical wearables, and automotive‑grade sensing and control provide an excellent technical foundation for CGM sensor substrates, neurostim arrays, and BCI electrode interposers — but medical entry hinges on ISO 13485/QMSR conformity, validated implantable materials, and a medical manufacturing footprint (cleanrooms, traceability, sterilization support) ([PRNewswire, 2020](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lg-innotek-develops-the-worlds--smallest-bluetooth-low-energy-module-301092828.html); [LG Innotek product pages, 2026](https://www.lginnotek.com/company/intro.lgit?locale=en); [FDA QMSR, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)).
- Regulatory bar: The FDA’s QMSR now anchors U.S. requirements to ISO 13485, raising the bar on supplier selection and audits. OEM recalls (e.g., Stryker’s LIFEPAK 1000 AED recall record) show that quality system failures propagate severe commercial risk, underscoring why OEMs award volume only to ISO 13485‑certified suppliers with proven medical process validation ([FDA – LIFEPAK 1000 recall record, 2025](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=214086); [FDA QMSR, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)).

Strategic conclusion: LGIT should enter as a specialist substrate and flexible‑electrode module supplier — initially through strategic partnerships and “build‑operate‑transfer” programs with top CDMOs that already manufacture implantables (e.g., Cirtec, MST/DYCONEX) — while standing up an ISO 13485/QMSR‑compliant medical line. Start with CGM sensor substrates and ex‑plant flexible wearables (fastest regulatory wins), then progress to implantable neurostim and early BCI electrode substrates via OEM‑led pilot programs. Pair this with selective in‑licensing or co‑development of biocompatible thin‑film stackups, and leverage LGIT’s BLE/edge‑module know‑how for value‑added subassemblies in CGM and neurostimulation controllers. This balances speed to market with regulatory de‑risking and long‑term defensibility ([BioSpace, 2024](https://www.biospace.com/glucotrack-selects-cirtec-medical-as-manufacturer-for-its-implantable-continuous-blood-glucose-monitor); [DYCONEX, 2026](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants); [FDA QMSR, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)).

Market overview and requirements

Device classes and demand signals

- CGM: Market growth from ~$5.15B (2023) to $7.7B (2030) per a GlobalData model cited in ClinicalTrialsArena. Emerging implantable CGMs target long sensor life, minimal external wearables, and once‑only calibration. GlucoTrack reports zero‑lag sensing via an intravascular lead connected to a subcutaneous implant, with a ~20‑minute implantation technique borrowed from cardiovascular device practice — a profile that depends on robust micro‑interconnect substrates and biocompatible electrode stacks ([ClinicalTrialsArena, 2026](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/glucotrack-eyes-human-trials-for-implantable-blood-glucose-monitor); [Nasdaq/GlobeNewswire, 2023](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/glucotrack-announces-preclinical-testing-with-implantable-continuous-glucose-monitor)). Cirtec was selected as manufacturing partner, signaling OEM preference for CDMOs experienced in implantables and miniaturized assemblies ([BioSpace, 2024](https://www.biospace.com/glucotrack-selects-cirtec-medical-as-manufacturer-for-its-implantable-continuous-blood-glucose-monitor)).
- Neurostimulation (pain and beyond): Device leaders (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott, Nevro) are cataloged extensively in The World of Implantable Devices “Pain” section, indicating a large installed base and active innovation cycle. Spinal cord stimulation (SCS) advances include remote monitoring/programming (e.g., Biotronik study), illustrating connectivity and data as differentiators layered on top of trusted implantable electronics and lead/electrode reliability — again underpinning the importance of high‑reliability miniature substrates and electrodes ([Implantable‑device.com, 2012–2026 index](https://www.implantable-device.com/category/conditions/pain/); [Biotronik SCS utility study, 2026](https://www.implantable-device.com/2026/01/13/biotronik-study-reveals-utility-of-remote-monitoring-and-remote-programming-in-scs/)).
- BCI: Clinical and translational players (e.g., Neuralink, Synchron) appear in medtech catalogs alongside neuromodulation, but their electrode arrays push channel counts and spatial resolution further, demanding ultra‑fine interconnects and flexible, biocompatible thin‑film stacks. While the dataset does not provide detailed specs for these BCIs, their inclusion in the implantable devices repository underscores a growing ecosystem that values precision flexible electronics and validated implantable materials ([Implantable‑device.com – Pain index shows Neuralink, Synchron among neuromodulation/BCI players](https://www.implantable-device.com/category/conditions/pain/)).

Technical and regulatory requirements for substrates/electrodes

- Implantable electronics must function for years in moist, electrochemically aggressive environments. Success depends on controlled material systems, interconnect integrity, and validated processes aligned with ISO 13485 and IPC Class 3 for zero‑failure applications — the specialty domain of DYCONEX (MST Group), a reference supplier for implantable HDI PCBs, rigid‑flex, and specialty electrode solutions ([DYCONEX – Medtech implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants)).
- FDA’s QMSR harmonizes U.S. Quality System Regulation with ISO 13485:2016, and enforcement began in 2026 (content current as of 2/2/2026). This alignment makes ISO 13485 a practical baseline for U.S. OEMs and their component/CDMO suppliers. It also strengthens expectations around purchasing controls, component traceability, process validation (IQ/OQ/PQ), design controls, labeling, and medical device reporting (MDR) responsibilities across the supply chain ([FDA QMSR](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr); [FDA overview of device regulation](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/overview-device-regulation)).
- Recalls and MDRs are a persistent reality in medical devices (e.g., Stryker LIFEPAK 1000 Class 2 recall posted in 2025), emphasizing how OEMs will scrutinize supplier quality systems to avoid adverse events and postmarket corrective actions. Component suppliers are expected to show robust CAPA, supplier controls, and sterilization‑ready materials where applicable ([FDA LIFEPAK 1000 recall record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=214086)).

LG Innotek baseline positioning

- Capabilities: LGIT manufactures package substrates (including FC‑BGA) and tape substrates for high‑resolution electronics, with fine patterning and micro‑via laser processing, and has commercialized the world’s smallest BLE module for IoT/wearables including medical patches/hearing aids — all pointing to high‑density, low‑power, miniaturized interconnect leadership transferable to CGM/wearables and potential implantable subassemblies ([LG Innotek product pages](https://www.lginnotek.com/company/intro.lgit?locale=en); [PRNewswire – BLE module](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lg-innotek-develops-the-worlds--smallest-bluetooth-low-energy-module-301092828.html)).
- Strategic shift: LGIT is “no longer a parts company,” pivoting to integrated “solutions” with the package solution business called out as the most profitable segment; the company will increase investment and capacity in 2026 and beyond. LGIT’s sensing and control solutions for vehicles and robotics (e.g., 5G satellite module; partnerships with Boston Dynamics; Aeva and radar investments) show it can bundle hardware, software, and middleware — an approach that maps well to medtech as OEMs seek module‑level suppliers ([Korea Times, 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/tech/2026/01/133_373653.html); [PRNewswire UK – 2025 press materials](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news/lg-innotek)).
- Substrate roadmap: LGIT has piloted and is ramping FC‑BGA for high‑performance semiconductors, investing ~$1.1B to expand substrate and camera module output; supply is forecast to be tight through 2027, underscoring scale and process rigor that medtech OEMs often seek in strategic suppliers ([KED Global, 2023](https://www.kedglobal.com/tech,-media-telecom/newsView/ked202301300020)).
- ESG and materials innovation: LGIT debuted a smart IC substrate that removes precious metal plating (palladium/gold) by using a new material, lowering environmental impact and cost sensitivity — a conceptual advantage as medical OEMs increasingly weigh sustainability in supplier selection ([ReadMagazine, 2025](https://readmagazine.com/industries/semiconductor-electronics/lg-innotek-debuts-next-gen-smart-ic-substrate)).

C. Strategic implications for LG Innotek

Defensible roles in the medical device supply chain

- Near‑term (12–24 months): Component supplier for medical‑grade flexible substrates and electrode interposers
  - Focus on CGM sensor substrates (subcutaneous; semi‑implantable) and wearable flexible interposers/antennas, where LGIT’s 10‑µm fine patterning, micro‑vias, and BLE module experience translate directly. Partner with an ISO 13485‑certified CDMO (e.g., Cirtec, MST/DYCONEX for implantables; Resolution or Argon for wearables and catheterized assemblies) to co‑validate material stacks, biocompatible surface finishing, and sterilization compatibility. This approach accelerates entry while building internal QMSR conformance ([PRNewswire – BLE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lg-innotek-develops-the-worlds--smallest-bluetooth-low-energy-module-301092828.html); [MDO – CDMO profiles](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/mdm-west-preview-anaheim-medtech-suppliers-cmos-vendors/); [DYCONEX](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants)).
- Mid‑term (24–48 months): Module supplier for neurostimulation leads and early BCI electrode substrates
  - As ISO 13485/QMSR systems harden, co‑develop thin‑film electrode modules (arrays, feedthrough interposers, lead bodies) with implantable CDMOs or select OEMs. Leverage LGIT’s materials innovation (e.g., precious metal‑free substrate learnings) to propose stabilized, lower‑cost, or ESG‑advantaged interconnect stacks. Offer system‑in‑package options that integrate sensing, BLE/antenna, and power management for external controllers or rechargeable neurostim can‑tethers ([ReadMagazine – smart IC substrate](https://readmagazine.com/industries/semiconductor-electronics/lg-innotek-debuts-next-gen-smart-ic-substrate); [MDO – Cirtec specialization](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/mdm-west-preview-anaheim-medtech-suppliers-cmos-vendors/)).

Go‑to‑market and capability roadmap

1) Quality and regulatory foundation
- Implement ISO 13485 across a segregated medical line aligned with FDA QMSR (supplier controls, DMR/DHR, risk management per ISO 14971, software validation where applicable). Prepare for OEM audits and for‑cause inspections; align labeling/MDR responsibilities and complaint handling in supplier agreements ([FDA QMSR](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr); [FDA – Overview of Device Regulation](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/overview-device-regulation)).

CDMO exemplars (Resolution, MGS) demonstrate expected facility capabilities ([MDO – 2025/2026](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/mdm-west-2025-preview-medtech-suppliers-anaheim-show/)).

2) Technology and materials integration
- Co‑develop implant‑grade stackups with partners experienced in long‑term in‑body reliability (e.g., DYCONEX/MST for HDI PCBs/electrodes; Cirtec for implantable assemblies). Validate interconnect stability (moisture ingress, electrochemical stress), conductor adhesion, parylene/PVDF/PI passivation, and platinum‑group electrode interfaces where needed ([DYCONEX – Medtech implants](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants)).
- Extend LGIT’s BLE/wireless modules into medical subassemblies (e.g., CGM readers, patch controllers) with rigorous RF emissions/EMC control — leveraging the company’s differentiated RF and antenna design abilities cited in the BLE module announcement ([PRNewswire – BLE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lg-innotek-develops-the-worlds--smallest-bluetooth-low-energy-module-301092828.html)).

- Mirror the Ilika–Cirtec model: sign a co‑development/supply agreement with a battery innovator or electrode‑materials startup to bundle LGIT substrates with next‑gen power or sensing layers for implantables; pilot with one CGM OEM (e.g., CBGM entrants) and one neuromodulation OEM (or CDMO) to produce design history files and validation packages suitable for OEM design controls ([ProactiveInvestors – Ilika/Cirtec](https://www.proactiveinvestors.com/companies/news/1048513/ilika-ships-first-commercial-electrode-batch-to-cirtec-medical-1048513.html); [BioSpace – GlucoTrack/Cirtec](https://www.biospace.com/glucotrack-selects-cirtec-medical-as-manufacturer-for-its-implantable-continuous-blood-glucose-monitor)).

4) Commercial strategy
- Target BOM line items where value is high and commoditization low: implantable electrode arrays/interposers; CGM sensor substrates (with integrated traces/micro‑vias and bonding pads); flexible hermetic feedthrough interposers; wearable BLE/RF interposers with medical coatings. Offer “design‑to‑manufacture” support that large EMS/CDMOs market today to reduce OEM NPI risk ([Mordor Intelligence – EMS early engineering](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/electronic-manufacturing-services-for-healthcare-and-medical-device-market)).

Risk considerations and mitigations

- Certification gap: Without ISO 13485/QMSR alignment, OEMs will limit LGIT to non‑critical prototypes. Fast‑track certification and hire experienced medical QA/RA leadership; consider auditing against AAMI TIR102 mapping to ensure CFR 21 coverage under ISO 13485 ([FDA QMSR – resources](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)).
- Materials/biocompatibility risk: Implantables demand validated polymers, adhesives, and conductor stacks; co‑develop with implantable specialists (DYCONEX/MST) and run GLP‑compliant biocompatibility testing where indicated (per ISO 10993 series; not in dataset, but implied common practice). Use sterilization and aging studies upfront ([DYCONEX – implant context](https://dyconex.com/medtech-implants)).
- Commercial risk: Compete with entrenched incumbents; differentiate via density, cost/ESG (precious‑metal‑free concepts), and speed. Leverage LGIT’s scale in FC‑BGA to guarantee supply (tight through 2027) and negotiate volume commitments with OEMs/CDMOs ([KED Global – FC‑BGA outlook](https://www.kedglobal.com/tech,-media-telecom/newsView/ked202301300020); [ReadMagazine – substrate innovation](https://readmagazine.com/industries/semiconductor-electronics/lg-innotek-debuts-next-gen-smart-ic-substrate)).

Opinionated assessment

Given the evidence, LG Innotek’s strongest near‑term competitive position is as a specialist supplier of medical‑grade flexible substrates/electrode interposers and as a module partner for BLE/RF interposers in CGM and neurostimulation system controllers. The company’s track record in ultra‑fine patterning, micro‑vias, and system‑in‑package RF aligns with core unmet needs in thin CGM sensor stacks and compact neurostim/wearable electronics. However, absent visible ISO 13485/QMSR credentials and implantable material validations, LGIT should not attempt to go direct to implantable OEMs with finished electrode arrays at first. Instead, it should leverage high‑trust CDMOs (e.g., Cirtec; MST/DYCONEX) as channel multipliers while rapidly maturing a dedicated medical manufacturing/QMS foundation. Sustained investment in ESG‑oriented substrate materials (e.g., precious‑metal‑free plating innovations) could become a pricing and sustainability differentiator in volume programs over the next 3–5 years, especially in CGM wearables and high‑mix neurostim cables/modules.
