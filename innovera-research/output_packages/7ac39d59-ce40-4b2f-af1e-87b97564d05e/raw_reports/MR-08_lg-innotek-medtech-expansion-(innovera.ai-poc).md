Executive Summary

LG Innotek (LGIT) has world-class chip-on-film (CoF) and flexible PCB fabrication capabilities at 10 µm-level resolution for IT and automotive applications. The company seeks entry into high-value medical segments where thin, flexible, high-density electrode substrates are foundational: continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and brain-computer interfaces (BCI). This report evaluates adoption-stage markers, segment-by-segment maturity, buyer familiarity and friction, adoption/expansion patterns, and path dependencies that can enable or block expansion for advanced substrates and electrode modules across North America and Europe (initial) with global expansion. We synthesize market indicators from thin-film/flexible implantable electrodes, neuromodulation electrodes, and flexible electronics in healthcare. We conclude with a supply-chain positioning strategy for LGIT and an overall maturity assessment with explicit guidance on whether “land-and-expand” go-to-market fits.

Key findings:
- Neuromodulation and CGM show mainstream to late-early adoption for high-density flexible substrates/electrodes; BCI remains early-stage but accelerating with clinical-stage firms specifying flexible implantable arrays (e.g., Neuralink, Precision Neuroscience) ([QY Research, 2026](https://www.qyresearch.com/reports/6158366/flexible-implantable-electrodes); [IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/nerve-stimulation-electrodes-market-forecast-points-higher-toward-2035-driven-by-neuromodulation-expansion/)).

- Buyer familiarity in neuromodulation is high; electrodes are strategic, and OEMs know the category (Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific are global leaders). BCI buyers are sophisticated but processes are still evolving; they frequently co-develop specifications with suppliers. CGM OEMs are highly exacting but keep sensing IP in-house; flexible interconnects and substrates are qualified via stringent supplier controls ([IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/nerve-stimulation-electrodes-market-forecast-points-higher-toward-2035-driven-by-neuromodulation-expansion/); [Trefis, 2026](https://www.trefis.com/data/companies/BSX)).
- Expansion motions are compatible once a supplier is on the AVL and processes are validated; however, ISO 13485 certification, biocompatible materials (e.g., LCP/PI), noble-metal electrode finishes, and full traceability with supplier audits are mandatory and create high initial friction, especially for implantables ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/); [Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

The recommended supply-chain entry points for LGIT are:
1) Near-term: non-implantable CGM transmitter and interconnect flexes (electronics-grade flexible circuits) and flexible hybrid electronics for wearable modules, leveraging existing IT/auto-grade precision while building ISO 13485-compliant QMS on a dedicated line.
2) Medium-term: co-development and CDMO partnerships for thin-film electrode subassemblies with biocompatible LCP/PI, noble-metal plating, and electrode coatings (with a partner or acquired capability).
3) Long-term: direct participation in implantable electrode arrays for neurostimulation and BCI after ISO 13485 certification and process validation; land with clinical-stage pilots and expand to commercial platforms.

Overall maturity assessment: The category is in late-early to early-mainstream adoption for neuromodulation and CGM components, and early adoption for BCI flex arrays. Expansion-led motions are feasible but require a staged approach: land via qualified, lower-risk wearable modules; secure ISO 13485 and biocompatible electrode stack capabilities; then expand into implantables and multi-program awards.

4. Adoption Friction vs. Familiarity Indicators

4.1. Familiarity indicators

4.2. Friction indicators
- Certifications and QMS: ISO 13485 alignment and robust supplier-control systems (traceability, audits, CAPA, change control) are essential; lack thereof is a deal-breaker for many OEMs. Electronics suppliers must demonstrate audit readiness and maintain declarations (e.g., RoHS/REACH) and traceability to lot/batch level ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).
- Materials/biocompatibility: Implant-grade LCP/PI substrates with embedded noble metals and electrode coatings (Pt, PtIr, IrO2, Ti) are required; coating reproducibility and adhesion under in vivo conditions are critical. LGIT currently lacks these stack/process validations ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).
- Post-market obligations: Suppliers to implantables may be drawn into CAPA/root-cause and field actions; OEMs need vendors prepared for long lifecycle support and documentation. Bar for switching suppliers is correspondingly high ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).

5. Typical Adoption Path Patterns

5.1. Land-and-expand dynamics in medtech components
Medtech hardware components rarely follow bottoms-up team adoption; instead, design-in decisions are made by cross-functional engineering, quality, and regulatory teams with procurement governance. However, the “land-and-expand” principle can apply within OEMs once a supplier achieves AVL status and successfully supports one program; expansion proceeds across adjacent programs, platforms, and geographies via QBRs, multi-site rollouts, and additional use cases—consistent with enterprise land-and-expand best practices adapted to regulated hardware contexts ([Re:Work, 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/post-sale-management/land-and-expand-model)).

Typical pattern:
- Land: Pilot or EVT/DVT builds on a single program (e.g., a CGM transmitter flex or a lead subassembly), with predefined success criteria (yield, reliability, accelerated aging, electrical performance).
- Validate: IQ/OQ/PQ-equivalent process validation for the component line, full traceability, and supplier audit pass. Establish change-control and documentation flows.
- Expand: Add SKUs on the same platform (e.g., different size/form factor), then cross over to adjacent programs (e.g., sacral neuromodulation, spinal cord leads) leveraging the same validated line, and eventually to new materials/coatings as trust develops ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/); [Re:Work, 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/post-sale-management/land-and-expand-model)).

5.2. Centralized purchasing and supplier governance compatibility
Enterprise procurement norms increasingly emphasize standardized, interoperable, and modular procurement architectures, supplier performance tracking, and SRM—behaviors that enable multi-year expansion once the supplier is integrated into the governance model. These norms reduce re-qualification friction for similar SKUs and help suppliers scale within accounts if they continuously meet KPIs (cost, quality, delivery) ([Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market)).

6. Buying-Behavior Compatibility with Expansion

6.1. Why expansion can work post-entry
- Procurement centralization and governance: Large enterprises prioritize supplier networks capable of meeting Quality, Cost, Logistics, Development, Management (QCLDM) metrics and standardized architectures. Once a supplier demonstrates performance and process maturity under governance, additional awards across divisions/sites become easier ([Caterpillar Careers, 2026](https://careers.caterpillar.com/en/jobs/xml/?rss=true); [Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market)).
- Regulatory inertia favors incumbents: The cost and risk of supplier change in implantables are high; thus, once qualified, suppliers enjoy stickiness and can expand within the account. This both increases customer lifetime value and suits expansion-led motions with multi-year roadmaps ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).

6.2. Where expansion is constrained
- Proprietary sensing IP (CGM): OEMs retain core sensor designs; suppliers are limited to interconnects/subassemblies. Expansion is possible (more SKUs, generations, geographies), but scope remains bounded; high-value sensing stacks may remain in-house or with long-term partners ([Precedence Research, 2026](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/flexible-electronics-in-healthcare-market)).
- Implantable electrode stack competency: Without proven biocompatible stacks and coatings, expansion into implantables is blocked; to overcome, suppliers must invest in materials, surface treatments, and validation expertise or partner with incumbents ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

7. Path Dependencies That Block Expansion

7.1. Regulatory and quality dependencies
- ISO 13485 certification and audit readiness: Many OEMs require it for critical components; lack thereof prevents onboarding or restricts to non-critical assemblies (e.g., non-patient-contact wearables). Supplier audits, CAPA, and traceability are ongoing obligations, not one-time gates ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).
- Change-control burden: Switching suppliers or altering stack-ups/coatings can trigger revalidation (accelerated aging, biocompatibility, reliability), delaying expansion. OEMs prefer incremental changes on validated lines ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).

7.2. Technical stack dependencies
- Biocompatible flexible substrates and coatings: Implant-grade LCP/PI with gold traces and Pt/PtIr/IrO2 electrode surfaces are de facto standards. Establishing reproducible plating/coating, via integrity, and adhesion under physiological stress is non-trivial; entry without these raises risk of underperformance and disqualification ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).
- High-density routing and microvias: 10 µm resolution is advantageous, but implantable stack-ups must balance density with long-term bio-stability and encapsulation; process windows differ from IT/auto and require medtech-specific validation ([Custom Market Insights, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/20/3221327/0/en/Latest-Global-Thin-film-Electrode-Market-Size-Share-Worth-USD-1-595-3-Million-by-2035-at-a-11-8-CAGR-Custom-Market-Insights-Analysis-Outlook-Leaders-Report-Trends-Forecast-Segmenta.html)).

7.3. Commercial dependencies
- Approved vendor list (AVL) inertia: Entry depends on passing rigorous sourcing and qualification; once on AVL, expansion is easier, but getting there demands demonstrated capacity, QMS maturity, and sometimes local/regional support consistent with enterprise norms of supplier management ([Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market); [Caterpillar Careers, 2026](https://careers.caterpillar.com/en/jobs/xml/?rss=true)).

- BCI remains early-stage with many firms in clinical trials building flexible array supply chains; specification and qualification still involve heavy co-development and rapid iteration rather than commodity buy ([QY Research, 2026](https://www.qyresearch.com/reports/6158366/flexible-implantable-electrodes)).

11. Buying-Behavior Detail: Friction, Familiarity, and Governance

11.1. Buyer familiarity
- Neuromodulation and CGM procurement/engineering teams are familiar with flex and electrode suppliers; they evaluate via strict metrics (impedance stability, mechanical fatigue, bio-compatibility, FMEA outputs). This lowers education burden but raises bar on process capability ([IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/nerve-stimulation-electrodes-market-forecast-points-higher-toward-2035-driven-by-neuromodulation-expansion/)).

11.2. Friction sources
- Supplier qualification and audit expectations: ISO 13485, traceability, RoHS/REACH compliance, ongoing surveillance; CAPA maturity is scrutinized. Wearables require robust supplier controls; implantables demand deeper evidence packages ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).
- Stack/finish requirements: LCP/PI, Au traces, electrode platinum-family coatings with proven long-term stability—these are the table stakes; absence is a blocker ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

11.3. Governance supports expansion
- Enterprise procurement and SRM architectures emphasize standardized modules, API-like supplier data flows (performance, risk), and regional/global scalability; this environment, once an entrant is qualified, enables structured multi-SKU, multi-program expansion and governance-backed supplier development ([Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market)).

12. Where LGIT Can Defensibly Participate (Supply Chain Positioning)

12.1. Near-term (0–12 months): Wearable CGM and external neuro devices
- Products: High-density flexible circuits/interconnects for CGM transmitters and external neuromodulation accessories; flexible hybrid electronics subassemblies.
- Requirements: Stand up a dedicated ISO 13485-aligned production line with end-to-end traceability, lot/batch records, supplier controls, and documented process controls; establish med-grade documentation, DHR/DMR equivalents, and change-control gates.
- Rationale: Matches LGIT’s CoF/10 µm flex strengths; lowers biocompatibility complexity vs. implantables; provides fastest route to AVL onboarding and initial revenue ([Precedence Research, 2026](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/flexible-electronics-in-healthcare-market); [eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).

12.2. Medium-term (12–24 months): Co-developed implantable subassemblies
- Products: Biocompatible flexible carriers (LCP/PI) for leads/cuff electrodes; begin electrode metallization journey (Au traces; partner for Pt/PtIr/IrO2 surfacing).
- Partnerships: JV or strategic alliance with an implantable electrode/coating specialist to leapfrog into electrode finishes and bio-coating reproducibility; co-locate process validation.
- Rationale: Accelerates credibility for implantables by combining LGIT density/precision with proven medtech materials/finishes; reduces time-to-certification and derisks initial clinical builds ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

12.3. Long-term (24–48 months): Full implantable electrode arrays and BCI substrates
- Products: End-to-end implantable flexible electrode arrays with noble-metal electrode finishes and encapsulation; BCI microelectrode arrays for cortical/subcortical interfaces.
- Investments: Complete ISO 13485 certification; electrode plating/coating lines; biocompatibility and accelerated aging labs; robust statistical process control tailored to medical reliability.
- Rationale: Captures the fastest-growing and highest-value segment (thin/flexible implantable electrodes); positions LGIT to win multi-year platform awards as an integrated CDMO ([QY Research, 2026](https://www.qyresearch.com/reports/6158366/flexible-implantable-electrodes); [Custom Market Insights, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/20/3221327/0/en/Latest-Global-Thin-film-Electrode-Market-Size-Share-Worth-USD-1-595-3-Million-by-2035-at-a-11-8-CAGR-Custom-Market-Insights-Analysis-Outlook-Leaders-Report-Trends-Forecast-Segmenta.html)).

13. Go-to-Market and Expansion Sequencing

13.1. Land-and-expand in regulated hardware
- Start small: Pilot with a single CGM or external neuromod application; define objective success criteria (yield, electrical performance, reliability).
- Document success: Internal case studies for OEM stakeholders; QBR participation and executive visibility—consistent with best practices for enterprise expansion adapted to component supply ([Re:Work, 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/post-sale-management/land-and-expand-model)).
- Multi-year roadmap: Year 1—land in CGM flex; Year 2—add SKUs and co-develop implantable carriers with partner; Year 3—launch own electrode-coating implantable arrays for early BCI/neuro indications. Governance-backed procurement (SRM/S2C integrations) facilitates cross-program expansion if KPIs hold ([Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market)).

13.2. Risk mitigation
- Compliance-first operations: Treat ISO 13485 and traceability as core frameworks; align supplier audits and surveillance; build CAPA muscle—these reduce expansion frictions and accelerate AVL additions ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).
- Material/finish validation: Partner early for electrode coatings; build in-house capability with tight SPC and surface analytics; establish redundant lines to meet OEM risk expectations ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).

14. Overall Maturity Assessment and Expansion Fit

14.1. Maturity assessment
- CGM (wearables): Early-mainstream to mainstream component adoption; stable leader OEMs; supplier competition on quality/cost; strong fit for LGIT near-term entry with med-grade governance. Maturity: High adoption, medium regulatory burden—expansion feasible post-AVL ([Precedence Research, 2026](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/flexible-electronics-in-healthcare-market)).
- Neurostimulation (implantables): Early-mainstream with growing indications and sophistication (directional, closed-loop). High buyer familiarity and established supplier ecosystems; barriers are high for new entrants without implantable-grade stacks/coatings. Maturity: Moderate-high adoption with high barriers—expansion possible after significant capability build or partnerships ([IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/nerve-stimulation-electrodes-market-forecast-points-higher-toward-2035-driven-by-neuromodulation-expansion/)).
- BCI (implantables): Early-stage but accelerating; co-development norm; buyers favor innovation speed and materials science collaboration. Maturity: Early adoption—suitable for pilot-led entry, with expansion tied to clinical progression ([QY Research, 2026](https://www.qyresearch.com/reports/6158366/flexible-implantable-electrodes)).

14.2. Fit for expansion-led GTM
- Yes—with caveats. Expansion-led motions align with enterprise procurement governance and regulatory inertia favoring incumbent suppliers. However, expansion only follows robust initial compliance (ISO 13485, traceability) and medtech-specific process validation. A staged entry (wearables → co-developed implantable carriers → full implantable arrays) maximizes probability of expansion across programs and indications while de-risking certification hurdles. OEM buying norms (SRM, centralized governance) amplify expansion potential once LGIT demonstrates sustained performance and compliance maturity, consistent with modern enterprise land-and-expand playbooks adapted to regulated components ([Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market); [Re:Work, 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/post-sale-management/land-and-expand-model); [eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).

15. Actionable Recommendations for LGIT

- Build compliance foundation now: Stand up an ISO 13485-aligned dedicated medical line; implement lot/batch traceability, CAR/CAPA workflows, and supplier audits; prepare technical files for OEM audits ([eWorkOrders, 2026](https://eworkorders.com/cmms-industry-articles-eworkorders/ultimate-oem-guide/)).
- Target CGM and external neuromod electronics first: Pursue non-implantable flexible modules and FHE subassemblies with top CGM OEMs to land quickly and learn medtech governance at scale ([Precedence Research, 2026](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/flexible-electronics-in-healthcare-market)).
- Secure implantable materials/finish capability: Fast-track partnerships or acquisitions for LCP/PI biocompatible flex and electrode coatings (Pt/PtIr/IrO2); co-locate validation to accelerate implantable qualifications ([Medical Device Network—DYCONEX, n.d.](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/contractors/electronics/dyconex1/)).
- Build a clinical co-development pipeline: Engage BCI/neuro startups in North America/Europe for pilot array programs; prioritize use cases with path to commercialization; use pilots to harden implantable process capabilities ([QY Research, 2026](https://www.qyresearch.com/reports/6158366/flexible-implantable-electrodes)).
- Plan expansion roadmaps by account: Treat each OEM as a 3-year expansion plan (pilot SKUs → family SKUs → adjacent indications → new materials/coatings). Use QBRs and governance metrics to demonstrate value and unlock cross-program awards ([Re:Work, 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/post-sale-management/land-and-expand-model); [Global Market Insights, 2025](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/procurement-software-market)).
