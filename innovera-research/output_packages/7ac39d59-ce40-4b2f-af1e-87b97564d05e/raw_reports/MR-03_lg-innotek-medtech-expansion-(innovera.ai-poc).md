Title: Advanced Medical Device Substrates for CGM, Neurostimulation, and BCI: Market Segmentation, Concentration, and Buyer Dynamics for a B2B Component/CDMO Entry

Executive summary

This report analyzes where a high‑precision flexible substrate manufacturer such as LG Innotek (LGIT) can defensibly participate in the medical devices value chain for three high‑value categories—continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and brain–computer interfaces (BCIs)—and how the markets segment, concentrate, and buy. We synthesize technical evidence from peer‑reviewed literature on electrode and flexible substrate requirements with market structure and growth data across BCI and medical device contract manufacturing to identify the most attractive subsegments, buyer roles, and concentration risks.

Key findings

- Segment size and growth. The global BCI market is projected at roughly US$2.40B in 2025, growing to US$6.16B by 2032 (14.4% CAGR), with non‑invasive solutions comprising ~60% of 2025 revenues; implantable BCI implants alone are estimated at US$351.3M in 2025 growing to US$1.18B in 2035 (12.9% CAGR) ([Coherent Market Insights, 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/brain-computer-interface-market-poised-141800272.html); [Future Market Insights, 2025](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/brain-computer-interface-implant-market)). The wearable patch market (a proxy for CGM/adhesive electronics and related substrates) is estimated between US$12.16B and US$13.12B in 2025 and projected to US$24.75B by 2034 (8.26% CAGR), with connected patches accounting for ~65% by 2025–2032 ([Straits Research, 2025](https://straitsresearch.com/report/wearable-patch-market); [Coherent Market Insights, 2025](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/market-insight/wearable-patch-market-2891)). Medical EMS/ODM and ISO 13485 contract manufacturing for healthcare are growing at ~7–8% CAGR globally, underpinning sustained outsourcing demand and opening a CDMO path for new entrants ([Market.us, 2025](https://market.us/report/ems-odm-market/); [Mordor Intelligence, 2026](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/electronic-manufacturing-services-for-healthcare-and-medical-device-market)).
- Technical pull for flexible, high‑density substrates. Modern neuroelectrode systems increasingly rely on biocompatible flexible polymers (polyimide, parylene C) and high‑density metallization (e.g., Pt, IrOx, TiN; PEDOT:PSS) to reduce tissue damage, impedance, and chronic failure—creating demand for 10‑µm‑class lithography, multilayer flexible interconnects, and stable encapsulation stacks ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5); [Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9); [Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1348434/full); [Lab on a Chip/RSC, 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/lc/d5lc00058k)). Flexible micro‑ECoG and depth arrays (e.g., µSEEG) explicitly use polyimide/parylene C substrates and PEDOT:PSS to capture higher‑fidelity signals with lower tissue disruption than traditional cylindrical electrodes—matching LGIT’s thin‑film strengths ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9)).
- Demand concentration and buyer power. For implantable neuro devices, spend concentrates among a short list of OEMs (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Abbott Neuromodulation, Synchron, Neuralink/Blackrock/Paradromics/Precision), large IDNs/hospitals as end‑users, and a handful of ISO 13485 CDMOs. EMS/ODM markets are moderately concentrated (top five ~55% share), and OEMs show low “stickiness” to any one supplier—indicating strong buyer leverage and price pressure for undifferentiated components ([BusinessResearchInsights, 2026](https://www.businessresearchinsights.com/market-reports/ems-and-odm-market-120174); [VentureOutsource, n.d.](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/first-tier-ems-odm-consolidation-why-manufacturing-merger-acquisition-not-happening)).
- Strategic implication for LGIT. The fastest, lower‑risk entry point is B2B component modules for connected CGM/patch‑type devices (flex circuits, CoF interposers, antennas/inductors, stacked sensor interconnects) while parallel‑building a medical QMS and ISO 13485 line. A near‑adjacent step is neuromodulation lead bodies and paddle/µECoG electrode‑substrate modules co‑developed with OEMs or via a CDMO JV. A longer‑horizon bet is flexible intracranial arrays (µECoG/µSEEG) where substrate/microfabrication quality materially drives device performance and switching costs ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5); [Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9); [Frontiers, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1348434/full)).

E. Strategic fit and entry options for LG Innotek

E1. Where LGIT’s capabilities match technical pull

Evidence from translational neuroelectronics points to three technical “pulls” that align with LGIT’s 10‑µm‑class CoF/flexible PCB strengths:

- Biocompatible flexible substrates (polyimide/parylene C) with multilayer metallization and vias at high density
  - Thin‑film µECoG/µSEEG and cortical grids rely on polyimide/parylene C with high electrode density to minimize tissue displacement and maximize signal coverage ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9); [Frontiers, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1348434/full)).
- Stable electrode interfaces and low‑impedance coatings
  - Pt/Ir, IrOx, TiN, and conducting polymers (PEDOT:PSS) improve charge injection and reduce impedance; chronic stability depends on adhesion stacks and encapsulation quality—fabrication rigor critical ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5)).
- Adhesion/encapsulation stacks to prevent delamination and moisture ingress
  - Robust polyimide–metal–dielectric interfaces and long‑term encapsulation are required for chronic implants; adhesion failure is a known mode of chronic device failure ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5)).

Conclusion: LGIT can create durable differentiation by integrating tight‑tolerance flex fabrication (10‑µm lines/spacing), electrode metallization/coatings, and medical‑grade adhesion/encapsulation stacks on polyimide/parylene C for both long‑wear patches and implantable arrays.

E2. Beachheads and productization pathways

- Beachhead 1 (near‑term; lowest regulatory friction): Connected CGM and clinical‑grade wearable patch modules
  - Offerings: medical‑grade FPC/FHE substrates, CoF interposers, ultra‑thin inductive coils/antennas, laminated sensor interconnects, battery tabs, and sterilization‑compatible encapsulants.
  - Rationale: Hospitals/homecare are leading end‑users; connected patches ~65% share; ISO 13485 EMS capacity is growing and receptive to new suppliers with robust process control ([Coherent Market Insights, 2025](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/market-insight/wearable-patch-market-2891); [Mordor Intelligence, 2026](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/electronic-manufacturing-services-for-healthcare-and-medical-device-market)).
  - Critical path: Establish ISO 13485 line and biocompatibility test panels (ISO 10993); co‑develop with CGM/patch OEMs to lock in mechanical stack (adhesives + flex).

- Beachhead 2 (mid‑term): Neuromodulation lead bodies and paddle/µECoG electrode substrates
  - Offerings: flexible polyimide paddle electrodes with Pt/Ir/TiN sites, shielded multilayer interconnects, robust adhesion/encapsulation stacks; manufacturing of micro‑ECoG grids for cortical mapping and investigational neurostim applications.
  - Rationale: Materials stacks (polyimide/parylene C, electrode coatings) match LGIT’s thin‑film strengths; OEMs value suppliers who can co‑engineer adhesion/encapsulation to extend chronic lifetimes ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5); [Frontiers, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1348434/full)).

- Beachhead 3 (long‑term): BCI implantable arrays (µECoG/µSEEG interposer‑substrates) and packaging interconnects
  - Offerings: high‑channel‑count thin‑film arrays (e.g., ≥1,024 channels), CoF/FFC harnesses for cranial connectors, integrated reference/ground shielding, PEDOT:PSS/IrOx deposition, hermeticizable flex feedthroughs.
  - Rationale: Flexible depth and cortical arrays are advancing into human studies and require scalable fabrication; few suppliers can guarantee high yield at electrode pitches ≤30–50 µm with stable encapsulation ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9); [Lab on a Chip/RSC, 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/lc/d5lc00058k)).

F. Technical landscape highlights informing component design

- Electrode–electrolyte interface and impedance
  - Linear and nonlinear properties at the interface drive stimulation safety and recording quality; coatings like PEDOT:PSS and IrOx increase effective surface area and lower impedance ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5)).

- Materials for chronic stability
  - Pt/Ir, Pt black, IrOx, TiN, and conducting polymers each offer trade‑offs in charge injection and mechanical robustness; encapsulation and adhesion to polyimide/parylene C are critical failure points over time ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5)).

- Flexible substrates reduce tissue reaction
  - Flexible arrays (polyimide/parylene C) conform to brain surfaces, minimizing motion‑induced damage vs rigid silicon; µSEEG and µECoG platforms explicitly demonstrate lower tissue disruption than cylindrical electrodes in rodent implants and are being tested in humans ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9); [Frontiers, 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1348434/full)).

- High‑density arrays in humans
  - Clinical‑grade high‑density arrays (Neuropixels, µECoG) show feasibility for large‑scale single‑unit and LFP recordings, but rigid probes can compromise chronic stability—reinforcing the opportunity for flexible, high‑channel substrates fabricated with fine lithography ([Lab on a Chip/RSC, 2025](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/lc/d5lc00058k)).

Appendix: Detailed evidence excerpts (selected)

- Flexible µSEEG fabrication choices (polyimide/parylene C) and PEDOT:PSS electrodes enable single‑unit recording in large animals/humans with less tissue damage vs cylindrical electrodes—validating demand for high‑density flexible substrates ([Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43727-9)).
- Chronic electrode stability depends on coatings and adhesion; PEDOT films, iridium oxide, and platinum black are widely reported to reduce impedance and improve recording quality, while failures often arise from delamination and encapsulation breach—underscoring the role of substrate fabrication and packaging processes as core value drivers ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-023-01021-5)).
