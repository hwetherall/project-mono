B3. Supply chain, certifications, and quality gates

- ISO 13485 and medical supply chain integration
  - ISO 13485 requires annual audits, formal supplier management, and embedded CGMP principles; many electronics contract manufacturers hold ISO 13485 even when OEMs keep CGMP accountability. Component suppliers with ISO 13485 materially de-risk OEM audits and accelerate qualification for clinical-grade wearables and implantables. (Matric, n.d.) ([Matric, n.d.](https://www.matric.com/medical-device-supply-chain))

- Implantable-grade PCB/FPCB competitors and benchmarks
  - DYCONEX positions as a leading PCB/FPCB manufacturer for medical devices, specializing in flexible, rigid-flex and high-reliability HDI interconnects meeting IPC Class 3—benchmarks LGIT must meet or exceed for implantable programs. (DYCONEX, n.d.) ([DYCONEX, n.d.](https://dyconex.com/))

- CDMO strategy and early engagement
  - OEMs increasingly seek CDMOs with end-to-end competencies (design, component manufacturing, assembly, sterilization/packaging, and regulatory documentation), consolidating vendor lists for scalability and quality uniformity. CDMOs are involved earlier (prototyping through validation) to manage complexity, reduce risk, and enable faster production pivots. (Alira Health, 2025–2026; Medical Device Online, 2020; Advanced Manufacturing, 2020) ([Alira Health, 2025–2026](https://alirahealth.com/education-hub/cdmo-medtech-market-trends-2025-and-2026/); [Medical Device Online, 2020](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/trends-in-medical-device-contract-manufacturing-what-medtech-oems-need-to-know-0001); [Advanced Manufacturing, 2020](https://www.advancedmanufacturing.org/industries/medical/manufacturers-expand-presence-in-medical-device-production/article_021bc53d-d3d9-4a8d-bb5f-a5cdb3df8a56.html))

J. Conclusion and opinion on venture claims

Based on recent analyst categorizations and regulatory developments, the defensible market boundary for LGIT encompasses: (1) clinical-grade wearable substrates and interconnects for CGM systems, (2) implantable neurostimulation electrode substrates/modules (DBS, SCS, VNS), and (3) emerging BCI electrode arrays for clinical-stage systems. Canonical frameworks (Wearable Medical Devices, Medical Electronics, Smart Implants, and Implantable Neurostimulator Electrodes) provide clear inclusion rules and highlight the critical difference between consumer-grade vs. clinical-grade wearables and between conventional vs. connected implants. North America remains the initial priority region, with Europe next and global expansion feasible via OEM programs and CDMO partnerships. (Coherent Market Insights, 2025; Future Market Insights, 2026; The Business Research Company, 2026) ([Coherent Market Insights, 2025](https://www.coherentmarketinsights.com/market-insight/wearable-medical-devices-market-841); [Future Market Insights, 2026](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/implantable-neurostimulator-electrode-market); [The Business Research Company, 2026](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/wearable-medical-devices-global-market-report))

However, the venture’s year-3 objectives—~$350M revenue, >30% operating margin, and >20% IRR/payback ≤3 years—are collectively aggressive for a new entrant lacking ISO 13485 certification, implantable-grade process IP, and finished-device experience. Industry evidence points to increasingly strategic, vertically integrated CDMO relationships, stringent iCGM and Class III quality controls, and entrenched Tier-1 ecosystems (e.g., Medtronic–Abbott integration), all of which lengthen qualification cycles and heighten supplier scrutiny. A credible pathway exists under a hybrid strategy: fast-tracking clinical-grade wearables (CGM transmitter/patch subsystems) while concurrently investing in implantable electrode capabilities, ideally through a bolt-on acquisition (~$35M) of an ISO 13485-certified interconnect/module specialist and/or a strategic CDMO partnership. Under best execution with 1–2 Tier-1 program wins in parallel, ~$150–$250M year-3 revenue could be achievable; reaching ~$350M would require simultaneous capture of a top-2 CGM subsystem and at least one major neuromodulation electrode family with accelerated validations. The >30% operating margin target appears optimistic without proprietary implantable IP or module-level value-add; a realistic medium-term objective would be to lift margins through implantable program mix, process uniqueness, and OEM lock-in over a multi-year horizon. (Medical Device Online, 2020; Advanced Manufacturing, 2020; PRNewswire/Medtronic, 2025; Drug Delivery Business News, 2026; Alira Health, 2025–2026) ([Medical Device Online, 2020](https://www.meddeviceonline.com/doc/trends-in-medical-device-contract-manufacturing-what-medtech-oems-need-to-know-0001); [Advanced Manufacturing, 2020](https://www.advancedmanufacturing.org/industries/medical/manufacturers-expand-presence-in-medical-device-production/article_021bc53d-d3d9-4a8d-bb5f-a5cdb3df8a56.html); [PRNewswire, 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/medtronic-advances-abbott-cgm-partnership-with-fda-submission-of-interoperable-insulin-pump-302437337.html); [Drug Delivery Business News, 2026](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/abbott-fda-warning-letter-libre-cgms/); [Alira Health, 2025–2026](https://alirahealth.com/education-hub/cdmo-medtech-market-trends-2025-and-2026/))

In summary, LGIT can credibly participate in medical device supply chains at the component/module layer for CGM, neuromodulation, and BCI—provided it invests in ISO 13485 QMS, aligns manufacturing to iCGM and Class III controls, and partners/acquires to fill implantable assembly, sterilization, and regulatory documentation gaps. The scale and margin aspirations are attainable only with disciplined program selection, accelerated qualifications via partnerships, and a portfolio mix that increasingly tilts toward high-barrier implantable electrode modules over time.
