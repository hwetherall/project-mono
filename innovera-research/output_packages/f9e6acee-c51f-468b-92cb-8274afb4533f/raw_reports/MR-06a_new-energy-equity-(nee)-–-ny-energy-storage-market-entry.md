Title: Competitor Identification and Market Share Analysis: Distributed Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) in New York State

Date: March 13, 2026

Executive summary

This report evaluates the competitiveness and investability of New York’s distributed battery energy storage market (standalone and solar-plus-storage) for Independent Power Producer (IPP) buyers and for a project-development entry by New Energy Equity (NEE). The analysis integrates state policy evolution (2018–2026), market structure and revenue mechanisms (VDER, Statewide Solar for All, NYSERDA retail incentives, Index Storage Credit), buyer demand signals (recent asset acquisitions and financings), developer competition, and interconnection feasibility indicators. The core finding is that distributed storage in New York—particularly in Con Edison territory (NYISO Zone J)—offers durable and scalable economics when projects stack the state’s retail storage incentives, VDER/Statewide Solar for All (S‑SFA) value, and targeted capacity/DER revenues, but risks rise materially outside downstate regions absent new utility or NYSERDA incentives and as locational VDER adders become exhausted. Downstate interconnection friction remains a gating constraint, but the state’s policy architecture and NY Green Bank participation reduce delivery risk. For NEE, a focused, partnership-led entry targeting downstate 4‑hour community-scale projects (5 MWac per node) with clear line of sight to retail incentives and S‑SFA participation can meet institutional return thresholds under realistic assumptions. Upstate distributed storage may require improved capacity prices and/or additional incentives to consistently pencil relative to downstate alternatives over the 2026–2030 window (with some recent evidence that capacity revenues are improving upstate). The report provides competitor profiles, estimated market shares, routes to market, and a decision framework for NEE. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024; DPS, 2025; Modo Energy, 2024; NineDot/NY Green Bank, 2024–2026; CleanCapital, 2026; Greenbacker, 2023) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf); [New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2024a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [NY Green Bank, 2024](https://greenbank.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Greenbank/Files/Portfolio/GB-tp-ninedot-cs-1-v1_acc-combined.pdf); [NineDot Energy, 2026](https://nine.energy/news); [LinkedIn/CleanCapital, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cleancapital_we-are-excited-to-announce-that-cleancapital-activity-7421564032048062464-bPuK); [Greenbacker, 2023](https://www.greenbackercapital.com/resources/greenbacker-acquires-solar-and-storage-portfolios-across-illinois-maine-and-new-york))

1. Scope, definitions, and methodology

- Industry: Energy / Clean Energy / Distributed Energy Storage Development
- Solution category: Distributed BESS (standalone; and AC-coupled or DC-coupled solar-plus-storage)
- Geography: New York State (with emphasis on Con Edison / Zone J)
- Business model: Project development for sale to IPPs (develop-and-sell) and/or joint ventures with IPPs for long-term ownership
- Target buyer: Independent Power Producers (IPPs), infrastructure funds, utility affiliates, and community solar consolidators
- “Distributed” definition: Distribution-connected BESS projects (front-of-meter and behind-the-meter), typically 1–20 MWac per node, with Con Edison territory assets often at the 5 MWac/4‑hour design point
- Sources: Public commission orders and NYSERDA documents, trade-press reporting, developer and financier announcements, and market research. All data are cited with links.

2. Policy and market context in New York

2.1. Storage targets and incentive structure

- The New York Public Service Commission (PSC) established a 6‑GW by 2030 storage target in its June 20, 2024 Order Establishing Updated Energy Storage Goal and Deployment Policy (Case 18‑E‑0130). The 2024 Order sets a statewide structure spanning bulk, retail (commercial, non-residential, community storage), and residential segments, explicitly funding 3,000 MW bulk (via the Index Storage Credit, ISC), 1,500 MW retail, and 200 MW residential, with total new NYSERDA funding approvals sized to achieve the expanded goal. The retail and residential segments use capacity-based $/kWh incentives (MWh block-style), with a 5 MWac/4‑hour cap per retail project (20 MWh). Projects must be in service by December 31, 2030, with prevailing wage and disadvantaged communities siting requirements, and downstate carve-outs (e.g., at least 30% in Zone J). The Order commits to at least three annual bulk solicitations and sets at least 20% of bulk awards for long-duration (≥8‑hour) storage. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf))

- The PSC’s February 13, 2025 decision approved NYSERDA’s retail and residential storage Implementation Plan and expanded eligibility to resources participating in NYISO’s DER program for the retail incentive. As of April 1, 2024, New York counted ~396 MW operating storage within ~1.3 GW total (operating plus contracted), and NYSERDA still had incentives available for 332 MW under existing programs at that time. The Commission underscored that storage deployments are expected to reduce future statewide electric system costs by nearly $2 billion. (DPS, 2025) ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan))

- DPS and NYSERDA also documented a material increase in projected program costs between the December 2022 6‑GW Roadmap and the March 15, 2024 update, driven by inflation and wholesale capacity price forecasts—an important signal for developers’ capex and incentive requirements. (DPS/NYSERDA filing letter, March 15, 2024) ([New York State Department of Public Service & NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf))

2.2. Revenue mechanisms: VDER, S‑SFA, retail incentives, NYISO markets

- VDER (Value Stack) for distributed assets compensated projects based on time- and location-specific value to the grid. In practice, robust, bankable VDER payments—especially Con Edison’s ~$284/kW‑year signals—drove disproportionate deployment downstate despite higher capex. Modo Energy estimates that Con Edison territory captured ~110 MW (36% of distributed BESS deployment), but by 2024, lucrative locational incentives were largely exhausted, weakening the pure-VDER pathway for new distributed BESS. (Modo Energy, 2024) ([Modo Energy, 2024a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- The Statewide Solar for All (S‑SFA) program channels a portion of community solar and storage revenues to low-income customers, creating an additional, policy-supported revenue stack for distributed storage paired with community solar. NineDot forecasts over $60 million of energy credits delivered over the coming decade from projects they committed to S‑SFA—demonstrating how S‑SFA can facilitate distributed BESS bankability and equity outcomes simultaneously. (Canary Media, 2026) ([Canary Media, 2026](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/ninedot-energy-raises-big-money-new-york-city))

- NYSERDA’s retail and residential storage incentives (approved in the 2024 Order and 2025 Implementation Plan) create a capacity-based incentive designed to fill the gap left as VDER locational adders fade, with segment carve-outs targeted to downstate zones (G–K), DACs, and project timelines through 2030. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024; DPS, 2025) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan))

- For bulk/utility-scale storage, NYSERDA’s Index Storage Credit (ISC) creates a 15‑year Strike-Reference revenue mechanism delivering bankable floor value while allowing upside; at least three annual solicitations are planned starting before June 2025, with at least 20% for long-duration. Although this does not directly apply to most distributed BESS, the ISC will shape the competitive landscape for grid-scale storage and relieve pressure to over-rely on distributed assets to meet the 2030 target. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf))

- NYISO market exposure for merchant storage has historically been insufficient to cover development costs in New York versus markets like ERCOT, reinforcing the value of policy-backed incentives for both bulk (ISC) and distributed (retail incentives, VDER/S‑SFA) assets. Location remains critical: NYC/Long Island present premium revenue opportunities despite higher build costs; upstate regions have trailed historically, though rising capacity price curves point to improving fundamentals. (Modo Energy, 2024b; LinkedIn analysis, 2026) ([Modo Energy, 2024b](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-battery-business-case-grid-scale-storage-buildout-energy-arbitrage-ancillary-services-capacity-market-index-storage-credit-chpe-transmission-project-reserve-margin); [Gaffney, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/joseph-gaffney_energystorage-bess-nyiso-activity-7435076425218449408-9VUM))

2.3. Interconnection and implementation capacity

- Distribution interconnection is a primary gating item for downstate distributed BESS, evidenced by NY Green Bank’s revolving interconnection loan for NineDot—upsized from $25 million to $50 million in June 2024 to support deposits for a growing portfolio. The facility explicitly targets interconnection bottlenecks and underwrites pipeline scale growth. (NY Green Bank, 2024) ([NY Green Bank, 2024](https://greenbank.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Greenbank/Files/Portfolio/GB-tp-ninedot-cs-1-v1_acc-combined.pdf))

- State agencies are building a grid-modernization and DER implementation capacity (e.g., NYSERDA Grid CONNECT briefing), and policy design continues to steer IOUs to procure services from third parties rather than rate-base utility-owned solutions—keeping independent developers central in the delivery chain. (NYSERDA, 2024; Utility Dive, 2021) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Publications/Research/Electic-Power-Delivery/24-33-NYSERDA-Grid-Connect-Grid-Modernization-Briefing.pdf); [Utility Dive, 2021](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-approves-utility-revenue-sharing-for-battery-storage-systems/594207))

4. Revenue stack and project economics: implications for NEE

- Then (2019–2023): Distributed storage economics were anchored by VDER credits—with Con Edison’s $/kW‑year signals sufficient to attract significant capital into NYC community-scale BESS, and developers like NineDot demonstrating replicable 5 MWac/4‑hour siting in small-lot urban contexts. (Modo Energy, 2024) ([Modo Energy, 2024a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Now (2024–2026): With VDER locational adders weakening in 2024, the retail storage incentive (MWh block-style, capacity-based), S‑SFA participation, and selective DER/DR revenue stacking (e.g., aggregator programs) are ascendant. This evolution replaces a single dominant program (VDER) with a more diversified but policy-reliant stack. For merchant-heavy strategies, NYISO revenue remains a weak foundation; policy-aligned structures are necessary to secure financeable floor value in the near term. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024; DPS, 2025; Modo Energy, 2024b) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2024b](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-battery-business-case-grid-scale-storage-buildout-energy-arbitrage-ancillary-services-capacity-market-index-storage-credit-chpe-transmission-project-reserve-margin))

- Near-term variability: DPS/NYSERDA’s 2024 Roadmap update noted cost escalation (inflation, capacity-price forecasts), implying that absolute incentive levels and headroom must be re-tested in models as program budgets are drawn. This also underscores the need to prioritize downstate siting where gross margin potential remains strongest. (DPS/NYSERDA, 2024) ([New York State Department of Public Service & NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf))

- Upstate upside: Updated capacity price curves point to rising capacity revenues across NYCA/Hudson Valley in the 2030s, potentially improving viability for upstate distributed assets—but the investability tends to remain more robust where retail incentives and S‑SFA can be layered alongside any capacity uplift. (LinkedIn analysis, 2026) ([Gaffney, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/joseph-gaffney_energystorage-bess-nyiso-activity-7435076425218449408-9VUM))

- Demand response/VPP aggregation (no- or low-capex customer-side resources)
  - CPower Energy: Aggregates customer load flexibility, offering demand response and DER/VPP participation in NY; claims 6.7 GW customer capacity across 23,000+ sites nationwide and has ongoing New York-focused market updates and programs. For some customers/utilities, DR can substitute for storage capacity, especially in near term.   - The 2025 SEPA/NCCETC snapshot shows 106 VPP/DER actions across 35 states plus DC; DOE targets 80–160 GW of VPP capacity by 2030—implying continued competition for “peak capacity” dollars between VPPs and BESS, though roles are complementary. (pv‑mag/SEPA, 2026) ([pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/02/states-and-utilities-took-106-actions-to-advance-vpps-in-2025-sepa-report/))

- Utility procurement of bulk storage (ISC) or peaker replacements
  - LIPA’s approvals of large BESS (KCE’s 50 MW/200 MWh and 79 MW/316 MWh) demonstrate bulk alternatives. (Energy-Storage.News, 2024) ([Energy-Storage.News, 2024](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-long-island-power-authority-approves-bess-projects-from-developer-key-capture-energy/))

- Managed EV charging and electrification load flexibility
  - Growth in managed EV charging and building load management under VPP programs may reduce the incremental capacity payments available to storage in certain hours, though winter reliability and multi-hour needs sustain a role for batteries. - Onsite C&I solutions (customer-owned storage or backup generation)
  - Generac’s expansion into C&I storage up to ~7 MWh and the lasting appeal of backup generation for resilience are a substitute for some customer sites; however, these typically lack the locational and system-level benefits targeted by VDER/S‑SFA and retail incentives.   - As states advance grid-modernization (e.g., NYSERDA Grid CONNECT), non-wires alternatives can defer capacity needs, moderately competing with distributed BESS where cost-effective. 6. Risks and scenario analysis

6.1. Policy stability and incentive accessibility

- Stability: The 2024 PSC Order and 2025 Implementation Plan institutionalize segment-specific targets and funding—materially reducing policy-risk for 2026–2030 development. Additionally, equity and siting requirements (DAC carve-outs, downstate targets) align with broader CLCPA mandates, reinforcing political durability. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024; DPS, 2025) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan))

- Accessibility: As VDER locational incentives downstate became exhausted in 2024, the retail incentive fills a crucial gap—but budget pacing and queue management will determine practical access. Developers with interconnection and shovel-ready sites (and NY Green Bank support) are best positioned to convert incentive access into COD. (Modo Energy, 2024; NY Green Bank, 2024) ([Modo Energy, 2024a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [NY Green Bank, 2024](https://greenbank.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Greenbank/Files/Portfolio/GB-tp-ninedot-cs-1-v1_acc-combined.pdf))

6.2. Interconnection feasibility and urban siting

- Interconnection is the dominant execution risk for distributed NYC projects. Evidence: dedicated NY Green Bank interconnection financing; NineDot’s use of small-footprint sites and long lead interconnection deposits; Con Edison’s complex interconnection environment. Early, diligent interconnection strategy and capital are must-haves. (NY Green Bank, 2024; NineDot fact sheet) ([NY Green Bank, 2024](https://greenbank.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Greenbank/Files/Portfolio/GB-tp-ninedot-cs-1-v1_acc-combined.pdf); [NineDot Energy, 2025](https://nine.energy/wp-content/uploads/NineDot-Fact-Sheet_Public_January-2025.pdf))

6.3. IPP buyer demand

- Buyer depth exists and is expanding: CleanCapital’s 2026 NYC BESS acquisition, Greenbacker’s NYC portfolio purchase, and NineDot’s ability to raise large-scale construction debt anchored to tax-credit transfers point to robust buyer and lender pools for distributed storage—in particular, those linked to VDER/S‑SFA and retail incentives. (CleanCapital, 2026; Greenbacker, 2023; Energy-Storage.News, 2026) ([CleanCapital, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cleancapital_we-are-excited-to-announce-that-cleancapital-activity-7421564032048062464-bPuK); [Greenbacker, 2023](https://www.greenbackercapital.com/resources/greenbacker-acquires-solar-and-storage-portfolios-across-illinois-maine-and-new-york); [Energy-Storage.News, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers/))

6.4. Merchant revenue exposure

- Pure-merchant distributed storage remains challenging in New York; even for grid-scale, Modo finds “missing money,” and the state introduced ISC to address it. Distributed developers must continue to stack policy-backed revenues (retail incentive, VDER/S‑SFA, DR/DER where available). Upstate may improve with capacity price rises, but downstate remains the bankable core for 2026–2030 given policy carve-outs and peaker-replacement priorities. (Modo Energy, 2024b; Gaffney, 2026) ([Modo Energy, 2024b](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-battery-business-case-grid-scale-storage-buildout-energy-arbitrage-ancillary-services-capacity-market-index-storage-credit-chpe-transmission-project-reserve-margin); [Gaffney, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/joseph-gaffney_energystorage-bess-nyiso-activity-7435076425218449408-9VUM))

7. Opinion: Does New York distributed storage offer durable, scalable economics for NEE?

In our view, yes—with a focused strategy. The architecture established by the PSC’s 2024 Order and the 2025 Implementation Plan provides multi-segment incentives with explicit downstate carve-outs and deadlines. The combination of retail storage incentives, S‑SFA/VDER participation, and emerging DER/VPP revenues form a financeable revenue floor for well-sited 4‑hour distributed projects in Con Edison territory. Competitor and buyer activity corroborate this: NineDot’s $431 million debt raise (28 projects/124 MW), Greenbacker and CleanCapital acquisitions, and NY Green Bank’s interconnection finance are strong proof points. At the same time, VDER locational headroom downstate is waning, making the retail incentive program the critical determinant of deal flow—and interconnection capacity is the biggest practical constraint. Upstate distributed storage can be compelling in specific nodes as capacity prices rise, but widespread bankability is still less consistent than downstate absent new targeted programs. Therefore, NEE should enter New York with a downstate-first, policy-aligned, partner-centric approach, prioritizing interconnection-secured assets and project structures that lock in retail incentives and S‑SFA participation.

8. Recommendations for NEE

8.1. Market entry strategy

- Focus geography and design:
  - Prioritize Con Edison (Zone J) and, secondarily, Zones G–K sites with favorable hosting capacity and land-use viability; deploy standardized 5 MWac/4‑hour designs to qualify under retail incentive caps and match NYC grid needs. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf))

- Revenue stacking:
  - Anchor projects with NYSERDA retail capacity-based incentives; target S‑SFA eligibility and long-term net-crediting relationships with high-credit offtakers (e.g., hospitals, universities) to enhance buyer appeal and bankability. (DPS, 2025; Canary Media, 2026; CleanCapital, 2026) ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Canary Media, 2026](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/ninedot-energy-raises-big-money-new-york-city); [CleanCapital, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cleancapital_we-are-excited-to-announce-that-cleancapital-activity-7421564032048062464-bPuK))

- Interconnection-first development:
  - Build interconnection as the program’s spine; consider NY Green Bank interconnection facilities to reduce working-capital drag and improve pipeline durability. Adopt a gated development process centered on interconnection feasibility and schedule. (NY Green Bank, 2024) ([NY Green Bank, 2024](https://greenbank.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Greenbank/Files/Portfolio/GB-tp-ninedot-cs-1-v1_acc-combined.pdf))

- Channel and partnerships:
  - JV or co-development with established NYC distributed storage originators to accelerate site control and interconnection queue positions (e.g., emulate Borrego-to‑Greenbacker pathway). Pre-market operating portfolios to IPP buyers (e.g., CleanCapital, Greenbacker) to validate exit pricing and tighten underwriting spreads. (Greenbacker, 2023; CleanCapital, 2026) ([Greenbacker, 2023](https://www.greenbackercapital.com/resources/greenbacker-acquires-solar-and-storage-portfolios-across-illinois-maine-and-new-york); [CleanCapital, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cleancapital_we-are-excited-to-announce-that-cleancapital-activity-7421564032048062464-bPuK))

8.2. Financial modeling guardrails

- Returns thresholds:
  - Underwrite using retail incentive plus S‑SFA and conservative NYISO/DR value; treat wholesale arbitrage upside as optionality. Ensure 10–15% downside protection from incentive/pricing shocks through robust EPC and interconnection contingencies, given 2024 cost escalations noted by DPS/NYSERDA. (DPS/NYSERDA, 2024; Modo Energy, 2024b) ([New York State Department of Public Service & NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf); [Modo Energy, 2024b](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-battery-business-case-grid-scale-storage-buildout-energy-arbitrage-ancillary-services-capacity-market-index-storage-credit-chpe-transmission-project-reserve-margin))

- Exit strategy:
  - Design asset packages in 25–40 MWac tranches (5–8 nodes) for sale to IPPs to meet scale thresholds; pre-align with buyer’s VDER/S‑SFA and ESG objectives (e.g., DAC location and peaker displacement). (Greenbacker, 2023; CleanCapital, 2026) ([Greenbacker, 2023](https://www.greenbackercapital.com/resources/greenbacker-acquires-solar-and-storage-portfolios-across-illinois-maine-and-new-york); [CleanCapital, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cleancapital_we-are-excited-to-announce-that-cleancapital-activity-7421564032048062464-bPuK))

- Timeline:
  - Target in-service by 2030 to retain incentive eligibility; embed prevailing wage compliance and DAC siting where feasible for selection advantages. (NYSERDA/PSC, 2024) ([New York State Public Service Commission, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf))

8.3. Go/no-go and gating criteria

- Go if:
  - Interconnection pre-application indicates feasible capacity within 24–36 months COD; candidate sites can accept standardized 5 MWac/4‑hour systems; retail incentive application windows and S‑SFA enrollment are within reach.

- No-go or re-scope if:
  - VDER/S‑SFA and retail incentives cannot be secured; host utility interconnection costs/timeframes exceed budgeted thresholds by >20–25%; offtaker credit quality or community acceptability issues emerge that threaten exit pricing.

10. Conclusion

New York’s distributed storage market has matured into a policy-aligned, financeable segment—especially in NYC—where retail incentives, S‑SFA/VDER participation, and DER aggregation combine to create durable revenue stacks. The 2024 PSC Order and 2025 Implementation Plan codify program architecture through 2030; interconnection readiness and incentive access determine winners. NineDot holds a commanding near-term pipeline in NYC, while CleanCapital and Greenbacker exemplify a liquid buyer base for operating assets. For NEE, the path to scalable, board-acceptable returns lies in:
- Concentrating on 5 MWac/4‑hour distributed nodes in Con Edison territory,
- Securing interconnection early with dedicated capital lines,
- Locking retail incentives and S‑SFA eligibility,
- Packaging projects for institutional IPP buyers,
- And treating merchant/market upside as optionality rather than a core assumption.

Upstate distributed storage merits a selective approach where capacity prices and incentive access improve the revenue stack; broader upstate scaling will likely follow clearer, locationally targeted programs or stronger market revenues. On balance, New York’s distributed storage market presents a timely, durable opportunity for NEE—with disciplined execution around interconnection, incentives, and partnerships.
