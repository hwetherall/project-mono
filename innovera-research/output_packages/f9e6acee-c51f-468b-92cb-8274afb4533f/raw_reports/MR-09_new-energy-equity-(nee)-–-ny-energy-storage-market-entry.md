Title: New York Distributed Energy Storage (BESS) Regulatory and Platform-Policy Landscape (2022–2026): Implications for Solar+Storage and Standalone Development for IPP Buyers

Date: March 13, 2026

Executive summary

This report evaluates whether New York State’s distributed energy storage market (both front-of-the-meter distributed and behind-the-meter) offers durable, scalable economics suitable for long-term entry by a solar developer pursuing a develop-and-sell and/or IPP partnership model. The analysis synthesizes federal and state regulatory regimes, ISO/utility platform policies, interconnection pathways, permitting and code requirements, incentives and tax credits, and cross-cutting trade and labor requirements, with emphasis on changes between 2022 and 2026 that directly affect project access, economics, and buyer demand.

Key findings for Board consideration

- Directionally favorable policy environment but nontrivial execution risk through 2026–2027:
  - New York materially updated its storage policy in 2024 with the NYPSC’s order establishing an updated statewide storage goal (widely cited as 6 GW by 2030) and an evolving programmatic structure under Case 18-E-0130, which continues to shape incentive access and segment definitions for distributed storage. The 2024 order is the primary anchor for state-level policy direction in the 2022–2026 window (NYSERDA, 2024) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf)).
  - NYISO is still in a phased trajectory to full FERC Order No. 2222 compliance by December 31, 2026 (per a FERC-approved extension), but has allowed DER and DERA participation in energy, ancillary services, and capacity on a staggered basis since 2023. The market rules continue to evolve, with a new Commission order affecting NYISO tariffs issued February 27, 2026 (effective March 1, 2026). This creates short-term rule volatility but ultimately expands market access for distributed BESS aggregations (RTO Insider, 2022; NYISO, 2026) ([RTO Insider, 2022](https://www.rtoinsider.com/31211-ferc-gives-nyiso-2026-complete-order-2222-compliance/); [NYISO, 2026](https://www.nyiso.com/tariffs-ferc-orders)).

- Material near-term cost and eligibility uncertainty from federal trade and tax policy:
  - Section 301 tariff increases and broader tariff “stacking” in 2024–2026 are lifting U.S. BESS equipment costs above 2023 levels and causing developers to delay or reprice projects. Analysts expect near-term U.S. storage additions to slow post-2025 without mitigating measures (Utility Dive, 2026) ([Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/trump-tariffs-battery-energy-storage/746849/)).
  - IRA domestic content bonus credit (10 percentage points) is materially more difficult for BESS to achieve under the January 2025 “elective safe harbor” update; DOE-assigned cost percentages for cells (52%) mean grid-scale projects using imported cells will generally miss the domestic content threshold unless U.S.-made cells are deployed. In practice, many distributed BESS will need to underwrite base ITC only (30% with PWA/A requirements met), with the bonus treated as upside rather than base case (Morgan Lewis, 2025; ANZA Renewables, 2025; Treasury, 2025) ([Morgan Lewis, 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/the-state-of-play-for-energy-storage-for-tax-credits); [ANZA Renewables, 2025](https://www.anzarenewables.com/blog/updated-domestic-content-elective-safe-harbor-guidance-whats-changing-in-2025/); [U.S. Treasury, 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2788)).

- Interconnection and siting remain gating factors; code and local law changes add complexity:
  - DPS’s Standardized Interconnection Requirements (SIR) continue to evolve, with smart inverter settings required since January 1, 2023 and equipment certification (e.g., UL 1741 SB) as of December 2025 shaping design and certification schedules for PV+ESS. Queue data is public, but constraints and upgrade costs are highly location-specific (NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).
  - Adoption of the 2025 Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code and the 2025 Energy Conservation Construction Code (ECCCNYS) took effect December 31, 2025, requiring design updates to align with current fire/life safety and energy performance standards—including those affecting stationary batteries and co-located PV systems (NY Housing Association, 2025; Scarinci Hollenbeck, 2026) ([NY Housing Association, 2025](https://www.nyhousing.org/news/adoption-of-the-2025-uniform-fire-prevention-and-building-code); [Scarinci Hollenbeck, 2026](https://scarincihollenbeck.com/client-alert/new-york-seqra-reform-building-codes-2025)).
  - Local governments are adopting BESS-specific ordinances with substantial setbacks and procedural requirements (e.g., Town of Hamden). This patchwork can substantially constrain site availability and timelines for distributed storage, even where state policy is supportive (Town of Hamden, 2025) ([Town of Hamden, 2025](https://www.hamdenny.com/download/battery-energy-storage-systems-local-law/)).

- Demand signals are positive but fragmented across utility DR/DLM, retail Value Stack, and ISO markets:
  - Utilities and NYSERDA continue to promote distributed residential and commercial storage through demand response and programmatic requirements (NYSERDA, 2026). Con Edison reports steady growth in customer-sited storage and ongoing utility-owned deployments in its service territory—indicating persistent buyer interest and interconnection of smaller distributed systems (Con Edison, 2024) ([NYSERDA, 2026](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2026-01-22-Utility-DLM-Program-Overview.pdf); [Con Edison, 2024](https://www.coned.com/en/about-us/media-center/news/2024/09-24/con-edison-customers-shine-during-climate-week)).
  - Order 2222 participation models, once fully enabled in NYISO software by end-2026, expand aggregator access to wholesale markets but add metering/telemetry and coordination obligations with distribution utilities that developers must reflect in data architectures and O&M budgets (VNF, 2020; NYISO, 2026) ([Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets); [NYISO, 2026](https://www.nyiso.com/tariffs-ferc-orders)).

Bottom-line viability for NEE (opinion): New York’s distributed BESS remains investable for an IPP buyer base under a conservative base case assuming (i) base 30% ITC with PWA/A compliance but no domestic content adder, (ii) tariff-elevated equipment pricing through 2026–2027, (iii) programmatic revenue stacking anchored in utility DLM/DR and retail tariff/Value Stack elements, with limited early reliance on merchant NYISO upside until Order 2222 software and tariff features stabilize post-2026, and (iv) proactive site screening to avoid stringent local ordinances and costly distribution upgrades. The most financeable near-term segment is co-located community solar + storage and distribution-connected standalone storage in constrained downstate networks with measurable DR/DLM and retail value, while pure merchant or ISO-only storage is generally not suited to NEE’s “develop-and-sell” profile in 2026 absent contracted revenue or buyer-specific tolling constructs. In all cases, diligence must include a live review of NYISO’s eTariff as updated per the February 27, 2026 Commission order and utility-specific interconnection/standby rates in the target utility territory (NYISO, 2026; NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYISO, 2026](https://www.nyiso.com/tariffs-ferc-orders); [NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

Method and scope note: The February 27, 2026 NYISO Commission order in ER26-713-000 is referenced by NYISO as the latest tariff update; the order text is not included here and must be consulted in the NYISO eTariff viewer for definitive terms (NYISO, 2026) ([NYISO, 2026](https://www.nyiso.com/tariffs-ferc-orders)).

A. Regulatory landscape

A1. Named regulatory regimes affecting the market (federal, state, local)

- Federal wholesale market access and aggregation:
  - FERC Order No. 2222 (2020) requires RTOs/ISOs to enable DER aggregations to participate in energy, capacity, and ancillary services markets; defines DERA as a market participant; requires locational/operational parameters, distribution factors, and coordinated utility review; discourages undue metering/telemetry barriers; and emphasizes ongoing ISO–utility–aggregator coordination (VNF, 2020) ([Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets)).
  - NYISO extension to complete full Order 2222 compliance by December 31, 2026, with phased participation starting in 2023 (RTO Insider, 2022) ([RTO Insider, 2022](https://www.rtoinsider.com/31211-ferc-gives-nyiso-2026-complete-order-2222-compliance/)).

- Federal tax credits and labor requirements under the IRA:
  - Investment Tax Credit (ITC) for standalone storage and solar+storage; 10 percentage point domestic content bonus (subject to complex safe harbor rules); prevailing wage and apprenticeship (PWA/A) requirements for the full 30% ITC credit value (U.S. Treasury, 2025; DOE, 2024; IRS, 2024/2025) ([U.S. Treasury, 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2788); [DOE, 2024](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/articles/webinar-details-how-industry-can-qualify-domestic-content-tax-credit-bonus); [IRS, 2024/2025](https://www.irs.gov/zh-hans/newsroom/treasury-irs-release-guidance-on-the-prevailing-wage-and-apprenticeship-requirements-for-increased-credit-and-deduction-amounts-under-the-inflation-reduction-act)).
  - January 2025 “elective safe harbor” update increases the assigned cost percentage of battery cells to 52% for grid-scale BESS, making domestic content qualification challenging without U.S.-made cells (Morgan Lewis, 2025; ANZA Renewables, 2025) ([Morgan Lewis, 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/the-state-of-play-for-energy-storage-for-tax-credits); [ANZA Renewables, 2025](https://www.anzarenewables.com/blog/updated-domestic-content-elective-safe-harbor-guidance-whats-changing-in-2025/)).

- Federal trade policy (tariffs and FEOC restrictions):
  - Section 301 tariff increases and policy volatility in 2024–2026 raise BESS import costs, with analysts expecting near-term U.S. additions to slow post-2025 (Utility Dive, 2026; Energy-Storage.news, 2026) ([Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/trump-tariffs-battery-energy-storage/746849/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/us-import-tariffs-increase-the-policy-risk-for-bess/)).
  - New FEOC (foreign entity of concern) restrictions added in 2025 to credits including 45Y/48E/advanced manufacturing tighten supply chain compliance, potentially affecting credit eligibility and sponsors’ procurement strategies (Holland & Knight, 2025) ([Holland & Knight, 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/intheheadlines/2025/12/us-renewables-battery-sectors-face-new-supply-chain-restrictions)).

- New York State energy storage framework:
  - NYPSC Order Establishing Updated Energy Storage Goal and Deployment Policy (Case 18-E-0130, June 20, 2024), which updates the statewide storage goal and policy direction, forming the programmatic basis for incentive design and market segmentation across retail and bulk-connected storage (NYSERDA, 2024) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf)).
  - Value of Distributed Energy Resources (VDER) Value Stack remains the framework for DER compensation at the retail interface, influencing solar+storage economics; while the foundational order predates 2022, it remains relevant for storage paired with DG (contextual baseline) (context from multiple DPS sources; see also DPS VG/VDER resources) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Interconnection and utility tariffs:
  - New York’s Standardized Interconnection Requirements (SIR) govern DG/ESS interconnection; recent resources include SIR redlines (March 2025 vs. August 2025), smart inverter settings (required since 1/1/2023), and UL 1741 SB certified equipment lists as of December 16, 2025; DPS publishes utility interconnection queue data (January 2026) (NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).
  - Standby service rates and riders by utility (e.g., Con Edison PSC 10 GR 20, PSC 12 Rates III & IV; Central Hudson SC 14; NYSEG SC 11; National Grid SC 7, etc.) affect BTM economics and must be modeled at project screening (NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Codes, siting, and environmental review:
  - 2025 Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code and 2025 ECCCNYS effective December 31, 2025; updated energy and safety standards apply to new permits filed thereafter (NY Housing Association, 2025; Scarinci Hollenbeck, 2026) ([NY Housing Association, 2025](https://www.nyhousing.org/news/adoption-of-the-2025-uniform-fire-prevention-and-building-code); [Scarinci Hollenbeck, 2026](https://scarincihollenbeck.com/client-alert/new-york-seqra-reform-building-codes-2025)).
  - SEQRA (6 NYCRR Part 617) governs environmental review; targeted reforms are being advanced to streamline housing (indirect signal of state-level willingness to reform permitting more broadly). DEC hosts regulations and guidance (NYSDEC, n.d.; Governor’s Office, 2026) ([NYSDEC, n.d.](https://dec.ny.gov/regulatory/permits-licenses/seqr); [Governor of NY, 2026](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/let-them-build-governor-hochul-unveils-landmark-reforms-cut-red-tape-and-build-more-housing)).
  - RAPID Act regulations (Parts 1100–1102) for major renewable energy and major electric transmission facility siting effective March 9, 2026, potentially accelerating transmission upgrades and renewable siting that can indirectly improve hosting capacity and interconnection feasibility for distributed storage (Cullen & Dykman, 2026; NY DPS, 2026; Lexology, 2025) ([Cullen & Dykman, 2026](https://www.cullenllp.com/blog/new-york-implements-revised-rules-for-major-electric-transmission-infrastructure-permitting/); [NY DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/news/rapid-act-regulations-set-lower-costs-and-accelerate-grid-modernization-new-york); [Lexology, 2025](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c8278f02-90a8-47fb-870b-f725a0c3167a)).
  - Local BESS ordinances may impose large setbacks and process steps, with meaningful impacts on site control viability (e.g., Hamden, NY updated November 6, 2025) (Town of Hamden, 2025) ([Town of Hamden, 2025](https://www.hamdenny.com/download/battery-energy-storage-systems-local-law/)).

A4. Demand-side effects of policy shifts

- NYPSC’s 2024 storage order (Case 18-E-0130): Strengthens the statewide policy foundation and program architecture for storage, signaling continuity of incentive pathways and support through 2030. This underpins pipeline development and IPP demand for projects with clear eligibility and program queues (NYSERDA, 2024) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf)).
- Order 2222 phased market access: Expanded DER/DERA participation in NYISO energy, ancillary services, and installed capacity markets increases the future addressable demand for aggregated distributed storage products that can be monetized by IPPs. However, near-term buyers may discount wholesale market upside until software and tariff features stabilize post-2026 (RTO Insider, 2022; NYISO, 2026) ([RTO Insider, 2022](https://www.rtoinsider.com/31211-ferc-gives-nyiso-2026-complete-order-2222-compliance/); [NYISO, 2026](https://www.nyiso.com/tariffs-ferc-orders)).
- Utility DLM/DR and retail frameworks: Continued utility DR and storage program activity, particularly in Con Edison territory, and persistent customer-sited storage deployments indicate ongoing demand signals for BTM storage paired with retail and DR value streams—attractive to IPPs seeking contracted or semi-contracted revenue layers (NYSERDA, 2026; Con Edison, 2024) ([NYSERDA, 2026](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2026-01-22-Utility-DLM-Program-Overview.pdf); [Con Edison, 2024](https://www.coned.com/en/about-us/media-center/news/2024/09-24/con-edison-customers-shine-during-climate-week)).
- IRA incentives: The base 30% ITC with PWA/A compliance remains a strong driver of demand; however, the domestic content adder is now more difficult to realize for BESS projects without U.S.-made cells, reducing incremental demand acceleration some developers expected (U.S. Treasury, 2025; Morgan Lewis, 2025; ANZA Renewables, 2025) ([U.S. Treasury, 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2788); [Morgan Lewis, 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/the-state-of-play-for-energy-storage-for-tax-credits); [ANZA Renewables, 2025](https://www.anzarenewables.com/blog/updated-domestic-content-elective-safe-harbor-guidance-whats-changing-in-2025/)).

- Tariffs and FEOC: Higher equipment costs and FEOC-linked eligibility uncertainty temper demand by tightening returns, especially on merchant-heavy or thinly contracted projects (Utility Dive, 2026; Holland & Knight, 2025) ([Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/trump-tariffs-battery-energy-storage/746849/); [Holland & Knight, 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/intheheadlines/2025/12/us-renewables-battery-sectors-face-new-supply-chain-restrictions)).

A5. Access-side effects of policy shifts

- Wholesale access: Order 2222 expands access for aggregations but increases metering/telemetry requirements and coordination with distribution utilities. DER aggregators serve as the single point of contact with the ISO and must maintain up-to-date DER rosters and data; distribution utilities can override ISO dispatch to maintain system safety (VNF, 2020) ([Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets)).
- Interconnection: SIR updates, smart inverter settings, and UL 1741 SB listing require compliance-prepared design; DPS queue transparency aids diligence, but network upgrades and hosting capacity constraints remain localized gating items (NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).
- Codes and siting: The 2025 Uniform Code adoption compels projects to meet updated fire/life safety and energy performance standards—particularly salient for BESS enclosure design, clearances, and protective systems; local ordinances can impose setbacks up to 1,000 feet in some jurisdictions, materially shrinking feasible siting areas for distributed, small-footprint systems (NY Housing Association, 2025; Town of Hamden, 2025) ([NY Housing Association, 2025](https://www.nyhousing.org/news/adoption-of-the-2025-uniform-fire-prevention-and-building-code); [Town of Hamden, 2025](https://www.hamdenny.com/download/battery-energy-storage-systems-local-law/)).
- Transmission and macro-permitting: RAPID Act regulations effective March 9, 2026 may shorten schedules for major transmission upgrades, indirectly benefiting distributed storage siting over time by improving grid conditions and reducing upgrade risks (Cullen & Dykman, 2026; NY DPS, 2026) ([Cullen & Dykman, 2026](https://www.cullenllp.com/blog/new-york-implements-revised-rules-for-major-electric-transmission-infrastructure-permitting/); [NY DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/news/rapid-act-regulations-set-lower-costs-and-accelerate-grid-modernization-new-york)).

A6. Compliance cost benchmarks and drivers

Given heterogeneous project scopes and rapidly evolving tariffs and FEOC guidance, precise cost “benchmarks” are project-specific. However, the following cost drivers are material and should be parameterized in financial models:

- ITC compliance cost drivers:
  - PWA/A: Meeting prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements increases labor cost and documentation/verification burden; IRS has committed resources to enforcement (IRS, 2024/2025) ([IRS, 2024/2025](https://www.irs.gov/zh-hans/newsroom/treasury-irs-release-guidance-on-the-prevailing-wage-and-apprenticeship-requirements-for-increased-credit-and-deduction-amounts-under-the-inflation-reduction-act)).
  - Domestic content adder: To qualify in 2025+, most grid-scale/distributed BESS must utilize U.S.-made cells due to the 52% assigned cost share; absent domestic cells, compliance pathways are unlikely to reach thresholds. The adder should not be included in the base case unless supply chain is locked and verifiable (Morgan Lewis, 2025; ANZA Renewables, 2025; U.S. Treasury, 2025) ([Morgan Lewis, 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/the-state-of-play-for-energy-storage-for-tax-credits); [ANZA Renewables, 2025](https://www.anzarenewables.com/blog/updated-domestic-content-elective-safe-harbor-guidance-whats-changing-in-2025/); [U.S. Treasury, 2025](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2788)).

- Tariffs and trade:
  - 2024–2026 tariff increases materially raise BESS equipment landed cost; some analyses suggest U.S. BESS system costs exceed 2023 levels; developers have accelerated completions in 2025 where possible and are otherwise repricing or delaying projects (Utility Dive, 2026; Morgan Lewis, 2025) ([Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/trump-tariffs-battery-energy-storage/746849/); [Morgan Lewis, 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/addressing-tariffs-and-trade-in-energy-storage-projects)).

- Interconnection and equipment compliance:
  - Smart inverter settings, UL 1741 SB certifications, and SIR conformance require engineering revisions and certified equipment procurement, affecting BOS costs and schedules (NYS DPS, 2026) ([NYS DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Codes and local permitting:
  - The 2025 Uniform Code adoption can require design adaptations for BESS enclosures, fire suppression, clearances, and egress, along with additional permitting coordination; local setback rules can drive larger land acquisition, alternative sites, or design complexity (NY Housing Association, 2025; Town of Hamden, 2025) ([NY Housing Association, 2025](https://www.nyhousing.org/news/adoption-of-the-2025-uniform-fire-prevention-and-building-code); [Town of Hamden, 2025](https://www.hamdenny.com/download/battery-energy-storage-systems-local-law/)).

- Data/telemetry and aggregation costs:
  - Under Order 2222, DERA participation involves ongoing telemetry, metering, and data management; RTO/ISOs must avoid undue barriers, but aggregators must maintain robust data pipelines, which imply incremental capex/opex for metering, communications, cybersecurity, and market reporting (VNF, 2020) ([Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets)).

A7. Data privacy and cybersecurity requirements

- Wholesale participation data flows:
  - Order 2222 compels clear data-sharing frameworks among RTO/ISO, DER aggregators, distribution utilities, and retail regulators; aggregators must manage metering/telemetry and ensure ongoing eligibility and operational coordination. While 2222 avoids prescriptive telemetry standards, it requires that ISO/market rules do not impose undue barriers—implying proportionate, risk-based requirements aligned to DER function (VNF, 2020) ([Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets)).

- New York cybersecurity posture (trend signal):
  - New York is increasingly imposing sector-specific cybersecurity requirements (e.g., water/wastewater rules finalized with grants for risk assessments), signaling a trend that could extend to critical energy infrastructure and DER aggregator interfaces over time (StateScoop, 2026) ([StateScoop, 2026](https://statescoop.com/water-wastewater-new-york-cybersecurity-regulations/)).

- Practical implications for BESS developers/aggregators:
  - Data collection, retention, and secure telemetry should be designed to utility/ISO aggregator requirements, with careful handling of customer and operations data. Contractual requirements in utility DR/DLM programs and ISO market participation often include cybersecurity provisions that flow down to project SPVs and vendors (NYSERDA, 2026; VNF, 2020) ([NYSERDA, 2026](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2026-01-22-Utility-DLM-Program-Overview.pdf); [Van Ness Feldman, 2020](https://www.vnf.com/ferc-issues-order-no-2222-to-increase-participation-of-distributed-energy-resource-aggregations-in-organized-markets)).

Conclusion

For a solar developer like NEE aiming to build a distributed storage pipeline in New York for eventual IPP exit, the 2022–2026 period consolidates a supportive policy foundation while exposing projects to significant procurement and rule volatility through at least 2026. A disciplined strategy that (1) prioritizes programmatic revenue, (2) treats ISO wholesale upside as post-2026 optionality, (3) locks base ITC but not the domestic content adder unless U.S.-cells are secured, and (4) aggressively screens for interconnection feasibility and local siting constraints will produce a bankable subset of projects capable of meeting Board hurdle rates. Project-level diligence against the March 1, 2026-effective NYISO tariffs and utility-specific interconnection/tariff regimes is mandatory at IC/MC gates, with sensitivity runs for tariff/trade escalation.
