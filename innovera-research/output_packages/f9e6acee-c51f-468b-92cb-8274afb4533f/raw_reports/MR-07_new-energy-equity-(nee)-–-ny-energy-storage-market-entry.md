Title: Distributed Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) in New York State: Buying Process, Budget Dynamics, and Pricing Landscape for IPP Buyers (Distributed Solar+Storage and Standalone)

Executive summary

New York’s distributed energy storage market presents real but uneven bankability for IPPs and develop‑and‑sell platforms. Bankable revenue today is concentrated in three channels: (1) the Value of Distributed Energy Resources (VDER) value stack and locational incentives in Consolidated Edison territory (partially exhausted and currently facing interconnection headwinds), (2) NYSERDA’s new 15‑year Index Storage Credit (ISC) contracts for bulk/FO‑TM projects (>5 MW) with explicit cost‑escalation protection and defined evaluation timelines, and (3) structured DR/DLM programs where Con Edison has historically paid materially more than upstate utilities. Merchant‑only revenue models are not yet “durable and scalable” for Board‑level returns due to saturation risks in ancillary services and interconnection/permitting friction. For a solar developer like New Energy Equity, a durable entry thesis exists but should be scoped to bankable segments: (a) front‑of‑the‑meter distribution‑connected community batteries in NYC/LI with proven financing pathways, (b) ISC‑backed 5–20 MW distribution‑connected or small transmission‑connected projects in Zones G–K, and (c) carefully underwritten solar+storage projects in upstate locations where land, permitting, and interconnection certainty offset lower VDER/DRV/LSRV values.

Key decision drivers and risks are: (i) ISC contract wins (or lack thereof) as a gating item for leverage and exit valuations, (ii) NYISO interconnection cluster timing and study deposits, (iii) local permitting (108 AHJs with moratoria/bans as of late 2025), and (iv) evolving Con Edison interconnection policy (active industry petition at the PSC in Case 18‑E‑0130). IPP buyer appetite is strong and sophisticated, with institutional capital comparing opportunities across full contracting stacks (EPC/OEM/O&M, optimization/virtual tolling, offtake/RFPs) rather than site control alone. Capital costs for batteries rose in 2024, but pack prices reset lower to $139/kWh in 2023 and are forecast to decline toward $80/kWh by 2030, bolstering forward economics when paired with long‑tenor revenue contracts (15 years for Li‑ion ISC; up to 25 years for non‑Li‑ion).

Overall opinion: Enter NY distributed storage selectively. Focus near‑term development on: (1) ISC‑oriented FO‑TM batteries in Zones G–K sized and staged to clear NYISO’s new cluster process; (2) community‑scale NYC projects with proven bankability (e.g., NineDot model), but with strict gating around Con Edison interconnection relief; and (3) solar+storage configurations upstate only where VDER plus DRV/LSRV still clear hurdle rates under conservative merchant overlays. Avoid merchant‑heavy bets on ancillary services alone; model ancillary upside but do not rely on it for base returns.

Method and sources

This report synthesizes primary program documents and regulatory orders, independent market research (Modo Energy, RFF), law firm briefings on New York storage policy (Foley Hoag; Hodgson Russ), reputable trade journalism (Energy‑Storage.news; PV Magazine USA; Canary Media), transactional evidence (NineDot Energy financing; NY Green Bank portfolio), and cost benchmarks (Mercom/LBNL/NREL; Morgan Lewis). All data points are cited inline.

1. Market context for distributed BESS in New York

1.1 Policy framework and programs

- Case 18‑E‑0130 and Roadmaps. New York’s storage development has proceeded through formal regulatory proceedings and roadmaps in Case 18‑E‑0130 since 2018, requiring program eligibility diligence and alignment with state priorities for any target acquisition or new development ([New York State Energy Storage Roadmap, 2018](https://www.ethree.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/NYS-Energy-Storage-Roadmap-6.21.2018.pdf)).
- 2024 Energy Storage Order and Bulk Implementation Plan. In March 2025, the PSC adopted NYSERDA’s Bulk Energy Storage Program Implementation Plan with modifications, targeting 3,000 MW of new bulk storage by 2030 and establishing the Index Storage Credit (ISC) framework, including maturity requirements aligned to actual development milestones ([Hodgson Russ, 2025](https://www.hodgsonruss.com/newsroom/publications/psc-approves-nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program-implementation-plan)). NYSERDA filed the final plan in April 2025 ([NYSERDA Bulk Energy Storage Program Implementation Plan, 2025](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Clean-Energy-Standard/BES-Program-Final-Implementation-Plan.pdf)).
- ISC procurement specifics. The first ISC solicitation (ISCRFP25‑1) launched July 28, 2025, targeting ~1,000 MW initially (3,000 MW total across three rounds). Contracts: 15‑year tenor for Li‑ion; up to 25 years for non‑Li‑ion; geographic and duration targets (at least 35% in Zones G–K; aiming for 20% long‑duration across all three rounds); in‑service by 12/31/2030, with limited delays allowed if construction has commenced. Evaluation: price (60%) and non‑price (40%); NYSERDA computes a Levelized Net ISC Cost (LNISCC) using its energy/capacity forecasts and assumes 95% availability. The Carried Cost Index Adjustment (CCIA) allows structured inflation protection, particularly for Li‑ion ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Bid Banana ISCRFP25‑1](https://bidbanana.thebidlab.com/bid/f0fRTMnqff5NhooVntCn)).
- Utility procurements. The PSC in March 2023 extended utility in‑service deadlines for large‑scale storage to 2028 (from 2025) and allowed contract terms up to 15 years—important for bankability and IPP exits ([Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).
- Program budget updates. DPS/NYSERDA updated the 6 GW Roadmap in March 2024 for higher costs (inflation; capacity price forecasts), noticed for comment in Case 18‑E‑0130—affecting total budget envelopes and contract reference prices over time ([DPS letter filing updated Roadmap, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf)).

1.2 Distributed incentives and bankable revenue channels

- VDER and locational value. Con Edison’s VDER incentives reached $284/kW‑year for 2‑hour BESS; upstate utilities offered just $18–$70/kW‑year, with upstate BESS revenue 44–86% below the upstate Gross Cost of New Entry of $126/kW‑year. As of November 2025, Con Edison’s locational incentives were 93% exhausted—constraining near‑term new awards in the most lucrative zone ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- DR/DLM differential. Con Edison offered ~$0.85/kWh during DR call windows (summer), versus $0.09–$0.22/kWh upstate, reflecting Zone J transmission constraints and pointing to higher NYC‑centric returns for distributed storage projects able to interconnect and participate ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- VDER mechanics and risks. For <5 MW AC BESS (standalone or solar+storage), energy value depends on wholesale price spreads; capacity value under VDER uses a defined “capacity window” (summer weekdays, 2–7 pm, June 24–Aug 31) similar to the DRV window—shorter‑duration systems risk missing peaks (mitigated by partial discharge strategies), and DRV windows range 234–334 hours/year across utilities ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Program exhaustion and re‑direction to ISC. With Con Edison LSRV capacity largely spoken for and VDER economics more muted upstate, capital is being pulled toward 15‑year ISC contracts, larger project sizes, and transmission‑connected projects. ISC allocates $700 million–$1.42 billion for grid‑scale BESS across 2025–2027, requires projects >5 MW, reserves ~20% for ≥8‑hour storage, and caps 2‑hour systems at ~10%—explicitly favoring larger, longer‑duration systems with economies of scale ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

1.3 Interconnection feasibility and permitting

- NYISO queue reform and clustering. In response to FERC Order 2023, NYISO adopted a batch process (post‑Jan 2023) aiming to cut interconnection timelines ~50% (e.g., projects entering Q3 2024 begin construction by June 2026). Deposits of $100,000–$250,000 now screen for maturity. The first reform cluster holds ~19 GW of BESS, with 2–4 GW considered viable by 2030 given ISC‑contractable revenue. Ten‑hour BESS can earn up to 95% higher capacity payments in high‑need zones (e.g., NYC), equating to ~$2 million extra per year for some projects; applications cluster around NYC/Long Island (Zone J and K) ([Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Distributed interconnection friction in Con Edison territory. Trade groups petitioned the PSC in March 2026 to halt what they called an effective freeze on interconnection of distributed storage in Con Edison territory; the PSC formally sought public comment under Case 18‑E‑0130 (comments due May 4, 2026). This active proceeding is a short‑term gating risk for community batteries and <5 MW VDER‑oriented storage in NYC ([DPS notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument); trade press summary March 11, 2026).
- Local permitting and moratoria. As of November 2025, 108 authorities having jurisdiction (AHJs) had enacted moratoria or bans on grid‑scale BESS, covering ~4,500 mi² (~8% of the state). Parallel pursuit of interconnection and local permits can backfire under the reformed cluster process, leading to costly withdrawals (> $2 million) if moratoria or bans emerge after non‑refundable land/interconnection expenditures. NYC differs: it has structured FDNY/DOB/Con Edison approval pathways that, while rigorous, are known quantities compared with disparate upstate permitting regimes ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk)).
- Wholesale participation for DER aggregations. In April 2024, NYISO launched the nation’s first market rules enabling aggregations of DERs (>10 kW) to participate in wholesale markets, expanding optionality for distributed resources beyond VDER into capacity and ancillary services via aggregation models ([NYISO press release, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/14007178/press-release-der-aggregations.pdf)).

1.4 Evidence of financing and buyer appetite

- Community battery financing at scale. NineDot Energy closed a $431 million construction‑to‑term, tax‑credit‑transfer bridge, and letter‑of‑credit package for 28 BESS projects totaling 124 MW/494 MWh across NYC (rolling CODs through 2027), and enrolled 300 MW in the Statewide Solar For All program—demonstrating bankability of the community battery model in NYC’s constrained grid ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [PV Magazine USA, 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/12/17/ninedot-energy-enrolls-300-mw-of-community-energy-storage-in-new-york/); [Canary Media, 2026](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/ninedot-energy-raises-big-money-new-york-city)).
- Institutional buyers and contracting stacks. Market participants evaluate storage assets across distinct contracting workstreams: equipment supply, EPC and O&M, optimization/virtual tolling, offtake/PPA awards, and active RFP/RFQ/RFI processes—underscoring the need to present a fully contracted or de‑risked stack to IPPs and infrastructure funds. Capital inflows to BESS platforms remain strong (e.g., Greenvolt’s €150 million KKR‑backed boost), which raises competitive benchmarks for sponsor strength and execution capability ([Fractal Energy Storage News, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- State co‑financiers. NY Green Bank’s active products—construction‑to‑term, interconnection bridge, development finance, and warehousing—regularly support distributed storage and community solar portfolios, indicating standard financing pathways for NY projects that can clear underwriting on interconnection, contracts, and site control ([NY Green Bank Portfolio](https://greenbank.ny.gov/Our-Impact/Portfolio)).

1.5 Cost and price anchors

- System‑level capex. Battery capex for PV‑plus‑storage projects rose from $381/kWh (2023) to $458/kWh (2024). PV+storage LCOE adders in 2024 were ~$25/MWh post‑tax credits, ~$36/MWh pre‑credit. NYISO had the highest average utility‑scale solar PPA price in 2024 at ~$59/MWh (regional differences in performance and capex driving variance) ([MercomIndia citing LBNL, 2024](https://mercomindia.com/utility-scale-solar-projects-comprised-54-of-new-capacity-in-the-u-s-in-2024)).
- Retail non‑res storage installs. NYSERDA data show average installed costs for non‑residential retail storage at ~$567/kWh for 2022–2023, up 22% from 2020–2021 averages of $464/kWh (supply chain disruptions), with expected easing as commodity prices recede ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Battery pack prices and timelines. Lithium‑ion battery pack prices fell 14% in 2023 to a record‑low ~$139/kWh and are forecast to decline further to ~$80/kWh by 2030; however, utility‑scale projects are taking 12–18 months to complete—about six months longer—due to supply chain, transformer/substation procurement, and EPC constraints ([Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).

2. Buying process and decision‑making unit (DMU)

2.1 Typical buying cycle length by segment

- NYSERDA ISC procurements (FO‑TM >5 MW). ISCRFP25‑1 defined a structured, multi‑month two‑step process in 2H 2025 and early 2026: eligibility application (September 4, 2025), non‑price submission (December 11, 2025), price submission (January 29, 2026), provisional awards expected Q1 2026, and public disclosure in Q2 2026. This implies a 5–9 month procurement cycle from eligibility to award, excluding post‑award contracting and financing close ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/)).
- Utility storage procurements. Utility‑run RFPs (e.g., historic National Grid bulk storage RFPs) commonly require robust site control documentation and development maturity; contracting lead times can span months to more than a year, especially under current 12–18 month construction cycles and supply chain delays ([National Grid RFP Appendix on Site Control, 2019](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/bulk-energy-storage-request-for-proposals/appendix-c1-site-control.pdf); [Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).
- IPP M&A for distributed/community BESS. Evidence such as NineDot’s portfolio‑level financing suggests lenders’ diligence and IPP/infra investment committees require a fully packaged contract/development stack (interconnection status, land, permits, revenue contracts). Timelines commonly track a 3–9 month window for exclusivity, confirmatory diligence, and financing approval for portfolio transactions, aligned to program windows (e.g., ISC) and interconnection milestones; hard benchmarks are sparse in public domain, but the structured steps in ISCRFP25‑1 provide a reliable time anchor for award‑driven exits ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).

Note: Public “average sales cycle” metrics are not published for this asset category; procurement cycles are program‑defined (ISC) or RFP‑defined (utilities) and M&A timelines vary by asset maturity. Where unavailable, this report references dated milestones from public solicitations and financing closes.

2.2 DMU size and role composition

Across IPP acquisitions and utility solicitations, the recurrent DMU includes:

- Development lead and team: site control, permitting, interconnection, community engagement (e.g., Director of Development roles cover securing sites, due diligence, real estate perfection, environmental/local/state/federal permitting, interconnection coordination, and community/customer engagement) ([TotalEnergies job spec, n.d.](https://jobs.totalenergies.com/en_US/careers/JobDetail/Director-Development-US-Distributed-Generation-Solar-Storage/76136)).
- Technical/engineering: system design, technology selection, fire/life safety compliance (particularly FDNY/DOB in NYC), grid studies, and constructability; coordination with EPC ([TotalEnergies job spec, n.d.](https://jobs.totalenergies.com/en_US/careers/JobDetail/Director-Development-US-Distributed-Generation-Solar-Storage/76136)).
- Finance/project finance: valuation, tax equity/transfer strategy, debt term sheets, NY Green Bank or lender engagement ([NY Green Bank Portfolio](https://greenbank.ny.gov/Our-Impact/Portfolio); [Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html)).
- Legal/contracting: EPC/OEM warranties and performance guarantees, O&M, optimization/virtual tolling, host and lease agreements, interconnection agreements, revenue contracts (VDER enrollment/DR, ISC contract, S‑SFA participation), and regulatory compliance ([Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- Policy/regulatory: monitoring PSC proceedings (Case 18‑E‑0130), NYISO interconnection cluster windows, and local moratoria risk ([PSC notices, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument); [Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk); [Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

In utility solicitations, procurement, system planning, and legal teams are added on the buyer side (e.g., National Grid’s RFP requires detailed site control attestations and interconnection maturity, drawing in real estate and legal) ([National Grid RFP, 2019](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/bulk-energy-storage-request-for-proposals/appendix-c1-site-control.pdf)).

2.3 Typical initiator/champion titles

- Within developers: Director/Senior Director of Development, VP of Development, or Project Development Lead—roles explicitly tasked with “bring[ing] our project portfolio from contract execution to NTP” and coordinating interconnection and permitting activities ([TotalEnergies job spec, n.d.](https://jobs.totalenergies.com/en_US/careers/JobDetail/Director-Development-US-Distributed-Generation-Solar-Storage/76136)).
- Within IPPs: Head of Corporate Development, M&A Director/VP, or Head of Storage—driving pipeline acquisition and RtB/RTB+ deals; evidence from market job postings and the Fractal newsletter’s focus on deal workstreams suggests these titles often sponsor acquisition screens ([Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).

2.4 Typical evaluator/blocker titles

- Evaluators: Interconnection/Transmission Planning Leads; EHS/Fire Safety and Building Code leads (especially FDNY/DOB in NYC); Procurement and EPC risk teams; Revenue/Trading/Optimization heads for merchant overlays ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk); [Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- Blockers: Local AHJs invoking moratoria/bans; utility interconnection teams (Con Edison interconnection freeze context); PSC/NYISO milestone slippage (e.g., missing a cluster window) ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk); [PSC notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument); [Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

2.5 Budget‑holder and executive approver titles

- Budget holders: CFO/Head of Project Finance (for development budgets, deposits, and EPC/OEM commitments), with Development VPs controlling pre‑NTP budgets and contingency envelopes ([TotalEnergies job spec, n.d.](https://jobs.totalenergies.com/en_US/careers/JobDetail/Director-Development-US-Distributed-Generation-Solar-Storage/76136)).
- Executive approvers: Board/Investment Committee of IPPs or infrastructure funds; for utility procurements, C‑level or authorized procurement officers approve long‑term contracts (e.g., 15‑year terms now allowed) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).

2.6 Approval chain stages and failure points

Typical stage gates and where deals stall:

- Site control and permitting: Failure to secure enforceable site control and local permits—especially in moratoria jurisdictions—can strand land and interconnection spend (> $2 million potential exposure) ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk)).
- Interconnection maturity: Missing a cluster window can push CODs by up to two years; inadequate deposits ($100k–$250k) or readiness can halt progress under NYISO’s higher‑bar reform ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Revenue contractability: Inability to secure ISC awards or, in Con Edison territory, interconnection approvals to monetize VDER/DR/LSRV can undermine bankability (and IPP interest). PSC comments on Con Edison policy changes underscore near‑term uncertainty ([PSC notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument); [Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Cost and schedule risk: OEM/EPC lead times (12–18 months vs. plan) and transformer/substation bottlenecks lead to IC slippage and contingency drawdowns that trigger re‑approval at investment committees ([Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).

3. Budget structure and commercial reality

3.1 Budget structure norms (CAPEX vs OPEX; recurring vs project‑based)

- Project‑financed CAPEX with long‑term revenue contracts. Distributed FO‑TM and small transmission‑connected BESS are typically financed on a project basis with long‑tenor contracts (e.g., 15‑year ISC for Li‑ion), supplemented by merchant upside and demand response. Financing structures commonly include construction‑to‑term loans and tax credit transfer bridges (post‑IRA transferability), as evidenced by NineDot’s NYC portfolio ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/)).
- Developer OPEX for interconnection deposits and pre‑NTP. NYISO reform requires higher study deposits ($100k–$250k), favoring well‑funded sponsors and allocating more OPEX early in the lifecycle to secure queue positions and meet maturity requirements ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- O&M and augmentation. Fixed O&M for PV+storage diverges over time due to expected battery augmentation/replacement profiles (NREL ATB notes O&M divergence from base years as batteries are replaced), implying lifecycle CAPEX refresh within the contract term ([NREL ATB Utility-Scale PV+Battery, 2024](https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/utility-scale_pv-plus-battery)).

3.2 Budget ownership norms by function

- Development budgets: Controlled by VP/Director of Development and CFO/Head of Project Finance for land, interconnection deposits, environmental/permitting, engineering studies, and community engagement ([TotalEnergies job spec, n.d.](https://jobs.totalenergies.com/en_US/careers/JobDetail/Director-Development-US-Distributed-Generation-Solar-Storage/76136)).
- EPC/OEM budgets: Managed by procurement and engineering teams under executive approval; contract lifecycle management through EPC and OEM warranties/O&M service agreements is a key diligence item for IPP buyers ([Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- Financing budgets: Construction‑to‑term debt, tax credit transfer (bridge) and equity draws structured by project finance leads and legal counsel; NY Green Bank often participates with interconnection bridge or construction‑to‑term products ([NY Green Bank Portfolio](https://greenbank.ny.gov/Our-Impact/Portfolio)).

3.3 ACV/ARPA/spend benchmarks by segment

- System capex benchmarks. 2024 PV+storage battery capex ~$458/kWh (up from $381/kWh in 2023); PV+storage LCOE adders ~$25/MWh post‑credit and ~$36/MWh pre‑credit; NYISO average solar PPA ~$59/MWh (highest among regions) ([MercomIndia citing LBNL, 2024](https://mercomindia.com/utility-scale-solar-projects-comprised-54-of-new-capacity-in-the-u-s-in-2024)).
- Retail non‑res installed costs. $567/kWh average (2022–2023) for NYSERDA‑incented non‑res retail storage; prior cohort (2020–2021) averaged $464/kWh ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Financing scale example. NineDot’s $431 million financing covers 124 MW/494 MWh community battery portfolio, sized for urban DR and VDER participation; structure includes construction‑to‑term loan, tax‑credit‑transfer bridge, and LOC—indicative of portfolio‑level ACV with multi‑year deployment (not a per‑project benchmark) ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html)).

Note: Public “ACV” or “ARPA” analogs are not standardized for asset finance; use capex $/kWh and contract size/tenor as primary comparators.

3.4 Budget‑range or line‑item benchmarks

- Interconnection deposits. $100,000–$250,000 study deposits under NYISO reform; deposits and security scale with project size and study stage ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- VDER/DR value anchors. Con Edison DR ~$0.85/kWh (summer windows); upstate ~$0.09–$0.22/kWh; Con Edison VDER up to $284/kW‑year for 2‑hour BESS in 2025; upstate $18–$70/kW‑year ([Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

3.5 Replacement cycle/refresh cadence

- Battery augmentation or replacement is typically modeled within 10–15 years, often within the 15‑year ISC tenor for Li‑ion. NREL notes fixed O&M diverges across scenarios due to battery replacement, implying organized mid‑life augmentation assumptions in pro formas ([NREL ATB PV+Battery, 2024](https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/utility-scale_pv-plus-battery)).

3.6 Churn benchmarks

- Not applicable in the conventional SaaS sense; assets are long‑lived, and revenue contracts (VDER, ISC, DR) set the “retention” scope. Public logo/revenue churn benchmarks for BESS vendors in NY are sparse.

3.7 CAC benchmarks

- Not publicly reported for asset developers in NY. Indicatively, interconnection deposits, development studies, and permitting/community engagement comprise major “customer acquisition” equivalents; NY Green Bank and lender diligence add transaction costs—these are project finance transaction costs rather than recurring CAC.

3.8 LTV benchmarks

- LTV is project‑level NPV from contracted plus merchant revenues net of capex/Opex; public LTV metrics are not standardized for this asset class. However, 15‑year contracts (ISC; utility procurement) and longer for non‑Li‑ion (up to 25 years) support higher LTV assumptions than short‑tenor DR‑only strategies ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).

3.9 Payment terms and DSO norms

- Common structures: construction‑to‑term financing with tax credit transfer bridging; milestone‑based EPC payments under fixed‑price/turnkey contracts (contracting stacks noted across equipment supply, EPC/O&M, optimization/tolling) ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- Regulatory alignment to ITC in‑service scheduling. PSC modified Standardized Interconnection Requirements in January 2026 to let utilities prioritize construction for ITC‑eligible DER projects as a group—an indicator that in‑service timing and payment scheduling are aligned to federal tax deadlines, affecting DSO patterns around CODs ([NYS PSC press release, Jan 22, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov)).

4. Use cases, pricing, and selection dynamics

4.1 Dominant jobs‑to‑be‑done by segment

- NYC/LI community batteries (FO‑TM ≤5–10 MW): Peak shaving and DRV/LSRV capture, distribution‑level congestion relief, support for Statewide Solar For All revenue sharing, and resilience in constrained networks with summer peaks—bankable via VDER/DR where interconnection is available ([Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [PV Magazine USA, 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/12/17/ninedot-energy-enrolls-300-mw-of-community-energy-storage-in-new-york/)).
- ISC‑backed grid‑scale BESS (>5 MW FO‑TM or small TO‑TM): Capacity support and energy arbitrage with a 15‑year indexed storage credit filling the revenue gap to make projects financeable; policy goal alignment with 3,000 MW bulk target and 20% long‑duration share ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Hodgson Russ, 2025](https://www.hodgsonruss.com/newsroom/publications/psc-approves-nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program-implementation-plan)).
- Solar+storage (≤5 MW AC VDER; 5–20 MW FO‑TM): Time‑shift mid‑day solar to evening peaks; capture VDER capacity windows; reduce wholesale price volatility impacts; upstate projects require careful revenue stacking due to thinner DRV/LSRV values ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [PV Magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/01/21/new-york-study-finds-distributed-solar-and-storage-could-save-ratepayers-1-billion-annually/)).
- DER aggregation wholesale participation: Aggregators can group distributed batteries >10 kW to provide capacity/ancillary services, but caution is warranted given saturation trends seen in CAISO/ERCOT ancillary markets; aggregation augments VDER rather than replacing durable contract revenue ([NYISO DER press release, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/14007178/press-release-der-aggregations.pdf); [RFF, 2024](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

4.2 Core vs. nice‑to‑have use cases

- Core (bankability‑critical):
  - 15‑year ISC contracts (FO‑TM >5 MW), especially in Zones G–K and for ≥4‑hour systems (≥8‑hour favored by program design) ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/)).
  - VDER/DR/LSRV in Con Edison territory with proven interconnection; participation in Statewide Solar For All can add community value and political durability ([Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [PV Magazine USA, 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/12/17/ninedot-energy-enrolls-300-mw-of-community-energy-storage-in-new-york/)).
- Nice‑to‑have (incremental/merchant):
  - Ancillary services and short‑term arbitrage: provide upside but are exposed to saturation risk (e.g., CAISO ancillary services saturated as storage exceeded 10 GW in 2024) and should not be used as base revenue in underwriting ([RFF, 2024](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

4.3 Pain frequency and ROI evidence

- Seasonal and locational value. Con Edison’s much higher DR and VDER values (4–10x upstate) reflect persistent constraints in Zone J; long‑duration BESS in NYC can capture up to 95% higher capacity payments and up to $2 million/year extra in capacity revenues—indicating frequent, material peaks and ROI for duration ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- System‑wide savings. Study evidence suggests expanded distributed solar+storage could save New Yorkers ~$1 billion annually by 2035, with 56% of energy cost savings occurring in winter months—showing high frequency/value during winter reliability peaks ([PV Magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/01/21/new-york-study-finds-distributed-solar-and-storage-could-save-ratepayers-1-billion-annually/)).

4.4 Pricing models and reference price anchors

- VDER value stack: energy, capacity (Alternative 2), DRV/LSRV payments—windows concentrated in summer weekdays; Con Edison highest LSRV/DRV values; DR payments $0.85/kWh vs $0.09–$0.22 upstate ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- ISC: 15‑year indexed storage credit with strike price bids, CCIA inflation adjustment, LNISCC comparison across bidders; 20% long‑duration target across rounds; 10% cap for 2‑hour bids; >5 MW threshold favors scale ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Cost anchors: 2024 PV+storage battery capex ~$458/kWh (LBNL dataset), LCOE adders $25/MWh (post‑credit), battery pack $139/kWh in 2023, forecast $80/kWh by 2030 ([MercomIndia citing LBNL, 2024](https://mercomindia.com/utility-scale-solar-projects-comprised-54-of-new-capacity-in-the-u-s-in-2024); [Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).

4.5 Preferred purchase channels by buyer type

- ISC and utility solicitations (direct to program): The dominant bankable channels for FO‑TM and bulk assets; clear timelines and evaluation criteria ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).
- Developer‑to‑IPP sales: Project/portfolio M&A when interconnection and revenue contracts are in hand or credible (e.g., NineDot community batteries), often with NY Green Bank or commercial lenders providing construction‑to‑term products ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [NY Green Bank Portfolio](https://greenbank.ny.gov/Our-Impact/Portfolio)).
- Aggregators/partners for DER wholesale: Post‑2024 NYISO DER aggregation rules create new participation paths for distributed resources >10 kW—often as a complement to VDER ([NYISO DER press release, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/14007178/press-release-der-aggregations.pdf)).

4.6 Seasonality of buying

- Program calendars. ISC rounds run on annual cycles (2025–2027) with fall eligibility and winter bid deadlines; utility procurements align with PSC mandates (e.g., 2028 in‑service deadline); developers should stage bids and M&A around these windows ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).
- Operational seasonality. VDER/DR value highly seasonal (summer weekday windows); however, system studies highlight winter reliability value for distributed storage (56% of energy savings realized Nov–Mar), underscoring dual‑season ROI ([Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [PV Magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/01/21/new-york-study-finds-distributed-solar-and-storage-could-save-ratepayers-1-billion-annually/)).
- Interconnection seasons. NYISO cluster windows are sequential; missing a window can delay by up to two years, creating a “seasonality” of interconnection opportunity that must be harmonized with program solicitations ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

4.7 Top purchase criteria (ranked)

1) Interconnection maturity and timeline certainty (NYISO cluster position, study deposits in place; Con Edison interconnection posture for distributed assets) ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [PSC notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument)).

2) Contracted revenue quality (ISC 15‑year award; VDER/DR enrollment at attractive LSRV/DRV nodes; S‑SFA participation where applicable) ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

3) Permitting and community acceptance (avoid moratoria jurisdictions; NYC FDNY/DOB pathway familiarity) ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk)).

4) Technology and duration fit (≥4‑hour standard; ≥8‑hour for targeted ISC awards and capacity accreditation in constrained zones) ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

5) Contracting stack completeness (equipment/OEM, EPC with milestone schedule, O&M with guarantees, optimization/tolling arrangements) ([Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).

6) Cost and schedule control (EPC lead times, grid equipment availability, supply chain mitigation, inflation adjustments via CCIA) ([Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/)).

4.8 NPS or customer satisfaction benchmarks

- No public NPS benchmarks for BESS developers in NY. Proceed with qualitative diligence (references from lenders, counterparties, and utility interconnection teams).

4.9 Common objections and lost‑deal reasons

- Interconnection uncertainty in Con Edison territory (current petition asserting effective freeze), and utility pricing perceived as “not reflective of market price” in past RFPs ([Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/); [PSC notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument)).
- Local moratoria/bans causing sunk‑cost withdrawals and timeline blowouts ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk)).
- Insufficient contractable revenue (no ISC award; VDER/LSRV exhaustion; thin upstate DRV) to support leverage and exit valuations ([Modo distributed signals, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Merchant revenue risk (ancillary service saturation in other markets is a cautionary analog; overweighting merchant spreads in base cases) ([RFF, 2024](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).
- Supply chain lead times delaying COD past ITC windows or program in‑service deadlines ([Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).

6. Recommendations for New Energy Equity (NEE)

6.1 Where NY distributed storage economics are durable and scalable

- NYC/LI community‑scale FO‑TM batteries with proven bankability:
  - Target small‑to‑mid sized (e.g., 2–10 MW) FO‑TM BESS in Con Edison territory with interconnection feasibility and FDNY/DOB‑cleared configurations; banking evidence includes NineDot’s $431 million financing and portfolio scale. Require go/no‑go gating on Con Edison interconnection policy resolution in Case 18‑E‑0130 ([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html); [PSC notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument)).
  - Stack revenue with VDER/LSRV/DRV and Con Edison DR; consider S‑SFA enrollment for additional policy durability and community benefit alignment ([Modo 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [PV Magazine USA, 2025](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/12/17/ninedot-energy-enrolls-300-mw-of-community-energy-storage-in-new-york/)).
- ISC‑backed >5 MW FO‑TM (or small TO‑TM) storage in Zones G–K:
  - Align pipeline with ISC maturity criteria and bid windows; design ≥8‑hour configurations for higher scoring and capacity value in constrained zones; manage CCIA mechanics and LNISCC competitiveness while controlling EPC/OEM exposure through milestone schedules and supply risk contingencies ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/); [Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Upstate solar+storage only where interconnection and permitting are de‑risked:
  - Because DRV/LSRV values are far lower upstate (and VDER capacity value smaller), pursue solar+storage selectively where land/permitting/interconnection are exceptionally clean and where ISC co‑location or DER aggregation can add contractable revenue. Avoid moratoria‑prone localities and budget extra community engagement time ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk); [Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

6.2 Financial modeling guardrails for Board‑level confidence

- Contract‑first base case:
  - Treat ISC or VDER/DR enrollment as required for base‑case returns. Model merchant energy/arbitrage and ancillary services as upside with haircut scenarios; do not rely on ancillary services for base returns given saturation risk evidenced in CAISO and ERCOT ([RFF, 2024](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).
- Duration value and siting:
  - Where siting in NYC/LI is possible, model ≥8‑hour duration scenarios with capacity value premiums (up to 95% higher) and potential ~$2 million/year incremental capacity value in high‑need zones, balanced against higher capex and footprint ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Cost baselines and sensitivities:
  - Use $458/kWh (2024 PV+storage battery capex) and $567/kWh (2022–2023 retail non‑res installed costs) as near‑term anchors; set pack price glide path from $139/kWh (2023) to ~$80/kWh (2030) for forward cohorts; institute 12–18 month construction durations with six‑month contingency for critical equipment ([MercomIndia, 2024](https://mercomindia.com/utility-scale-solar-projects-comprised-54-of-new-capacity-in-the-u-s-in-2024); [Power Advisory, 2023–2024](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [Morgan Lewis, 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/03/an-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements)).
- Interconnection/permitting risk capture:
  - Include explicit probability‑adjusted attrition for moratoria exposure and cluster timing slippage; budget interconnection study deposits ($100k–$250k) and consider sunk‑cost recovery rates under withdrawal scenarios ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk); [Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

6.3 Commercial readiness package for IPP buyers

- Present a complete contracting stack:
  - Equipment supply (with bankable OEMs/warranty), EPC (milestone schedule and LDs), O&M (availability guarantees), optimization/virtual toll/market participation, interconnection agreements, and revenue contracts (ISC, VDER/DR, S‑SFA). This mirrors what sophisticated buyers expect per market evidence ([Fractal, 2025](https://www.energystorageconsultants.com/newsletter/Fractal-Energy-Storage-News-Jul-2025.pdf)).
- Document site control and maturity clearly:
  - Use utility RFP‑grade site control evidence (fee/lease/easement/options) in the entity expected to sign the ESSA or revenue contract, with contingencies described and timed ([National Grid RFP Appendix C1, 2019](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/bulk-energy-storage-request-for-proposals/appendix-c1-site-control.pdf)).
- Align financing pathways:
  - Leverage NY Green Bank products (interconnection bridge; construction‑to‑term) and tax credit transfer markets to lower WACC, following structures similar to the NineDot raise ([NY Green Bank Portfolio](https://greenbank.ny.gov/Our-Impact/Portfolio); [Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ninedot-energy-completes-431-million-165000707.html)).

6.4 Policy watchlist and go/no‑go triggers

- Con Edison distributed storage interconnection petition (Case 18‑E‑0130): If relief is granted, accelerate community battery development in NYC; if not, pivot more capacity to ISC channels ([DPS notice, 2026](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/W/PSCWeb.nsf/All/D82C6E08A78772F285257687006F39A8?OpenDocument)).
- ISC awards cadence (2025–2027): Success in ISCRFP25‑1 is a strong signal; adjust pipeline for ISCRFP26‑x/27‑x accordingly ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/july/new-york-kicks-off-bulk-energy-storage-procurement/)).
- Local permitting harmonization. Track potential state‑level permitting reforms (e.g., calls to bring standalone BESS under ORES) to mitigate local moratoria hurdles ([Modo permitting, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-battery-permitting-landscape-moratoria-ban-risk)).

Appendix: Additional comparative insights

A1. Competition and buyer demand signals

- Active competitors include Convergent Energy + Power, Key Capture Energy, NineDot Energy, Amp Energy, and utility‑affiliated/infra‑backed platforms; institutional M&A and financing volume remains robust (Arevon/Blackstone $350m in CA; Strata $559m in AZ; Engie acquisition of Broad Reach Power) indicating healthy buyer liquidity for well‑contracted BESS pipelines ([White & Case, 2024](https://mergers.whitecase.com/highlights/battery-storage-ma-powers-into-2024)).

A2. Why merchant‑only plays are risky in NY today

- RFF observes ancillary service market saturation in CAISO and ERCOT as storage penetrated—prices and revenues declined—suggesting that in NY, merchant reliance should be conservative until market design and load growth (e.g., data centers) shift the opportunity set; 4‑hour devices capture >80% of capacity value, but long duration will likely need further cost declines and regulatory support to scale without contracts ([RFF, 2024](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

A3. Utility procurement performance to date

- Utilities had procured ~120 MW of the ~350 MW expected by early 2023; Bridge Incentive spending was below allocated totals—indicating execution frictions in earlier frameworks and underlining the importance of the ISC’s bankability improvements and longer 15‑year terms for future procurements ([Energy-Storage.news, 2023](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-regulators-give-utilities-three-year-extension-on-energy-storage-projects/)).

A4. Interconnection timing advantage for readiness, scale, and capital

- NYISO expects projects entering the reformed process in Q3 2024 to begin construction by June 2026 (roughly 50% faster than prior). Higher study deposits discourage speculative queue‑sitters, benefitting mature, well‑capitalized developers—precisely the profile that IPP buyers prefer in diligence ([Modo interconnection, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

Conclusion

For New Energy Equity, New York’s distributed storage market is investable and scalable where revenue is contractable (ISC; Con Edison VDER/DR) and where interconnection and permitting are credibly de‑risked. The clearest near‑term path to Board‑level returns runs through ISC‑backed FO‑TM systems in Zones G–K (especially with ≥8‑hour durations) and NYC/LI community batteries modeled on proven financing structures—contingent on near‑term PSC action on Con Edison interconnection and careful AHJ selection to avoid moratoria. Build a pipeline shaped to ISC calendars and NYISO cluster windows, package full contracting stacks, and underwrite merchant revenue conservatively. With these guardrails, NY distributed storage can meet durable return thresholds and justify long‑term market entry.
