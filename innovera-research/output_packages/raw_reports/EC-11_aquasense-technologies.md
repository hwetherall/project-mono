LIMITATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────
• Google Trends reflects general web interest; B2B procurement investigations often happen offline or via industry portals not captured.  
• Job-posting data skews toward larger entities that publicize roles; utility districts with under 50 staff often recruit locally and are under-counted.  
• Headcount figures use LinkedIn self-reported employment; not all employees maintain profiles, and layoffs/additions reflect with lag.  
• “IoT-enabled Water Leak Detection and Pipe Monitoring” is not a standardized phrase; search tools treat it as a multi-term query, resulting in smaller volumes than underlying component terms.

────────────────────────────────────────────
KEY TAKE-AWAYS
────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Problem awareness is high and still climbing; “water main break” & “aging water infrastructure” searches grew ~30-50 % in two years.  
2. Solution curiosity is growing faster than the problem itself; IoT- or AI-tagged phrases rising 50-60 % YoY from a low base.  
3. Utilities are hiring both more field survey labor AND, faster, data/analytics talent, signaling a transition phase.  
4. Competitor hiring patterns confirm investment is shifting toward software, data science, and U.S. market penetration.  
5. Term-comparison data shows the market vocabulary is still settling; evangelizing a clear, memorable category name will influence search demand in your favor.

Bottom line: Quantitative signals (search, hiring, RFPs, VC) all align with a market moving from awareness to active experimentation with IoT leak-detection solutions, particularly in North America, with Europe 12-18 months behind in volume but similar trajectory.
