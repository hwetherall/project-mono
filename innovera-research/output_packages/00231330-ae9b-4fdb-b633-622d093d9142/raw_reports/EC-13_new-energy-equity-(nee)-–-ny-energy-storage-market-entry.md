Title: Analogous Markets and Adoption Trajectories for Distributed BESS and Solar+Storage Development: Implications for Entering New York’s ~5 MW Distributed Storage Market

Executive summary

New Energy Equity (NEE) is assessing a long-term entry into New York’s distributed battery energy storage (BESS) and solar+storage market (approximately 5 MW class). This report analyzes 2–4 markets with structurally similar dynamics to New York’s distribution-level storage segment and synthesizes technology adoption curves to inform timing and strategy. The core question is whether New York’s distributed BESS projects can underwrite Board-level return thresholds amid uncertain revenue stacks (tariffed incentives vs. merchant exposure), interconnection feasibility, IPP buyer demand, and policy durability.

Key findings

- New York’s distributed BESS sits at different points on the adoption curve by sub-geography and program:
  - Con Edison (Zone J, New York City) is post-inflection for distribution-level storage under VDER/LSRV/DRV, but near-saturation of the most attractive locational incentives limits new greenfield opportunity without alternative contracted revenue (e.g., ISC for >5 MW) or new LSRV cycles. In November 2025, Con Edison’s locational incentives were 93% exhausted, with only 7 MW remaining across three Manhattan networks ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - Upstate (Zones A–F) remains pre-inflection due to a wide value gap: Con Edison’s DRV-equivalent payments reached ~$0.85/kWh versus $0.09–$0.22/kWh for upstate utilities, leaving upstate BESS revenue 44–86% below estimated gross cost of new entry, discouraging broad deployment without additional retail incentives ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - New York’s 2024 PSC order created a new retail storage incentive ($675M for 1,500 MW; plus $100M residential) and a bulk market Index Storage Credit (ISC) ($1.33–$2.94B over three rounds), with retail capped at 5 MW/20 MWh per project and bulk requiring >5 MW. These incentives directly target NEE’s “~5 MW class” opportunity and are designed to raise contracted revenue enough to bridge gaps left by VDER alone ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

- Analogous markets show that:
  - California’s distributed BESS surged when state incentives (SGIP) and tariff reforms (NEM 3.0/Net Billing) combined with capacity/resource adequacy requirements—an inflection triggered by policy design that created long-term, bankable revenue plus clear operational use cases (backup power and peak-shifting). New York’s retail storage incentive and VDER construct share this “tariffed bankability” trait, but VDER prohibits simultaneous wholesale-market participation, limiting merchant upside compared with California’s pathways ([SEIA/Benchmark, 2026; CEC SGIP documentation via industry syntheses](https://seia.org/research-resources/energy-storage-market-outlook-q1-2026/); [SolarTech Energy Systems, 2025](https://solartechonline.com/blog/sgip-california-self-generation-incentive-program-guide/)).
  - ERCOT’s BESS buildout demonstrates what happens when interconnection and merchant market access are fast and flexible: installations scale rapidly, but returns hinge on price volatility and ancillary services. This is a caution for NEE: New York’s distributed segment is more policy-anchored (VDER, retail incentives) with stricter market-participation rules—returns will rely on tariff durability rather than merchant upside. Still, ERCOT illustrates that interconnection speed and operational flexibility (EMS) are decisive drivers of realized value even in policy-heavy markets ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).
  - Massachusetts and New Jersey show how northeastern states have catalyzed adoption with state-led, multi-year storage procurements and community solar expansion. Long-duration carve-outs and alignment with capacity markets (e.g., PJM BRA) enhance bankability for hybrids. New York’s 15-year ISC with carve-outs for 8-hour storage is a close structural analogue, even though ISC is for >5 MW and VDER retail incentives target ≤5 MW ([Energy-Storage.News, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/massachusetts-selects-1-2gw-of-bess-through-rfp/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/06/new-jersey-greenlights-3-gw-of-community-solar-awards-355-mw-energy-storage/)).

- Technology adoption curves:
  - Utility-scale BESS expanded sharply through 2025, with 5.3 GW installed in Q3 2025 and a five-year forecast lift of 15%; four-hour duration dominates queues (~86% in NYISO), aligning with the value peak-shifting use case ([ACP/Wood Mackenzie, 2025](https://cleanpower.org/news/us-energy-storage-monitor-q3-2025-woodmackenzie-acp/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
  - Distributed BESS in New York is bifurcated: Con Edison’s LSRV/DRV drove concentrated development; upstate adoption lags due to lower DRV and the same project costs—hence the policy need for retail incentives for ≤5 MW projects ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Timing:
  - New York’s distributed BESS market outside Con Edison appears pre-inflection but primed for acceleration as new retail incentives award capacity over the next cycles. Con Edison territory remains attractive only in specific, still-open networks and where local DR participation augments value; otherwise, ISC-eligible (>5 MW) grid-scale projects offer a clearer contracted path, but that is outside the ≤5 MW scope. For the ≤5 MW class, NEE should target near-term retail incentive windows and the highest-DRV/LSRV networks, with selective solar+storage hybrids to capture E-value (RECs) under VDER (E-value does not apply to battery-only systems) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

Bottom line opinion

- Proceed with a selective, phased Go focused on:
  1) ≤5 MW front-of-the-meter retail storage and solar+storage in Zones G–K (Lower Hudson Valley and NYC/Long Island) where DRV/LSRV or local DR/tariff adders are strongest, paired with NYSERDA’s retail incentive to reach bankable revenue floors; and
  2) Concurrent development of >5 MW “near-distribution” projects for ISC participation in later solicitations as a hedge and future scale path (even if not in the immediate scope), given ISC’s 15-year contracts and the limited upside of VDER-only storage in most upstate areas.
- Avoid broad upstate-only battery-only plays under VDER until retail incentive awards are secured and interconnection feasibility is confirmed.
- Emphasize solar+storage where site and offtake allow, to unlock VDER E-value (fixed 25-year rate), recognizing the trade-off with storage charging losses.

1. Background: New York’s distributed BESS context

1.1 Demand-side drivers and reliability needs

New York’s electricity demand outlook is shifting rapidly due to large, energy-intensive economic development (e.g., data centers, chip fabs, manufacturing). As of July 2025, 29 large load projects in NYISO’s interconnection queue could add nearly 6,055 MW of load, with 2,500–4,000 MW expected on the system by 2035—a significant headwind for summer and emerging winter peaks ([NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)). NYISO’s 2024 Reliability Needs Assessment (RNA) further noted some large loads may be flexible and assumed ~1,200 MW of peak-period demand curtailment, underscoring the system value of demand reduction and storage-facilitated flexibility ([NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).

1.2 Program architecture

- VDER (Value Stack) for ≤5 MW projects: Compensates distributed resources for energy (LBMP), capacity, environmental value (E-value for renewable injections), DRV (Demand Reduction Value), and potentially LSRV (Location-Specific System Relief Value) in targeted distribution networks. Compensations vary by utility territory and are paid on net injections during defined windows (e.g., DRV: 2–7 pm, summer weekdays). Crucially, projects on VDER generally cannot concurrently participate in NYISO wholesale markets ([Con Edison tariff Rider R; Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [National Grid VDER 2026 statement](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf)).
- NYSERDA 2024 Energy Storage Order: Set a 6 GW storage target by 2030 and created new funding:
  - $700M–$1.42B (est.) for 3,000 MW of bulk (>5 MW) storage via the Index Storage Credit (ISC), with at least three annual solicitations and at least 20% of awards for 8+ hour duration.
  - $675M for 1,500 MW of “retail” distributed storage (front-of-the-meter and behind-the-meter), with a per-kWh capacity-based incentive; maximum incentive eligibility for retail projects is 5 MW at 4 hours (20 MWh).
  - $100M for 200 MW of residential storage.
  The order also embeds Climate Act equity requirements and locational goals (e.g., ≥35% of bulk/front-of-the-meter capacity in NYISO Zones G–K) ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

- Con Edison (Zone J) vs. upstate split: VDER value has been substantially higher in Con Edison due to network constraints, with DRV/LSRV values an order of magnitude above upstate utilities. Con Edison offered ~$0.85/kWh in demand-response call periods in 2025 (four windows per day during June 24–September 15), while upstate DR programs paid ~$0.09–$0.22/kWh—a 4–10x spread. As a result, Con Edison captured a disproportionately high share of statewide distributed BESS deployments relative to its geographic footprint, whereas upstate volumes remained thin ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

1.3 Near-term economics for ≤5 MW storage and solar+storage hybrids

- Battery-only under VDER: Revenue comes from energy (LBMP), capacity during the capacity window (Alt 2 in VDER for hybrids, but battery-only capacity treatment varies), and DRV. Battery-only systems do not earn E-value (renewable environmental credits), which is reserved for injections of renewable generation (e.g., solar). DRV is locked for 10 years; after that, values can reset or be removed—posing a tail risk to long-term underwriting ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Solar+storage: In addition to energy and DRV, solar+storage earns E-value for all grid injections (whether from the array directly or from the battery discharging solar-charged energy). E-value in some tariffs (e.g., National Grid’s February 2026 statement) is materially significant (e.g., $0.03103/kWh), and fixed (tariff-limited) over long periods; this can strengthen bankability, but storage charging losses reduce MWh injections and thus E-value relative to solar-only ([National Grid VDER statement, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Retail incentive: The per-kWh capacity-based incentive for ≤5 MW projects under the 2024 Order is the crucial new revenue component designed to close the viability gap upstate and to sustain downstate development as DRV/LSRV windows saturate ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

2. Methodology: Selecting and evaluating analogues

Analogues were chosen for structural similarity with New York’s distributed BESS market along:
- Revenue design: Tariffed incentives and/or long-term contracts vs. merchant markets.
- Buyer type: Utility-anchored programs, capacity markets, or developer-driven merchant plays.
- Interconnection: Distribution-level constraints vs. transmission-level reforms.
- Technology profile: Four-hour lithium-ion dominance, EMS value stacking, solar+storage pairing.

We foreground recent, authoritative sources (RTO/ISO operators, state agencies, Wood Mackenzie/ACP, Modo Energy, RFF) and newer reporting where available (2024–2026). Where blog content appears in the source set, it is not used unless corroborated by stronger sources. We include intraregional contrasts (e.g., Con Edison vs. upstate) when they illustrate structural drivers.

3. Analogous markets and comparable trajectories

3.1 Analogue 1: California distributed BESS (SGIP + tariff reforms + RA)

- Description: California’s behind-the-meter and small front-of-the-meter BESS markets scaled under the Self-Generation Incentive Program (SGIP), evolving retail tariffs (NEM 3.0/Net Billing), and resource adequacy (RA) constructs that value battery contributions to evening peaks. The state’s policy mix explicitly targets reliability, emissions, and customer resilience (e.g., wildfire PSPS events), with significant incentives for low-income/disadvantaged communities ([SEIA/Benchmark, 2026](https://seia.org/research-resources/energy-storage-market-outlook-q1-2026/); [SolarTech Energy Systems, 2025](https://solartechonline.com/blog/sgip-california-self-generation-incentive-program-guide/)).

- Why valid:
  - Similar problem type: integrating distributed batteries to manage net load peaks and defer grid upgrades.
  - Buyer types: Incentive-driven utility programs and RA obligations comparable to New York’s VDER/DRV and bulk procurement needs.
  - Technology dependency: Four-hour Li-ion batteries optimized to shift solar generation into evening hours—equivalent to New York use cases ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

- Adoption timeline:
  - Early (2016–2019): SGIP expansion and maturation; initial BTM deployments with resilience use cases; project costs declining.
  - Inflection (2020–2023): PSPS wildfire de-energizations, RA tightening, and NEM reforms drive a step-change in BESS attachment rates to solar; BESS becomes a standard component of residential and C&I projects; utility-scale batteries also surge.
  - Mainstream (2024–2025): Continued growth with CA and TX accounting for the majority of new U.S. capacity; residential/CCI segments fluctuate but remain material as policy (SGIP) and retail tariffs evolve ([ACP/Wood Mackenzie, 2025](https://cleanpower.org/news/us-energy-storage-monitor-q3-2025-woodmackenzie-acp/)).

- Key lessons:
  - Bankable incentives aligned to clear operational windows (e.g., RA hours) unlock financing at scale. New York’s retail incentive for ≤5 MW can function similarly to SGIP by delivering a revenue floor atop VDER ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).
  - Tariff design drives siting and configuration: e.g., Net Billing in CA pushed pairing with storage to capture evening export value; in NY, VDER DRV/LSRV windows and E-value create distinct operating strategies and favor solar+storage in some locations ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
  - Equity allocations and locational targeting accelerate adoption where grid value and community benefits are highest—mirrored in New York’s G–K siting goals ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

- Where analogy breaks:
  - California allows broader wholesale-market participation and RA monetization pathways than NY’s VDER allows for ≤5 MW. In NY, VDER projects cannot simultaneously participate in NYISO wholesale markets, constraining merchant upside ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - Policy cadence differs: CA’s SGIP has long-run continuity with evolving buckets; New York’s retail incentive is newer, and DRV/LSRV cycles in Con Edison are near exhaustion in some networks.

- Sources: ([SEIA/Benchmark, 2026](https://seia.org/research-resources/energy-storage-market-outlook-q1-2026/); [SolarTech Energy Systems, 2025](https://solartechonline.com/blog/sgip-california-self-generation-incentive-program-guide/); [RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/); [ACP/Wood Mackenzie, 2025](https://cleanpower.org/news/us-energy-storage-monitor-q3-2025-woodmackenzie-acp/)).

3.2 Analogue 2: ERCOT (Texas) grid-scale and near-distribution BESS (merchant-led)

- Description: ERCOT is an energy-only market with streamlined interconnection and high renewable penetration, producing volatile price spreads. BESS value stacks rely heavily on energy arbitrage and ancillary services, with little to no state incentive overlays—allowing rapid merchant-led scaling.

- Why valid:
  - Similar technology (4-hour Li-ion) and EMS sophistication for capturing arbitrage and ancillaries.
  - Illustrates how interconnection speed, not just policy, determines realized buildout—a key consideration for NY distribution-level interconnection timelines ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

- Adoption timeline:
  - Early (pre-2020): Limited BESS participation.
  - Inflection (2020–2023): Rapid growth as solar/wind scale and price volatility increases; interconnection is comparatively fast; merchant capital flows in.
  - Mainstream (2024–2026): ERCOT and CAISO together account for ~60% or more of new U.S. additions; batteries provide meaningful peak support; however, revenues fluctuate year-to-year, exposing merchant risk ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

- Key lessons:
  - Interconnection process and queue discipline are decisive. Even with supportive policies, slow/stochastic interconnection erodes bankability—mirrored in NYISO’s high historical queue attrition (91% drop-out) and the significance of distribution-level utility processes for ≤5 MW ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
  - EMS optionality is critical. BESS projects need configurable strategies for DR windows, capacity hours, and arbitrage; this is true even for VDER-only projects because of narrow operating windows (DRV/capacity) and LBMP variability ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

- Where analogy breaks:
  - ERCOT’s merchant exposure is far greater than NY’s distributed VDER programs. NY ≤5 MW projects rely on tariffed values and incentives with restricted wholesale access.
  - ERCOT’s speed-to-market is unmatched; NY’s distribution interconnection (and permitting, especially in NYC) is more complex.

- Sources: ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/)).

3.3 Analogue 3: Massachusetts mid-duration and state-led procurements

- Description: Massachusetts adopted a comprehensive policy to procure 5,000 MW of storage by 2030, launching a 1,500 MW RFP for mid-duration systems with subsequent tranches in 2026, 2027, and by 2030. This reflects an explicit capacity and winter reliability strategy in a cold-climate, heating-electrification context similar to New York’s winter peak concerns ([Energy-Storage.News, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/massachusetts-selects-1-2gw-of-bess-through-rfp/)).

- Why valid:
  - Similar regional fundamentals (New England), winter reliability constraints, and policy-led long-term contract design.
  - Structurally analogous to New York’s ISC bulk program (15-year payments, long-duration carve-outs).

- Adoption timeline:
  - Early (2017–2022): SMART and targeted pilots set the stage; recognition of winter peak issues grows.
  - Inflection (2024–2026): 2024 legislation mandates 5 GW by 2030; 2025–2026 RFPs ramp procurement.
  - Mainstream (2026–2030): Contracted capacity scales with successive RFPs.

- Key lessons:
  - Long-duration carve-outs create diversified portfolios and align storage with seasonal reliability goals; New York’s ISC long-duration requirement (≥20% awards for 8+ hours) follows this logic ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).
  - Multi-year procurement calendars enable developer readiness and financing.

- Where analogy breaks:
  - Massachusetts procurement is primarily utility-scale; New York’s question is centered on ≤5 MW distributed projects under VDER and retail incentives. However, the long-term contracted approach informs how NY’s ISC can support bankability for >5 MW projects that NEE may consider in parallel.

- Sources: ([Energy-Storage.News, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/massachusetts-selects-1-2gw-of-bess-through-rfp/)).

3.4 Analogue 4: New Jersey (GSESP storage tranches and 3 GW community solar expansion)

- Description: New Jersey is scaling transmission-scale storage via the Grid-Scale Energy Storage Program (GSESP), with Tranche 2 (645 MW) now open; the state targets 2,000 MW by 2030. In parallel, the NJBPU opened a 3 GW block for community solar, which will drive distributed siting and potential solar+storage pairings. The program envisioned alignment with PJM’s Base Residual Auction (BRA) to secure capacity revenues—another form of contracted bankability layered on top of state incentives ([pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/06/new-jersey-greenlights-3-gw-of-community-solar-awards-355-mw-energy-storage/); [Renewable Energy World, 2026](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-storage/battery/why-new-jersey-is-going-all-in-on-energy-storage-and-distributed-solar/)).

- Why valid:
  - Close geographic, market, and policy environment to New York; distributed solar and storage bolstered via state-led capacity procurement and community solar growth.
  - Demonstrates how pairing community solar with storage and aligning solicitations to capacity markets (PJM) unlocks financeability—analogous to combining VDER with retail incentives in New York.

- Adoption timeline:
  - Early (2019–2023): SuSI and pilot phases.
  - Inflection (2024–2026): GSESP Tranche 1 and 2, plus 3 GW community solar expansion.
  - Mainstream (2026–2030): Progress toward the statutory storage target of 2,000 MW.

- Key lessons:
  - Competitive solicitations and synchronization with capacity markets reduce merchant risk and drive adoption speed.
  - Community solar expansion provides scalable interconnection and offtake templates for hybrids.

- Where analogy breaks:
  - PJM capacity constructs differ from NYISO’s VDER and ISC; New York’s VDER excludes wholesale market participation for VDER projects, limiting capacity-market capture to non-VDER or bulk projects.
  - New Jersey’s regulatory cadence and timelines differ from NYSERDA’s 2025–2027 ISC schedule and the new retail incentive cycles.

- Sources: ([pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/06/new-jersey-greenlights-3-gw-of-community-solar-awards-355-mw-energy-storage/); [Renewable Energy World, 2026](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-storage/battery/why-new-jersey-is-going-all-in-on-energy-storage-and-distributed-solar/)).

4. Technology adoption curves

4.1 BESS adoption nationally

- The U.S. BESS market accelerated through 2025, with 5.3 GW installed in Q3 2025 and 2025 year-to-date surpassing all of 2024 installations. Utility-scale growth dominated, with California and Texas accounting for most capacity; the five-year utility-scale forecast was revised upward by 15% in late 2025 ([ACP/Wood Mackenzie, 2025](https://cleanpower.org/news/us-energy-storage-monitor-q3-2025-woodmackenzie-acp/)).
- Forecasts expect 2026 installations to reach 70 GWh/35 GW across all segments, with cumulative utility-scale BESS approaching 500 GWh by 2030, indicating a steepening curve for grid-scale and a steady rise for behind-the-meter (BTM) storage ([SEIA/Benchmark, 2026](https://seia.org/research-resources/energy-storage-market-outlook-q1-2026/)).

4.2 New York’s BESS adoption profile and queue friction

- NYISO’s reformed interconnection process (cluster study, parallel advancement) faces its first test: 19 GW of BESS in the cluster, but historically 91% of queue entrants drop out; realistic operations by 2030 are 2–4 GW unless ISC awards and other supports expand. Four-hour duration dominates (~86% of queued capacity), underscoring the core arbitrage/peak-shaving use cases ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
- The ISC (bulk) is redirecting capital to larger, transmission-connected projects with 15-year contracts and wholesale participation, while VDER (distributed) continues to anchor ≤5 MW projects—these regimes bifurcate the state’s adoption curve. Distributed BESS under VDER remains heavily locational ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

4.3 VDER and distributed economics

- VDER compensates by component: energy (LBMP), capacity (Alt 2 for hybrids), E-value (for renewable injections), DRV (defined summer windows), and LSRV (targeted networks). Con Edison’s DR-equivalent payments (~$0.85/kWh) vs. upstate (~$0.09–$0.22/kWh) explain the geographic concentration of BESS in Zone J; as LSRV availability nears exhaustion, new projects must lean on the retail incentive or alternative DR streams ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Example tariff values: National Grid’s February 2026 VDER statement lists E-value at $0.03103/kWh (fixed, tariff-dependent) and DRV windows (2–7 pm, weekdays, Jun 24–Sep 15 for post-2018 projects), which define operational strategies for solar+storage and battery-only systems, respectively ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf)).

4.4 EMS and duration patterns

- Across U.S. markets, 2–4-hour Li-ion batteries capture most value by shifting daytime solar to evening peaks and participating in ancillary services where allowed. In New York’s distributed VDER segment, EMS must optimize to narrow DRV and capacity windows and arbitrage LBMP while respecting that wholesale-market participation is not permitted for VDER projects ([RFF, 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/reports/charging-up-the-state-of-utility-scale-electricity-storage-in-the-united-states/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

5. Trajectory synthesis: Where New York’s ≤5 MW distributed BESS stands

5.1 Position on the adoption curve

- Con Edison, Zone J:
  - Post-inflection but supply-constrained in key networks. The 93% exhaustion of locational incentives (LSRV) by November 2025 indicates a matured, saturated micro-market. Remaining opportunity depends on uncovering the last open networks, leveraging retail incentives, stacking DR programs, and/or coupling with solar for E-value. High permitting rigor and urban siting complexity remain practical constraints ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Zones G–K (Lower Hudson Valley beyond NYC):
  - Late-early to early-growth. Higher locational needs than upstate, prioritized by the 2024 Order (≥35% of bulk/front-of-the-meter awards sited G–K), combined with the retail incentive, set the stage for distributed BESS acceleration. Bankable revenue depends on landing retail incentive awards and securing distribution interconnections in stress points ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).
- Upstate (Zones A–F):
  - Pre-inflection. VDER component values (DRV/LSRV) and DR programs are weaker, undermining financeability for standalone BESS. The new retail storage incentive is likely a prerequisite for bankability; solar+storage hybrids can add E-value to close gaps. Without awards, many projects will not clear return thresholds ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf)).

5.2 Confidence level: Moderate

- Strong: Evidence on locational value spread (Con Edison vs. upstate) and near-saturation of LSRV suggests a matured micro-market in NYC and slow adoption upstate absent new incentives ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Moderate: The exact pacing of NYSERDA retail incentive awards and distribution interconnection throughput remains uncertain; interconnection and permitting are decisive but localized.
- Weak: DRV/LSRV program evolution post-2035 and long-run VDER policy shifts are uncertain; DRV resets after 10 years create tail risk ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

5.3 Key caveat vs. analogues

- VDER’s prohibition on simultaneous wholesale-market participation for distributed projects reduces merchant optionality relative to California and ERCOT analogues. The ISC fills the contractability gap—but for >5 MW. NEE’s ≤5 MW target must principally rely on VDER components and the new retail incentive; solar+storage E-value may be the critical swing factor for hybrid economics in many load zones ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

6. Timing implications for NEE

6.1 Is the market pre-inflection, at inflection, or post-inflection?

- Con Edison/Zone J: Post-inflection but capacity-limited; incremental opportunities remain where LSRV/DRV and DR stack is still rich or where retail incentive can be layered to backfill revenue floors. Expect fierce interconnection competition and stringent permitting.
- Zones G–K (outside NYC): At or near inflection if NYSERDA retail awards are timely; structuring projects to meet locational and equity targets will improve odds of award and interconnection.
- Upstate (A–F): Pre-inflection, pending retail incentive uptake; isolated, targeted sites (e.g., constrained feeders) may pencil, especially as solar+storage hybrids.

6.2 Recommended entry timing and structure

- Immediate (Q2 2026): Submit for NYSERDA retail storage incentive opportunities in Zones G–K, prioritizing:
  - Front-of-the-meter ≤5 MW storage adjacent to constrained distribution assets where DRV/LSRV or equivalent locational benefits still exist.
  - Solar+storage hybrids in utility territories where E-value is material and offtake arrangements (remote crediting/community models) create bankable MWh flows ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Short-term (2026–2027): Build a two-track pipeline:
  - Track A (≤5 MW): Replicate successful templates across multiple utilities, focusing on highest DRV/DR program payments and E-value capture; secure interconnects early to avoid queue backlogs.
  - Track B (>5 MW hedge): Advance a small set of >5 MW projects to bid into ISC solicitations (2026–2027), recognizing that ISC bankability (15-year contracts) may outperform VDER-only in most upstate contexts ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

6.3 Risk management and underwriting notes

- Policy durability:
  - DRV is ten-year fixed; risk of value decline thereafter. Model conservative post-year-10 tails and emphasize retail incentive as an up-front bankability lever ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
  - E-value (for solar+storage) is fixed long-term (tariff-defined); battery-only does not receive E-value—tilting economics toward hybrids where interconnection and site conditions allow ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf)).
- Interconnection feasibility:
  - Distribution-level interconnection is the gating item for ≤5 MW; pursue early scoping with utilities and target feeders where DRV/LSRV signals need.
- Market evolution:
  - ISC directs large volumes to grid-scale; NYISO queue evidence suggests only 2–4 GW may reach COD by 2030 without increased contracted revenue programs—retail incentives for ≤5 MW are thus pivotal for distributed segment growth ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).

7. Implications for NEE’s Go/No-Go decision by March 31, 2026

7.1 Synthesis

- Positive indicators:
  - New York’s 2024 Order directly targets ≤5 MW projects with a retail incentive designed to address the very bankability gap constraining upstate VDER-only storage. This is the principal mechanism to meet return thresholds in Zones A–F and to continue scaling in G–K where LSRV is constrained ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).
  - Demand growth from large loads (2.5–4.0 GW by 2035) will intensify peak and local network constraints, structurally supporting DRV-like value streams over time ([NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).

- Negative indicators:
  - VDER-only battery economics in upstate utilities remain weak relative to cost of entry; Con Edison LSRV is near-saturated; queue drop-out risk and permitting friction can erode pipeline conversion ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - DRV tail risk after 10 years and uncertainties on future program design require conservative terminal value assumptions ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

7.2 Recommendation: Selective Go

- Enter New York’s ≤5 MW distributed BESS market with a focused strategy:
  - Prioritize Zones G–K front-of-the-meter and solar+storage hybrids that can combine VDER (energy/capacity/DRV + E-value for hybrids) and the retail storage incentive.
  - Opportunistically pursue the last remaining Con Edison network pockets with strong LSRV/DRV and DR program compatibility.
  - Build a complementary >5 MW ISC-eligible pipeline for 2026–2027 solicitations to diversify contracted revenue exposure and scale platform capabilities.

- Required enablers before final investment committee approvals:
  - External model validation using Con Edison and National Grid tariff schedules (DRV/E-value/capacity windows) and NYSERDA’s retail incentive mechanics; stress test LBMP exposure and DRV post-year-10 scenarios ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
  - Engage utilities early on interconnection feasibility and timeline risk; target feeders known to be included in DRV/LSRV planning cycles.
  - Confirm EPC cost trends and availability; while battery costs rose 22% in 2022–2023, signs of easing supply chains suggest improvement—validate 2026–2027 turnkey costs in New York jurisdictions ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

8. Additional analysis: Revenue-stack specifics for ≤5 MW in New York

- Energy arbitrage: LBMPs adjusted for losses; the narrow DRV/capacity windows bias operations to summer weekday afternoons; EMS must also capture shoulder-period spreads where margin exists ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- Capacity (VDER Alternative 2): For solar+storage, capacity credit is earned on injections during the capacity window (summer weekdays, 2–7 pm, approximately June 24–August 31). There are ~240–245 capacity hours per year; projects often charge batteries with solar before 2 pm to maximize revenue ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- E-value (environmental): Applies to renewable energy injections; solar+storage earns E-value on all injected kWh, including those discharged from batteries if charged by solar. This long-dated revenue is central to solar+storage bankability; however, net MWh injected decline due to round-trip losses ([National Grid, 2026](https://www.nationalgridus.com/media/pdfs/billing-payments/electric-rates/upstate-ny/2026/stat_vder-cred_102_effective_january_30_2026.pdf); [Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).
- DRV: Paid either $/kW-month (legacy) or $/kWh during defined summer weekday windows. DRV is locked for 10 years; thereafter, revaluation risk is material. DRV values are highest in NYC and Long Island, and meaningfully lower upstate ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Retail incentive (≤5 MW): Per-kWh capacity-based payment intended to raise revenue to financeable levels; competitive solicitation cadence and budget allocation will determine timing and clearing price bands ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

9. Limitations and uncertainties

- Interconnection: Distribution interconnection rules and utility study queues are localized; results vary by feeder and substation. While federal interconnection reforms improved transmission-level study throughput in 2024, they are less determinative for distribution-level assets. Early utility engagement is essential ([Wood Mackenzie, 2025](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/us-grid-interconnection-agreements-increase-33-in-2024-according-to-wood-mackenzie/)).
- Policy durability: VDER constructs have evolved since 2017; DRV and LSRV cycles have finite terms. The 2024 Order provides new retail storage funding but program specifics (award pacing, budget sufficiency) determine scalability ([NYSERDA, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).
- Market cannibalization: As more solar builds, daytime LBMPs may decline, compressing arbitrage margins; DRV/LSRV target peak windows help mitigate this for distributed assets, but economics increasingly depend on contracted/tariffed revenues ([Power Advisory, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

10. Conclusion

New York’s distributed BESS market (≤5 MW) is neither uniformly nascent nor mature; it is heterogeneous. The Con Edison segment is post-inflection but constrained by near-saturated locational incentives. Outside NYC, the market is pre-inflection but primed for acceleration if NEE can secure NYSERDA retail storage incentives and interconnection on feeders where DRV/LSRV or DR participation is meaningful. Solar+storage hybrids will often be superior to battery-only due to E-value capture. The 2024 Order’s retail incentive is the decisive policy lever linking analogue lessons (California’s SGIP/RA model and New Jersey/Massachusetts procurements) to a bankable path in New York’s ≤5 MW class.

Recommendation: Proceed with a selective Go focused on Zones G–K solar+storage and front-of-the-meter ≤5 MW storage projects that win retail incentives and align with high-DRV/DR feeders; concurrently, prepare a small >5 MW ISC-eligible pipeline as a hedge and scale pathway. This dual approach balances near-term distributed opportunities with medium-term contracted growth, acknowledging interconnection realities and the bifurcated nature of New York’s storage adoption trajectory.
