Scope and definitions used
- Distributed BESS: front-of-the-meter, distribution-connected systems in the ≈1–20 MW class (focus ≈5 MW), including standalone and solar+storage.
- Solar+storage project development: distributed PV paired with BESS at distribution level (community solar, C&I, feeders).
- Dollar values generally reflect system market value (installed systems, including hardware and EPC). “Project development” service revenue is a subset of this total; few public sources break it out cleanly.

3) Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) – New York State, distributed BESS and solar+storage
Capacity-based SAM anchor (policy-driven):
- New York State energy storage target: 6 GW by 2030, with programs specifically supporting bulk and retail/distribution-level storage. In Oct 2023, the NY Public Service Commission (PSC) approved the framework to achieve 6 GW by 2030, including large-scale procurements and retail incentives (total program funding authorization widely reported in the multi‑billion-dollar range). Source: New York PSC Order adopting the Energy Storage Roadmap framework for 6 GW by 2030, Oct 12, 2023 (government order; primary).
- Within this, the PSC framework includes:
  - Bulk storage procurements (Index Storage Credit) and
  - Retail/distribution-level storage incentives (customer-sited/community/C&I).
  - Multiple official summaries cite 3,000 MW bulk and 1,500 MW retail/distribution-level programs as core planks toward the 6 GW target. Source: PSC Order (Oct 12, 2023); NYSERDA/DPS Energy Storage Roadmap 2.0 filings (government; primary). Note: Access to NYSERDA site was blocked by Cloudflare during this compilation; confirm exact allocations/budgets directly.

Dollar-value SAM estimate for NY distributed BESS (2026–2030 build window):
- Method: Apply reasonable installed-cost ranges for 4-hour Li-ion systems to the retail/distribution-level target tranche most relevant to ≈5 MW class.
- Cost benchmarks:
  - NREL 2024 ATB and recent U.S. benchmarks place 4-hour front-of-the-meter Li‑ion system installed costs roughly in the $300–500/kWh range in the mid/late-2020s (trajectory improving toward 2030), equivalent to ≈$1.2–2.0M per MW of 4-hour storage. Sources: NREL Annual Technology Baseline 2024, Storage Cost and Performance data (government lab; primary). Also consistent with recent U.S. public benchmarks and industry trackers in 2023–2024.
- Retail/distribution-level tranche sizing:
  - If the retail/distribution-level program is ≈1,500 MW statewide, and assuming the lion’s share is built 2026–2030, the implied system spend is roughly:
    - Low: 1,000 MW × $1.2M/MW = $1.2B
    - Base: 1,200 MW × $1.6M/MW = $1.9B
    - High: 1,500 MW × $2.0M/MW = $3.0B
  - Indicative 2026–2030 SAM for NY distributed BESS: ≈$1.2B–$3.0B (system CAPEX), with mid-case ≈$1.8–$2.2B, contingent on buildout pace, duration mix (2–4 hours), and cost trajectory. Sources: PSC Order (Oct 12, 2023) for capacity targets; NREL ATB 2024 for cost range (primary).
- Solar+storage subset in NY:
  - New York’s 10 GW distributed solar-by-2030 target (NY-Sun expansion approved 2022) creates an attach opportunity for pairing storage at the distribution level. Actual attach rates vary by utility tariff (VDER), interconnection, incentives, and fire code constraints. A 10–30% attach-rate sensitivity on remaining distributed PV build could translate into several hundred MW of paired storage through 2030.
  - Quantifying this subset precisely requires current NYSERDA NY‑Sun pipeline data and each utility’s paired-storage incentive/VDER economics; treat as a meaningful but uncertain fraction of the distributed BESS SAM. Sources: NY PSC/NY-Sun expansion to 10 GW by 2030 (Apr 2022 Commission approval; government); utility VDER tariffs.

Important caveats for SAM
- Local fire-safety code and siting constraints (particularly downstate/NYC) can impact feasible siting of ≈5 MW class batteries and may slow realization of the retail tranche; timelines and siting costs should be stress-tested. Policy durability and the final design of incentive tranches also affect realized SAM.

4) Market structure – fragmentation, maturity, and notable players
- Maturity: Rapidly growing but still emerging at the distributed scale in NY. Policy-driven demand (PSC framework), IRA incentives (standalone storage ITC with adders), and falling costs support growth. However, code/siting, interconnection queues, and evolving NYISO/utility market rules mean execution risk remains.
- Fragmentation: Highly fragmented on the development side for ≈5 MW distribution-connected projects. Numerous regional developers compete, often partnering with national integrators and IPPs for offtake/exit.
- Representative participants:
  - Distributed storage developers active in NY: NineDot Energy (NYC-focused distribution-level BESS), Convergent Energy + Power (C&I and distribution-level), Agilitas Energy (Northeast standalone storage), Nexamp (community solar+storage), Catalyze, CleanCapital, Standard Solar, EDP Renewables (DG), and others.
  - System integrators/vendors commonly used at this size class: Tesla (Megapack), Fluence, Powin, Wärtsilä, Sungrow. Source: MarketsandMarkets North America BESS Market (Apr 2026) lists Tesla, GE Vernova, Honeywell, BYD, LGES as key players in the region (market research firm).
- Buyer universe (IPP/asset owners): AES Clean Energy, EDP Renewables, NextEra, RWE, Plus Power, Arevon, East Point Energy (Equinor), and infrastructure funds acquiring operating portfolios. For ≈5 MW assets, portfolio buyers often value standardized tech stacks and long-term service frameworks.
