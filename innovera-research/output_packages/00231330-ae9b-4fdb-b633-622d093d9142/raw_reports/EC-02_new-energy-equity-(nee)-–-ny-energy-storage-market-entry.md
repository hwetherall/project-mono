Title: Competitive Landscape Analysis — New Energy Equity (NEE) Entry into New York Distributed Energy Storage and Solar+Storage Development

Date: March 13, 2026

Executive Summary

New York State’s storage market is in a rapid but uneven transition driven by the Public Service Commission’s (PSC) 6 GW by 2030 target, the launch of NYSERDA’s bulk Index Storage Credit (ISC) program (1 GW per year in 2025–2027), and the exhaustion of lucrative downstate Value of Distributed Energy Resources (VDER) locational adders. Distributed front-of-the-meter (FTM) BESS up to 5 MW and 5 MW AC community solar+storage projects continue to be developed, particularly where VDER values remain high and interconnection is feasible, but upstate VDER economics lag well below gross cost of new entry (CONE), creating a challenging revenue gap without additional stackable programs. Meanwhile, NYISO interconnection queue reforms (FERC Order 2023) and the first cluster study are accelerating viable projects and screening out speculative submissions, while interconnection constraints—especially in Con Edison’s territory—still present a material execution risk for distributed developers. Against this backdrop, the most credible path to meeting NEE’s Board financial thresholds involves a staged entry focused on:

- Downstate distributed FTM BESS and hybrid community solar+storage in Con Edison networks with residual VDER opportunities, Dynamic Load Management (DLM) participation, and targeted S-SFA-driven energy affordability enrollments; and
- Selective upstate distributed solar+storage where circuits have favorable hosting capacity and stackable incentives (RSIP, VDER DRV/LSRV) can be validated via nodal analysis, coupled with potential bilateral contracts or utility solicitations to close the revenue gap; and
- Optional participation in 2026–2027 ISC bulk solicitations via 5–20 MW interconnection strategies where NEE’s organizational scale and capital partnerships can support 15-year indexed contracts.

The competitive field in distributed BESS is anchored by specialized NYC/downstate developers (NineDot Energy, Summit Ridge Energy, Calibrant), established independent developers and IPPs with NY pipelines (Key Capture Energy, Convergent Energy + Power, Greenbacker, Aspen Power, PowerBank), and sophisticated analytics/optimization providers (Modo Energy, Ascend Analytics, Stem) that materially affect project siting, bidding, and operational outperformance. The dominant approach in the 5 MW-class remains to stack VDER, RSIP, DLM, occasional NY Green Bank financing, and wholesale participation (where permitted) while pursuing interconnection on non-network circuits or at strategically sited network locations that have cleared safety and fire-code hurdles. Critical gaps persist in: (1) bankable upstate revenue without ISC or locally targeted procurement, (2) interconnection capacity transparency (especially in Con Edison networks), and (3) mid-market developer capabilities to systematically beat zonal reference prices and optimize multi-program stacking.

On balance, the New York distributed storage market is attractive for a targeted entry with disciplined siting, rigorous revenue modeling grounded in NY-specific tools, and early partnerships for financing and asset optimization. A broad “go” is not warranted; a focused “go” with guardrails is.

- The VDER/Con Edison locational incentives that propelled NYC deployment (as high as $284/kW-year) are 93% exhausted (as of Nov 2025), leaving only 7 MW available in three Manhattan networks—sharply narrowing the downstate VDER window but not eliminating value in adjacent networks and stackable programs (e.g., DLM) (Modo Energy). Upstate VDER incentives ($18–$70/kW-year) left BESS revenues 44–86% below a $126/kW-year upstate CONE—implying careful site selection and/or revenue augmentation are essential upstate (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- NYSERDA’s ISC program is shifting capital toward grid-scale storage with 15-year indexed contracts; cluster-study projects target CODs in 2027–2032; submissions concentrate around NYC/Long Island where capacity/reserve needs boost value; 10-hour systems can earn up to 95% higher capacity payments in Zone J (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-bulk-energy-storage-solicitation-battery-fire-trump/756197/)).
- NYISO’s first reformed cluster study includes ~30.9 GW of accepted battery applications across zones, with Zone G (Hudson Valley) leading and significant volumes in Zones A, C, F, J, K; interconnection reforms aim to cut time-to-construction by ~50% for projects entering in Q3 2024 (YSG Solar; Modo Energy) ([YSG Solar, 2025](https://www.ysgsolar.com/thought-leadership/publications/nyiso-cluster-study-2023/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- State incentives across retail/residential (RSIP) and bulk (ISC) plus policy targets (CLCPA) remain strong; PSC’s June 20, 2024 Order authorized 4.7 GW of new procurements (3 GW bulk via ISC; 1.5 GW retail; 200 MW residential) and codified locational equity requirements for zones G–K (PSC/NYSERDA; SYSO; Barclay Damon) ([PSC, 2024](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/MatterManagement/MatterFilingItem.aspx?FilingSeq=329364&MatterSeq=55960); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/); [Barclay Damon, 2025](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/new-york-psc-approves-nyserdas-billion-dollar-bulk-energy-storage-programwith-modifications)).

Market Context: Policy, Interconnection, and Revenue Stacks

- State targets and procurements. New York’s PSC established a 6 GW by 2030 storage target (June 20, 2024), authorizing 4,700 MW of incremental procurements: 3,000 MW bulk (ISC), 1,500 MW retail, and 200 MW residential. Bulk ISC solicitations run annually (2025–2027), and at least 20% of awards target long-duration (8+ hour) resources, with two-hour systems capped at 10% but still eligible (PSC; Barclay Damon; SYSO) ([PSC, 2024](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/MatterManagement/MatterFilingItem.aspx?FilingSeq=329364&MatterSeq=55960); [Barclay Damon, 2025](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/new-york-psc-approves-nyserdas-billion-dollar-bulk-energy-storage-programwith-modifications); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/)).
- ISC mechanics and siting arbitrage. ISC monthly payments/receipts equal Strike Price minus Reference Price (REAP + RCP), providing revenue certainty over 15 years. Because Reference Prices are zonal averages, nodal siting and real-time arbitrage can outperform zonal references; Modo highlights nodes in Zone F with ~$14/MW-day arbitrage premiums (≈6%) and Long Island nodes that underperform day-ahead spreads but may beat Reference Prices via real-time spreads with advanced operations (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage)).
- VDER economics and exhaustion. VDER has driven 300% more distributed BESS than utility-scale in NY, with Con Edison incentives ($284/kW-yr for 2-hour BESS) dwarfing upstate offerings ($18–$70/kW-yr). As of Nov 2025, Con Edison’s locational incentives were 93% exhausted (only 7 MW left in three Manhattan networks). The first ISC solicitation is re-directing capital to larger, transmission-connected projects with 15-year contracts (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Interconnection and queue reform. NYISO adopted cluster studies with readiness screens (deposits $100k–$250k, maturities), aiming to reduce waits by ~50%; projects entering Q3 2024 could start construction by June 2026. Cluster projects target COD 2027–2032; Northern NY remains less attractive for arbitrage; NYC/Long Island submissions cluster due to high load and reserve needs (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Zonal geography and pipeline scale. NYISO’s first cluster accepted 30.9 GW of BESS, with Zone G leading (5,695 MW), followed by C (4,871), F (4,049), A (4,038), K (3,996), J (3,309), and others; top POIs include New Scotland 345 kV (1,350 MW) and Oakdale 345 kV (1,091 MW) (YSG Solar) ([YSG Solar, 2025](https://www.ysgsolar.com/thought-leadership/publications/nyiso-cluster-study-2023/)).
- Interconnection at distribution level. For ≤5 MW projects, the SIR governs interconnections; DPS maintains utility queue data, hosting capacity maps, and working group resources. Con Edison has raised constraints on storage interconnections; stakeholders (NY-BEST, NYSEIA) sought data transparency on these capacity constraints (DPS; NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo) ([DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).
- Reliability and locational need. NYISO’s five-year reliability view identified needs in NYC (2026), Long Island (2027), and Hudson Valley (2030), linked to peaker retirements and insufficient investment signals—suggesting capacity value concentration in zones J/K/G (IPPNY) ([IPPNY, 2024](https://www.ippny.org/page/media--publications-2/news/ippny-statement-on-nyiso-five-year-reliability-report-1121.html)).

3) DIY Alternatives

Many developers manage NY entry with internal tools and targeted consulting to minimize costs before building a pipeline. The most common DIY alternatives include:

DIY Alternatives and Trade-offs

- Internal spreadsheets plus public calculators:
  - Basic VDER Stack Models: Many developers use NYSERDA’s stock VDER calculators augmented with custom spreadsheets. However, stock tools often rely on historical, not forward-looking prices. SEA and Ascend emphasize limitations and propose improvements like forward LBMP curves, proper ICAP alternative mechanics, and capacity accreditation updates to align with market changes—improving accuracy relative to stock tools (SEA; Ascend) ([SEA, 2026](https://www.seadvantage.com/tools/calculators/ny-vder_calculator/); [Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources)).
  - ISC Revenue Pro Forma: Developers mimic ISC mechanics (Strike minus Reference) but often lack nodal/real-time arbitrage nuance. Modo’s analyses show meaningful premiums can be captured only with nodal siting and real-time optimization—beyond simple spreadsheets (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage)).
- Utility and State data portals:
  - DPS SIR, queues, and hosting capacity maps: The Department of Public Service provides standardized interconnection requirements (≤5 MW), utility interconnection queue data, and hosting capacity maps—critical for screening circuits and potential network constraints (DPS) ([DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).
  - Con Edison constraint monitoring via industry memos: Trade associations have flagged capacity constraints in Con Edison territory, underscoring the need for early, evidence-backed engagement with utilities and ombuds (NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo) ([NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).
- Outsourced analytics and market research:
  - Modo Energy research, Enverus interconnection reports, and consultant memos help developers identify viable interconnection paths and utility-friendly proposals, demonstrating how “study-ready” submissions are now essential under Order 2023 (pv magazine USA; Concentric Energy Advisors) ([pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/12/interconnection-queues-cut-across-new-renewable-and-fossil-source-timelines/); [Concentric Energy Advisors, 2025](https://ceadvisors.com/clogged-interconnection-queues-are-hindering-renewables-development-but-reforms-are-underway/)).
- Specialist permitting/engineering services:
  - In NYC, developers frequently retain FDNY/UL 9540A-savvy consultants (e.g., EticaAG), EPC partners with storage integration experience, and third-party EMS providers (e.g., Stem) to pass safety reviews and deliver stackable program value (EticaAG; Distributed Energy Infrastructure) ([EticaAG, 2026](https://eticaag.com/navigating-new-yorks-bess-incentive-landscape/); [Distributed Energy Infrastructure, 2023](https://www.distributedei.com/portfolio)).
- Capital stack workarounds:
  - NY Green Bank facilities to fund interconnection deposits and equipment procurement; state grants under RSIP; tax credit transfer markets for solar+storage hybrids (Inside Climate News; pv magazine USA; Energy-Storage.news) ([Inside Climate News, 2025](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/23062025/new-york-battery-storage-system-wind-solar/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/03/u-s-energy-storage-developers-secure-over-2-billion-in-latest-capital-infusion/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/community-energy-storage-developer-ninedot-gets-us25-million-financing-from-nyserda/)).

Pros and cons of DIY:
- Pros: Lower upfront costs, flexibility, faster internal iteration; avoids overreliance on vendors; leverages public data and state financing.
- Cons: Risk of under-modeling revenue, missing nodal premiums, overestimating VDER where locational incentives are spent, and underestimating interconnection/pre-permitting timelines—leading to failed diligence with buyers or stranded queue positions (Modo Energy; DPS; NY-BEST/NYSEIA) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).

4) Do-Nothing Analysis

What happens if NEE does not pursue a solution (i.e., avoids NY distributed entry and refrains from rigorous external validation)?

- Missed procurement windows and value inflection:
  - ISC solicitations are 2025–2027; with 30.9 GW already in the queue and developers shifting toward ISC for bankability, waiting risks missing the best years to secure indexed contracts and interconnection slots, especially in zones with reliability needs (YSG Solar; Utility Dive; IPPNY) ([YSG Solar, 2025](https://www.ysgsolar.com/thought-leadership/publications/nyiso-cluster-study-2023/); [Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-bulk-energy-storage-solicitation-battery-fire-trump/756197/); [IPPNY, 2024](https://www.ippny.org/page/media--publications-2/news/ippny-statement-on-nyiso-five-year-reliability-report-1121.html)).
  - Con Edison VDER locational incentives are nearly exhausted; late movers will not match returns realized by first-wave NYC deployments and will face higher reliance on merchant/DLM stacking (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Interconnection queue risks and delays:
  - Even with Order 2023 reforms, missing cluster windows imposes multi-year delays; projects failing maturity/readiness screens will be deprioritized; distribution-level capacity constraints in Con Edison territory hamper NYC projects without early engagement (Modo Energy; DPS; NY-BEST/NYSEIA) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).
- Competitive disadvantage in buyer markets:
  - IPP buyers increasingly demand projects with validated nodal economics and multi-program stacking; developers lacking NY-specific modeling (VDER/ISC/DLM/RSIP) or waiting for “perfect clarity” risk delivering subpar pipelines or losing buyer confidence during diligence (Modo Energy; Stem; Ascend) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage); [Stem, 2025](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/One-Pager-Maximizing-Battery-Storage-Revenue-in-New-York-Mar-2025.pdf); [Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources)).
- Opportunity cost relative to national storage boom:
  - U.S. storage deployments (2025) exceeded 28 GW/57 GWh and are expected to reach 35 GW/70 GWh in 2026 despite policy noise; capital availability remains robust—early 2026 saw $2B+ in storage financing; waiting may reduce NEE’s relevance to buyers scaling in NY (Utility Dive; pv magazine USA) ([Utility Dive, 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/600-gwh-of-us-energy-storage-expected-by-2030-benchmark-seia/813638/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/03/u-s-energy-storage-developers-secure-over-2-billion-in-latest-capital-infusion/)).

Conclusion: Inaction now likely results in weaker economics (lost VDER value), slower interconnection, and reduced buyer interest as ISC/VDER-savvy competitors lock up the best nodes and networks.

5) Landscape Summary

- Total competitors identified: 9 direct competitors with verifiable NY activity from provided sources (NineDot Energy, Convergent Energy + Power, Key Capture Energy, Calibrant, Summit Ridge Energy, Aspen Power, PowerBank, Greenbacker Renewable Energy, Qcells). Additional national platforms (e.g., CleanCapital, Hecate, East Point, Jupiter Power) are active regionally but lack NY-specific storage citations in this source set; they are likely counterparties in broader Northeast portfolios (TomorrowIQ list indicates bid activity in MA) ([TomorrowIQ, 2025](https://www.tomorrowiq.com/blog/massachusetts-awards-first-major-energy-storage-procurement)).
- Market maturity: Growing and segmenting. Distributed BESS historically outpaced utility-scale installations by 300% in NY due to VDER bankability; the 2025 ISC launch is redirecting capital to grid-scale with 15-year revenue certainty—indicating a maturing procurement ecosystem alongside a tightening distributed opportunity set downstate (Modo Energy) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Dominant approach: For 5 MW-class assets, the dominant approach remains VDER-driven monetization (especially in Con Edison territory), layered with RSIP incentives, DLM participation, and optional wholesale exposure. As VDER locational adders downstate are nearly spent, developers pivot to: (a) NYC networks with residual value, (b) carefully selected upstate circuits where DRV/LSRV and solar synergy help, and (c) bulk ISC participation for larger assets or portfolio-level strategies (Modo Energy; DPS; Barclay Damon) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [Barclay Damon, 2025](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/new-york-psc-approves-nyserdas-billion-dollar-bulk-energy-storage-programwith-modifications)).
- Gaps no current player fully addresses:
  - Bankable upstate revenue gap: Upstate VDER alone lags CONE by 44–86%, and few standardized mechanisms (outside ISC) close this delta for 5 MW-class assets; opportunities include utility RFPs, bilateral CfDs with LSEs/large buyers, and precise nodal siting to beat zonal references (Modo Energy; YSG Solar) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [YSG Solar, 2025](https://www.ysgsolar.com/thought-leadership/publications/nyiso-cluster-study-2023/)).
  - Con Edison interconnection transparency: Persistent uncertainty around available capacity and technical constraints hampers distributed project bankability; developers require deeper utility engagement and documented readiness plans (NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo) ([NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).
  - Systematic ISC outperformance tools for mid-market developers: While large platforms invest in analytics and forecasting to beat ISC references, there is a gap in turnkey, NYISO-specific performance toolchains for 5–20 MW operators—suggesting value in partnerships with analytics/optimization vendors (Modo Energy; Stem; Ascend) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage); [Stem, 2025](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/One-Pager-Maximizing-Battery-Storage-Revenue-in-New-York-Mar-2025.pdf); [Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources)).
- Fragmentation level: Moderate. A handful of specialized downstate players (NineDot, Summit Ridge, Calibrant) dominate NYC distributed BESS; several regionally active developers/investors (Greenbacker, Aspen, PowerBank, Key Capture) build portfolios across upstate and downstate. The market is consolidating around capabilities (siting analytics, permitting, and program stacking) more than pure land pipelines.

Strategic Implications and Recommendations for NEE

Opinion: Pursue a focused market entry (“go with guardrails”), concentrating on opportunities where NEE can credibly meet Board return thresholds by mitigating revenue, interconnection, and policy risks through targeted siting, rigorous modeling, and partnerships. Avoid broad exposure to upstate standalone BESS unless alternative revenue (e.g., utility RFPs, bilateral CfDs, or targeted DRV/LSRV) closes the CONE gap; deploy solar+storage where community solar value can be captured under VDER.

Recommended entry thesis

- Geographic focus:
  - Downstate (Zones J/K and adjacent G/H/I): Prioritize distributed FTM BESS in Con Ed and PSEG-LI territories where (a) VDER residual value remains and (b) DLM/affordability (S-SFA) stacking is feasible; pursue sites near peaker-replacement value and in DACs to align with PSC equity targets (≥35% of bulk/front-of-meter capacity in G–K; ≥30% in Zone J) (PSC; Energy-Storage.news) ([PSC, 2024](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/MatterManagement/MatterFilingItem.aspx?FilingSeq=329364&MatterSeq=55960); [Energy-Storage.news, 2024](https://www.energy-storage.news/new-york-launches-42-5mw-of-bess-projects-including-like-for-like-gas-turbine-replacer/)).
  - Upstate (Zones A–F): Emphasize 5 MW AC community solar+storage on circuits with high hosting capacity and favorable substation conditions; target DRV/LSRV-enhanced VDER and consider alignment with local utility solicitations or bilateral CfDs to secure floor revenues. Carefully screen nodes in Zones F/C where nodal premiums have historically exceeded zonal references in ISC analysis—applicable insight even for distribution-level pricing under LBMP-linked VDER (Modo Energy; DPS) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage); [DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).
- Asset class and duration:
  - 5 MW BESS: In NYC networks, target 2–4h paired with operational strategies to capture DLM and peak pricing; in higher-need zones near capacity constraints, consider 6–8h to enhance ICAP-linked value (VDER ICAP Alternatives and NYISO accreditation trends). For larger near-bulk (>5 MW) or aggregated portfolios, consider 8–10h where capacity and reliability needs justify higher Strike Prices under ISC, noting Modo’s estimates of up to 95% higher capacity payments for 10-hour systems in NYC (Modo Energy; Ascend) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources)).
- Revenue stack and procurement:
  - Short-term: Exploit remaining VDER opportunities (including DRV/LSRV), RSIP rebates, and S-SFA enrollments to bolster project bankability; integrate DLM where operationally feasible. Use NY Green Bank for development financing and post-COD debt where applicable (Modo Energy; Inside Climate News; pv magazine USA) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Inside Climate News, 2025](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/23062025/new-york-battery-storage-system-wind-solar/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/03/u-s-energy-storage-developers-secure-over-2-billion-in-latest-capital-infusion/)).
  - Medium-term: Prepare to bid into 2026–2027 ISC solicitations for 5–20 MW projects at transmission nodes near favorable POIs (e.g., New Scotland, Oakdale) to secure 15-year indexed revenue certainty; employ nodal analytics to justify competitive Strike Prices and plan operations to outperform Reference Prices (YSG Solar; Modo Energy) ([YSG Solar, 2025](https://www.ysgsolar.com/thought-leadership/publications/nyiso-cluster-study-2023/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage)).
- Interconnection and permitting:
  - Distribution-level: Use DPS SIR resources, hosting capacity maps, and utility queue data to pre-screen feeders/substations; document readiness (site control, UL 9540A compliance, FDNY strategy where needed). Engage the Interconnection Ombuds early; leverage the NY-BEST/NYSEIA dialogue with Con Edison to anticipate constraints and develop alternative network targets (DPS; NY-BEST/NYSEIA) ([DPS, 2026](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA, 2025](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/policy_files/policy_2025/11.5.25_NY-BEST_NYSEIA_Memo_.pdf)).
  - Transmission-level (ISC candidates): Align with cluster study timelines; ensure readiness screens are satisfied (interconnection request status, permitting pathways); use Modo’s interconnection reform guidance to prevent multi-year slide from missing cluster windows (Modo Energy; Barclay Damon) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Barclay Damon, 2025](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/new-york-psc-approves-nyserdas-billion-dollar-bulk-energy-storage-programwith-modifications)).

Risk controls and partnerships

- Modeling rigor: Adopt or subscribe to at least one independent VDER valuation platform (Ascend or SEA) and one ISC/nodal analytics source (Modo) to validate internal pro formas and support IPP buyer diligence. Commission third-party memos at IC (investment committee) gates (Ascend; SEA; Modo) ([Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources); [SEA, 2026](https://www.seadvantage.com/tools/calculators/ny-vder_calculator/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage)).
- Operational optimization: Partner with EMS/asset managers (e.g., Stem) for DLM/VDER/wholesale stacking and real-time spread capture to exceed zonal references; embed these services in buyer-facing O&M proposals to de-risk offtake (Stem) ([Stem, 2025](https://www.stem.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/One-Pager-Maximizing-Battery-Storage-Revenue-in-New-York-Mar-2025.pdf)).
- Financing readiness: Engage NY Green Bank early for development facilities; align with equity providers active in NY distributed markets (e.g., platforms similar to Aspen, Greenbacker) and craft exit optionality (sale at NTP, after IA, or post-COD) to satisfy IPP buyer preferences (Inside Climate News; Mercom; pv magazine USA) ([Inside Climate News, 2025](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/23062025/new-york-battery-storage-system-wind-solar/); [Mercom, 2026](https://mercomcapital.com/aspen-power-acquires-first-two-projects-of-18-mw-solar-portfolio/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/02/03/u-s-energy-storage-developers-secure-over-2-billion-in-latest-capital-infusion/)).

Guardrails for Go/No-Go by March 31, 2026

- Approve a focused NY entry if:
  - At least three downstate distributed BESS sites can be identified with clear interconnection paths and documented residual VDER/DLM value, achieving IRR within Board thresholds using third-party valuation.
  - At least two upstate hybrid solar+storage community projects on favorable circuits pass IC with independent VDER modeling and a documented buyer interest list.
  - NEE secures at least one analytics subscription (Modo, Ascend/SEA) and one EMS/optimization partner (e.g., Stem) under LOI to support outperformance claims during buyer diligence.
- Defer entry if:
  - No downstate circuits with feasible interconnection and residual VDER/DLM value are identified; or
  - Upstate projects cannot close the 44–86% VDER-to-CONE gap via solar synergy and stackable incentives; or
  - NEE cannot resource ISC analytics and bid-readiness by Q3 2026 for a 2026–2027 submission (Modo) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
