Title: Analyst and Expert Coverage of Distributed BESS and Solar+Storage Project Development for New York Market Entry (2024–2026)

Executive Summary

New Energy Equity (NEE) is considering long-term entry into New York’s distributed battery energy storage system (BESS) and solar+storage market (approximately 5 MW class). Across the last 24 months, analyst and expert coverage converges on three critical realities that directly inform NEE’s Go/No-Go decision for March 31, 2026:

- Policy tailwinds exist but are uneven across segments and are time-sensitive. New York’s Public Service Commission (PSC) set a 6 GW by 2030 storage target in June 2024, accompanied by significant new funding via NYSERDA, including $675 million for retail distributed projects (commercial, non-residential, community) and $100 million for residential, and a separate Index Storage Credit (ISC) program to procure 3,000 MW bulk storage with at least 20% long-duration awards. However, for distributed projects, the most bankable locational VDER incentives in Con Edison territory are substantially exhausted, compressing returns, and there is evidence developers are pivoting capital toward bulk ISC solicitations where revenue floors are more financeable (via 15-year contracts) than merchant/VDER-only stacks. Policy durability is strong at the state level, but execution risks (interconnection backlogs, siting/moratoria, wage requirements, localization to disadvantaged communities) remain material (Energy-Storage.news; Modo Energy; Foley Hoag; SYSO) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025](https://www.energy-storage.news/policy-and-regulation-risks-are-a-common-theme-in-europe-and-us-energy-storage-markets/); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/may/nyserda-releases-draft-rfp-for-2025-bulk-storage-procurement/); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/)).

- Revenue stack volatility is high and trending toward contracted structures over pure merchant. Analysts (Deloitte; Wood Mackenzie, via Energy-Storage.news) and industry forums emphasize margin compression in merchant markets and investor preference for tolls/floors or contracted revenue, particularly for projects above modest scale. New York’s distributed (≤5 MW) projects have historically relied on VDER’s value stack for bankability; however, exhaustion of Con Edison’s locational incentives and evolving wholesale dynamics under NYISO’s marginal capacity accreditation (MRI) have weakened the financeability of purely VDER-driven standalone storage. Conversely, the ISC for bulk (≥5 MW, transmission-interconnected) is drawing capital due to revenue certainty. This dynamic does not eliminate distributed plays but raises the bar: returns likely hinge on capturing remaining retail incentive blocks, optimized VDER stacking (DRV/capacity/E), NY-Sun solar credits in hybrids, and/or revenue from NYISO’s DER Participation Model aggregation—and are achievable only in select locations and structures. The market is bifurcating between contracted (ISC or PPA-backed solar+storage) and highly selective distributed opportunities with durable locational value (Deloitte; Energy-Storage.news; Modo Energy) ([Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html); [Energy-Storage.news, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/us-grid-scale-bess-market-could-shrink-by-almost-a-third-in-2026-wood-mackenzie-says); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Interconnection feasibility improved procedurally but remains the pacing item; siting is a non-trivial gating risk. NYISO’s interconnection queue reforms (FERC Order 2023 compliance) and clustering could reduce wait times by ~50% for certain bulk projects, while distributed projects rely on utility interconnection processes that remain congested in high-value zones. Only ~9% of projects entering NYISO’s historical queue reach operation, and batteries dominate the new queue (with >19 GW in 2026), but analysts expect New York to be late relative to the 6 GW goal absent expanded ISC cycles, continued cost declines, and streamlined permitting. Local moratoria or community pushback can abruptly derail projects, particularly in Long Island/NYC, despite state targets and NYPA-driven public development. For 5 MW class distributed projects, DSIP-level constraints, network hosting limits, and segment-specific rules must be navigated early, with readiness and site control now prerequisites for bankability (Modo Energy; Enverus; Energy-Storage.news) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Enverus, 2026a](https://www.enverus.com/ebooks/2026-iso-dynamics-and-market-trends); [Energy-Storage.news, 2025b](https://www.energy-storage.news/hecate-cancels-650mw-long-island-new-york-bess-project)).

Our recommendation is a conditional, phased “Go” for NEE, centered on a 2026–2027 pilot portfolio of 10–20 MW across 3–5 distributed sites, only if NEE can (1) lock NYSERDA retail incentive blocks early, (2) co-locate with community solar to harvest NY-Sun plus VDER Environmental Value (where feasible), (3) underwrite returns with conservative post-LSRV VDER assumptions and validated wholesale price/capacity models reflecting NYISO MRI, (4) secure prioritized distribution interconnection positions and community support, and (5) keep optionality open to bulk ISC participation via partnerships or JV structures. Absent these risk mitigations and early incentive lock-in, a broad New York distributed storage market entry is unlikely to meet Board return thresholds in the 2026–2028 window (Modo Energy; Foley Hoag; SYSO; Power Advisory; NYISO; Deloitte) ([Modo Energy, 2026b](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/may/nyserda-releases-draft-rfp-for-2025-bulk-storage-procurement/); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/); [Power Advisory LLC, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [NYISO, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/-/press-release-nyiso-releases-new-white-paper-detailing-drivers-of-rising-electricity-prices); [Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html)).

Scope and Method

This report synthesizes analyst and expert coverage since January 2024, with emphasis on 2025–2026, across reputable analyst houses (Deloitte, IDC), market intelligence firms (Wood Mackenzie as quoted/summarized in industry outlets), ISO market analysts (Enverus), policy/market specialists (Modo Energy, SEIA), and trade press (Energy-Storage.news, PV Magazine USA). Where Gartner/Forrester primary BESS development coverage was not identified, the closest relevant analyses and rankings are noted. New York–specific regulatory and program updates were included where public summaries exist from legal advisories, NYSERDA program communications, and market analysts. All claims are accompanied by APA-style in-text citations with hyperlinks.

2. Market Narrative: Consensus, Divergence, and Maturity

Consensus points across analysts and experts:

- Storage growth remains strong but uneven. US installations accelerated through 2025; 2026 remains robust but is sensitive to policy and interconnection. Pairing with solar is dominant in utility-scale and rising in distributed segments (Deloitte; pv magazine USA; SEIA) ([Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/11/five-data-points-defining-the-u-s-energy-storage-revolution/); [SEIA, 2026](https://seia.org/research-resources/energy-storage-market-outlook/)).

- Merchant risk is rising; contracted structures and floors are favored. A global and US theme: investors seek revenue certainty; storage developers increasingly look to long-term contracts (e.g., ISC) or solar+storage PPAs. High volatility in ancillary/energy arbitrage is insufficient alone for bankable financing at scale (Energy-Storage.news; Deloitte) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/no-project-above-100mw-is-fully-merchant-bankability-in-focus-as-energy-storage-summit-2026-kicks-off); [Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html)).

- Interconnection and siting are bottlenecks. NYISO’s backlog is acute; new Cluster Study process helps but throughput remains constrained; only a small share of historical queue culminates in COD. Local permitting can derail otherwise viable projects; early community engagement is critical (Enverus; Modo Energy; Energy-Storage.news) ([Enverus, 2026a](https://www.enverus.com/ebooks/2026-iso-dynamics-and-market-trends); [Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Energy-Storage.news, 2025b](https://www.energy-storage.news/hecate-cancels-650mw-long-island-new-york-bess-project)).

- Policy tailwinds are necessary but not sufficient. New York’s 6 GW target and NYSERDA funding programs are material supports, but execution (procurement cadence, DER program design, queue alignment, capacity accreditation impacts) determines actual bankability. Analysts expect New York to struggle to fully hit 6 GW by 2030 unless ISC is expanded and administered efficiently in sync with interconnection (Energy-Storage.news; Foley Hoag; SYSO) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/us-grid-scale-bess-market-could-shrink-by-almost-a-third-in-2026-wood-mackenzie-says); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/may/nyserda-releases-draft-rfp-for-2025-bulk-storage-procurement/); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/)).

- Load growth from electrification and AI/data centers intensifies peaking needs. NYISO anticipates ~1.9 GW growth from building/transport electrification within 10 years and more than 2.5 GW from large industrial/data centers by 2035; winter peaks and north–south constraints raise LMP variability—favorable for storage near NYC/Long Island (Wood Mackenzie; NYISO) ([Wood Mackenzie, 2026a](https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-power-market-outlooks-winter-202526-hot-topics-for-miso-nyiso-and-pjm/); [NYISO, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/-/press-release-nyiso-releases-new-white-paper-detailing-drivers-of-rising-electricity-prices)).

Where analysts diverge or highlight nuanced tensions:

- 2026 trajectory: Some sources highlight potential slowdown (WoodMac: -29% risk in US grid-scale deployments), while others note strong additions and cumulative growth (pv magazine USA forecasting 70 GWh in 2026). Both views can coexist regionally: constrained ISOs (NYISO/ISONE/PJM) may lag even as ERCOT/CAISO continue rapid additions (Energy-Storage.news; pv magazine USA) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/us-grid-scale-bess-market-could-shrink-by-almost-a-third-in-2026-wood-mackenzie-says); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/11/five-data-points-defining-the-u-s-energy-storage-revolution/)).

- Distributed vs bulk: Distributed storage/VPP narratives emphasize growth (Deloitte; SEIA), but in New York, the bankability of distributed BESS has tightened due to VDER locational incentive exhaustion in Con Edison service area. Analysts (Modo Energy) indicate capital is shifting to bulk ISC solicitations; distributed viability persists only in selective geographies with remaining incentives or strong DRV/capacity values and in solar+storage hybrids (Deloitte; Modo Energy) ([Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

Market maturity classification:

- For the US overall, storage is in a growth phase with increasing maturity in certain functions (e.g., solar-paired utility storage, grid-forming capabilities, standardization of RFOs, bankability criteria). For New York distributed BESS specifically, the market is transitional: moving from policy-supported VDER-heavy bankability (2018–2023) toward tighter economics requiring either (a) newly funded retail incentives, (b) aggregation under FERC 2222, (c) solar+storage hybrids with NY-Sun support, and/or (d) selective siting in high-value networks. Bulk ISC procurement is more mature in terms of contracting mechanism but still early in award cadence. Net: moderate maturity for distributed BESS in NY with execution risks; moderate-to-advanced maturity for bulk storage contracting; overall trend is advancing but gated by interconnection and siting.

7. New York–Specific Deep Dive: Policy, Interconnection, Economics

- Policy scaffolding and funding:
  - PSC 2024 Order set 6 GW by 2030; approved additional NYSERDA funding: ~3,000 MW bulk (ISC procurement, $1.33–$2.94B est. total incentive depending on market net revenue), $675M retail for 1,500 MW, $100M residential for 200 MW; residential/retail use per-kWh capacity-based incentives; retail cap of 5 MW at 4-hour (20 MWh); prevailing wage for ≥1 MWac; geographic equity via DACs and NYISO zone allocations; in-service by 12/31/2030 unless extended. At least 20% of bulk awards for ≥8-hour duration (SYSO summary; Foley Hoag) ([SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/may/nyserda-releases-draft-rfp-for-2025-bulk-storage-procurement/)).

  - Retail/Residential: NYSERDA launched $775M for residential and retail energy storage in April 2025, covering the distributed market that NEE targets; incentives structured to revive distributed growth but subject to block exhaustion dynamics similar to NY-Sun/VDER in prior cycles (Energy-Storage.news, 2025c) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025c](https://www.energy-storage.news/tag/nyiso)).

- Interconnection:
  - NYISO’s Cluster Study adopted per FERC Order 2023; two tracks (Fast Track for mature projects, Cluster for earlier-stage); for projects entering Q3 2024, construction by June 2026 is an aspirational but improved timeline; deposits increased ($100k–$250k+) to deter speculation; >19 GW of BESS in the new cluster, but high historical attrition (~9% success rate) persists. Batteries dominate both tracks; Zone J/K proposals concentrate where arbitrage and capacity value are highest (Modo Energy, 2025a) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

  - Distributed interconnection (utility DSIP level) remains case-by-case with hosting capacity constraints in NYC networks; timing aligned with utility processes and targeted “Fast Track” pathways where available. NEE should treat interconnection timeline as a primary risk variable in financial modeling, with milestone-gated capital allocation.

- Economics and revenue stack for distributed BESS:
  - VDER Value Stack historically provided bankability, especially in Con Edison territory through Locational System Relief Value (LSRV) and DRV; ConEd LSRV at ~$284/kW-yr (2-hour BESS) fell to ~$140/kW-yr post-exhaustion by late 2025 (93% exhausted; ~7 MW remaining). This materially reduces fixed floors that lenders used to size debt; distributed BESS deployment slowed in NYC absent replacement incentives (Modo Energy, 2026a) ([Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

  - Retail incentives (2025–2026 blocks) can partially restore bankability for 5 MW/20 MWh class projects; projects must optimize DRV/capacity windows and energy arbitrage; risk remains that wholesale price suppression during sunny hours can compress arbitrage value for solar+storage unless storage shifts to evening peaks consistently (Power Advisory; Ascend Analytics) ([Power Advisory LLC, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [Ascend Analytics, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/vder-new-yorks-valuable-distributed-energy-generation-opportunity)).

  - Cost benchmarks: Retail installations averaged ~$567/kWh in 2022–2023 amid supply chain tightness; analysts expect stabilization/declines through H2 2026 as supply diversifies and prices cool—improving returns if incentives are captured (Power Advisory; pv magazine USA) ([Power Advisory LLC, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/11/five-data-points-defining-the-u-s-energy-storage-revolution/)).

  - DER Participation Model (NYISO): Offers potential for aggregation into wholesale markets; however, practical widespread participation remains limited thus far; marketing claims (e.g., $/MW-yr) require verification via detailed dispatch and market forecasts adjusted for MRI accreditation and seasonal peak shifts. NEE should view DER PM upside as optionality, not base case (Modo Energy, 2026a) ([Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Solar+storage hybrids (≤5 MW):
  - Community solar remains strongest in NY nationally (624 MWdc added in 2025), offering synergy to co-locate storage for DRV/capacity peak support and to tap NY-Sun program funds. However, Maine’s contraction and overall 2025 slowdown signal that program design/timing matter; in NY, hybrid projects can benefit from 25-year fixed Environmental Value rates for solar (where applicable) but must weigh charging losses and opportunity cost of energy dispatch (SEIA; Power Advisory) ([SEIA, 2026b](https://seia.org/research-resources/solar-market-insight-report-2025-year-in-review/); [Power Advisory LLC, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

- Load and capacity dynamics (NYISO):
  - Winter-peaking and capacity market reforms (separate summer/winter curves) and MRI accreditation reshape storage capacity value. Longer-duration systems (8–10 hours) may capture disproportionately higher capacity payments in load-constrained zones (Modo Energy analysis projects up to ~95% higher capacity payments for 10-hour assets in NYC), but project costs and site requirements increase; for 5 MW class, 4-hour remains the practical norm given capex and footprint tradeoffs (Wood Mackenzie; Modo Energy) ([Wood Mackenzie, 2026a](https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-power-market-outlooks-winter-202526-hot-topics-for-miso-nyiso-and-pjm/); [Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).

- Siting and community acceptance:
  - PSC/DAC requirements drive geographic targeting (≥35% in disadvantaged communities; ≥30% in Zone J; ≥5% in Zones G/H/I/K), but siting friction—moratoria, fire safety concerns, and local planning resistance—can derail projects (Energy-Storage.news examples in Long Island/Upstate). Robust emergency response plans and local engagement are increasingly mandated/expected (Energy-Storage.news; SYSO) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025c](https://www.energy-storage.news/tag/nyiso); [Energy-Storage.news, 2025b](https://www.energy-storage.news/hecate-cancels-650mw-long-island-new-york-bess-project); [SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/)).

8. Implications for NEE’s Financial Thresholds and Risk Controls

- Base case economics for 5 MW/20 MWh standalone distributed BESS in 2026–2028:
  - Without remaining LSRV or new retail incentive blocks, modeled returns often miss mid-teens equity IRRs given post-exhaustion fixed floor reductions (e.g., ConEd from ~$284 to ~$140/kW-yr) and rising complexity of stacking DRV, VDER capacity windows, and energy arbitrage under MRI and winter-peaking changes. Merchant-only exposure is not recommended (Modo Energy; Power Advisory) ([Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Power Advisory LLC, n.d.](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue)).

- Enhanced returns pathways:
  - Capture NYSERDA retail incentive early (2025–2026 block participation).
  - Co-locate with community solar to access NY-Sun and steady environmental value streams; optimize dispatch to prioritize DRV/capacity windows and shoulder/winter peaks.
  - Aggregation into NYISO DER PM as supplemental upside (not base case).
  - Target high-value networks within Con Edison where remaining LSRV/DRV potential or distribution constraints yield premium compensation.
  - Incorporate capacity value improvements via duration upsizing only if site/CapEx support; otherwise, remain at 4 hours where bankability is stronger.

- Cost levers:
  - Negotiate with top-tier integrators (per WoodMac rankings) for bankable warranties and availability guarantees; time procurement to H2 2026 price stabilization; consider DC-coupled hybrid architecture where solar co-location allows efficiency gains in charging (Wood Mackenzie; pv magazine USA) ([Wood Mackenzie, 2024](https://www.woodmac.com/reports/power-markets-global-battery-energy-storage-system-bess-integrator-rankings-2024-150297542/); [pv magazine USA, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/11/five-data-points-defining-the-u-s-energy-storage-revolution/)).

- Interconnection/siting risk treatment:
  - Milestone-based investment (interconnection deposits, studies) with defined stop-loss points.
  - Early and continuous community engagement; codify fire safety and first responder training obligations; confirm local zoning; ensure emergency response plans consistent with NYSERDA program expectations (SYSO) ([SYSO, 2026](https://www.sysotechnologies.com/2026/02/02/nyserdas-bulk-energy-storage-program/)).

- Buyer demand and exit optionality:
  - IPP buyers prefer contracted revenue and aversion to merchant-only: developers report global tilt to floors/tolls; in US, many buyers favor contracted or hybrid revenue models. For distributed portfolios, credible offtakers include utilities/aggregators for DR/DER PM programs and community solar subscribers. Ensure PPAs/creditworthy aggregators if tolling aggregators are pursued (Energy-Storage.news; Deloitte) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2026](https://www.energy-storage.news/no-project-above-100mw-is-fully-merchant-bankability-in-focus-as-energy-storage-summit-2026-kicks-off); [Deloitte Insights, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/renewable-energy/renewable-energy-industry-outlook.html)).

9. Scenario Outlook for NEE (2026–2030)

- Downside (No-Go triggers):
  - Failure to secure 2025–2026 retail incentive blocks; interconnection queues stall projects beyond 2028 COD; siting moratoria grow; VDER adjustments reduce DRV/capacity windows further; wholesale price volatility declines as renewables saturate midday; MRI further derates short duration, eroding capacity value.
  - Financial outcome: 5 MW class distributed projects fail to meet Board return thresholds; capital better deployed in bulk ISC or other ISOs.

- Base case (Selective-Go):
  - NEE secures 2–4 distributed sites (10–20 MW/40–80 MWh aggregate) with retail incentives; co-locates at least half with community solar (capturing NY-Sun and environmental value); achieves DSIP interconnection milestones within 18–24 months; designs for winter-peaking support; uses conservative post-LSRV modeling; holds option to layer DER PM revenue.
  - Financial outcome: Portfolio-level equity IRRs in low-to-mid teens achievable with controlled capex, optimized dispatch, and incentive capture; longer-dated returns depend on ongoing DRV/capacity recalibrations and VPP monetization progress.

- Upside (Accelerated-Go):
  - NYSERDA expands or replenishes retail blocks; DER PM aggregation frameworks mature with reliable settlement; ISC expands (indirectly raising buyer demand for storage portfolios); equipment costs fall faster than expected by 2027; hyperscaler/data center loads in NYISO Zones J/K accelerate network support procurements.
  - Financial outcome: Returns improve 150–300 bps; larger pipeline justified (50–100 MW distributed over 2027–2030).

10. Decision Framework and Recommendations

Recommendation: Conditional, phased Go

- Phase 1 (by Q2–Q4 2026): Pursue a tightly scoped pilot of 10–20 MW distributed BESS/solar+storage across 3–5 sites in Con Edison and one downstate Zone (H/K) with:
  - Early application to NYSERDA retail storage incentives; co-apply with NY-Sun for hybrid projects.
  - Target networks with remaining DRV/LSRV potential or proven locational need (consult utility maps/hosting capacity).
  - Secure community support and pre-consult with fire safety officials; budget for emergency response training and permitting counsel.
  - Conservative revenue modeling: use post-LSRV floors, updated DRV/capacity windows, MRI-consistent capacity credit, winter-peak dispatch, and price volatility ranges from Enverus/WoodMac; validate via independent third-party bank model.
  - Optionality on DER PM aggregation; set revenue floors via aggregator tolling if feasible.

- Phase 2 (2027–2028): Scale only if pilot meets Board thresholds and demonstrates repeatability in interconnection and permitting; add 20–40 MW more across proven circuits; evaluate JV pathway for bulk ISC to diversify revenue.

- Governance:
  - Establish investment gates: interconnection study acceptance, incentive reservation, site control/permitting certainty, financing term sheet.
  - Procurement: Shortlist integrators with proven NY track records (use WoodMac rankings as one input) and require strong availability warranties and augmentation plan.

- Risk register:
  - Policy: Track NYSERDA block exhaustion, VDER recalibrations, NYISO capacity rule updates.
  - Interconnection: Time-bound milestones; allow contingency budget for upgrades.
  - Siting: Early community engagement; alternative sites in pipeline; fire safety compliance.
  - Market: Hedge strategy for price exposure; consider partial contracting (tolling/aggregation pilots).
  - Supply chain: Stage orders to capture H2 2026 stabilization; multi-vendor flexibility.

In our judgment, a broad, immediate, and unconstrained New York distributed market entry would be high-risk for NEE in 2026. However, a disciplined, incentive-driven pilot with hybridization where feasible, plus rigorous interconnection/siting governance, offers a credible path to meet Board-defined returns. If incentive capture fails or interconnection timelines slip materially, NEE should pause distributed expansion and redirect capital toward bulk ISC (via partnerships) or other ISOs with clearer merchant/contracted revenue stacks.
