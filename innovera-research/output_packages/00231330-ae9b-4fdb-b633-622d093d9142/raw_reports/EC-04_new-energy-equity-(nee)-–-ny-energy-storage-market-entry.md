Title: Third-Party Reviews, Failure Patterns, and Workaround Evidence for Entering New York’s Distributed Energy Storage Market (≤5 MW BESS and Solar+Storage)

Date: March 13, 2026

Executive Summary

New Energy Equity (NEE) is evaluating a long-term entry into New York’s distributed battery energy storage system (BESS) and solar+storage market at ~5 MW scale. The core question is whether projects can reliably meet Board-defined returns under realistic assumptions given revenue stack uncertainty (policy incentives versus merchant exposure), interconnection feasibility, Independent Power Producer (IPP) buyer demand, and policy durability. Based on a synthesis of third-party reporting, market analyses, and operational research:

- Revenue stack reality. Distributed storage in New York historically banked on VDER (Value of Distributed Energy Resources) fixed components in Con Edison territory, where Locational System Relief Value (LSRV) reached $284/kW-year for 2-hour BESS pre-exhaustion and exceeded gross CONE, but these incentives are now largely exhausted. Post-exhaustion fixed-value revenue in Zone J fell roughly 50% to $140/kW-year, removing the debt-sizing “floor” lenders relied on. Upstate utilities’ historical value signals ($18–$70/kW-year) left 44–86% revenue gaps versus an upstate CONE of $126/kW-year. With distributed incentives tightening, bankability has shifted to larger, grid-scale projects competing for NYSERDA’s 15-year Index Storage Credit (ISC) contracts, not available to <5 MW distributed assets. This materially raises merchant and market-execution risk for distributed BESS—especially upstate—and narrows the near-term go-to-market aperture for 5 MW class projects, unless paired with compelling local value and program eligibility or hybridization with community solar. (Modo Energy, multiple pieces, 2025–2026; Power Advisory, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030); [Power Advisory, 2025](https://www.poweradvisoryllc.com/reports/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue))

- Interconnection and permitting headwinds. NYISO’s cluster study reform has accelerated study timelines and filtered speculative queues, but attrition remains extreme (historically 91% dropout), and local opposition/moratoriums have emerged as a first-order risk limiting developer returns—illustrated by delays and legal challenges even for ~1 GWh grid-scale projects, and moratoriums in some NY towns. These risks are as material as interconnection and financing, particularly for siting distributed BESS in dense communities. (Modo Energy, 2025–2026; Energy-Storage.news, 2025–2026) ([Modo Energy, 2025b](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030); [Energy-Storage.news, 2025](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-opposition-permitting-struggles-stall-progress-on-northland-powers-1gwh-bess-in-new-york/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2026a](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-authorities-enact-moratoriums-on-large-scale-bess-development-in-oregon-new-york/))

- Buyer demand signals. IPP/financier appetite for community-scale, distributed BESS in NYC remains demonstrably strong when bankable revenue structures and program alignment exist. NineDot Energy secured $431 million in debt financing to build 28 NYC distributed BESS projects (124 MW/494 MWh), with participation in VDER/demand response and utility low-income programs (Statewide Solar for All). This points to a viable secondary market for well-sited, program-aligned portfolios, but also underscores concentration of favorable economics in Zone J and the importance of structured revenues and social license to operate. (Energy-Storage.news, 2026; Canary Media, 2026) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2026b](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers/); [Canary Media, 2026](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/ninedot-energy-raises-big-money-for-small-batteries-in-new-york-city))

- Operations and data maturity gaps. Post-COD value realization is challenged by fragmented data, supplier accountability friction, and incident investigation burden. A 2026 survey of 117 BESS pros found 59% cite significant effort for root-cause analysis, 47% struggle holding suppliers to performance promises, and 50% lack a single source of truth—indicating ongoing O&M “shadow spend” and risk to achieved returns. (TWAICE survey; IndexBox webinar recap) ([TWAICE, 2026](https://www.twaice.com/guides-reports/bess-pros-survey-report-2026?null=); [IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/2026-bess-pros-survey-webinar-key-industry-priorities-and-challenges/))

- Concrete opinion: Proceed, but only via a focused, risk-managed entry pathway. Under current market conditions, NEE can meet return thresholds in New York at 5 MW scale primarily by:
  - Targeting specific Con Edison networks and near-Zone J/Zone G opportunity areas where local value is highest and community acceptance is more tractable, and pairing BESS with community solar where eligible to access NY-Sun and Retail Storage Incentive programs and stack VDER/DR revenues; and
  - Prioritizing program-anchored, portfolio-based development with early, structured buyer engagement to de-risk exit pricing; while
  - Instituting gold-standard community engagement and permitting protocols from day one to limit local opposition drag; and
  - Augmenting internal financial modeling with specialized external validation and bank-grade documentation, given policy and capacity price evolution and the post-VDER-exhaustion landscape.

Where strong program alignment, site control, and community license cannot be established, or where projects are purely upstate distributed BESS relying on merchant/VDER residuals, we view likelihood of meeting Board-required returns as low to moderate given observed revenue gaps and interconnection/permit risks.

1) Market Context: Revenue Stack, Bankability, and Policy Durability

1.1 Revenue stack reality for distributed BESS and solar+storage

- VDER/LSRV as bankability linchpin—now diminished:
  - Con Edison’s VDER incentives (2-hr BESS) peaked near $284/kW-year, exceeding a Zone J gross CONE of $183/kW-year by $101/kW-year, helping distributed BESS/s+s clear lender hurdles despite high build costs and limited offtake alternatives. However, LSRV capacity is ~93% exhausted as of Nov 2025, with only ~7 MW remaining across three Manhattan networks. Post-exhaustion, fixed VDER components fell by ~50% (to $140/kW-year), eroding the debt-sizing floor and chilling new distributed BESS buildout within NYC. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))
  - Upstate utility VDER values were only $18–$70/kW-year, leaving 44–86% revenue gaps relative to an upstate gross CONE of $126/kW-year, explaining why controlled capital flowed to Con Ed territory despite higher capex. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Distributed hybrid eligibility and examples:
  - Developers continue to pursue 5 MW AC solar+storage hybrids that can access NY-Sun and Retail Storage Incentive Programs—and the federal ITC—e.g., PowerBank’s NY-Cloverdale Rd and NY-Holland Glnwd projects (press). While press releases are not definitive bankability indicators, they confirm ongoing eligibility pathways and developer behavior to stack incentives in the distributed segment. (PowerBank, 2025–2026; RenewableEnergyMagazine, 2025; Yahoo Finance, 2025) ([PowerBank, 2026](https://powerbankcorp.com/powerbank-announces-new-5-mw-distributed-solar-and-battery-energy-storage-system-project-in-new-york/); [Renewable Energy Magazine, 2025](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/5-mw-ac-distributed-solar-and-bess-20251209); [Yahoo Finance, 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/5-mw-ac-distributed-solar-120700176.html))

- Grid-scale shift via ISC not directly available to <5 MW distributed:
  - ISC (2025–2027) offers 15-year index contracts totaling $0.7–$1.42B for >5 MW projects, with explicit allocations favoring longer-duration and larger-scale assets, and reference prices highest in Zone J. The program is emerging as the bankability bridge for grid-scale storage, with 19 GW in the first cluster study but only 2–4 GW likely COD by 2030. Distributed projects must compete without this backstop unless they scale or structure around transmission interconnections and eligibility. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Complementary DER value and program stacking:
  - Demand response and Con Ed’s CSRP/NYSERDA DLRP have been viable value components for C&I-scale systems; real case study evidence shows BTM assets coordinated for DR and demand charge management. However, platform maturity, dispatch optimization, and program stacking are operationally non-trivial and require capable EMS/control. (Energy Toolbase case study) ([Energy Toolbase, 2024](https://www.energytoolbase.com/))

- Policy durability and evolving capacity outlook:
  - NYISO’s Demand Curve Reset interim report underscores that capacity price inputs and methodology are subject to periodic updates, with September 2024 incorporating newer data. That ongoing adjustment introduces forecasting uncertainty into capacity-linked revenues for both wholesale-anchored assets and DERs participating indirectly through VDER capacity components. (NYISO DCR Interim Final, 2024) ([NYISO, 2024](https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/46168401/NYISO-Staff-DCR-Interim-Final-Report.pdf))
  - At the same time, studies suggest expanded distributed solar+storage could reduce wholesale price volatility and deliver $1B/year system savings by 2035—creating policy impetus to sustain DER compensation trajectories, particularly for winter reliability. This is supportive of long-term DER value but not a guarantee of near-term tariff levels. (PV Magazine USA reporting on Synapse study, 2026) ([PV Magazine USA, 2026a](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/01/21/new-york-study-finds-distributed-solar-and-storage-could-save-ratepayers-1-billion-annually/))

1.2 Interconnection feasibility and permitting headwinds

- NYISO interconnection queue reform:
  - Transition to clustered studies starting 2023 with the first batch in Aug 2024 aims to halve interconnection timelines; still, timing risk remains, and missing cluster windows can defer projects by cycles. Larger deposits ($100k–$250k) and milestone rigor are filtering queues and raising readiness requirements. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025b](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage))
  - Batteries dominate the queue, with Fast Track projects nearing IAs and Cluster Study projects targeting 2027–2032 CODs. Historically, 91% of interconnection requests drop out; projects failing to secure ISC (for grid-scale) are likely to withdraw—indicating the need for strong contracted revenue pathways. (Modo Energy, 2025–2026) ([Modo Energy, 2025b](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030))

- Local opposition and moratoriums:
  - Despite new 5 MW+ storage incentives, local authorities in New York have successfully delayed/halted projects via permitting challenges, lawsuits, and moratoriums; Northland Power’s 1 GWh BESS faced local headwinds despite favorable macro policy. Numerous towns have taken anti-BESS positions, making community and political risk a development-stage return determinant as material as market economics. (Energy-Storage.news, 2025–2026) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-opposition-permitting-struggles-stall-progress-on-northland-powers-1gwh-bess-in-new-york/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2026a](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-authorities-enact-moratoriums-on-large-scale-bess-development-in-oregon-new-york/))
  - NYC-specific zoning reforms (“City of Yes for Carbon Neutrality,” Dec 2023) broadened siting pathways, indicating that downstate policy instruments can unlock distributed BESS. Nevertheless, local controversies (e.g., NineDot’s Middle Village site near a school) highlight the need for high-touch engagement and risk budgets even in “policy-friendly” zones. (NineDot policy page; Queens Chronicle local report) ([NineDot Energy, 2024](https://nine.energy/regulatory); [Queens Chronicle, 2026](https://www.qchron.com/editions/central/ninedot-to-go-ahead-with-midville-bess/article_e7e2811f-f08e-5882-b7d8-c89ca9f5b445.html))

1.3 Buyer demand and secondary market dynamics

- Strong appetite for distributed BESS portfolios in NYC with structured revenues:
  - NineDot’s $431M debt financing (Natixis CIB-led) for 28 community-scale BESS (124 MW/494 MWh) signals lender and buyer confidence when project portfolios monetize VDER, DR programs, and align with social programs like Statewide Solar for All, with commercial operations expected on a rolling basis through 2027. (Energy-Storage.news, 2026; ESS News Round-up, 2026) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2026b](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers/); [ESS News, 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/02/11/us-round-up-ninedot-closes-431m-financing-for-nyc-e-storage-inks-503-mwh-texas-deal-flexgen-brings-174-mw-online/))
  - Major investors (e.g., Carlyle/Manulife through NineDot equity) and law firm transaction rosters show ongoing capital formation around distributed portfolios, suggesting durable exit optionality if assets are packaged with robust documentation and program alignment. (Kirkland & Ellis deal roster) ([Kirkland & Ellis, 2026](https://www.kirkland.com/lawyers/m/mendoza-kristin-l-pc))
  - Nevertheless, outside of NYC, limited DER aggregation and lack of widespread NYISO DER participation indicate weaker revenue substitution for lost VDER floors—dampening buyer demand for pure upstate distributed BESS merchant exposure near term. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

3) Workaround Evidence: What Teams Do Without a “Dedicated Product”

3.1 Manual processes and spreadsheet-based financial modeling

- Spreadsheet-heavy financial modeling persists:
  - Finance teams routinely rely on Excel/Sheets for FP&A and project modeling; as data complexity and refresh cadence increase, spreadsheets struggle with version control, collaboration, and auditability—risks exacerbated in incentive-driven project finance. (Cube Software, 2026; Sievo, 2026) ([Cube, 2026](https://www.cubesoftware.com/blog/financial-planning-and-analysis-fpa); [Sievo, 2026a](https://sievo.com/en/resources/spend-analysis-101))
  - In distributed energy contexts, the reliance on spreadsheets to consolidate tariff, DR event history, and interconnection milestones creates governance risk (multiple versions, manual formula errors, data silos), particularly for Board-level investment cases requiring audit-ready assumptions. (Sievo, 2026) ([Sievo, 2026b](https://sievo.com/en/resources/spend-analysis-101))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: High among small-to-mid developers; moderate among scaled platforms.
- Cost: 0.25–1.0 FTE per portfolio for model maintenance and versioning; additional $25k–$150k annually in “rework” and review costs for IC packs and lender due diligence, rising with portfolio size.

3.2 Internal tools and modeling platforms purpose-built for NY value stacks

- Specialist modeling to replace NYSERDA calculators:
  - Ascend Analytics’ PowerVAL provides detailed VDER asset valuation for solar, storage hybrid, and standalone storage. It claims ~40% higher value realization relative to the NYSERDA VDER calculator via more sophisticated optimization, reflecting “real world dynamics.” This suggests that developers often need external tooling to avoid under/overestimation of revenues and to build lender-ready cases—i.e., “workaround” for limitations in default calculators. (Ascend Analytics, 2025) ([Ascend, 2025](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/new-york-vder-program-value-of-distributed-energy-resources))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: Moderate among professionalized developers; low among first-timers.
- Cost: $50k–$200k/year for software licensing and analyst time (varies); still requires domain expert oversight to calibrate DR, peak windows, and interconnection timing assumptions.

3.3 Outsourced financial modeling and diligence augmentation

- Financial modeling outsourcing sees rising demand:
  - 2026 trends point to companies outsourcing model builds, scenario packs for IC/lenders, returns bridges, and recurring refresh services—explicitly to cope with forecasting agility and auditability demands. Buyers value governance, QA, and maintained version controls as premium differentiators in a world where GenAI and automation are entering finance workflows. (Magistral Consulting, 2026) ([Magistral, 2026a](https://magistralconsulting.com/financial-modeling-outsourcing-trends-in-2026/); [Magistral, 2026b](https://magistralconsulting.com/financial-modeling-outsourcing-trends-in-2026/))
- Statement of Work (SOW) integration and visibility are also trending, indicating a normalization of using external vendors for discrete deliverables with more rigorous governance (e.g., modeling, interconnection tracking, permitting progress dashboards). (RedOak Tech, 2026) ([RedOak Tech, 2026](https://redoaktech.com/the-future-of-sow-spend-in-2026/))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: Moderate and growing for developers entering new markets or adding storage for the first time.
- Cost: $75k–$300k per 6–9 month market entry program plus ad-hoc per-project model banks ($10k–$50k each) and quarterly update retainers.

3.4 Interconnection and permitting workarounds

- Toolkits and process updates:
  - IREC’s BATRIES toolkit (Mar 2022) emerged to address eight core barriers to energy storage interconnection; DOE guidance emphasizes updating interconnection rules to explicitly include storage pairing. These toolkits indicate that developers and regulators needed “manual” governance improvements—often requiring developer-led education and structured submissions—to prevent delays. (IREC, 2022; DOE, 2022) ([IREC, 2022](https://irecusa.org/resources/toolkit-and-guidance-for-the-interconnection-of-energy-storage-and-solar-plus-storage/); [DOE, 2022](https://www.energy.gov/eere/solar/articles/success-story-improving-interconnection-solar-energy-and-battery-storage))
  - Interconnection tracking: third-party queue trackers (e.g., Interconnection.fyi) have grown as developers need out-of-band tools to monitor status and find comparable queue precedents, underscoring the lack of a unified, developer-facing “single source of truth.” (Interconnection.fyi, example) ([Interconnection.fyi, n.d.](https://www.interconnection.fyi/project/ercot-25inr0764))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: High (toolkits); Moderate (queue trackers).
- Cost: Staff time to adopt toolkits (50–150 hours per project early-stage); queue tracking tools subscriptions ($5k–$30k/year) plus analyst time.

3.5 DER program participation and EMS/controls

- Revenue stacking via DR and value stack optimization:
  - Case studies show deployments sized for demand charge management, DR participation (Con Ed CSRP/NYSERDA DLRP), and microgrid capabilities. Execution requires EMS software and consistent monitoring—implying specialized integration beyond generic project finance modeling. (Energy Toolbase, 2024) ([Energy Toolbase, 2024](https://www.energytoolbase.com/))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: Moderate in NYC C&I and community-scale segments.
- Cost: EMS licensing and integration ($10k–$100k per site lifetime), plus performance management labor (0.1–0.25 FTE per 5–10 site pod).

3.6 Policy and program engagement “workarounds”

- Active regulatory monitoring:
  - Developers like NineDot publicly track and advocate around NYC zoning, statewide low-income program design, and federal tax credit adders—indicating that policy fluency and proactive engagement are unofficial “workarounds” to navigate evolving eligibility and value capture. (NineDot advocacy page) ([NineDot Energy, 2024](https://nine.energy/regulatory))

Estimated prevalence and cost:
- Prevalence: Moderate among experienced DER developers.
- Cost: Policy counsel/consultants ($50k–$200k/year) plus internal time.

4) Shadow Spend Evidence

This spend substitutes for in-house staff but persists even when internal teams exist, driven by bandwidth and governance needs. (Magistral, 2026) ([Magistral, 2026a](https://magistralconsulting.com/financial-modeling-outsourcing-trends-in-2026/))

(RedOak Tech, 2026) ([RedOak Tech, 2026](https://redoaktech.com/the-future-of-sow-spend-in-2026/))

5) Failure Pattern Synthesis

5.1 Dominant failure modes

- Local approval and permitting risk at the outset:
  - Well-documented across multiple New York jurisdictions, including lawsuits/moratoriums that can halt otherwise-bankable projects. Returns are exposed to community and political risk as materially as to market economics; protracted delays erode NPV and appetite from secondary buyers wary of reputational and legal drag. (Energy-Storage.news, 2025–2026; local press) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2025](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-opposition-permitting-struggles-stall-progress-on-northland-powers-1gwh-bess-in-new-york/); [Energy-Storage.news, 2026a](https://www.energy-storage.news/local-authorities-enact-moratoriums-on-large-scale-bess-development-in-oregon-new-york/); [Queens Chronicle, 2026](https://www.qchron.com/editions/central/ninedot-to-go-ahead-with-midville-bess/article_e7e2811f-f08e-5882-b7d8-c89ca9f5b445.html))

- Incentive exhaustion and revenue erosion:
  - In Zone J, the exhaustion of fixed LSRV diminished the “floor” used by lenders, cutting fixed revenues ~50% and tightening bankability for new distributed projects; upstate VDER values remain insufficient on their own for standalone distributed BESS to clear returns reliably. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Interconnection attrition and readiness gating:
  - Cluster reform raises capital commitments and readiness standards; historical 91% attrition underscores how many projects cannot bridge from queue to IA to COD without contracted revenue and capex certainty—conditions tougher for <5 MW DERs not eligible for ISC. (Modo Energy, 2025–2026) ([Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030))

- DER aggregation shortfall:
  - Though DER aggregation could theoretically stack DRV with wholesale revenues, New York has not seen widespread participation—reducing realistic optionality to replace exhausted VDER components in near term. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- O&M data fragmentation and supplier accountability:
  - Post-COD, operators cite lack of a single source of truth, heavy lift for root-cause analysis, and difficulty holding vendors to performance SLAs—adding ongoing operational risk to returns realization. (TWAICE, 2026; IndexBox, 2026) ([TWAICE, 2026](https://www.twaice.com/guides-reports/bess-pros-survey-report-2026?null=); [IndexBox, 2026](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/2026-bess-pros-survey-webinar-key-industry-priorities-and-challenges/))

5.2 Biggest underserved need

- Bankable, program-aligned revenue floors for distributed-scale BESS outside of narrow Con Edison geographies—and a replicable path to de-risk local opposition early. Today, bankability tends to concentrate in NYC (VDER legacy, DR, special programs) and grid-scale ISC-eligible projects. The “missing middle” is a standardized way for 5 MW class assets—especially upstate—to secure predictable revenue without betting on merchant spreads or speculative DER aggregation. Without that, interconnection attrition and permitting headwinds will continue to choke viable distributed BESS supply for IPP buyers.

5.3 Workaround prevalence

- Workarounds prevalence: High.
  - Spreadsheets and internal models: High.
  - Specialized valuation tools (Ascend), EMS/controls: Moderate to high for sophisticated teams.
  - Outsourced modeling and SOW governance: Moderate and rising.
  - Policy engagement and toolkits (IREC/DOE): High among active developers.
  - Third-party queue tracking: Moderate.

6) Implications for NEE’s Board Decision and Go/No-Go Framework

6.1 Where and how NEE can meet return thresholds

- Highest-likelihood pathways (2026–2028 CODs):
  - NYC/Con Edison footprint or adjacent networks where:
    - Remaining or successor local relief constructs, DR programs, and community solar program structures can be stacked for distributed BESS/hybrids; and
    - Zoning reforms (City of Yes) reduce permitting friction, and community engagement can be structured early with transparent safety cases (NFPA 855, UL 9540A test data) and site conditioning to lower opposition risk. (NineDot regulatory; local opposition reporting shows the counterfactual) ([NineDot Energy, 2024](https://nine.energy/regulatory); [Queens Chronicle, 2026](https://www.qchron.com/editions/central/ninedot-to-go-ahead-with-midville-bess/article_e7e2811f-f08e-5882-b7d8-c89ca9f5b445.html))
  - Hybrid solar+storage at 5 MW AC leveraging NY-Sun plus Retail Storage Incentive Programs and federal ITC—mirroring PowerBank’s approach—to establish a “program floor” while optimizing operational upsides via DR and value stack residuals. (PowerBank press; Renewable Energy Magazine) ([PowerBank, 2026](https://powerbankcorp.com/powerbank-announces-new-5-mw-distributed-solar-and-battery-energy-storage-system-project-in-new-york/); [Renewable Energy Magazine, 2025](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/5-mw-ac-distributed-solar-and-bess-20251209))

- Moderate-likelihood pathways:
  - Zone G (Hudson Valley) with careful siting to balance moderate Net CONE, foreseeable tight local capacity margins (thermal retirements), and comparatively easier build cycles than Zone J. This region is central to early storage development but is more oriented to grid-scale battery economics than distributed DER. Distributed projects here must lean heavily on hybridization and DR programs to offset lack of ISC eligibility. (Modo Energy, 2026) ([Modo Energy, 2026a](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030))

- Low-likelihood pathways near term:
  - Upstate standalone distributed BESS relying primarily on VDER remainder and merchant opportunities. Materials show persistent value gaps relative to CONE and limited DER aggregation uptake to fill the void. (Modo Energy, 2025) ([Modo Energy, 2025a](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

6.2 Buyer demand and exit positioning

- NYC distributed portfolios show robust buyer and lender appetite (NineDot precedent), especially when packaged with:
  - Programmatic revenue structures and policy alignment (VDER legacy, DR, social programs like S-SFA),
  - Strong interconnection status and permit risk mitigation,
  - Operations readiness with consolidated data and supplier performance guardrails to address known O&M frictions. (Energy-Storage.news; Canary Media; TWAICE) ([Energy-Storage.news, 2026b](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers/); [Canary Media, 2026](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/ninedot-energy-raises-big-money-for-small-batteries-in-new-york-city); [TWAICE, 2026](https://www.twaice.com/guides-reports/bess-pros-survey-report-2026?null=))

6.3 Conditions precedent for a “Go” decision by March 31, 2026

- Program-anchored pipeline:
  - A minimum tranche (e.g., 20–30 MW across 5–8 distributed sites) in Con Edison or near-Zone J/Zone G with hybrid eligibility (NY-Sun + Retail Storage Incentive), DR program comparables, and modeled DER value that has been validated by an external, bank-grade model (Ascend/PowerVAL-type calibration).
- Permitting and community:
  - Portfolio-level community engagement plan with pre-filing listening sessions, independent safety experts, and documented emergency response protocols; formal letters of support where feasible; escalation pathways for high-sensitivity sites (e.g., near schools).
- Interconnection readiness:
  - Cluster timing and distribution-level screening completed; advanced queue positions with clear upgrade costs and construction windows; internal interconnection tracking and change-control discipline established.
- Operations and data plan:
  - EMS/control selection with proven DR integration; post-COD data architecture that eliminates “multiple portal” fragmentation and documents supplier SLAs, error code centralization, and incident playbooks.
- Buyer engagement:
  - Early dialogues with IPP/acquirers to shape portfolio specs (duration, grid services, contract tenor of site leases) and align on exit valuation methodologies (P50 vs P90 revenue stacks, debt sizing conventions post-VDER LSRV).

Absent these conditions, the base-case probability of meeting Board return thresholds is materially diminished by observed failure patterns.

7) Recommendations and Next Steps

7.1 Strategic posture

- Focused entry, not broad market sweep:
  - Target a first-wave portfolio where the combination of geography (Con Ed/Zone J—select Zone G), hybrid eligibility, and community license is strongest. Avoid speculative upstate standalone distributed BESS reliant on merchant spreads or unproven DER aggregation participation near term.

- Build-to-sell with programmatic floors:
  - Develop a portfolio for divestiture to IPP buyers seeking operating/near-term COD assets with structured DER monetization, mirroring established buyer appetite evidenced by recent financings.

7.2 Capability and process

- External model validation and audit readiness:
  - Commission an external valuation stack (e.g., Ascend PowerVAL or comparable) to validate NEE’s internal models; insist on full assumption books, QA checklists, version controls, and lender-facing outputs to accelerate diligence.

- Interconnection and permitting governance:
  - Adopt IREC/DOE toolkit guidance to standardize submissions and utility coordination. Stand up a portfolio interconnection tracker; assign clear stage gates and go/no-go triggers per site.

- Community engagement program:
  - Create a repeatable playbook with third-party safety experts, standardized FAQs, and rapid-response protocols to preempt opposition. Allocate explicit risk budgets and alternative-site options.

- Operations readiness against known frictions:
  - Select EMS/control partners with proven CSRP/DLRP integration; implement data centralization for error codes and performance metrics; pre-negotiate SLAs and accountability clauses to mitigate supplier friction.

7.3 Decision timeline and checkpoints (by March 31, 2026)

- Complete pipeline screening and site control for at least 20 MW in target geos.
- Externalize the revenue stack validation and deliver Board-ready returns bridges (base, downside, and exhaustion/DR variability).
- Secure preliminary interconnection screens and community engagement kickoffs for each site.
- Initiate buyer soundings; obtain non-binding IOIs reflecting market appetite for the contemplated portfolio structure.

If these deliverables cannot be met by the decision date, we recommend a “defer” with a 90-day extension tied to milestone attainment rather than a hard “no,” given the strategic value of a NYC-distributed footprint.

Concluding Opinion

The distributed storage opportunity in New York is bifurcated. In NYC/Con Edison territory and select adjacent networks, a well-structured, hybridized, and community-licensed portfolio can clear Board return thresholds and find ready secondary buyers. Upstate standalone distributed BESS projects face severe revenue-stack risk in the near term. Given Board timing by March 31, 2026, a focused “go” is justified under strict conditions: program floor access, verified interconnection readiness, institutional-grade modeling/auditability, and proactive permitting/community engagement. Otherwise, we recommend deferment or a pivot to grid-scale ISC-eligible development as a parallel track, with the understanding that competition and capital intensity are higher but policy bankability is also stronger.
