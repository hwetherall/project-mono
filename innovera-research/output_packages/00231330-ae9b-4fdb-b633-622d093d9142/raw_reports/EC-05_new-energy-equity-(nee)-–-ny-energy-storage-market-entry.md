Title: Entering New York’s Distributed Energy Storage Market: Prevalence and Cost of Key Risks for 4–5 MW BESS and Solar+Storage Projects

Executive summary

New Energy Equity (NEE) faces a standard but material developer risk bundle when entering New York’s distributed battery energy storage system (BESS) market at the ~5 MW class: (1) revenue uncertainty as lucrative VDER locational incentives have mostly exhausted in Con Edison territory, (2) interconnection feasibility and timeline variability under the NY Standardized Interconnection Requirements (SIR), (3) buyer demand cyclicality and price sensitivity as investors pivot from merchant-like structures to contracted revenue, and (4) policy durability over 15–20-year asset lives. The best recent data show:

- Revenue certainty has tightened for distributed BESS. Con Edison’s VDER LSRV incentives, which provided fixed revenue floors, are 93% exhausted as of November 2025, reducing fixed compensation for 2-hour BESS from about $284/kW-year to ~$140/kW-year and constraining near-term project bankability in NYC without stacking other programs or moving to longer-duration assets or hybrid configurations. Upstate VDER values of $18–$70/kW-year leave 44–86% revenue shortfalls versus the Gross Cost of New Entry (GCONE) benchmark of $126/kW-year, signaling that additional revenue (e.g., DLM, NYISO DER aggregation) or capex incentives are often required to clear investor hurdles in those territories ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Incentive scaffolding is strong through 2030, but program choice is critical around the 5 MW threshold. New York’s 6 GW Energy Storage Order (June 20, 2024) funds: (a) bulk storage (≥5 MW) via 15-year Index Storage Credit (ISC) contracts (at least three solicitations; 20% long-duration carveout) and (b) retail and residential via capacity-based $/kWh incentives (RSIP) with a retail cap at 5 MW/20 MWh. Bulk has ISC bankability; retail/4.99 MW has up-front RSIP but relies on VDER/DLM/DER aggregation for recurring value. Contracts ≥1 MWac trigger prevailing wage requirements and all funded projects must be in service by December 31, 2030 ([NYSERDA/PSC Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [Barclay Damon, 2024](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/psc-updates-its-energy-storage-goals-establishes-roadmap-to-deploy-6-gw-of-storage-capacity-by-2030); [NYSERDA Order PDF, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf)).

- Interconnection remains the top execution risk in NYC and Lower Hudson Valley. Industry groups flagged new Con Edison capacity constraints limiting interconnections in late 2025 and requested DPS intervention to improve transparency and adherence to SIR/CESIR practices. The DPS maintains active queue and SIR process materials (updated through January 2026) and is pursuing process improvements (e.g., multiple CESIR options). Risk is geography-specific; NYC/Lower Hudson is more constrained but also offers the best compensation signals ([NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo to DPS, Oct 2025](None); [DPS Distributed Generation Information, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [Joint Utilities DSP Newsletter, June 2024](None)).

- IPP/buyer demand exists but is selective and price-sensitive. The market has capital for bankable structures: NineDot raised $431 million for 28 distributed BESS in NYC (124 MW/494 MWh), and Calibrant commissioned multiple front-of-the-meter projects leveraging RSIP and local IDA support. The ISC program is explicitly “redirecting capital” toward 15-year contracts and larger grid-scale projects. Distributed projects that lack fixed revenue floors (e.g., post-LSRV) must build credible stacks with DLM and DER aggregation or co-located solar revenue to meet buyer thresholds ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025b](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025c](https://www.energy-storage.news/calibrant-begins-operating-three-distributed-front-of-the-meter-bess-in-new-york); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Trend: near-term distributed BESS economics are challenging in NYC post-LSRV and markedly challenging upstate without major stacking. However, RSIP launched April 2025 with $775 million for retail/residential is live and supports capex; bulk ISC solicitations (2025–2027) support 15-year contracted revenue. Developers are pivoting configurations to either fit RSIP at 4.99 MW (and stack VDER/DLM/aggregation) or step up to ≥5 MW bulk to chase ISC awards. Interconnection reforms are ongoing, but NYC capacity constraints and long lead times persist. On net, the problem of “meeting board-defined returns with realistic revenue stacks” remains prevalent but is solvable in downstate geographies with disciplined project design and contracting; it is more difficult in upstate on a merchant-like basis ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [DPS DG Info, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

Opinion: Proceed with a “Go—with guardrails.” For a long-term platform entry, NEE should (1) target Con Edison and Lower Hudson (Zones J, G–K) for 4.99 MW RSIP-backed BESS and co-located solar+storage, (2) apply selectively for ≥5 MW ISC-backed bulk projects only where interconnection is credible, (3) standardize revenue stacks (VDER + DLM + NYISO DER aggregation) and buyer-aligned offtake (e.g., hybrid tolling) to de-risk merchant exposure, and (4) build an NYC-centric interconnection competency. Upstate standalone BESS exposure should be limited unless paired with compelling solar or long-duration attributes to achieve required returns ([NYSERDA/PSC Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).

2. Background: New York’s storage programs affecting 4–5 MW projects

- Targets and budgets: PSC’s June 20, 2024 Order expanded the storage goal to 6 GW by 2030 and approved nearly $2 billion in incentives across bulk (3,000 MW), retail (1,500 MW), and residential (200 MW) segments. The order builds on the 2018 program and incorporates updated cost forecasts (2024 NPV) ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf); [NY-BEST, 2024](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/Roadmap_2.0_Order_Overview_0.pdf); [Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/up-to-300m-more-required-for-2030-energy-storage-goal-new-york-road-map/711619/)).

- Programs and revenue design:
  - Retail/Residential (RSIP): Launched April 30, 2025 with $775 million (retail: $675 million; residential: $100 million). Incentives are fixed $/kWh of installed capacity (first-come, first-served), with retail incentives available up to 5 MW (cap at 20 MWh). Projects can be standalone or paired with clean on-site generation; they must provide customer value via tariffs (e.g., VDER), NYISO DER aggregation, or demand charge management/DLM ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).
  - VDER Value Stack: Compensates distributed assets for energy (LBMP), capacity (ICAP), environmental value (E), demand reduction (DRV), and locational system relief (LSRV). LSRV (fixed, locational) was the key fixed pillar in Con Edison territory for 2-hour BESS until exhaustion in 2024–2025 ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - Bulk (ISC): 15-year index contracts; developers bid a Strike Price that is netted against a Reference Price composed of NYISO energy and capacity market indices; ISC pays the difference when Strike > Reference. At least three annual solicitations (2025–2027), ≥20% long-duration (8+ hour) awards, and program parameters cap 2-hour systems at ~10% of awards, pushing the market toward longer-duration or larger-scale projects ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [Barclay Damon, 2024](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/psc-updates-its-energy-storage-goals-establishes-roadmap-to-deploy-6-gw-of-storage-capacity-by-2030); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Siting and equity requirements: At least 35% of benefits must flow to disadvantaged communities (DACs). For bulk and front-of-the-meter retail projects, ≥35% of procured capacity must be in NYISO Zones G–K (Lower Hudson Valley, NYC, Long Island), ≥30% in Zone J (NYC), and ≥5% in Zones G, H, I, or K. Projects ≥1 MWac must pay prevailing wage; all funded projects must be in service by Dec 31, 2030 unless extended ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf)).

Implication for the 5 MW class: At 4.99 MW AC, a project can access RSIP up-front incentives but will rely on VDER/DLM/NYISO DER (merchant-like) for recurring value. At ≥5 MW AC, a project becomes bulk and can compete for 15-year ISC contracts that substantially improve bankability, but will face more stringent interconnection (often transmission-level or high-voltage distribution) and competition dynamics. The “sizing decision” is strategic and materially changes the revenue risk profile ([NY-BEST, 2024](https://cdn.ymaws.com/ny-best.org/resource/resmgr/Roadmap_2.0_Order_Overview_0.pdf); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

3. Prevalence data: How widespread is the problem NEE faces?

3.1 Revenue-stack uncertainty is widespread and material

- Con Edison’s LSRV exhaustion: Con Edison’s locational VDER incentives ~93% exhausted as of November 2025; only ~7 MW remained across three Manhattan networks. Pre-exhaustion, 2-hour BESS saw ~$284/kW-year fixed compensation; post-exhaustion, this fell to ~$140/kW-year for similar assets, roughly halving fixed revenue and undermining debt sizing for distributed BESS in NYC. This transition is the core revenue uncertainty problem for most NYC-distributed BESS entrants in 2024–2026 ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Upstate compensation still below GCONE: Upstate IOUs offered $18–$70/kW-year to 2-hour BESS under VDER, leaving BESS revenue 44–86% below an upstate GCONE benchmark of $126/kW-year. This indicates that, without additional stacking or capex subsidies, many upstate distributed BESS projects struggle to reach investor hurdles—hence, the “uncertain economics” problem is widespread for developers targeting upstate markets ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Market pivot to contracted revenue: The ISC program’s 15-year structure is “redirecting capital” toward larger sizes and grid connections, explicitly because wholesale-only or VDER-only revenues could not reliably replace the bankability of fixed floors. This demonstrates that revenue-stack uncertainty is sufficiently prevalent to shift development strategies statewide ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

3.2 Interconnection feasibility/timeline uncertainty is common

- NYC-specific constraints: Industry associations (NY-BEST and NYSEIA) alerted DPS in October 2025 that Con Edison announced significant capacity constraints for storage interconnections, creating “substantial market uncertainty.” They requested DPS to direct Con Edison to abide by signed interconnection agreements, finish CESIR studies using current limits and forecast data, share capacity constraint data by substation, and publish the over-5 MW Utility System Impact Study (USIS) queue. This is direct evidence that interconnection uncertainty affects a broad swath of developers in NYC as of late 2025 ([NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo, 2025](None)).

- Systemic process under reform: DPS’s SIR framework and the Joint Utilities’ ongoing edits (including multiple CESIR options) and hosting capacity map improvements signal that interconnection uncertainty is an industry-wide pain point with active remedial efforts—i.e., a problem of broad prevalence, though solution trajectory is positive ([DPS DG Information, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [Joint Utilities Newsletter, June 2024](None)).

3.3 Buyer demand and policy durability: selective and program-driven

- Buyer activity demonstrates selectivity and structure dependence:
  - NineDot’s $431 million financing for 28 NYC distributed BESS (124 MW/494 MWh) shows strong buyer capital for NYC-located, incentive-accessing assets (likely with site control, permits, and revenue stack visibility). This is not a general solution to merchant exposure; it indicates buyer appetite for bankable structures in the right geographies ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025b](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers)).
  - Calibrant’s three distributed front-of-the-meter BESS in Westchester leveraged RSIP and local IDA support, underscoring the importance of capex incentives and local facilitation in offtake/land models (20-year leases) ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025c](https://www.energy-storage.news/calibrant-begins-operating-three-distributed-front-of-the-meter-bess-in-new-york)).

- Policy durability: The PSC’s 2024 Order is comprehensive and includes multiple annual procurements, equity carve-outs, prevailing wage, and clear program budgets. RSIP was launched in April 2025 and is live. The PSC approved NYSERDA’s draft bulk implementation plan (March 2025) with modifications, indicating continued regulatory follow-through. Policy instability risk appears moderated through 2030, with durability anchored in the 6 GW target and active procurements ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf); [PSC Bulk Implementation Plan Order, Mar 2025](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Order-Approving-Bulk-Implementation-Plan-With-Modifications.pdf); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).

Prevalence synthesis: The revenue-stack and interconnection uncertainty problems affect the majority of NYC-distributed BESS projects in 2024–2026 (due to LSRV exhaustion and capacity constraints), and affect most upstate projects even more strongly due to lower compensation. Buyer demand exists but is contingent on structure; policy scaffolding is durable through 2030. Thus, a large share of potential entrants face exactly the uncertainties NEE is evaluating.

4. Per-incident cost: Direct and indirect costs of the problem

We convert the revenue-stack shortfalls and interconnection delays into per-incident cost estimates grounded in recent NY-specific values.

4.1 NYC distributed BESS: loss of LSRV floor (post-exhaustion)

- Data points:
  - Pre-exhaustion fixed compensation: ~$284/kW-year for 2-hour BESS in Con Edison.
  - Post-exhaustion: ~$140/kW-year; a ~$144/kW-year reduction in fixed annual revenue per kW.
  - Remaining LSRV capacity is de minimis (~7 MW), meaning new projects largely face the post-exhaustion regime ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Per-incident cost for a 4.99 MW (4,990 kW) 2-hour BESS:
  - Annual fixed revenue loss vs. pre-exhaustion = 4,990 kW × $144/kW-year = $718,560 per year.
  - If the developer originally underwrote at or near pre-exhaustion values (or needed that floor to size debt), the immediate impact is either reduced leverage (higher cost of capital) or reduced IRR. Without precise debt sizing, we conservatively treat the loss as a revenue shortfall of ~$0.72 million annually per project in Zone J, unless offset by DLM, DER aggregation, or hybrid solar revenues ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

4.2 Upstate distributed BESS: revenue gap vs. GCONE

- Data points:
  - Upstate kW-year range: $18–$70/kW-year.
  - GCONE benchmark: $126/kW-year.
  - Shortfall: $56–$108/kW-year.

- Per-incident cost for a 4.99 MW BESS:
  - Annual shortfall range = 4,990 kW × $56–$108/kW-year = $279,440–$538,920 per year (versus a notional breakeven). This revenue gap must be closed with other stack elements or lower capex/O&M to hit returns ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

4.3 Interconnection delay: lost earnings from commissioning slippage

- Assumption: For NYC BESS relying primarily on kW-year-like compensation (post-exhaustion ~$140/kW-year + variable elements), the “time value” of COD slippage can be proxied by pro rata lost annual revenue.
- Example: 6-month delay:
  - Lost fixed revenue ≈ 0.5 × 4,990 kW × $140/kW-year ≈ $349,300 (not including variable NYISO/ICAP/ancillary upside or DLM, which could add 10–30% variability). If LSRV were hypothetically available, the 6-month loss at $284/kW-year would be ≈ $708,000; post-exhaustion it is ≈ $349,300 ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Direct rework/engineering costs for interconnection redesigns are highly case-specific and not quantified in publicly available NY sources here. The DPS SIR materials and industry memo evidence the existence of such costs but not standardized figures. We record this component qualitatively with the note that redesigns typically entail tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars in additional studies, engineering, and permitting in developer experience; quantification would require NEE’s historical bid and EPC data ([DPS DG Information, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo, 2025](None)).

4.4 Countervailing capex incentives (RSIP)

- RSIP provides up-front $/kWh incentives, reducing net capex and thereby mitigating the revenue gap. Precise $/kWh step levels can vary by tranche and territory; the program design sets a maximum retail incentive up to 5 MW and 20 MWh per project. Thus, the per-incident “cost” of lost revenue can be partially offset by RSIP awards where projects are eligible; availability is first-come, first-served and time-sensitive. The net economic effect depends on the incentive level at the time of reservation and the speed to COD ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).

5. Annual cost per organization: Typical developer exposure

We model a developer with an NYC-centric strategy attempting five distributed projects/year:

- Portfolio A (RSIP track): 3 projects at 4.99 MW (NYC), 2-hour BESS, post-LSRV; target stack: RSIP capex incentive + VDER (non-LSRV) + DLM + NYISO DER aggregation.
- Portfolio B (mixed geography): 2 projects upstate at 4.99 MW (merchant-like VDER), 2-hour BESS.

Assumptions (sourced and estimated):

- NYC fixed compensation: $140/kW-year (post-LSRV). Shortfall vs. pre-2024 underwriting or “required floor” depends on NEE’s board thresholds; here we treat the “problem” cost as the delta to pre-exhaustion levels to illustrate revenue compression sensitivity ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Upstate gap vs. GCONE: $56–$108/kW-year ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
- Interconnection delays: One 6-month slip on one NYC and one upstate project per year (frequency assumption; needs NEE validation).
- RSIP: Applied and awarded for all 4.99 MW projects (mitigates capex but not recurring revenue; amount unspecified in cited sources; effect acknowledged qualitatively) ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).

Annual cost-of-problem (illustrative):

- NYC revenue compression (3 projects): 3 × $718,560 = $2,155,680 per year (vs. prior LSRV-floor era). This is the “lost fixed revenue” relative to pre-exhaustion assumptions; actual board thresholds may differ. If NEE’s base case already uses post-exhaustion values, this item becomes zero by definition; the “problem” then becomes achieving sufficient upside stacking to recover target IRR ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Upstate GCONE gap (2 projects): 2 × midpoint[($279,440–$538,920)] ≈ 2 × $409,180 = $818,360 per year (absent additional stacking/contracting). This is the revenue shortfall to breakeven; if DLM/DER aggregation are secured, this cost declines accordingly ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Interconnection delay losses (NYC + upstate, one each): $349,300 (NYC) + assume $200,000 upstate (lower kW-year base) ≈ $549,300 per year. This excludes rework engineering costs and assumes 6-month slips for two out of five projects; frequency is an estimate informed by ongoing NYC constraints and systemic queue pressures ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo, 2025](None); [DPS DG Info, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

Total illustrative annual cost-of-problem per organization (five-project developer): ≈ $2.16M + $0.82M + $0.55M = $3.53 million per year, before considering RSIP offsets and any upside from NYISO/ICAP/ancillary participation, DLM performance payments, or hybrid solar revenue. This is a directional estimate demonstrating scale; actual net costs depend on the success of stacking and contracting strategies.

6. Industry-wide cost in New York

Given we lack a comprehensive count of in-development distributed BESS projects by IOU territory and duration class, we bound an order-of-magnitude estimate using market signals:

- Addressable market proxy: RSIP retail target is 1,500 MW statewide (not all will be 2-hour BESS; many may be solar+storage). If 30–40% ultimately deploys as BESS or solar+storage with 2-hour batteries in NYC/Lower Hudson (Zones G–K) to meet the PSC’s location targets and peaker displacement goals, that is 450–600 MW of distributed BESS likely competing with post-LSRV compensation in Con Edison territory ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-Energy-Storage-Order-Overview.pdf); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/)).

- NYC lost floor cost (illustrative): For each 1 MW 2-hour BESS, revenue compression vs. pre-LSRV is ~$144,000/year. For 450–600 MW of such projects, annual “lost floor” equals ~$64.8–$86.4 million/year. This represents the collective shortfall developers must overcome through stacking and contracting. If the actual mix includes more long-duration or solar+storage hybrids, per-MW figures will differ ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Upstate GCONE gap cost (illustrative): Suppose 400–600 MW of upstate distributed BESS attempts are pursued through 2030 (some may be canceled). The annual gap of $56–$108/kW-year implies an industry-wide shortfall of $22.4–$64.8 million/year at 400 MW and $33.6–$97.2 million/year at 600 MW. Stacking and RSIP reduce this, but absent those, the gap illuminates why many upstate projects struggle to transact at target pricing ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Interconnection delay cost: NYC’s Con Edison constraints imply a non-trivial fraction of projects experience 3–12 months of slippage. If 25% of 450–600 MW faces a 6-month delay at an average effective kW-year payout of $140/kW-year, the industry-wide annualized lost-revenue from this subset could be (0.25 × 450–600 MW × $140,000/MW-year × 0.5) ≈ $7.9–$10.5 million/year in time-value losses—excluding redesign costs or opportunity costs of deferral ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo, 2025](None)).

Summing these simplified components suggests that, for the distributed BESS segment alone, tens of millions of dollars per year in “cost-of-problem” (lost floors, revenue gaps vs. GCONE, and delay-driven time-value losses) are at stake statewide. This excludes bulk ISC projects (contracted; less merchant risk but highly competitive) and excludes capex mitigation from RSIP, which can meaningfully improve net outcomes.

7. Frequency data: Incident, failure, and attrition rates

- Revenue floor loss frequency (NYC): With LSRV ~93% exhausted, the majority of new NYC distributed BESS projects post-2025 are exposed to the lower fixed kW-year regime. Incidence approaches “most new projects” absent new locational tranches ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Interconnection delay frequency (NYC): While not quantified by DPS, the NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo indicates widespread constraints reported by Con Edison; the request for data transparency and queue publication underscores that delays are not isolated. We conservatively assume delays occur in a material minority (e.g., 20–40%) of NYC distributed BESS attempts in 2024–2026, pending formal utility data disclosure ([NY-BEST/NYSEIA memo, 2025](None); [DPS DG Information, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Project attrition (grid-scale context): NYISO’s first “reformed” cluster study reportedly holds 19 GW of BESS applications, with 2–4 GW deemed viable by 2030 given contractable revenue under ISC—an implied 79–89% attrition at grid scale. Although distributed projects follow the SIR track rather than NYISO queue, this magnitude signals that revenue bankability and interconnection feasibility together produce high attrition across storage classes in New York today ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

8. Trend direction

- Worsening (2019–2025): LSRV exhaustion in Con Edison territory reduced fixed compensation for 2-hour distributed BESS by ~50%, removing the “floor” lenders used for debt sizing and hindering the NYC distributed buildout absent other revenue (VDER DRV/ICAP, DLM, or NYISO DER aggregation). Upstate VDER remains insufficient versus GCONE, reinforcing merchant exposure ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Improving/mixed (2025–2027): RSIP (April 2025) injects $775 million for retail/residential with clear rules; bulk ISC solicitations (2025–2027) provide 15-year contracts, redirecting capital to bankable structures and larger projects. Joint Utilities and DPS are iterating interconnection processes (multiple CESIR options) and maintaining hosting capacity data. NYC constraints persist but transparency and process interventions are being pursued. On balance, bankability is improving where developers align with programs (RSIP at 4.99 MW; ISC at ≥5 MW), but merchant exposure remains elevated elsewhere ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/); [Barclay Damon, 2024](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/psc-updates-its-energy-storage-goals-establishes-roadmap-to-deploy-6-gw-of-storage-capacity-by-2030); [DPS DG Info, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Policy durability: The 6 GW Order, with explicit budgets, carve-outs, wage standards, and DAC targets, plus active implementation orders and program launches through 2025, supports confidence in policy durability to 2030. This reduces “policy instability” as a primary driver of the problem for well-aligned projects ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf); [PSC Bulk Implementation Plan Order, 2025](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Order-Approving-Bulk-Implementation-Plan-With-Modifications.pdf)).

9. Cost-of-problem synthesis (math shown; sourced vs. estimated)

We present per-customer annual cost estimates under three archetypes relevant to NEE, using frequency × cost-per-incident:

9.1 Archetype 1: NYC 4.99 MW RSIP-eligible 2-hour BESS (post-LSRV)

- Cost-per-incident (revenue shortfall vs. pre-exhaustion floor): $718,560/year per project (sourced from Modo kW-year values).
- Frequency: If underwriting relied on LSRV-like floors, frequency ≈ 1 (i.e., affects nearly all new projects as LSRV is exhausted). If underwriting has adjusted to post-exhaustion values, this item is zero-by-definition and the “problem” is instead the difficulty of stacking sufficient alternative revenues (not quantified here).
- Delay cost: 6-month slip = ~$349,300 lost fixed revenue per project (sourced).
- Annual cost (one project, with one 6-month slip): $718,560 + $349,300 = $1,067,860 (sourced values; frequency of delay is estimated at 1 in 1 for illustrative purposes). If delay odds are 1-in-3, expected annual delay cost would be ~$116,433 and expected total ~$834,993 ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

9.2 Archetype 2: Upstate 4.99 MW RSIP-eligible 2-hour BESS

- Cost-per-incident (revenue gap vs. GCONE): $279,440–$538,920/year per project (sourced).
- Frequency: High (affects most upstate projects absent extraordinary stacking). Assume 1.
- Delay cost: Using a lower base (e.g., $70/kW-year midpoint), 6-month slip ≈ 4,990 × $70 × 0.5 ≈ $174,650 (estimated).
- Annual cost (one project): midpoint gap $409,180 + $174,650 ≈ $583,830 (mixed sourced/estimated; delay base estimated from sourced range) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

9.3 Archetype 3: ≥5 MW ISC-targeted storage (bulk)

- Cost-per-incident: Primarily bid/award risk. Without an ISC award, bankability is limited; with award, merchant risk compresses materially. The “incident” is failure to win ISC plus sunk development/interconnection costs and time-value losses. Lacking public averages, we present a qualitative framework rather than a numeric. However, Modo indicates the ISC program offers $700 million–$1.42 billion in funding and is channeling capital toward 15-year contracts; initial cluster holds 19 GW of BESS with only 2–4 GW viable under ISC revenue by 2030, implying high competitive attrition. The cost-per-incident is thus: interconnection study fees + engineering + site control + bid costs + 12–24 months of overhead for a non-awarded asset. Quantification requires NEE’s internal development cost benchmarks ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf)).

| Bankability | Depends on VDER and stack; post-LSRV is challenging in NYC; stronger with solar+storage or DLM | High if awarded (15-year term); competitive solicitations |
| Duration tilt | 2-hour common historically; RSIP neutral but cap at 20 MWh | ≥20% long-duration (≥8 hours); 2-hour systems capped at ~10% of awards |
| Geographic tilt | Con Edison territory historically favored by VDER (now near-exhausted LSRV); upstate lower compensation | Emphasis on bulk needs; Zone J/G–K siting requirements apply to achieving equity/peaker displacement goals |
| Wage/Timing | Prevailing wage ≥1 MW; in-service by 12/31/2030 | Prevailing wage; award and COD per solicitation; 2030 target horizon |

11. Implications for NEE’s board decision and replicability at scale

11.1 Where distributed BESS can clear returns

- Con Edison/Lower Hudson focus: Despite LSRV exhaustion, Con Edison territory still exhibits the highest Reference Prices and historic demand for fixed locational value—this translated into higher VDER compensation historically and continues to make NYC the most promising geography for stacking DLM, NYISO DER aggregation, and hybrid solar value, especially with RSIP lowering capex. Recent developer successes (NineDot financing; Calibrant projects) validate buyer demand for well-structured NYC assets. The trade-off is interconnection constraint risk and urban permitting complexity ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Energy-Storage.News, 2025b–c](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers)).

- Upstate exposure: Purely merchant-like upstate projects are generally below GCONE based on VDER alone; to meet returns, NEE should prioritize hybrid solar+storage configurations (leveraging solar PPAs/VDER energy credits) and demand response/DLM. Consider exploring longer-duration batteries where utility programs reward winter peak reliability and where community solar/storage policy momentum is growing, given recent economic studies showing statewide ratepayer benefits from distributed solar+storage expansion by 2035 ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [PV Magazine, 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/01/21/new-york-study-finds-distributed-solar-and-storage-could-save-ratepayers-1-billion-annually/)).

11.2 Configuration decision at ~5 MW

- 4.99 MW track (RSIP): Faster access to capex incentives; bankability must come from stacking and hybridization. Advantage: shorter timelines in some cases (e.g., Catalyze’s Bronx project reached COD in 171 working days), but such timelines are not typical and depend on site and local processes. Replicability hinges on interconnection siting skill and DER aggregation partner relationships ([Catalyze, 2024](https://catalyze.com/2024/08/08/catalyze-launches-first-standalone-battery-energy-storage-system-bess-project-in-the-bronx)).

- ≥5 MW track (ISC): Consider stepping to 5–10 MW for bulk solicitations where interconnection pathways are credible and land/control is solid. ISC materially reduces merchant risk, but success is bid- and queue-dependent; failure-to-award becomes the main “incident.” This path is more replicable as a platform strategy if NEE can build competitive bid capabilities and manage cluster-study dependencies ([Barclay Damon, 2024](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/psc-updates-its-energy-storage-goals-establishes-roadmap-to-deploy-6-gw-of-storage-capacity-by-2030); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

11.3 Offtake models and buyer alignment

IPP buyers increasingly prefer structured offtake that de-risks merchant exposure. For distributed BESS and hybrids, consider:

- Tolling or capacity-based offtake for a portion of capacity (baseline cash flow), with a merchant sleeve for upside (common 2025–2026 pattern in storage transactions).
- For solar+storage hybrids, leverage solar PPAs/VDER energy value as the predictable anchor, with the battery as a volatility hedge or capacity enhancer.
- Align performance guarantees with DLM/VDER delivery points and interconnection constraints to avoid penalty exposure if constraints emerge post-signature ([LevelTen, 2024](https://leveltenenergy.com/post/announcing-marketpulse-storage-beta)).

12. Risk register and mitigations

- Revenue-stack uncertainty (high in NYC post-LSRV; very high upstate)
  - Mitigations: Co-locate with solar (boost VDER E/LBMP); enroll in DLM with conservative DRV/ICAP assumptions; pursue NYISO DER aggregation with reputable partners; explore longer-duration where compensated; maintain RSIP queue position and COD discipline to avoid tranche step-downs ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025a](https://www.energy-storage.news/nyserda-funding-us775-million-for-distributed-energy-storage/); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).

- Interconnection feasibility and timeline uncertainty (very high in NYC)
  - Mitigations: Early CESIR scoping; substation-by-substation vetting; engage DPS Ombuds; plan for alternative interconnection feeders; include redesign contingency; prioritize sites with demonstrated hosting capacity headroom; consider stepping to ≥5 MW if ISC bulk pathways and transmission-level interconnections are attainable ([DPS DG Info, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

- Buyer demand and pricing (selective)
  - Mitigations: Pre-market portfolios with multiple structures (tolling/hybrid PPAs); tie energy storage dispatch to buyer’s peak/off-peak needs; present standardized revenue models with empirical sensitivity ranges validated by third-party analytics (e.g., Modo-calibrated assumptions) ([Energy-Storage.News, 2025b–c](https://www.energy-storage.news/ninedot-energy-closes-us431-million-for-new-york-bess-ameresco-completes-multiple-tax-credit-transfers)).

- Policy durability (moderate risk; improving)
  - Mitigations: Anchor development to RSIP and ISC calendars; meet DAC siting thresholds and prevailing wage; stage CODs within the 2030 deadline; monitor PSC working groups for interconnection and DER aggregation rule changes ([PSC/NYSERDA Order, 2024](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/2024-06-6GW-Energy-Storage-Order.pdf); [DPS DG Info, 2025–26](https://dps.ny.gov/distributed-generation-information)).

13. Decision framework for NEE (March 31, 2026)

- Go—with guardrails:
  - Geography: Focus initial 8–12 projects in Con Edison and Lower Hudson (Zones J, G–K) to align with PSC location priorities and higher compensation signals.
  - Size: Split pipeline: 60–70% at 4.99 MW RSIP-eligible (speed and replicability), 30–40% at ≥5 MW for ISC bids where interconnection is credible.
  - Configuration: Favor solar+storage in upstate; consider longer-duration in NYC where program carve-outs or DLM/ICAP winter peaks pay premiums.
  - Offtake: Standardize a “hybrid tolling + merchant sleeve” template; pre-negotiate with 2–3 repeat IPP buyers.
  - Interconnection: Stand up an NYC-focused interconnection team or partner; adopt a substation screen and a go/no-go gate pre-CESIR fee outlay.
  - Modeling: Validate VDER/DER aggregation assumptions with third-party analytics (e.g., Modo-calibrated references), set conservative capacity revenue, and explicitly model LSRV = $0 scenarios in NYC.
  - Governance: Use board thresholds keyed to post-LSRV NYC and upstate midpoints; accept higher hurdle rates for upstate; require a signed DLM or comparable contracted revenue component before NTP.

- No-Go conditions:
  - If RSIP tranche availability is depleted and no near-term replenishment is scheduled for target geographies.
  - If Con Edison interconnection data (upon DPS-required disclosure) indicate multi-year feeder/substation moratoria across NEE’s core siting zones.
  - If ISC bid outcomes in 2026 show clearing prices that imply sub-threshold IRRs for ≥5 MW projects given NEE’s cost structure.

14. Conclusion

The “uncertainty whether NY distributed BESS can meet board returns” is prevalent and economically material in 2024–2026—particularly due to LSRV exhaustion in NYC and low upstate VDER values relative to GCONE. However, the PSC’s 2024 Order, NYSERDA’s RSIP launch in 2025, and the ISC bulk procurements together create a pathway to bankable revenue stacks when developers adopt the right configurations, siting geographies, and offtake structures. Interconnection remains the binding constraint in NYC and requires specialized competency. Under these conditions, NEE should proceed with a focused, program-aligned market entry emphasizing (a) NYC/Lower Hudson 4.99 MW RSIP projects with robust stacking or hybridization and (b) selective ≥5 MW ISC bids where interconnection is credible—eschewing broad upstate standalone BESS exposure until a contracted component or higher-duration premium is secured.
