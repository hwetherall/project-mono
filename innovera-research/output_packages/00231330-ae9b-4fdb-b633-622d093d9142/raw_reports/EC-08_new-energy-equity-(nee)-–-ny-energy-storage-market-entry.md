Title: External Forces Creating Urgency for Market Entry into New York’s Distributed Energy Storage (BESS and Solar+Storage) Market, 2026–2030

Executive Summary
This report assesses the external forces creating urgency for New Energy Equity (NEE) to make a go/no-go decision by March 31, 2026 on entering New York State’s distributed energy storage market (standalone BESS and solar+storage hybrids, ~5 MW class). The analysis integrates state policy direction (PSC/NYSERDA), NYISO market reforms, interconnection dynamics, incentive program timing, and competitive and cost trends. It focuses on how these forces affect revenue certainty, interconnection feasibility, buyer demand, and policy durability for distributed-scale projects that would be sold to Independent Power Producers (IPPs) and renewable asset buyers.

Key conclusions:
- Overall urgency is high for 2026–2028 to secure program eligibility, interconnection queue position, and revenue certainty before potential policy/design recalibrations. For distributed-scale projects, the strongest forcing functions are (1) approved and active retail/residential storage incentives under the PSC’s 2024 Energy Storage Order and 6 GW Roadmap; (2) NYISO’s 2023–2024 interconnection queue reform (cluster study) that accelerates but also filters projects; and (3) New York’s accelerating large-load growth and reliability needs that elevate capacity value and de-risk merchant components of the revenue stack ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054); [NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).
- Incentive and procurement timing matters: the PSC approved NYSERDA’s retail and residential storage Implementation Plan in February 2025; those components are live. NYSERDA’s Index Storage Credit (ISC) centralized procurement is functioning as a practical filter for project readiness, with initial rounds closed and strike price submissions due by January 29, 2026 in the first round, signaling compressed bid windows that favor projects already advanced in NYISO’s interconnection process ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
- Interconnection is the gating factor: NYISO’s backlog doubled (2018–2025), and 91% of projects historically dropped out. Even after reform, only 2–4 GW of stand-alone battery capacity is likely to reach operation by 2030 out of a >19 GW pipeline, reflecting stiff attrition, capital intensity and readiness filters. For distributed-scale projects, distribution-level interconnection processes and distribution hosting capacity constraints remain crucial, with NY-Sun’s 10 GW Roadmap calling for targeted distribution upgrades and cost-sharing mechanisms to ease bottlenecks ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030); [NYSERDA, n.d.](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/email/nysun/informational-roadmap.pdf); [Utility Dive, 2023](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-interconnection-rule-queue-renewable-energy/689289/)).
- Buyer demand and value stack evolution: Capacity and ancillary-service value is expected to strengthen in high-need zones (e.g., NYC/Long Island) and increasingly in Upstate zones facing tight capacity margins due to thermal retirements and load growth. Four-hour batteries dominate the queue and remain the bankable baseline, but 10-hour assets in high-need zones can capture significantly higher capacity value; distributed-scale assets can complement these market dynamics by monetizing retail incentives, NYISO DER participation (now explicitly compatible with the retail storage incentive), and demand charge management/Value of DER opportunities where applicable ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Policy durability is strong through 2030: The PSC’s 2024 Energy Storage Order operationalizes the 6 GW by 2030 target across bulk, retail, and residential sectors. As of April 1, 2024, ~396 MW is operating, 1.3 GW is procured/under contract, and ~332 MW in incentives remained; the state is materially short of 6 GW, implying continued procurement and programmatic support through the decade. At the same time, ongoing program design adjustments (e.g., ISC program scope, eligibility) and interconnection cost visibility can shift economics on short notice, making near-term program windows especially consequential ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).

Recommendation in brief: A focused, staged market entry with 2–4 distributed BESS or solar+storage projects advanced to near-NTP by late 2026 would allow NEE to secure incentive eligibility, test interconnection feasibility in targeted utility territories with known hosting capacity, and validate a bankable revenue stack leveraging the PSC-approved retail incentive and NYISO DER participation. Concentrating first assets in zones with better interconnection prospects and clear buyer interest (e.g., Hudson Valley/Zone G and selected distribution feeders with demonstrated capacity) provides the best risk-adjusted path while protecting return thresholds. This approach aligns with the strongest forcing functions and timing pressures through 2026–2028.

1. Background and Context
NEE is weighing long-term entry into the New York distributed energy storage market (standalone BESS and solar+storage, ~5 MW class). The core uncertainties include:
- Revenue stack robustness: balance of incentive-backed revenues (e.g., retail storage program, ISC) versus merchant exposure (capacity, energy arbitrage, ancillary services).
- Interconnection feasibility: evolving NYISO and utility distribution interconnection processes, potential network upgrade costs and timelines.
- IPP buyer demand: appetite for distributed-scale, incentive-supported assets with validated operating strategies.
- Policy durability: stability of state storage and solar programs through 2030.

New York’s policy framework has crystallized with the PSC’s 2024 Energy Storage Order and subsequent Implementation Plan approval in February 2025 for retail and residential storage programs. Simultaneously, NYISO’s interconnection reforms (FERC Order 2023 compliance; cluster studies) and statewide load growth (data centers, chip fabs, and manufacturing) are reshaping both development feasibility and the merchant components of storage revenues. Together, these forces are creating a time-sensitive window for developers who can advance bankable, incentive-eligible projects with credible interconnection paths and realistic merchant strategies ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054); [NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).

3. Trend Urgency: Is the Problem Getting Worse Over Time?
Evidence across policy, interconnection, and market trends indicates increasing urgency for developers and buyers:

- Scale of the storage build still ahead:
  - As of April 1, 2024, New York had about $200 million awarded supporting ~396 MW of operating energy storage; a total of 1.3 GW of storage is either operating or under contract, with an additional ~581 MW under contract and ~332 MW of incentives still available at that time. To achieve 6 GW by 2030, the state must accelerate deployment roughly 4–5x from current contracted-and-operating levels over the next four years. The PSC notes expected statewide electric system cost reductions of nearly $2 billion from the 6 GW Roadmap, reinforcing continued program momentum ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).
- Interconnection pressure increasing despite reforms:
  - NYISO’s interconnection backlog doubled from 176 projects in 2018 to 350 in 2025; nationwide, the median time from request to COD extended to five years for projects built in 2022 (up from three years in 2015), spurring FERC Order 2023 and cluster study adoptions. These data indicate structural queue congestion that will not resolve overnight, even with process improvements ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage); [Utility Dive, 2023](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-interconnection-rule-queue-renewable-energy/689289/)).
- High attrition likely to persist:
  - Modo Energy’s analysis shows that 91% of projects entering NYISO’s interconnection queue historically dropped out; only 2–4 GW of stand-alone storage could reasonably reach operation by 2030 out of >19 GW proposed, barring expanded ISC programs, faster permitting/interconnection, and improved capital costs. This means bankable, incentive-eligible projects will command premium buyer interest, and the pipeline of truly financeable distributed BESS will be scarce ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
- Demand growth intensifies market value and system need:
  - Large-load interconnection requests surged from six projects (1,045 MW) in 2022 to 29 projects (6,055 MW) by July 2025, with 2,500–4,000 MW expected on-system by 2035, concentrating value in zones with tight capacity margins and pushing price signals favorable to storage. NYISO’s 20-year outlook forecasts statewide capacity needs to triple by 2040 (to 100–130 GW), including up to 40 GW of dispatchable emissions-free resources to replace retiring fossil generation—a structural tailwind for storage value. These are strong indicators that merchant components (capacity/ancillaries, arbitrage) will remain relevant and potentially improve over time in high-need zones ([NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue); [Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054)).
- Program alignment and distribution readiness:
  - The NY-Sun 10 GW Roadmap recommends specific interconnection policy improvements including utility planning modifications, expansion of hosting capacity investments, and Cost-Sharing 2.0. This signals growing alignment between distribution system planning and DER deployment needs, supportive of 5 MW-class solar+storage adoption—but also implies developers should target feeders and utilities with known hosting capacity expansion trajectories to reduce interconnection risk ([NYSERDA, n.d.](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/email/nysun/informational-roadmap.pdf)).

Net effect: The “problem” NEE is solving—achieving bankable distributed storage returns despite revenue and interconnection uncertainty—is not abating. Queue congestion, attrition, and competition for incentives are intensifying, even as long-run value signals strengthen due to load growth and decarbonization mandates. The near-term window (2026–2028) is decisive for securing positions in programs and queues that de-risk project economics.

4. Trigger Events
Organizations typically act—or accelerate decision-making—upon specific, observable triggers:

- Opening/closing of ISC and retail storage incentive blocks
  - Why it causes action: These events define bankable revenue floors and eligibility timing; failing to bid or apply in-window can delay projects a year or more, jeopardizing financing and return targets.
  - Predictability/frequency: Announced by NYSERDA/PSC; initial rounds in 2025–2026 with potential follow-on cycles; frequency is episodic and subject to policy adjustments.
  - Source: ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).

- Interconnection study milestones (cluster study acceptance, System Impact Study results, interconnection agreement issuance)
  - Why: These outputs determine cost exposure, feasibility, and timing—core to investment committees and buyer due diligence; negative results cause pivots or exits.
  - Predictability/frequency: Cluster-driven cadence (e.g., annual or semi-annual batches), with milestone schedules published by NYISO/TOs; outcomes are uncertain and can trigger rapid reprioritization.
  - Source: ([Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054)).

- PSC orders or program design changes (e.g., Implementation Plan approvals or modifications)
  - Why: Changes can expand eligibility (e.g., allowing NYISO DER participants to access retail incentives), improve stacking options, or shift incentives—directly affecting returns.
  - Predictability/frequency: PSC agendas and orders are public but outcomes vary; typically several material orders annually.
  - Source: ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).

- Announcements of large-load connections or local thermal retirements
  - Why: These create near-term capacity tightness and potential local price volatility; IPPs and developers recalibrate siting strategies and duration choices accordingly.
  - Predictability/frequency: Variable; load announcements are increasingly frequent; thermal retirements follow known compliance timelines but can shift with economics.
  - Source: ([NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).

- Comparable state procurements awarding capacity (e.g., MA 1.3 GW Round 1 awards)
  - Why: Signal where capital is flowing and what project attributes clear; causes IPPs to seek NY assets that meet similar thresholds and diversifies regional exposure.
  - Predictability/frequency: Annual or biannual in neighboring states; timelines compressed (e.g., six weeks to bid in MA), rewarding ready projects.
  - Source: ([TomorrowIQ, 2025](https://www.tomorrowiq.com/blog/massachusetts-awards-first-major-energy-storage-procurement)).

- Third-party acquisitions or capital raises in NY renewables (e.g., CIP’s 1.3 GW wind acquisition)
  - Why: Indicates buyer demand; storage co-location or portfolio firming opportunities may drive IPP appetite for BESS acquisitions.
  - Predictability/frequency: Opportunistic; track M&A newsflow.
  - Source: ([Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/copenhagen-infrastructure-onshore-wind-new-york/713299)).

5. Budget and Procurement Timing
- State program and procurement cycles:
  - Retail/residential storage incentives are active following PSC approval (Feb 2025), suggesting continuous or block-based application windows over 2025–2027 until budgets are subscribed. The ISC centralized procurement features discrete solicitation rounds with fixed submission deadlines (e.g., strike prices in Jan 2026 for the first round), favoring developers already advanced in interconnection ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
  - The NY-Sun MW Block Program (to achieve 10 GW by 2030) provides rolling incentives with funding collected through 2032, aligning with multi-year planning and enabling solar+storage co-development in targeted segments, including LMI/DACs. Interconnection policy improvements are expected to be incorporated into future utility capital plans, implying periodic planning/budget windows that can be anticipated at the utility level ([NYSERDA, n.d.](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/email/nysun/informational-roadmap.pdf)).
- IPP and buyer timing:
  - IPPs typically align acquisitions with internal fiscal calendars and market windows: buying late-stage or NTP-ready assets shortly after interconnection milestones, program awards (e.g., ISC or retail incentive confirmations), and receipt of interconnection cost certainty.
  - Compressed procurement windows (e.g., MA’s six-week bid period in 2025) and episodic ISC rounds suggest buyers will accelerate diligence and term sheets to capture awarded projects before deadlines. This favors sellers who can present a clean, time-bound narrative: incentive secured, interconnection costs bounded, and clear merchant strategy aligned with local capacity needs ([TomorrowIQ, 2025](https://www.tomorrowiq.com/blog/massachusetts-awards-first-major-energy-storage-procurement)).

6. Urgency Synthesis
- Overall urgency: High.
- Strongest forcing function: The combination of PSC-approved storage programs (retail/residential; ISC) and NYISO’s reformed but still capacity-constrained interconnection process creates the most immediate urgency. These determine eligibility, revenue certainty, and feasibility—together, they define whether projects can achieve board-required return thresholds.
- When urgency peaks: 2026–2028. The early ISC rounds (and potential subsequent cycles), active retail storage incentives, and the first waves of cluster study outcomes will determine which projects can proceed on time and on budget. This period also captures increasing large-load connections and potential tightening capacity margins that may improve merchant revenue opportunities.
- Which segments feel urgency most:
  - Developers of distributed BESS and solar+storage who must secure retail incentives, verify distribution interconnection feasibility, and demonstrate NYISO DER participation compatibility now that PSC explicitly allows stacking.
  - IPPs and renewable acquirers who need contracted and merchant-viable assets to meet portfolio return thresholds and hedge exposure to rising capacity prices in high-need zones.
  - Utilities and regulators (secondary stakeholders) facing reliability pressures and equity commitments under the CLCPA and NY-Sun Roadmap.

7. Implications for NEE and Concrete Next Steps
Given the external forces and timing:
- Focus initial entry on markets and feeders with favorable interconnection prospects and clear buyer interest:
  - Prioritize utility territories with demonstrated distribution hosting capacity and active DER planning that aligns with NY-Sun Roadmap recommendations; the Hudson Valley (Zone G) has been a center of early storage development with moderate costs and tightening capacity margins, attracting developer interest and potentially facilitating buyer exits ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
- Align project attributes with program and market value:
  - For 5 MW-class assets, optimize for the PSC-approved retail storage incentive and explicitly plan participation in NYISO’s DER program to maximize stackability now allowed under the Implementation Plan. Design control strategies to firm local solar production, reduce distribution peaks (where applicable), and capture capacity/ancillary services through aggregation.
  - Default to four-hour BESS for bankability unless local capacity accreditation and buyer appetite justify extended durations; in high-need zones, evaluate whether five-to-ten-hour configurations materially enhance capacity value and IPP exit pricing ([Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Advance a limited pilot pipeline to NTP in 2026:
  - Develop 2–4 projects with diversified siting (e.g., two in Zone G, one in Zone J/K if distribution-level paths are viable) to validate a repeatable underwriting case and meet buyer criteria (incentive certainty, interconnection cost bounded, merchant upside justification).
  - Secure early eligibility/application to retail storage incentives; monitor ISC rounds for any paths applicable to distribution-level projects or hybrid configurations in future cycles ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).
- Strengthen interconnection diligence and partner with local experts:
  - Adopt a “fail fast” approach at system impact stages; invest in pre-application/hosting capacity analyses with utilities; leverage cost-sharing avenues where available under evolving utility plans.
  - Consider joint development or acquisition of advanced queue positions with established NY developers to shorten time-to-NTP.
- Prepare for buyer due diligence:
  - Build a transparent merchant revenue model aligned with local capacity needs and allowable program stacking. Where possible, reference nodal spread uplift potential from real-time operations to exceed ISC-style reference prices in analogous cases (e.g., LI nodes outperforming zonal averages), even if ISC is not directly applicable to retail-incented assets, to help buyers frame upside scenarios ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage)).

9. Foundational-to-Advanced Narrative Synthesis
- Foundational policy direction:
  - New York’s 2024 Energy Storage Order and the 6 GW by 2030 Roadmap operationalize storage deployment across bulk, retail, and residential sectors, with active program implementation for retail and residential storage and centralized procurement (ISC) for bulk-scale assets. This establishes a durable foundation for storage as a core grid resource for affordability, reliability, and decarbonization ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).
- System needs and reliability:
  - NYISO projects dramatic capacity growth needs (100–130 GW by 2040), including up to 40 GW dispatchable emissions-free resources to replace retiring fossil fleets. Meanwhile, large-load additions (data centers, fabs) accelerate demand-side urgency and value for flexible resources. Together, these create long-run merchant tailwinds for storage while increasing near-term policy prioritization ([Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054); [NYISO, 2025](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-intensive-projects-in-nyiso-s-interconnection-queue)).

  - Despite reforms, interconnection remains the principal bottleneck and cost risk. NYISO’s cluster study approach accelerates timelines but imposes readiness and capital hurdles, filtering out speculative projects. - Distributed solar+storage alignment:
  - The NY-Sun 10 GW Roadmap (through 2030) and its interconnection policy recommendations signal increasing coordination between distribution planning and DER deployment—advantageous for 5 MW-class solar+storage that can relieve feeders, serve LMI/DACs, and support resilience. These assets can also participate in NYISO DER programs, and, under PSC’s Implementation Plan, still qualify for retail storage incentives—a powerful stacking pathway ([NYSERDA, n.d.](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/email/nysun/informational-roadmap.pdf); [New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).
- Competitive and technological considerations:
  - Four-hour BESS remains the commercial workhorse, but duration tailoring to local capacity needs (e.g., 8–10 hours) can command higher capacity accreditation in constrained zones; examples like the 20 MW / 8-hour zinc-bromide Sugar Maple BESS targeting an October 2028 COD illustrate diversification of chemistries and durations being proposed in NY—though bankability and operating track record still favor Li-ion at 4 hours in most cases ([NYSERDA, n.d.](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Bulk-Storage-Incentives/Sugar-Maple-BESS_Att_B_Public_Project_Summary.pdf); [Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).

10. Risk Considerations and Mitigations Mapped to External Forces
- Interconnection cost/time risk:
  - Mitigation: Early-stage utility coordination; pre-application reviews and feeder targeting; engage local partners with permitting and utility coordination expertise; consider queue position acquisitions where prudent ([Utility Dive, 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-york-iso-reforms-interconnection-queue-launches-cluster-study/724054); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-interconnection-queue-reform-cluster-study-battery-storage)).
- Incentive design and eligibility risk:
  - Mitigation: Apply early; design projects to comply with prevailing wage and program-specific siting/participation rules; exploit PSC-approved stacking of retail incentive with NYISO DER participation; monitor ISC updates and eligibility expansions ([New York State Department of Public Service, 2025](https://dps.ny.gov/news/psc-approves-energy-storage-implementation-plan)).
- Revenue stack variability (merchant exposure):
  - Mitigation: Site in zones with robust capacity and ancillary value signals; model nodal spreads and real-time arbitrage uplift potential; consider duration adjustments where capacity accreditation materially enhances returns ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/nyiso-where-to-build-index-storage-credit-nyserda-zonal-reference-price-arbitrage); [Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/new-york-battery-storage-buildout-nyiso-reform-and-market-outlook-2030)).
- Buyer exit risk:
  - Mitigation: Build an offering geared to IPP diligence: (1) incentive award/eligibility in hand; (2) interconnection costs bounded; (3) merchant strategy with evidence of local value; (4) permitting and community engagement documented; (5) standardized O&M and safety protocols.

11. Final Opinion
Based on the weight and recency of the sources and the intersection of policy, interconnection, and market forces, the urgency for NEE to act in New York is high in 2026–2028. The strongest driver is the availability of PSC-backed incentives and procurement programs combined with NYISO’s interconnection filtering—which together will determine which distributed projects attain bankability and buyer demand at acceptable returns. While uncertainties remain (notably around federal ITC timing in the provided sources and evolving ISC program design), the balance of evidence supports a focused, staged entry with a small initial portfolio advanced to NTP in 2026. This approach allows NEE to leverage live incentives, demonstrate interconnection feasibility, and validate a bankable revenue stack for IPP buyers while protecting downside risk.
