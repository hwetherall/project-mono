Research Report: How Practitioners and Target Customers Talk About Entering New York’s Distributed BESS and Solar+Storage Market

Date: March 13, 2026

Executive Summary

- Bottom line for NEE: Practitioner language in 2024–2026 consistently centers on bankability anxiety, policy flux (VDER exhaustion vs. new Retail/Residential Storage Programs and the Index Storage Credit), interconnection bottlenecks, and buyer (IPP) preference for de-risked revenue structures (floors, tolling) rather than merchant exposure. Community discussion volume is high and intensifying near leadership and legal channels, signaling real budget-holder attention. Based on this evidence, a conditional “Go” is warranted if NEE can secure revenue floors (NYSERDA retail incentives, tolling, or equivalent) and interconnection milestones up front; a broad, merchant-exposed entry would likely miss Board return thresholds. A focused, pilot-sized pipeline in Con Edison territory (and selected Rest-of-State blocks) aligned to the Retail/Residential Storage Program and optional DER aggregation—pre-packaged for IPP buyers—offers the best near-term fit. Without revenue floors or interconnection certainty, the project set should be deferred. ([DPS/NYSERDA, 2024 letter](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf); [Modo Energy, 2024–2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Energy Central/Hodgson Russ, 2025–2026](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/new-york-state-public-service-commission-approves-retail-and-residential-lJqO2AVhDCPDbhD); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/march/new-york-s-bulk-energy-storage-procurements-to-commence-in-june-2025/); [NYISO FAQ](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-storage-frequently-asked-questions))

- What practitioners say:
  - “No floor, no debt”: exhaustion of Con Edison’s VDER LSRV cut fixed revenue support ~50%, removing the debt-sizing floor lenders relied on; distributed build slowed without new floors (e.g., Retail Storage incentives) or contracts (e.g., tolling) ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities)).
  - “Policy keeps moving the goalposts”: DPS/NYSERDA updated program budgets due to inflation and capacity price forecasts; updates proceeded with limited-scope comments, reinforcing uncertainty around incentive sufficiency and timing ([DPS/NYSERDA, 2024 letter](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf)).
  - “Queue purgatory”: developers cite interconnection backlogs as gating item; NYISO’s cluster holds massive queued capacity with only a fraction likely viable by 2030, a signal of systemic constraints ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Energy Central on interconnection](https://energycentral.com/c/um/grid-interconnection-queue-gets-longer-and-longer)).

- Demand/buyer signals:
  - IPPs/financiers prefer 15-year Index Storage Credit (ISC) style floors or tolling/floor-plus-upside structures to support 60–70% debt sizing; purely merchant is viewed as high-cost-of-capital and timing-sensitive, with widening bid-ask spreads in 2025 where revenues compressed ([Modo Energy, Q3 2025 US roundup](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/ercot-pjm-caiso-nyiso-us-bess-research-roundup-q3-2025); [LinkedIn practitioner discussions on tolling and floors](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/merchant-vs-contracted-bess-projects-new-revenue-models-singal-xaorc); [Energy-Storage.news asset mgmt takeaways](https://www.energy-storage.news/operational-risk-optimisation-data-tolls-and-more-battery-asset-management-summit-europe-key-takeaways/)).

- Go/No-Go guidance for March 31, 2026:
  - Conditional Go (pilot): 20–40 MW across 4–8 distributed projects, weighted to Con Edison networks with remaining value, enrolling in NYSERDA Retail Storage Incentives (and ISI where applicable), or paired with pre-negotiated tolling/floor contracts; package DER aggregation as optional upside but not core underwriting. Gate on interconnection maturity and executable host-community engagement (fire safety readiness). Expand only upon first closings to IPP buyers.
  - No-Go conditions: inability to secure a revenue floor; repeated interconnection delays exceeding 12–18 months beyond baseline; lack of IPP term sheets reflecting favorable takeout pricing.

Market and Policy Context (Foundational)

- DPS/NYSERDA Roadmap Update (Mar. 15, 2024): Agencies re-examined “projected program costs and budgets” due to material cost increases (inflation, wholesale capacity price forecasts). Substantial revisions triggered another public comment process—comments limited to the updates. Implication: incentive design and budgets across residential, retail, and bulk sectors remained in flux, increasing developer underwriting uncertainty. ([DPS/NYSERDA, 2024 letter](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/Energy-Storage/Energy-Storage-Roadmap.pdf))

- 2024 Order: Updated Energy Storage Goal and Deployment Policy to 6 GW by 2030, including 1,500 MW retail and 200 MW residential; additional bulk storage to be procured through ISC-style solicitations. ([PSC Order, Jun. 20, 2024](#))

- Retail and Residential Energy Storage Program Implementation (approved with modifications, Feb. 14, 2025):
  - Retail Program: eligible up to 5 MW/20 MWh AC; BTM and FTM distribution-connected projects; min 10-year warranty; in service by Dec. 31, 2030 (unless extended). Projects receiving VDER compensation are eligible for the Statewide Solar for All (SSFA) Program. PSC directed NYSERDA to include NYISO DER Aggregation participants as eligible for Retail Program—an important link to potential wholesale stacks. ([Hodgson Russ client alert](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/new-york-state-public-service-commission-approves-retail-and-residential-lJqO2AVhDCPDbhD))
  - Residential Program: $100 million allocation; ConEd Block 1 at $250/kWh (10 MW/25 MWh), ROS Block 1 at $200/kWh (10 MW/25 MWh); 40% capacity to Inclusive Storage Incentive (ISI) at $450/kWh Block 1 (10 MW/25 MWh). ([Hodgson Russ client alert](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/new-york-state-public-service-commission-approves-retail-and-residential-lJqO2AVhDCPDbhD))

- Bulk Storage via Index Storage Credit (ISC):
  - Structure: 15-year “strike-minus-reference” monthly settlement that provides a revenue floor while preserving upside to market revenues; aim to enable ~3 GW grid-scale storage by 2030; first RFP by June 30, 2025; bids weighted 60% price/40% non-price. LIPA/NYSERDA to coordinate for LIPA territory ISC-equivalent. Monthly paybacks to NYSERDA capped at negative strike price if REAP > strike. ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/march/new-york-s-bulk-energy-storage-procurements-to-commence-in-june-2025/); [Modo Energy, Q3 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/ercot-pjm-caiso-nyiso-us-bess-research-roundup-q3-2025))

- Distributed vs. utility-scale deployment signals:
  - Distributed BESS outpaced utility-scale by 300% historically due to bankable VDER revenue; Con Edison’s locational incentives (LSRV) at ~$284/kW-year vs. upstate $18–70/kW-year drove siting in Zone J despite higher build costs. But by Nov. 2025, ConEd’s locational incentives were ~93% exhausted—only 7 MW remained in three Manhattan networks—cutting fixed revenues in half (from ~$284 to ~$140/kW-year), removing debt-sizing floors lenders used and hindering new builds. Developers looked to ISC (grid-scale) and other structures for revenue certainty. ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- NYISO market rules and DER Aggregation:
  - NYISO first to allow full ESR participation; capacity eligibility for 2-hour resources; developing hybrid model rules and DER aggregation pathways. DER aggregation could theoretically stack VDER DRV with wholesale revenues, but broad participation has not yet materialized. ([NYISO FAQ](https://www.nyiso.com/-/energy-storage-frequently-asked-questions); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Cost and supply chain:
  - NYSERDA reported average installed costs for non-residential retail storage rose ~22% from 2020–2021 ($464/kWh) to 2022–2023 ($567/kWh), reflecting supply chain and commodity pressures; industry expects easing, with broader analyses noting 2024 BNEF lithium-ion pack prices at ~$115/kWh (storage pack specifics vary), suggesting partial relief for 2025–2026 procurement. ([LinkedIn analysis citing NYSERDA program data](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue-kinross); [Morgan Lewis citing BNEF 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/a-2025-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements))

- Interconnection:
  - Queue congestion is national; NYISO’s first reformed interconnection cluster included ~19 GW of BESS, with only 2–4 GW expected viable by 2030, a proxy for project attrition due to contractable revenue limits and grid constraints. Practitioners call interconnection delays “purgatory,” pushing developers to hybridization and distribution siting where feasible. ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Energy Central interconnection overview](https://energycentral.com/c/um/grid-interconnection-queue-gets-longer-and-longer))

Interpretation:
- The most acute pain signal in distributed NY BESS is loss of bankable floors (VDER exhaustion) and the need to substitute that with new Retail Storage incentives, floor-plus-upside contracts, or ISC-like constructs. Practitioners explicitly tie debt sizing and takeout valuations to predictable revenue streams.
- Interconnection and local acceptance form a parallel pain track—projects must hit permitting and interconnection milestones to be “contractable.” The PSC’s fire safety emphasis signals cross-jurisdictional sensitivities that developers must preempt.

Many practitioner threads center on “how to rebuild floors” (retail blocks, ISC, tolls) rather than optimizing merchant-only strategies—an indicator of intense, persistent pain.

Advanced Synthesis: Connecting Branches

- Distributed-to-grid-scale capital migration: With VDER exhaustion, distributed development momentum waned, and developers sought long-term floors via ISC at grid-scale—yet distributed opportunities re-open via Retail and Residential Programs. This creates a bifurcated path: distributed re-enters bankable territory if Retail/ISI blocks can be secured; otherwise, developers pursue 15-year ISC for larger assets. The two tracks co-exist, but buyer preferences push both to floor-based structures ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities); [Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/march/new-york-s-bulk-energy-storage-procurements-to-commence-in-june-2025/)).

- Interconnection as the “make-or-break” workflow: Even with incentives/floors, buyers emphasize interconnection maturity and community/fire safety readiness. PSC’s fire-safety direction elevates local acceptance and operational preparedness. Thus, technical milestones (hybridization design, first-responder training plans, code compliance) become not only permitting essentials but also commercial value drivers in takeout negotiations ([Hodgson Russ bulk fire safety](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/psc-approves-nyserda-s-bulk-energy-storage-program-implementation-plan-q6VAIHJfx6XeiLA)).

- Financing architecture converges on floors and long-tenor: Practitioner language coalesces around “tolling,” “floor-plus-upside,” and “strike-minus-reference.” Bankers and IPPs favor these to restore leverage potential. Distributed projects can mimic this architecture via Retail blocks and third-party tolls; grid-scale via ISC. The language—and economics—are convergent across scales; NEE should mirror this in deal design and marketing collateral ([LinkedIn practitioner analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/merchant-vs-contracted-bess-projects-new-revenue-models-singal-xaorc); [Modo Q3 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/ercot-pjm-caiso-nyiso-us-bess-research-roundup-q3-2025)).

- IRR sources: Practitioners warn that IRR headlines can mislead if driven by terminal values rather than operating cash flows; floor-based revenue de-risks IRR composition. For a develop-and-sell shop like NEE, development margin realization depends less on long-run merchant assumptions and more on the credibility of the floor contract, interconnection status, and diligence pack—directly aligning with “IRR sources” discipline ([Milazzo LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/timmilazzo_irr-for-real-estate-development-tactica-activity-7050146812216664064-H7ep)).

Implications for NEE’s Go/No-Go and Project Design

- Revenue architecture for distributed (~5 MW AC) assets:
  - Anchor on NYSERDA Retail Storage blocks (ConEd and ROS), leveraging ISI where applicable; explicitly incorporate eligibility for NYISO DER aggregation as an optional upside pathway but avoid underwriting core returns to aggregation given limited practical scale so far. Build buyer-ready term sheets for tolling/floor-plus-upside to supplement where block incentives are insufficient or exhausted. ([Hodgson Russ, 2026](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/new-york-state-public-service-commission-approves-retail-and-residential-lJqO2AVhDCPDbhD); [Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Siting strategy:
  - Prioritize Con Edison networks or adjacent load pockets where compensation remains highest and buyer interest strongest; however, manage expectations as LSRV exhaustion diminished the automatic floor. For ROS, select zones with DRV/DR programs and favorable upgrades. Avoid purely revenue-challenged upstate zones without additional contract support. ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))

- Interconnection and permitting:
  - Institute strict internal “contractability gates”: interconnection request acceptance, system impact milestones, preliminary protection studies, and documented fire-safety plans (peer review, ERP, annual FD training commitments). Package these with environmental and community engagement artifacts for buyer diligence. This directly addresses practitioner pain regarding “queue purgatory” and local acceptance. ([Energy Central interconnection](https://energycentral.com/c/um/grid-interconnection-queue-gets-longer-and-longer); [Hodgson Russ fire safety](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-biz/post/psc-approves-nyserda-s-bulk-energy-storage-program-implementation-plan-q6VAIHJfx6XeiLA))

- Cost and procurement management:
  - Procure with clear RFQs emphasizing warranty deliverables, performance curves, and thermal management to reduce hidden degradation risks; consider modest oversizing aligned with OEM warranty guidance to avoid “gotchas” (noting industry norm of 10–12% oversizing to maintain warranties). Monitor 2025–2026 price trajectories (BNEF/WoodMac) but continue to model conservatively given potential tariffs and logistics volatility. ([Energy-Storage.news asset mgmt](https://www.energy-storage.news/operational-risk-optimisation-data-tolls-and-more-battery-asset-management-summit-europe-key-takeaways/); [Morgan Lewis citing BNEF 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/a-2025-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements))

- Buyer (IPP) packaging:
  - Create a standardized “Exit Diligence Pack”:
    - Revenue floor evidence: Retail block award letters; draft tolling/floor contracts; DER aggregation participation plan (optional).
    - Interconnection maturity: queue status, studies, expected upgrades, cost-sharing; substation mapping; schedule risk register.
    - Safety/community: ERP, FD training MOUs, code compliance crosswalk, AHJ correspondence.
    - Financial model with IRR sources attribution and downside sensitivities; tax equity vs. transfer analysis where relevant to buyer’s capital stack.
  - This aligns with practitioner emphasis on bankability and leadership-level scrutiny.

Quantitative heuristics for underwriting and Board thresholds

- Revenue references:
  - ConEd VDER LSRV pre-exhaustion ~$284/kW-year vs. post-exhaustion ~$140/kW-year; ROS $18–70/kW-year; ROS revenues 44–86% below upstate gross CONE of $126/kW-year. NEE should not assume pre-exhaustion levels; use post-exhaustion floors plus Retail incentives or tolling to reach target revenue floors enabling debt sizing. ([Modo Energy, 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/nyiso-new-york-distributed-bess-signals-grid-scale-opportunities))
  - ISC-style floors: While primarily for bulk projects, they reveal buyers’ preferred structure—15-year strike-minus-reference with monthly settle and payback caps—useful analogues for distributed floor-plus-upside deals. ([Foley Hoag, 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/energy-and-climate-counsel/2025/march/new-york-s-bulk-energy-storage-procurements-to-commence-in-june-2025/); [Modo Q3 2025](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/ercot-pjm-caiso-nyiso-us-bess-research-roundup-q3-2025))

- Cost references:
  - NYSERDA program-reported non-residential retail installs averaged ~$567/kWh in 2022–2023 (up from ~$464/kWh in 2020–2021); 2025–2026 procurements may benefit from general pack price declines seen circa 2024, but distributed BOS, safety, and interconnection costs in NYC can keep all-in costs elevated vs. utility-scale. Underwrite at conservative $450–$550/kWh turnkey until vendor bids firm. ([LinkedIn analysis citing NYSERDA](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/battery-systems-5-mw-begin-capturing-new-york-vder-revenue-kinross); [Morgan Lewis citing BNEF 2024](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/a-2025-update-on-utility-scale-energy-storage-procurements))

- Debt sizing heuristics:
  - With credible floor revenue (Retail block or toll), expect 60–70% leverage potential; merchant-weighted stacks support 20–30% debt at materially higher WACC—misaligned with develop-and-sell takeouts. ([LinkedIn practitioner analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/merchant-vs-contracted-bess-projects-new-revenue-models-singal-xaorc); [Energy-Storage.news asset mgmt takeaways](https://www.energy-storage.news/operational-risk-optimisation-data-tolls-and-more-battery-asset-management-summit-europe-key-takeaways/))

- IRR sources:
  - Require attribution: >70% of base-case project value from contracted/floor revenues; merchant upside included only as optional upside case. This prevents “IRR masking” where appreciation or speculative merchant tails drive outcomes. ([Milazzo LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/timmilazzo_irr-for-real-estate-development-tactica-activity-7050146812216664064-H7ep))

Conclusion and Opinion

Practitioner discourse in 2024–2026 New York is dominated by a clear throughline: without a credible revenue floor and interconnection maturity, distributed BESS projects will not meet buyer and lender thresholds; with floors and permitting readiness, a replicable, de-risked development margin is achievable—even as policy mechanisms evolve. VDER exhaustion in ConEd removed the historic floor and exposed the fragility of merchant-heavy underwriting; PSC/NYSERDA responded by standing up new Retail/Residential programs and ISC bulk procurements to restore bankability, albeit with updated budgets and evolving details.

Given NEE’s develop-and-sell strategy, a merchant-exposed market entry is unlikely to clear Board returns. However, a focused pilot that locks in floor revenues—through Retail Storage Incentives, ISI where applicable, and/or private tolling—combined with disciplined interconnection/permitting gates and a buyer-ready diligence pack, should meet the Board’s risk-adjusted hurdle. The “conditional Go” therefore hinges on landing those floors and gates early. Should the company fail to secure them on the first 2–3 assets by late 2026, it should pause and redirect resources to ISC-aligned grid-scale opportunities where 15-year state-backed floors are accessible or to other states with clearer DER value streams.

In short: Proceed, but only where NEE can speak—and structure deals—in the practitioner’s language of floors, contractability, and interconnection maturity. That is the market’s currency in New York’s 2026 distributed storage landscape.
