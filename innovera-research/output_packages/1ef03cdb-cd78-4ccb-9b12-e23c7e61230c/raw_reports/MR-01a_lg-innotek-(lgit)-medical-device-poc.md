Title: Defining Market Boundaries for Precision Flexible Substrate and Electrode Components in Medical Devices (CGM, Neurostimulation, BCI)

Executive summary
This report defines and bounds the market for precision flexible substrate and electrode components in medical devices—focused initially on North America and Europe with global expansion considerations—across three high-value application families: continuous glucose monitoring systems (CGMS), neurostimulation, and brain–computer interfaces (BCI). It synthesizes regulatory and classification frameworks (NAICS, NACE), analyst segmentations (CGM market methodologies), technical scope (flexible electronics and neural electrodes), and B2B buyer segmentation to construct inclusion and exclusion rules, regional scope conventions, and use-case boundaries. It also evaluates the veracity of widely cited venture claims (growth, investment intensity, margin structure), applies OECD trade-dependency thinking to component supply risk, and compares entry pathways for LG Innotek (LGIT): remaining a component/substrate supplier, moving upstream into modules, or operating as a CDMO/strategic partner.

Key findings
- Canonical market labels are fragmented because “precision flexible substrate and electrode components” sit at a supply-tier interface between medical device OEM markets (e.g., “CGM market”) and enabling component categories (flexible electronics, FPC/FPCBs, thin-film electrodes). In North America, NAICS-based anchors for medical-device components are 334510 (electromedical and electrotherapeutic apparatus manufacturing) for device-integrated assemblies and 334419 (other electronic component manufacturing) for flexible electronic subassemblies, while in Europe the updated NACE Rev. 2.1 framework maps to Divisions C26 (computer, electronic and optical products) and C32.5 (medical and dental instruments and supplies) for finished medical goods and specialized components (NAICS guidance; Eurostat NACE update) ([Census, n.d.](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/year/2022/guidance/understanding-naics.html); [Eurostat, 2023-02-10](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/main/news/whats-new/press-releases/2023-02-10)).
- Product inclusion rules for CGM components are anchored by analyst methodologies that split the market by component (sensors, transmitters, receivers), with sensors the dominant revenue driver; neurostimulation systems include generators, leads/electrodes, and programming/analysis services; BCI components include high-density electrode arrays, flexible substrates, feedthroughs, and implantable/interconnect assemblies (Technavio 2024; CPT/HCPCS references; neural electrodes literature). Exclusions typically remove non-medical wearables and consumer-only patches from regulated device totals (where analyst reports focus on medical indications) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
- Geographic scope conventions: “North America” is usually US + Canada; “Europe” aligns to EU/EFTA in many analyses; “APAC” varies but includes China, Japan, Korea; “ROW” or “Rest of World” captures remaining regions. These conventions hold across CGM analyst coverage (Technavio 2024) and broader sensor market sizing (IDTechEx 2025) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051)).
- Customer-segment boundaries for B2B are best defined using firmographic segmentation (industry codes, size, region), needs-based segmentation (implantable vs wearable, sterilization/biocompatibility, integration level), and behavioral procurement patterns (design controls, validation burden, quality systems). These are standard in go-to-market and segmentation practice for B2B markets (Qualtrics 2026; Demandbase 2026) ([Qualtrics, 2026](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/strategy-research/what-is-market-segmentation/); [Demandbase, 2026](https://www.demandbase.com/faq/types-of-market-segmentation/)).
- Use-case boundaries: CGM equals regulated continuous glucose monitoring (medical indications) across homecare and clinical pathways; neurostimulation includes deep brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerve, vagus, sacral, and cranial stimulation; BCI spans invasive, partially invasive (e.g., endovascular stents), and non-invasive modalities—with invasive/partially invasive most relevant to implant-grade flexible substrates and electrode arrays (Frontiers 2021; BiopharmaTrend BCI industry review; Nature 2024/2025 technical overviews) ([Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/); [Nature, 2024-03-06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00948-w)).
- Venture claim verification: CGM growth is strong but not uniformly “explosive”—Technavio (2024–2028) shows ~6.8% CAGR; Mordor Intelligence (2026–2031) shows ~15.4% CAGR and high concentration among Abbott/Dexcom/Medtronic. Net: robust, with acceleration where reimbursement expands and sensors lengthen wear times. Neurostimulation/BCI growth narratives are supported by clinical expansion and regulatory activity (Breakthrough designations, early feasibility trials), yet market fragmentation and regulatory evidence demands keep BCI revenue more nascent; North American investment intensity is significant (e.g., Neuralink US trials, Blackrock’s FDA Breakthrough device), consistent with the claim of strong NA investment. Margin-structure claims are not corroborated by authoritative public sources; industry proxies suggest high-precision, medical-grade customization can command higher gross margins than commodity FPC/FPCBs, but headline ranges (10–60%) require caution without audited benchmarks (Technavio 2024; Mordor Intelligence 2025; Blackrock/INBRAIN activity; and manufacturing margin proxies) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [Mordor Intelligence, 2025](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market); [Life Science Intelligence, 2021-11](https://www.lifesciencemarketresearch.com/insights/medtech-startups-blackrock-neurotech-aptihealth-appliedvr-more)).
- For LGIT, the most defensible entry posture is to: (1) build a regulated-grade flexible substrate/electrode component line focused on implantable/wearable biocompatible stacks (polyimide, parylene C, PEDOT:PSS interfaces) for CGM and neurostimulation; (2) selectively move up into subassemblies/modules for CGM sensors (where replacement cycles create recurring revenue) and neurostimulation leads; and (3) operate as a CDMO partner for startups/next-gen BCI developers—using CDMO market momentum to bridge into OEM accounts while mitigating clinical and regulatory burden of owning a finished device (CDMO industry growth 2025–2033). Prioritize North America and EU OEMs/startups with clear regulatory roadmaps and evidence generation plans; use OECD trade-dependency analysis to reduce single-region supplier risks in critical films, coatings, and metallization stacks (MarketDataForecast 2025; Vector Biomed 2025) ([MarketDataForecast, 2025-11](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/cdmo-market); [Vector Biomed, 2025](https://vectorbiomed.com/resource/what-economic-or-market-trends-are-having-the-biggest-impact-on-cdmo-capacity-and-pipelines-in-2025-and-what-will-2026-bring/)).

- Technical and regulatory scope: Clinical neurostimulation/BCI interfaces and flexible materials grounded in peer-reviewed literature; neurostimulation coding and device categories cross-checked via outpatient coding references and FDA device pathway snapshots (Frontiers 2021; Nature 2024/2025; FDA De Novo database 2026) ([Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [Nature, 2024-03-06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00948-w); [FDA, 2026-03-09](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/device-classification-under-section-513f2-de-novo)).
- Segmentation conventions and B2B buyer definitions: Based on current best practices in market segmentation (firmographic, needs-based, behavioral) to properly demarcate enterprise OEMs, system integrators, startups, and research institutions (Qualtrics 2026; Demandbase 2026) ([Qualtrics, 2026](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/strategy-research/what-is-market-segmentation/); [Demandbase, 2026](https://www.demandbase.com/faq/types-of-market-segmentation/)).

A. Market boundary definition

A1. Canonical market definition
- Primary category anchors:
  - “Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) market” (device-level category used by analysts and OEMs). Standard segmentation divides by component (sensors, transmitters, receivers) and end-user (home care, hospitals, others), across regions (NA, Europe, APAC, ROW) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).
  - “Neurostimulation devices” or “neuromodulation devices” (device-level category spanning DBS, SCS, VNS, PNS, sacral nerve, cranial systems). Regulatory coding (CPT/HCPCS) and FDA device categories reinforce inclusion of generators, leads/electrodes, and programming/analysis services ([Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [FDA, 2026-03-09](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/device-classification-under-section-513f2-de-novo)).
  - “Brain–computer interface (BCI)” (technology category that spans invasive, partially invasive, and non-invasive systems; at the component level, high-density electrode arrays, flexible substrates, implantable electronics, and interconnects are core). Industry analyses commonly split by invasiveness and clinical/industry use cases ([BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/)).
- Component-level enabling category:
  - “Medical flexible electronics / flexible substrates / flexible printed circuits (FPC/FPCB)” and “thin-film electrodes” as enabling sub-markets that feed the above device categories. Wearable sensors market taxonomies treat CGM as a key medical segment; implantable flexible electrodes are delineated by materials (polyimide, parylene, PEDOT:PSS, graphene) and form factors (arrays, cuffs, sieve electrodes) in the technical literature ([IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [Nature, 2024-03-06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00948-w)).

- Industrial classification anchors:
  - NAICS (U.S.):
    - 334510 Electromedical and Electrotherapeutic Apparatus Manufacturing — covers finished electromedical devices such as pacemakers, neurostimulators, and monitoring systems (mapping LGIT’s module-level assemblies into device-integrated subassemblies) ([Census, n.d.](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/year/2022/guidance/understanding-naics.html)).
    - 334419 Other Electronic Component Manufacturing — covers electronic component manufacturing not elsewhere classified; appropriate for flexible electronic substrates, FPC/FPCBs, and precision thin-film interconnects supplied as components to OEMs ([Census, n.d.](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/year/2022/guidance/understanding-naics.html)).
    - Depending on productization (e.g., surgical leads), 339113 (Surgical Appliance and Supplies Manufacturing) may also be relevant for certain implantable accessories; however, electronic content generally anchors to 334510 for finished systems ([Census, n.d.](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/year/2022/guidance/understanding-naics.html)).
  - NACE (EU; Rev. 2.1):
    - C26 Manufacture of computer, electronic and optical products (component and subassembly manufacturing).
    - C32.5 Manufacture of medical and dental instruments and supplies (finished medical equipment and certain specialized parts). The 2023 update notes new positions for emerging activities, enabling better mapping for novel flexible/bioelectronic components in medical supply chains ([Eurostat, 2023-02-10](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/main/news/whats-new/press-releases/2023-02-10)).

A2. Alternate labels and synonyms
Common adjacent labels across analyst, regulatory, and academic domains include:
- Medical flexible electronics; flexible medical substrates; medical FPC/FPCB; chip-on-film (CoF) for medical; ultra-thin flexible interconnects; thin-film medical device components; flexible biosensor patch substrates (often consumer/clinical boundary).
- CGM components; glucose biosensor filaments; implantable glucose sensors (e.g., Eversense).
- Neurostimulation electrode substrates; cranial/spinal/peripheral nerve electrode arrays; cortical arrays; cuff/sieve electrodes; sacral nerve leads.
- BCI components; high-density electrode arrays; neural probes; intracortical microelectrode arrays; ECoG grids; endovascular stent-based electrode arrays.
- Medical CDMO flexible electronics; biocompatible flexible substrate supply; implant-grade PI/parylene/PEDOT:PSS stacks.

Labels imply different boundaries:
- “Medical flexible electronics” can include consumer wellness devices; regulated market definitions generally exclude non-medical wearables unless explicitly stated (IDTechEx distinguishes medical segments) ([IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051)).
- “CGM” in medical analyst reports excludes fingerstick-only glucose monitoring and non-invasive wellness-only devices unless specified; component splits include only CGM system components (sensors, transmitters, receivers) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).
- “Neurostimulation” in clinical/regulatory contexts includes implantable generators/leads and programming services but excludes diagnostic-only EEG/MEG systems (distinct device categories) ([Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
- “BCI” spans invasive/non-invasive; for implant-grade flexible substrates, the relevant boundary is invasive or partially invasive systems requiring implantable flexible electrodes and hermetic interconnects (Frontiers; BiopharmaTrend) ([Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/)).

A3. Product-type inclusion rules
- CGM market (analyst convention):
  - Components: sensors (disposable filaments/implants), transmitters, receivers; sensors dominate revenue due to replacement cycles (Technavio segments precisely this way; Mordor Intelligence emphasizes sensors’ revenue share and growth trajectory) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [Mordor Intelligence, 2025](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).
  - End-users: home care, hospitals, others (e.g., clinics); geography: NA, Europe, APAC, ROW (Technavio 2024) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).
  - Manufacturing scope implication: flexible substrates and thin-film electrode structures integrated into sensor filaments, transmitters (PCB/FPC), and receivers are within scope as upstream components feeding these units (IDTechEx sensor anatomy sections) ([IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051)).

- Neurostimulation (clinical/regulatory convention):
  - Device elements: implantable generators (pulse generators/receivers), leads/electrodes (e.g., cranial, spinal, peripheral), and programming/analysis sessions (CPT/HCPCS codes). Inclusion extends to the electrode substrates and interconnects (flexible polyimide/parylene), including revisions/removals (CPT 61880) and device analysis/programming (CPT 95970/95983/95984) which imply active system use (Outpatient codes 2026) ([Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
  - Materials and form factor inclusion: polyimide/parylene cortical arrays, cuff/sieve electrodes for peripheral nerve stimulation; flexible arrays for DBS/ECoG; coatings (e.g., PEDOT:PSS) that enable low-impedance, conformable interfaces (Frontiers 2021; Nature 2024) ([Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [Nature, 2024-03-06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00948-w)).

- BCI (industry convention):
  - Inclusion of invasive and partially invasive electrode arrays (e.g., Utah arrays, graphene ECoG grids, stent-based arrays), implantable electronics, hermetic feedthroughs, flexible connectors/substrates; non-invasive EEG/fNIRS BCIs are a separate sub-market with weaker demand for implant-grade flexible substrates (BiopharmaTrend overview; Frontiers electrode reviews) ([BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

A4. Product-type exclusion rules
- CGM market reports generally exclude:
  - Fingerstick-only glucose meters.
  - Non-regulated wellness patches that are not cleared for medical monitoring.
  - Broader “wearable sensor” devices that are not CGM-specific (e.g., heart-rate wristbands) unless a report explicitly spans all wearables (Technavio 2024 component-based CGM taxonomy sets clear boundaries to CGM components only) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).

- Neurostimulation boundaries exclude:
  - Purely diagnostic electrophysiology devices (e.g., EEG/MEG without stimulation).
  - Non-electrical neuromodulation modalities (e.g., TMS, focused ultrasound) unless a report explicitly includes them; component focus here is on implantable electrical stimulation requiring electrodes/leads (CPT/HCPCS codes target implantable neurostimulator services) ([Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

- BCI boundaries for implant-grade flexible components exclude:
  - Non-invasive BCIs (dry EEG headsets) where substrate needs are often conventional PCB/plastics rather than implant-grade thin films.
  - Consumer neurotech/wellness headbands (non-medical), except where hybrid clinical-research use requires elevated quality/reliability (BiopharmaTrend delineates invasive vs non-invasive markets) ([BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/)).

A5. Geographic scope conventions
- North America: commonly US, Canada; sometimes includes Mexico in specific trade contexts but analyst CGM reports generally focus on US and Canada as distinct sub-regions within NA (Technavio 2024) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).
- Europe: typically EU member states and EFTA; region-specific regulatory classifications and NACE usage apply (Eurostat 2023 NACE Rev. 2.1) ([Eurostat, 2023-02-10](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/main/news/whats-new/press-releases/2023-02-10)).
- APAC: varies but usually includes China, Japan, Korea, others; ROW (Rest of World) aggregates remaining markets. The regional framing appears consistently in CGM market segmentations (Technavio 2024) and wearable sensors (IDTechEx 2025) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051)).
- Trade-dependency considerations: OECD’s 2024 framework provides a way to identify “concerning” dependencies based on concentration, substitutability, and criticality—useful for mapping risks if flexible substrate inputs (e.g., PI films, conductive inks, microfabrication services) concentrate in single geographies (OECD 2024) ([OECD, 2024-04](https://www.oecd.org/trade/documents/towards-demystifying-trade-dependencies.pdf)).

A6. Customer-segment boundaries (B2B)
- Enterprise buyer types in scope:
  - Medical device OEMs (CGM leaders: Abbott, Dexcom; neurostimulation leaders: Medtronic, Boston Scientific; diversified: Medtronic covers CGM and neurostim).
  - System integrators and EMS/ODM with medical units (Jabil, Flex) that assemble modules/subassemblies for OEM programs.
  - Medical CDMOs and specialized device-component firms (Integer for implantables, Murata/Linxes for components).
  - BCI system developers (Neuralink, Synchron, Blackrock Neurotech) and new entrants (Echo) who require implant-grade electrode arrays and flexible substrates.
  - Academic medical centers and research institutions conducting early feasibility—potential development partners for new electrode/substrate tech (BiopharmaTrend spotlight on BCI companies) ([BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/)).

- Segmentation practice:
  - Firmographic: by industry (NAICS/NACE), company size, region, and R&D focus (Qualtrics 2026; Demandbase 2026) ([Qualtrics, 2026](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/strategy-research/what-is-market-segmentation/); [Demandbase, 2026](https://www.demandbase.com/faq/types-of-market-segmentation/)).
  - Needs-based: implantable-grade biocompatibility and sterilization; microfabrication tolerances for high-density arrays; traceability and QMS (ISO 13485); flexible stack materials and coatings (Frontiers/Nature) ([Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [Nature, 2024-03-06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-00948-w)).
  - Behavioral: procurement preferences (single-source vs dual-source), willingness to co-develop with CDMO partners, regulatory and evidence generation posture, speed-to-market pressures (Vector Biomed on dual sourcing and nearshoring trends for capacity/reliability) ([Vector Biomed, 2025](https://vectorbiomed.com/resource/what-economic-or-market-trends-are-having-the-biggest-impact-on-cdmo-capacity-and-pipelines-in-2025-and-what-will-2026-bring/)).

A7. Use-case boundaries
- CGM:
  - Regulated medical monitoring of interstitial glucose in diabetic populations; end-users include home care (dominant), hospitals, and other clinical settings; scope includes sensor filaments (disposable), transmitters, receivers, and integrated mobile/software ecosystems (Technavio 2024; Mordor 2025) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [Mordor Intelligence, 2025](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).
  - Emerging wellness usage by non-diabetics is noted as a driver but generally sits outside core regulated CGM revenue unless reports explicitly include it; the industry trend is convergence via app ecosystems and data analytics (Mordor 2025) ([Mordor Intelligence, 2025](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- Neurostimulation:
  - Therapeutic stimulation for Parkinson’s, epilepsy, depression (DBS), chronic pain (SCS), bladder/bowel dysfunction (sacral), vagus/peripheral indications; procedural coding underscores inclusion of generator revisions/replacements and programming (CPT/HCPCS). Use case is therapy delivery, not diagnostic monitoring (Outpatient codes 2026; Frontiers 2021) ([Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

- BCI:
  - Assistive control for severe paralysis (cursor, keyboard, wheelchair, prosthetic control); intraoperative neuromodulation/monitoring; exploratory therapeutics. Invasive arrays (intracortical/ECoG) and partially invasive stent-electrode arrays are the implant contexts where high-precision flexible substrates/electrodes are critical (BiopharmaTrend; Frontiers material/interface reviews) ([BiopharmaTrend, 2025](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/neurotech/top-10-companies-shaping-the-brain-computer-interface-landscape-invasive-vs-non-invasive-bci-technology-650/); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

Market dynamics and growth claims

CGM demand trajectory
- Technavio (published July 2024) estimates CGM market growth of USD 2.94B over 2023–2028 at a 6.78% CAGR, with North America contributing 50% of growth, and sensors as the fastest-growing component. Segmentation covers sensors, transmitters, receivers; end-users include home care, hospitals, others; geographies include NA, Europe, APAC, ROW ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis)).
- Mordor Intelligence (2025) projects higher momentum 2026–2031: global CGM market from USD 15.33B (2026) to USD 31.38B (2031), 15.42% CAGR; highlights concentration (Abbott 56.74%, Dexcom 35.20%, Medtronic 6.88% in 2024)—a duopoly-like structure with high R&D investment and recurring revenue via sensor replacement cycles ([Mordor Intelligence, 2025](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

Implications for LG Innotek (LGIT): scoping and go-to-market

Component vs module vs CDMO
- Component supplier: Targets NAICS 334419 pathway supplying flexible substrates/FPC/FPCBs/thin-film electrodes, chip-on-film (CoF) interconnects for:
  - CGM sensor filaments (flexible electrode stacks), transmitters (FPC/rigid-flex), and receivers.
  - Neurostim leads/electrode arrays (polyimide/parylene substrates with noble-metal/PEDOT:PSS interfaces).
  - BCI arrays/interposers/connectors (with hermetic feedthrough partners).
  Advantages: lower regulatory burden than finished devices; leverages LGIT’s fine-line CoF manufacturing and ultra-thin flex capabilities; aligns with concentrated OEM buyer set. Risks: incumbent control over critical components; qualification timelines; price pressure if perceived as commodity unless differentiated by implant-grade materials and process IP (IDTechEx CGM anatomy; Frontiers flexible substrates) ([IDTechEx, 2025](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/wearable-sensors-market-2025/1051); [Frontiers, 2021-05](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

- Module/subassembly supplier: Moves into CGM transmitter/receiver subassemblies or neurostim lead modules. Requires ISO 13485 manufacturing, sterilization-aware processes, and tighter regulatory interfaces. Potentially higher margins and stickier programs via validation burden. Risks include greater working capital, sustaining engineering, and liability sharing (Technavio CGM component dominance suggests module play is feasible; neurostim coding underlines value of reliable leads/programming) ([Technavio, 2024-07](https://www.technavio.com/report/continuoglucose-monitoring-market-analysis); [Outpatient Codes, 2026-01-01](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

- CDMO partner: Offers development-through-manufacturing services for flexible bioelectronics (CGM sensors, neurostim/BCI electrodes), targeting startups/scale-ups and certain OEM programs. The CDMO market is expanding (global CDMO ~USD 160B in 2025; projected ~USD 383B by 2033, 11.52% CAGR), with strong demand for integrated end-to-end services and regional resilience (MarketDataForecast 2025; Vector Biomed 2025). Advantages: premium pricing on development services, strategic stickiness, co-ownership of process IP; Risks: service complexity, regulatory QA/QC investments, and the need to balance capacity/utilization (MarketDataForecast 2025; Vector Biomed 2025) ([MarketDataForecast, 2025-11](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/cdmo-market); [Vector Biomed, 2025](https://vectorbiomed.com/resource/what-economic-or-market-trends-are-having-the-biggest-impact-on-cdmo-capacity-and-pipelines-in-2025-and-what-will-2026-bring/)).

Our opinion and recommendations
- LGIT should define its initial market boundary as “biocompatible, implantable- and wearable-grade flexible substrates and electrode components” serving two priority device families: CGM (sensor substrates/electrodes; transmitter FPC/rigid-flex) and neurostimulation (lead/electrode substrates). These segments have validated demand drivers, clear inclusion rules, and component architectures that exploit LGIT’s CoF and fine-pattern flex strengths.
- Build a CDMO-lite capability to attract BCI innovators and next-generation neurostim programs: development services (materials stacks, electrode coatings, microfabrication) plus ISO 13485 manufacturing. This pathway provides higher-value engagement and potential margin uplift while creating defensible know-how around implant-grade process windows.
- Adopt OECD trade-dependency diagnostics to manage upstream material concentration (polyimide/parylene films, conductive polymers, noble metals) and secure dual-region (NA/EU) supply and production footprints—now valued by OEMs and CDMOs seeking resilience and nearshoring.
- Validate margin structure empirically: target pilot engagements with CGM/neurostim OEMs and BCI startups; measure gross margin at each level (component vs module vs CDMO) given actual process yields, scrap, sterilization requirements, and QA overhead. Avoid overcommitting to modules unless qualification barriers generate durable switching costs and defendable margin.
- In North America and Europe, align to NAICS 334419/334510 and NACE C26/C32.5 classifications for positioning, procurement discoverability, and regulatory alignment. Use standard B2B segmentation (firmographic + needs-based) to structure account tiers and partnership models.

Conclusion
A coherent boundary for LGIT’s entry sits at the intersection of regulated medical device component supply and advanced flexible bioelectronics. The most defensible initial scope is implant-grade flexible substrates and electrodes for CGM and neurostimulation, with targeted CDMO-style engagements in invasive/partially invasive BCI. Market definitions from analyst taxonomies (CGM components and end-use splits), clinical/regulatory coding (neurostimulation device elements), and technology literature (materials and array designs) provide inclusion and exclusion rules. Geographic conventions (NA, Europe) and trade-dependency analysis support a regionally resilient supply-chain design. Growth is robust in CGM (with potential acceleration) and meaningful in neuromodulation/BCI (innovation-led), but margin assumptions must be validated program-by-program. Executed this way, LGIT can leverage its CoF and fine-line flex strengths to become a strategic supplier and development partner in high-value medtech segments.
