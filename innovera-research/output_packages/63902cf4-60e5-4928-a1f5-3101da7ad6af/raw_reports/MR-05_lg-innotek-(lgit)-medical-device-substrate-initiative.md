Title: Value Chain, Whitespace, and Economics for Flexible Substrate & Electrode Components in Wearable & Implantable Medical Devices (CGM, Neurostimulation, BCI)

Executive summary
This report maps the end-to-end value chain, identifies whitespace, and analyzes the economics for flexible substrate and electrode components used in continuous glucose monitoring systems (CGMS), neurostimulation, and brain–computer interfaces (BCI), with emphasis on North America and Europe initially and global expansion thereafter. It evaluates whether LG Innotek’s (LGIT) advanced flexible/fine-pattern substrate capabilities (e.g., chip‑on‑film, 10 µm photolithography, R2R production) provide defensible, monetizable differentiation in these medical markets and recommends an entry level (component vs module vs integrated system) considering regulatory, certification, and access barriers.

Key findings
- Profit pools and control points concentrate at OEM platform and regulatory/reimbursement gates. CGM OEMs like Dexcom sustain gross margins in the low-to-mid 60% range, reflecting brand, platform data integration, and reimbursement power, whereas component suppliers and EMS/CDMO players typically operate at materially lower operating margins, evidenced by LG Innotek’s 2024 operating margin of 3.3% and camera-module business margin pressure to roughly 3% in recent years (2013–2024), underscoring a structural gap between upstream manufacturing and downstream system economics (PortersFiveForce; LG Innotek IR; The Elec) ([PortersFiveForce.com, 2025](https://portersfiveforce.com/blogs/competitors/dexcom); [LG Innotek IR, 2024](https://www.lginnotek.com/ir/finance.lgit?locale=en); [The Elec, 2026](https://thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=5191)).
- Medical-grade flexible hybrid electronics (FHE) and flexible implantable electrodes have grown rapidly, but the supply base for GMP-grade, traceable R2R flexible substrates and multi-layer thin-film electrodes remains fragmented, with consortia such as NextFlex seeking to bridge lab-to-line gaps and IDTechEx noting the emergence (not prevalence) of FHE contract manufacturers and R2R pilot/production lines—suggesting white space at the intersection of high-volume flexible substrate manufacturing and medical regulatory/compliance (Manufacturing USA/NextFlex; IDTechEx) ([Manufacturing USA, 2025](https://www.manufacturingusa.com/studies/nextflex-pioneers-next-generation-electronics); [IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950)).
- Regulatory and requalification friction (ISO 13485, IEC 60601, 21 CFR Part 820), plus materials substitution sensitivity (e.g., conductive ink formulation changes), are major bottlenecks; any process or material change can trigger requalification and lengthy delays, creating high switching costs and “design-in” stickiness for qualified component suppliers (SemiEngineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
- Materials supply risk is non-trivial for printed/flexible electronics (e.g., silver in conductive inks is a critical raw material in the EU). European producers remain dependent on imported nanomaterial dispersions; copper/carbon alternatives are progressing but not yet equivalent, indicating pockets of supplier power upstream (Market Data Forecast) ([Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market)).
- Market opportunities: flexible implantable electrodes (~$2.9B in 2026, 11.6% CAGR from 2025) and a large, expanding CGM market (approaching $29B by 2030 per industry coverage; $13.6B by 2034 per a conservative view) with strong OEM concentration create scope for specialized component/module plays tied to OEM platforms (Research and Markets; Drug Delivery Business News; Medical Device Network) ([Research and Markets, 2026](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6226271/flexible-implantable-electrode-market-report?srsltid=AfmBOorT9ZmhDle5ZHBkTW1_ByKjyDGybkxPi0FU9FWLTWIWPrbcU5p2); [Drug Delivery Business News, 2025](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/the-top-cgm-stories-of-2025/); [Medical Device Network, 2025](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/features/inside-dexcom-strategy-to-stay-ahead-in-the-13-bn-cgm-market/)).
- Recommendation for LG Innotek: Enter as a medical-grade component/module supplier, not a system OEM. Near-term focus on:
  - CGM/wearable biosensor components: GMP, ISO 13485–qualified R2R flexible electrode webs and medical FPCBs with IPC-1782B traceability; target OEMs (Dexcom, Abbott, Medtronic) and medtech CDMOs (Integer, Cirtec) with NPI-to-volume scale pathways (IDTechEx; TBRC; IPC) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950); [The Business Research Company, 2026](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/medical-device-contract-manufacturing-global-market-report); [I-Connect007, 2025](https://iconnect007.com/article/139669/ipc-releases-newest-list-of-standards-updates-revisions/139666)).
  - Mid-term optionality into implantable flexible electrode modules (e.g., high-density polyimide thin-film arrays with AIROF/IrOx, PI substrates, FOWLP ASIC interconnects) through JDM/co-development with neurostimulation/BCI OEMs and specialist CDMOs, acknowledging heavier regulatory burdens and longer commercialization cycles (Nature; SemiEngineering; Frontiers) ([npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-018-0027-0); [Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/); [Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.622923/full)).

A. Value chain structure

A1. Stage-by-stage value chain map
Upstream inputs and platforms
- Advanced materials:
  - Conductive inks and films (Ag/AgCl, Au, Pt, Ir; silver nanowire inks; carbon inks) for printed electrodes and interconnects (Agfa, DuPont, Creative Materials; NovaCentrix photonic curing) ([Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market); [Creative Materials, 2026](https://www.creativematerials.com/products/medical-electrode-films/); [PalmSens/FlexMedical, 2026](https://www.palmsens.com/product/flexmedical-gold-carbon-electrodes/)).
  - Substrates and encapsulants (polyimide, PDMS/silicones, medical-grade adhesives and hydrogels from Dow, 3M, Henkel, Wacker, Shin‑Etsu) ([NatLawReview, 2026](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/real-time-cgm-patch-market-2026-rising-demand-continuous-glucose-monitoring)).
  - Implant-grade metals/films (Pt, IrOx/AIROF, Au, TiN) and biocompatible polymers (PI, LCP), with process constraints (e.g., polymer temperature limits) shaping fabrication (Frontiers) ([Frontiers, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.622923/full)).
- Equipment and process enablers:
  - Additive/printing (screen, inkjet, aerosol jet), R2R lines, laser patterning, photonic curing (NovaCentrix), microfabrication photolithography to ≤10 µm, laser structuring, sputtering, electroplating (IDTechEx; Converting Quarterly) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950); [Converting Quarterly, 2025](https://convertingquarterly.com/flexible-hybrid-electronics-realistic-near-term-outlook/)).
  - Packaging and test for medical ICs (FOWLP, embedded die, hermetic sealing, sterilization validation) (Semiconductor Engineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).

Midstream component manufacturing
- Flexible substrates and interconnects:
  - Medical FPCB, chip‑on‑film interposers, multilayer flexible circuits, stretchable interposers; clean processing for biocompatibility and sterilization tolerance (IDTechEx; SemiEngineering) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950); [Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
- Printed electrode webs and sensor laminates:
  - CGM patch electrodes (enzyme/electrochemical layers on PET/PI; Ag/AgCl reference; Au/C counter/working electrodes), mass-produced via screen printing and R2R (PalmSens/FlexMedical; Creative Materials) ([PalmSens/FlexMedical, 2026](https://www.palmsens.com/product/flexmedical-gold-carbon-electrodes/); [Creative Materials, 2026](https://www.creativematerials.com/products/medical-electrode-films/)).
- Implantable flexible electrode arrays:
  - Polyimide- or silicone-based thin-film arrays with noble-metal sites; advanced processes (AIROF/IrOx activation, PEDOT:PSS, glassy carbon, micro-LED integration for opto-electrodes) (npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering; Frontiers) ([npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-018-0027-0); [Frontiers, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.622923/full)).
- Hybrid integration:
  - FHE assemblies combining printed sensors with thinned Si dice, printed interconnects, and flexible PCBs; “print what you can, place what you can’t” (IDTechEx) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950)).

Downstream module and system integration

- Verification, validation, and regulatory:
  - ISO 13485 QMS, IEC 60601 electrical safety, ISO 10993 biocompatibility, sterilization validation, clinical studies, design controls (Semiconductor Engineering; Promwad) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/); [Promwad, 2026](https://promwad.com/news/wearable-medical-device-design-trends-2026)).

A3. Economic value flow and profit-pool distribution
- OEM/platform layer captures a disproportionate share of margin due to:
  - Integration of sensors with proprietary algorithms, data services, and reimbursement networks (Dexcom lead in intensive insulin users; premium pricing; gross margin low‑to‑mid 60%) ([PortersFiveForce.com, 2025](https://portersfiveforce.com/blogs/competitors/dexcom)).
  - Market concentration and established brands, especially in CGM where the top players dominate share (NatLawReview) ([NatLawReview, 2026](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/real-time-cgm-patch-market-2026-rising-demand-continuous-glucose-monitoring)).
- Midstream CDMO/EMS and component suppliers:
  - Compete on cost, quality, and compliance; margins structurally lower. LG Innotek’s consolidated operating margin was 3.3% in 2024, and its camera-module business saw operating margin compression to ~3% amid OEM pricing pressure, a cautionary analog for component-level businesses without platform control (LG Innotek IR; The Elec) ([LG Innotek IR, 2024](https://www.lginnotek.com/ir/finance.lgit?locale=en); [The Elec, 2026](https://thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=5191)).
- Upstream materials:
  - Specialty inks and noble metals can exhibit pricing power due to scarcity and qualification lock-in; silver classified as a critical raw material in the EU reflects supply concentration and volatility (Market Data Forecast) ([Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market)).

Implication: Profit pools concentrate at OEMs and, selectively, at specialized upstream materials with high switching costs. Component suppliers can enhance value capture by moving “up and in” (modules, qualified manufacturing with traceability, co-development) and by owning compliance-critical process IP that raises switching costs (SemiEngineering; IDTechEx) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/); [IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950)).

A4. Physical or operational flow structure
- CGM patch (illustrative):
  1) Material prep: R2R printing of electrode layers (Ag/AgCl reference; Au/C working/counter), enzyme and membrane deposition; photonic curing; die-cutting (PalmSens; Creative Materials) ([PalmSens/FlexMedical, 2026](https://www.palmsens.com/product/flexmedical-gold-carbon-electrodes/); [Creative Materials, 2026](https://www.creativematerials.com/products/medical-electrode-films/)).
  2) Substrate/interposer: flexible PCB/CoF manufacturing with biocompatible stack-ups; placement of AFE/SoC, BLE, battery; test (IDTechEx) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950)).
  3) Lamination/assembly: laminate electrode film to module; apply skin adhesives/hydrogels; seal waterproof e-skin enclosure; in-line monitoring and serialization for traceability (Promwad; IPC‑1782B) ([Promwad, 2026](https://promwad.com/news/wearable-medical-device-design-trends-2026); [I-Connect007, 2025](https://iconnect007.com/article/139669/ipc-releases-newest-list-of-standards-updates-revisions/139666)).
  4) Sterilization/pack-out: validate sterilization, shelf life; distribute through pharmacy/DME channels with payer codes.
- Implantable flexible electrode array:
  1) Thin-film fabrication on PI/silicone with noble-metal sites; microfabrication (≤10 µm features), ALD/PECVD passivation; AIROF activation; impedance testing (npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering; Frontiers) ([npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-018-0027-0); [Frontiers, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.622923/full)).
  2) Cable/connectorization and hermetic feedthrough integration; 3D packaging (FOWLP) for ASIC interfacing; soak/sterilization validation (Semiconductor Engineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
  3) Preclinical/clinical builds through ISO 13485 CDMOs; design changes trigger requalification (SemiEngineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).

B. Whitespace analysis

B6. Underserved stages or workflows
- GMP-grade R2R printed electrode substrates for medical wearables:
  - IDTechEx and NextFlex highlight the maturing but still nascent availability of contract FHE manufacturing with R2R pilot/production lines. The strongest near-term traction is in wearable patches; however, consistent GMP-grade, ISO 13485–aligned, in-line monitored R2R electrode production remains limited, especially with full serialization and audit trails (IDTechEx; NextFlex; Converting Quarterly) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950); [Manufacturing USA, 2025](https://www.manufacturingusa.com/studies/nextflex-pioneers-next-generation-electronics); [Converting Quarterly, 2025](https://convertingquarterly.com/flexible-hybrid-electronics-realistic-near-term-outlook/)).
- Scale-up gap from research-grade flexible electrodes to clinical-grade implantables:
  - Academic and preprint literature demonstrates rapid-prototyped PDMS/Au subdural arrays via laser patterning with promising performance, but translation to chronic, sterilization-validated, implant-grade arrays requires industrialized thin-film processes, biostability, and qualified packaging—an area served by few specialized firms (bioRxiv; Frontiers; npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering) ([bioRxiv, 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.05.709791); [Frontiers, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.622923/full); [npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-018-0027-0)).
- Medical IC packaging for implantables (FOWLP/embedded die) with biocompatibility and long-term reliability:
  - While fan-out and embedded die packaging are common in consumer devices, their adaptation to implantables faces extreme durability and biocompatibility constraints; optimized supply options are scarce (Semiconductor Engineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
- Materials supply chain resilience for printed electronics:
  - EU reliance on imported conductive inks and critical silver creates supply risk; alternative copper/carbon are not yet fully equivalent, suggesting room for vertically integrated or partnered materials strategies (Market Data Forecast) ([Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market)).

B7. Workflow friction points
- Requalification drag:
  - Design/fabrication/material changes trigger requalification cycles that extend development time; component/process stability is paramount and raises switching costs (Semiconductor Engineering) ([Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
- Traceability and regulatory documentation burden:
  - OEMs and regulators increasingly expect lot-level traceability (IPC‑1782B), biocompatibility files (ISO 10993), and electrical safety documentation (IEC 60601); these add overhead and slow supplier onboarding, especially for non-medical incumbents (I‑Connect007/IPC; Promwad; Mighty Studios) ([I-Connect007, 2025](https://iconnect007.com/article/139669/ipc-releases-newest-list-of-standards-updates-revisions/139666); [Promwad, 2026](https://promwad.com/news/wearable-medical-device-design-trends-2026); [Mighty Studios, 2026](https://www.mighty-studios.com/insights/biocompatibility-and-materials-selection-for-wearable-medical-devices)).
- Materials availability and substitution risks:
  - Silver being critical and imported nanoparticles reliance increase lead time/price volatility; substitutions require requalification (Market Data Forecast) ([Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market)).
- Supplier consolidation by OEMs:
  - OEMs are streamlining supplier networks, raising barriers to entry and privileging end-to-end CDMOs; newcomers must offer differentiated capabilities plus compliance maturity (Alira Health) ([Alira Health, 2026](https://alirahealth.com/education-hub/cdmo-medtech-market-trends-2025-and-2026/)).

B9. Profit-pool vs solution-coverage mismatch
- High-value OEM layers are well served by incumbents in CGM; however, their supply chains still face friction in upstream flexible electrode substrates (qualification, traceability, material risk). Given OEMs’ need for reliable, compliant, high‑throughput suppliers, a medical-grade flexible substrate/electrode offering that reduces requalification risk and improves traceability is a whitespace adjacent to large profit pools—where a qualified supplier can command durable, design‑in volumes even if per‑unit margins remain modest (IDTechEx; Market Data Forecast; SemiEngineering) ([IDTechEx, 2024](https://www.idtechex.com/en/research-report/flexible-hybrid-electronics/950); [Market Data Forecast, 2026](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/europe-printed-electronics-market); [Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).
- Implantable arrays are high complexity and lower volume but under‑served at scale; a module-level play (arrays + interconnect + packaging pathway) developed with neuro- OEM/CDMOs can capture premium pricing and deep lock‑in due to high switching costs (npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering; SemiEngineering) ([npj Microsystems & Nanoengineering, 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-018-0027-0); [Semiconductor Engineering, 2024](https://semiengineering.com/challenges-grow-for-medical-ics/)).

Conclusion and opinion
LG Innotek’s advanced flexible substrate and fine-pattern electrode manufacturing is relevant and potentially defensible in medical wearables and implantable electrodes if paired with medical-grade QMS, traceability, and validated biocompatible processes. Profit pools will continue to concentrate at OEM platforms, but suppliers with qualified, high‑throughput, traceable R2R electrode and medical FPCB capabilities can secure durable design‑in positions and steady revenue, particularly in CGM where volumes and regulatory stickiness favor stable upstream partnerships. In implantables/BCI, a module-level strategy in partnership with neuro‑OEMs/CDMOs offers higher technical differentiation and switching costs, albeit with longer, riskier cycles. Therefore, LGIT should enter as a B2B component/module supplier, not a system OEM, sequencing CGM/wearable components first and implantable electrode modules second. This approach balances monetizable differentiation with regulatory pragmatism and leverages LGIT’s scale and process innovation while mitigating the margin compression risks experienced in commodity consumer components.
