Adoption maturity and expansion dynamics for flexible substrates and electrode components in medical devices (CGM, neurostimulation, BCI)

Executive summary

- Market maturity varies sharply by segment. Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) is mainstream and structurally consolidated in North America and Europe, with strong reimbursement, mature regulatory practices (UDI, recalls/corrections), and entrenched integration pathways into automated insulin delivery (AID). Neurostimulation (e.g., spinal cord stimulation, deep brain stimulation) is in late-early to mid-scale adoption, with accelerating innovation (closed-loop approvals) and growing procedural throughput, but persistent device complexity, regulatory friction, and reimbursement heterogeneity. Brain–computer interfaces (BCI) remain pre-mainstream (early clinical feasibility), with small revenue bases, high technical uncertainty (e.g., electrode migration, biocompatibility), and heavy reliance on co-development among startups, academic centers, and specialized suppliers (U.S. Food & Drug Administration [FDA], 2024; FDA, 2025; Coherent Market Insights, 2024; Orion Market Research [OMR], 2026a; OMR, 2026b; CNBC, 2024; Global Times, 2026; Nature Portfolio, 2025; Sequenex, 2025).

- Implications for a new entrant with advanced flexible manufacturing (e.g., LG Innotek, with 10 μm photolithography, flexible FPCB, R2R): 
  - Defensible differentiation is strongest where fine features, reliability under flexion, and scalable roll-to-roll (R2R) economics intersect with unmet component capacity and performance requirements: next-gen flexible leads/arrays for neurostimulation; high-density multi-channel BCI arrays and cranial/epidural ribbons; and medical-grade flexible PCBs and interposers for wearable biosensors and CGM transmitter modules (Nature Portfolio, 2025; PCBonline, 2025; Technavio, 2025a; MCL, 2025).
  - In CGM “electrochemical glucose sensor” stacks, core sensor chemistry and electrode IP are closely held by OEMs; commoditized layers (flex-PCB, interconnects, shielding, patch-level electrodes, encapsulation) are more addressable for a newcomer, provided ISO 13485-grade QMS, IEC 60601/biocompatibility process libraries, and supplier qualification can be demonstrated (Sequenex, 2025; PCBonline, 2025).

- Adoption and expansion dynamics:
  - CGM and neurostimulation purchases are centralized, top-down, and governed by design controls and change-control requirements; once designed in and approved, component vendors are “locked” for the product lifecycle. Expansion is feasible across programs (new product introductions, NPIs) rather than within a launched SKU (Devices@FDA PMA supplements and labeling changes; IntuitionLabs, 2026; FDA, 2024; FDA, 2025).
  - BCI spreads via co-development and clinical feasibility pilots, with outsized opportunity to land early as a design partner and expand to adjacent arrays, leads, and cranial interposers as platforms mature (OMR, 2026b; CNBC, 2024; Nature Portfolio, 2025).

- Entry posture recommendation:
  - Enter as a medical FPCB/electrode component and CDMO partner, not as a system OEM. Lead with ISO 13485-certified flexible substrate/electrode processes, biocompatible thin-film stacks (e.g., Pt, IrOx, Au, Ti/Pt/Au on polyimide/silicone/PEEK), and R2R-enabled high-yield lithography at 10 μm features. 
  - “Land” in lower-risk subassemblies (CGM transmitter flex, patch electrode carriers) and in innovation-forward programs (closed-loop neurostimulation leads, BCI research-grade arrays), then “expand” across SKUs and platforms as a single supplier under vertically integrated CDMO models that OEMs increasingly prefer (Alira Health, 2026; Statifacts, 2025; Grand View Research, 2025; MTEC, 2026; Medical Design Briefs, 2026).

- Overall maturity assessment:
  - The market is mixed: CGM=mainstream and consolidated; Neurostimulation=expanding and innovation-rich; BCI=nascent but strategically important. Expansion-led go-to-market is viable if anchored in early design-in for NPIs and platform co-development; post-approval vendor swaps face high regulatory and operational friction (Devices@FDA, 2017; IntuitionLabs, 2026).

1. Scope, definitions, and relevance to LG Innotek

This report examines the adoption maturity and expansion dynamics for flexible substrate and electrode components across three medical device clusters—CGM sensors, neurostimulation, and BCI—first in North America and Europe, then with a global lens. The focus is on B2B component supply and contract development/manufacturing (CDMO) models addressing:

- Electrochemical glucose sensor electrode and CGM patch electrode technology
- Wearable biosensor flexible PCB (medical FPCB component supplier)
- Biocompatible thin-film electrode fabrication (e.g., Pt/IrOx/Au on polyimide)
- CoF-based medical device components and implantable flexible circuits
- High-density multi-channel neural electrodes and electrode arrays for neurostimulation/BCI

This analysis assumes LG Innotek (LGIT) has advanced chip-on-film (CoF), 10 μm photolithography, flexible FPCB, and roll-to-roll (R2R) production competencies, but no prior medical device footprint. The core question is whether those competencies are monetizable and defensible in CGM, neurostimulation, and BCI supply chains—and at what level (component, module, or integrated system).

2. Adoption-stage markers for analogous solutions

2.1 CGM (continuous glucose monitoring)

Observable mainstream markers in North America and Europe:

- Regulatory maturity and postmarket controls: multiple CGM platforms in the market; active recall/correction management with traceability through UDI; FDA recall and software correction actions for Abbott Libre 3 sensors (risk of inaccurate high readings) and Dexcom G7/ONE+ app alerting logic underscore both installed-base scale and mature surveillance infrastructure (FDA, 2024; FDA, 2025).
- Interoperability and ecosystem integration: CGM integration with AID systems is routine, and top-tier OEMs are expanding cross-company integrations (e.g., Medtronic using Abbott’s Instinct sensor in MiniMed 780G) (Drug Delivery Business News, 2025).
- Reimbursement and coverage expansion: North America leads with 38% global CGM share (2024), supported by strong reimbursement; expected CMS proposals to expand coverage into some non–insulin-treated type 2 populations further signal mainstream penetration into broader segments (Sequenex, 2025; Drug Delivery Business News, 2026).
- Implantable CGM acceptance: The FDA’s Eversense approval included robust clinical evidence and an 8–0 advisory vote that benefits outweigh risks; this marks regulatory openness to novel sensor formats, albeit with rigorous safety oversight (U.S. advisory acceptance and post-approval study commitments) (FDA, 2018).

Together, these are adoption markers of a mature category with ongoing incremental innovation and significant installed base.

2.2 Neurostimulation

- Approval momentum for advanced control: Medtronic’s Inceptiv closed-loop SCS gained FDA approval in April 2024 (real-time adaptive control), demonstrating regulator acceptance of more complex feedback-driven therapy (Coherent Market Insights, 2024).
- Market and clinical signals: Technavio estimates the implantable neurostimulation segment at $6.18B (2024), with North America contributing ~39.5% of growth; closed-loop systems reportedly improve personalization efficiency by ~30%, and regional reimbursement improvements (e.g., sacral neuromodulation) reduce wait times in Europe by up to 20% (Technavio, 2025a; 2025b; 2025c).
- Pipeline breadth: Broader neuro-implant growth (DBS, cranial, sacral, peripheral nerve, cochlear) is observed: OMR projects the neural implants market from $7.9B (2024) to $26.8B (2035), 11.8% CAGR, with enabling trends like 3D printing of patient-specific implants and less invasive surgeries (OMR, 2026a).

These markers place neurostimulation in a mid-maturity phase: expanding indications, rising device complexity, but still heterogeneous in adoption and procurement by therapy area.

2.3 Brain–computer interface (BCI)

- Early clinical trials and platform emergence: Precision Neuroscience and others are conducting human feasibility testing; Neuralink’s high-profile human trial reportedly faced electrode retraction issues—highlighting unsolved challenges in flexible but stretchable interfaces (CNBC, 2024; Global Times, 2026).
- Market size and trajectory: OMR estimates BCI at $280.2M (2025) growing to $865.0M by 2035 (12% CAGR), small in absolute terms relative to CGM or neurostimulation (OMR, 2026b).
- Academic and materials innovation: A recent Nature npj Biomedical Innovations review emphasizes flexible brain electronic sensors advancing wearable/implantable BCI modalities and reporting high-channel-count human brain electrodes—a marker of intense R&D, not yet industrialized at scale (Nature Portfolio, 2025).

BCI bears classic early-stage markers: low revenue base, high R&D intensity, technical risk, and co-development among startups, academia, and a handful of specialized suppliers.

3. Segment-by-segment adoption maturity differences

3.1 Geography

- North America
  - CGM: Largest market by share with strong reimbursement, smartphone integration, and established cloud ecosystems; buyer familiarity is high (Sequenex, 2025).
  - Neurostimulation: North America drives ~39.5% of growth; strong hospital infrastructure and adoption in chronic pain and epilepsy (Technavio, 2025a; 2025c).
  - BCI: Leading in clinical pilots via venture-backed and academic centers; early-stage procurement and R&D-centric purchasing (CNBC, 2024; OMR, 2026b).

- Europe
  - CGM: Widespread adoption in key countries; stringent MDR compliance and postmarket vigilance similar to FDA; AID adoption rising with pan-European variability (Sequenex, 2025).
  - Neurostimulation: Streamlined reimbursement for sacral neuromodulation shortened procedural queues up to 20% in established markets, indicating health system familiarity and operational experience (Technavio, 2025c).
  - BCI: Active academic centers; more conservative clinical adoption pace than NA, with robust ethics/regulatory oversight (Nature Portfolio, 2025).

- Asia-Pacific (for global expansion)
  - CGM: Fastest growth (China, India) with regulatory modernization and local manufacturing; rising digital health initiatives (Sequenex, 2025).
  - Neurostimulation & BCI: Growing investment but more variable reimbursement and early-stage platform activity.

3.2 Company size and buyer type

- Large OEMs (e.g., Abbott, Dexcom, Medtronic, Boston Scientific)
  - High familiarity with flexible medical electronics; integrate component suppliers under strict design controls (21 CFR 820 equivalents) and ISO 13485 supplier qualification; procurement is centralized and cautious (PCBonline, 2025; IntuitionLabs, 2026).
  - Strategic partnerships (e.g., Medtronic + Abbott Instinct) and supplier base rationalization trends reduce the number of approved suppliers—pushing preference for vertically integrated CDMOs (Drug Delivery Business News, 2025; Alira Health, 2026).

- Startups and platform companies (e.g., BCI startups, innovative neurostimulation)
  - Higher openness to co-development and new materials/processes; shorter decision cycles but greater regulatory support needs; often work through specialized CDMOs (OMR, 2026b; Alira Health, 2026).

- Academic and research institutions
  - Drive custom prototype arrays, research-grade electrodes, and early-in-human trials; require flexible, low-volume, high-precision fabrication and documentation suitable for IDE submissions (Nature Portfolio, 2025).

4. Adoption friction versus familiarity indicators

4.1 Indicators of buyer familiarity

- Standardization of medical PCB/FPCB requirements: ISO 13485-certified PCB suppliers and detailed regulatory expectations (FDA device regulation, IEC 60601 safety, ISO 10993 biocompatibility) reflect a mature understanding of quality and compliance in medical electronics (PCBonline, 2025; PCB Directory—U.S. ISO 13485 count).
- CGM product stewardship: UDI traceability and established recall/correction practices signal that buyers and regulators anticipate and manage lifecycle risks (FDA, 2024; FDA, 2025).
- Neurostimulation: Clinical facilities adopting closed-loop approaches and reimbursement improvements indicate established therapeutic roles and buyer confidence in device categories (Coherent Market Insights, 2024; Technavio, 2025c).

4.2 Friction and education needs

- Regulatory change-control and supplier qualification: Devices@FDA logs show frequent supplements even for labeling and process changes (e.g., Enlite PMA supplements S034–S038), highlighting path-dependent compliance and the friction of switching or modifying components post-approval (FDA Devices@FDA PMA index; Devices@FDA PMA labeling update).
- Rising enforcement on contract manufacturing oversight: FDA enforcement attention to quality agreements, data integrity, and change control is increasing; sponsors must ensure robust oversight of partners, creating barriers for new suppliers without an established medical QMS and digital traceability (IntuitionLabs, 2026).
- BCI materials and mechanics complexity: Reports of electrode migration and the need for stretchable, conformal interfaces show technical immaturity; buyers (startups, clinicians) require co-development and iterative validation (Global Times, 2026; Nature Portfolio, 2025).

Net: CGM and neurostimulation buyers are familiar with the category and its supply chain demands but impose high entry friction through supplier qualification, QMS expectations, and regulatory path-dependencies. BCI buyers understand needs but face unresolved technical risks, increasing the need for supplier co-development and education.

5. Typical adoption path patterns

5.1 CGM

- Top-down, platform-driven procurement with design-in at NPI: OEMs qualify suppliers for specific subassemblies (e.g., wearable biosensor flexible PCB, sensor electrodes, interconnects). Vendor changes after clinical validation trigger regulatory supplements or re-testing; therefore, OEMs tend to “land” a supplier during development and keep them for the lifecycle (Devices@FDA—supplements; IntuitionLabs, 2026).
- Ecosystem lock-ins: AID ecosystems create tie-ins between CGM and pump/controller firms; e.g., Medtronic integrating Abbott’s Instinct sensor. Such strategic partnerships both signal maturity and create closed gateways for component newcomers unless they enter as a qualified subsystem supplier to either party (Drug Delivery Business News, 2025).

5.2 Neurostimulation

- Co-development of electrodes/leads: Therapy performance depends on lead geometry, materials (Pt/IrOx), and mechanical properties; major OEMs partner closely with lead suppliers. New closed-loop platforms increase electrode/channel density and material requirements, favoring suppliers able to co-engineer arrays and flex interposers (Coherent Market Insights, 2024; Technavio, 2025a).
- Expansion through platform adjacency: After landing one lead/array program, expansion typically proceeds to adjacent indications (e.g., SCS to PNS; cranial to cortical arrays) rather than through mid-life component substitutions in existing SKUs.

5.3 BCI

- Bottom-up co-development: Early programs start as research-grade arrays for animal/human feasibility; suppliers iterate on materials, encapsulation, and density in collaboration with clinical teams. Expansion is tied to platform progression (IDE to pivotal) and spinouts, with opportunities to supply multiple startups and academic cores (Nature Portfolio, 2025; CNBC, 2024; OMR, 2026b).

6. Buying-behavior compatibility with expansion

- Consolidation and vertical integration: OEMs are shrinking approved supplier rosters and prefer vertically integrated CDMOs that can take on design-for-manufacture, materials qualification, and modular subassemblies under one roof—driven by speed-to-market, quality assurance, and supply chain resilience (Alira Health, 2026; Statifacts, 2025; Medical Design Briefs, 2026).
- Outsourcing growth supports supplier-led expansion across programs: The U.S. accounted for ~21.8% of global medical device outsourcing revenue in 2025; total market projected to grow at double-digit CAGRs through 2034—creating room for qualified newcomers to expand once they have won initial programs (Grand View Research, 2025; Statifacts, 2025; Meditech Insights, 2025; OrthoSpine News/DataM Intelligence, 2025).
- However, within-SKU upsell is constrained: Post-approval component changes face regulatory supplements and re-validation; therefore, the prudent expansion motion is cross-SKU/platform rather than within an approved product (Devices@FDA PMA supplements; IntuitionLabs, 2026).

7. Path dependencies that block expansion

- Regulatory design and change control:
  - Even labeling changes require PMA supplements (e.g., instructions to add second overtape for Medtronic Enlite sensor) (Devices@FDA PMA, 2017).
  - Process/manufacturer/sterilizer changes often necessitate supplements (Medtronic iPro2 Enlite S034–S038). Such path-dependencies make mid-life supplier changes costly and slow (Devices@FDA PMA index).
  - Manufacturing changes without appropriate notification have triggered FDA findings in drugs/devices; robust quality agreements and change control are mandatory (IntuitionLabs, 2026).

- Postmarket vigilance/UDI:
  - CGM recalls/corrections demonstrate the regulatory expectation for traceability and corrective action; suppliers must support UDI-linked traceability and field actions (FDA, 2024; FDA, 2025).

- Materials and biocompatibility libraries:
  - Changes to electrode metallurgy, polymer substrates, or encapsulants trigger new biocompatibility and sterilization validations (ISO 10993; IEC 60601 context). Suppliers without pre-qualified stacks face extended onboarding (PCBonline, 2025; NextPCB, 2025).

- Systems integration lock-in:
  - CGM + pump + controller ecosystems and proprietary algorithms (e.g., interoperable ACE pumps, iAGCs) create architectural barriers that channel component opportunities toward incumbents and pre-qualified CDMOs (Drug Delivery Business News, 2025; Devices@FDA regulatory categories).

- Institutional procurement and supplier consolidation:
  - OEMs are streamlining supplier lists and rewarding vertical integration, reducing room for single-capability vendors unless they arrive with turnkey CDMO value (Alira Health, 2026).

8. How LG Innotek’s competencies map to market needs

8.1 Where 10 μm lithography, flexible FPCB, and R2R add defensible value

- Neurostimulation leads and arrays:
  - Increasing channel counts and closed-loop sensing demand fine-feature conductor routing and electrode geometries on flexible substrates (polyimide/silicone). 10 μm photolithography and R2R can enable high-density, repeatable arrays for SCS/DBS/PNS and cranial epidural ribbons, with integrated shielding, via-in-pad, and plated through-holes on flex as required (Technavio, 2025a; Nature Portfolio, 2025).
  - Differentiators: tight registration, long-length flex harness processing, multi-layer flex routing, thin-film metallization stacks (Ti/Pt/Au; Pt/IrOx) with controlled impedance and adhesion; laser microvia and coverlay precision; encapsulation compatibility for chronic implants.

- BCI high-density multi-channel neural electrodes:
  - Research and early clinical platforms require rapidly iterated designs with ultra-fine features, low-profile interposers, and precise electrode sites. LGIT’s 10 μm capabilities and R2R can support both prototyping and scale-up, with potential to add stretchable interconnect geometries (serpentine, mesh) and hybrid thin-film/elastomer stacks to address motion and migration risks reported in the field (Nature Portfolio, 2025; Global Times, 2026).

- CGM subassemblies:
  - While electrochemical sensor IP is closely held, adjacent components—wearable biosensor flexible PCBs (patch interposers), transmitter flex/rigid-flex boards, shielding, and antennas—are addressable. R2R can improve unit economics and yield for high-volume CGM patches. The addition of medical-grade traceability and testing (e.g., IPC Class 3, 100% netlist, AOI/X-ray) positions LGIT credibly as a medical FPCB component supplier (Sequenex, 2025; SFCircuits, 2025; PCBonline, 2025).

8.2 Where differentiation is weaker or riskier

- Full electrochemical glucose sensors:
  - OEMs hold core chemistry/electrode-stack IP and have entrenched sensor suppliers and internal fabs. Breaking in at the sensor-electrode IP layer requires long co-development cycles and significant regulatory chemistry validation; this is a long-horizon play, better pursued via JV or as a specialized sub-process (plating/passivation) partner rather than as a standalone entrant (Sequenex, 2025; FDA, 2018).

- Integrated neurostimulation or BCI systems:
  - System-level IP (algorithms, firmware, therapy protocols, clinical evidence) is not aligned with LGIT’s substrate-level strengths; regulatory and clinical barriers are high. Competing with Medtronic/Boston Scientific/Neuralink as a system OEM is strategically misaligned.

10. Go-to-market (GTM) motions: land, expand, and risk controls

10.1 Land strategy

- Quality and compliance foundation
  - Implement and certify ISO 13485 QMS; align to 21 CFR 820 fundamentals; document IEC 60601 electrical safety considerations for medical electronics and ISO 10993/sterilization compatibility in materials libraries (PCBonline, 2025).
  - Build digital traceability (UDI-ready), quality agreements (QAA) templates, and risk-based supplier qualification programs aligned with heightened FDA oversight trends (IntuitionLabs, 2026; FDA, 2024).

- Offerings and proof points
  - Medical-grade flexible substrate and electrode process library: documentable thin-film stacks, adhesion/aging data, sterilization survival, and accelerated lifecycle testing.
  - Demonstrate IPC Class 3 builds (netlist, TDR), microvia reliability, and flex endurance; provide validated DFM/DFX toolkits for medical customers (SFCircuits, 2025; MCL, 2025).
  - Reference designs and R&D kits for neuro/BCI arrays (e.g., polyimide-based microelectrode arrays with customizable site geometry and density).

- Channel and ecosystem
  - Target strategic CDMO partnerships and OEMs seeking vertically integrated suppliers; supplier-base consolidation trends favor comprehensive offerings (Alira Health, 2026; Statifacts, 2025; Meditech Insights, 2025).
  - Engage academic medical centers and research consortia for BCI/neuro pilots; supply to clinical investigators accelerates visibility and design-ins (Nature Portfolio, 2025).
  - Present at MD&M West and similar venues with case studies on power/biocompatible flex integration and supply-chain resilience (Medical Design Briefs, 2026).

10.2 Expand strategy

- Cross-program/platform expansion vs in-SKU upsell
  - Given change-control friction, prioritize new NPIs and adjacent platforms (e.g., from SCS to PNS; from CGM transmitter flex to next-gen transceiver modules) rather than mid-life part substitutions (Devices@FDA PMA supplements; IntuitionLabs, 2026).

- Vertical integration levers
  - Add value through module assembly (cleanroom PCBA, bonding, encapsulation), test development (AOI, X-ray, burn-in), and sterilization logistics via qualified partners to align with OEM preference for fewer, broader-scope suppliers (Statifacts, 2025; Alira Health, 2026; MTEC, 2026).

- Regionalization and resilience
  - Offer dual-region capacity (NA/EU) and nearshoring options; post-pandemic medtech emphasizes multilocal, shock-resilient footprints (Medical Design Briefs, 2026; Alira Health, 2026).

10.3 Risk controls

- Quality agreements and change management
  - Codify change notification thresholds and validation responsibilities; anticipate PMA/510(k) supplement triggers for material/process shifts (IntuitionLabs, 2026; Devices@FDA PMA index).

- Postmarket readiness
  - Ensure UDI-compatible serialization and lot tracking to support OEM field actions (FDA, 2024; FDA, 2025).

- Materials governance
  - Maintain a controlled biocompatible materials database (ISO 10993 testing references; sterilization compatibility) to shorten onboarding and mitigate re-validation cycles (PCBonline, 2025; NextPCB, 2025).

13. Competitive landscape and positioning

Known competitors across segments include Jabil, Flex, Integer, Murata, and large OEMs with internal capacity (Abbott, Medtronic). Positioning for a new entrant:

- Differentiation axis:
  - Fine-feature (≤10 μm) lithography on flexible substrates at R2R scale.
  - Documented biocompatible thin-film stacks for chronic implants.
  - Vertically integrated CDMO services (DFM, flex routing co-design, cleanroom assembly, test, serialization) aligned to supplier-base consolidation trends (Alira Health, 2026; Statifacts, 2025; MTEC, 2026).

- Target buyer fit:
  - CGM OEMs and system integrators for transmitter flex and patch interposers; medtech startups needing wearable biosensor flex-PCBs; neurostimulation OEMs for electrode arrays/leads; BCI platform companies and academic centers for custom high-channel arrays (Sequenex, 2025; Technavio, 2025a; Nature Portfolio, 2025).

14. Roadmap: Technical and regulatory enablement

- Quality/regulatory:
  - ISO 13485 certification; 21 CFR 820-aligned design controls; IEC 60601 know-how; ISO 10993 material biocompatibility master files (PCBonline, 2025).
  - UDI-compatible traceability, eQMS, and digital batch records; establish robust quality agreements (IntuitionLabs, 2026).

- Process and materials:
  - Thin-film electrode stacks (Ti/Pt/Au; Pt/IrOx) on polyimide/silicone/PEEK; metallization adhesion and corrosion data; hermeticity and encapsulation strategies for implants.
  - Stretchable geometries for BCI/neuro (serpentine interconnects; hybrid elastomeric substrates) to reduce migration-induced failures noted in early reports (Nature Portfolio, 2025; Global Times, 2026).

- Manufacturing and test:
  - IPC Class 3 flex, laser microvias, impedance control; AOI, X-ray, netlist, TDR; cleanroom assembly; sterilization compatibility (EO, gamma) (SFCircuits, 2025; MCL, 2025; NextPCB, 2025).

- Business development:
  - Strategic alliance with a medtech CDMO to accelerate supplier qualification; parallel piloting with BCI/neuro startups and academic labs to seed future programs (Alira Health, 2026; MTEC, 2026).

15. Overall maturity assessment and GTM fit

- Overall maturity by segment:
  - CGM: Mainstream. Procurement is consolidated, regulatory oversight is rigorous, and ecosystems are integrated. Entry viable at component/module level with high compliance bar; expansion is cross-program (FDA, 2018; 2024; 2025; Sequenex, 2025; Drug Delivery Business News, 2025).
  - Neurostimulation: Late-early to mid-maturity. Approvals for closed-loop and growing indications expand the TAM. Co-development for electrode arrays/leads is the natural entry; expansion follows platform adjacencies (Coherent Market Insights, 2024; Technavio, 2025a; 2025c).
  - BCI: Early. R&D-led co-development dominates; technical risks and clinical pathways are evolving. Component supplier/CDMO roles with academic/startup partners best fit near term (Nature Portfolio, 2025; CNBC, 2024; OMR, 2026b).

- Fit of expansion-led GTM:
  - Expansion-led motions fit this market if they are:
    - Anchored in early NPI design-ins and platform co-development (rather than in-market sku swaps).
    - Executed through a vertically integrated CDMO value proposition that OEMs prefer under supplier consolidation trends.
    - Supported by a robust quality/regulatory foundation enabling OEMs to trust the supplier across multiple programs.
  - Attempting to expand within a single launched SKU is constrained by path dependencies (PMA/510(k) supplements, biocompatibility re-validation, ecosystem lock-ins). Therefore, the strategic expansion vector should be across the buyer’s roadmap (new CGM SKUs, new neuro indications, new BCI cohorts) and across multiple buyers in similar use-cases (Devices@FDA PMA; IntuitionLabs, 2026; Alira Health, 2026).

Conclusion

For a flexible-electrode/substrate specialist with 10 μm photolithography and R2R scale, the medical opportunity is defensible and monetizable—provided the entry is at the component/module CDMO level with medical-grade quality systems and co-development capability. Near-term revenue and credibility are most accessible in CGM subassemblies (wearable biosensor flex-PCBs, transmitter rigid-flex modules) and neurostimulation electrode arrays/leads, where fine-feature flex and biocompatible thin-film processes matter and procurement accepts qualified newcomers. BCI is a strategic, longer-horizon bet that rewards early co-development relationships.

The adoption landscape’s maturity (CGM=mainstream; neuro=expanding; BCI=nascent) implies land-and-expand is viable, but expansion must be cross-program rather than within an approved SKU. Success requires: ISO 13485 certification; rigorous change control and traceability; a validated materials/process library (ISO 10993/sterilization); and an integrated CDMO offering that aligns with OEMs’ supplier consolidation and resilience strategies. Entering as a systems OEM is not advised; component/module CDMO partnering yields better risk-adjusted returns and leverages LGIT’s core manufacturing strengths.
