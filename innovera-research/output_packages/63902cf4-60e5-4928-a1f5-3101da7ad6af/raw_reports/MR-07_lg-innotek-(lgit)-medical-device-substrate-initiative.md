Title: Buying Process, Budget Dynamics, and Pricing Landscape for Flexible Substrate and Electrode Components in Medical Devices (CGM, Neurostimulation, BCI)

Author: [Your Organization]

Date: March 12, 2026

Executive Summary

Medical wearables and implantables are converging on higher-density, miniaturized, and clinically validated sensing interfaces. Regulatory clarification around wellness-versus-medical devices and the first De Novo classifications for autonomous CGM platforms signal a maturing path for integrated wearable sensors, while brain–computer interfaces (BCIs) are scaling electrode densities and channels with thin-film, flexible microelectrode arrays. For a supplier such as LG Innotek (LGIT) with deep competencies in chip-on-flex (CoF), fine-feature photolithography (~10 µm), flexible PCBs, and roll-to-roll (R2R) processes, the strategic question is not whether capability exists, but where in the medical value chain that capability translates into defensible, monetizable differentiation.

Key findings

- Buying cycles are long and cross-functional. Complex OEM programs often take two to three years from evaluation to mass production in adjacent advanced-electronics verticals, and medical Class III programs add supplier qualification, biocompatibility, and FDA inspection layers that further extend timelines and broaden the decision-making unit (DMU) across R&D, Quality, Regulatory, Procurement, and executive operations leadership (Quanergy SEC filing; FDA QMSR guidance; ISO 10993 guidance) ([SEC, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794621/000119312522003213/d192398d424b3.htm); [FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility)).

- Regulatory forces are reshaping supplier scrutiny. FDA’s Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) alignment with ISO 13485 elevates risk-based expectations and explicitly allows inspection of supplier audit reports; for combination products, device-related QMSR expectations are layered atop drug or biologic CGMPs (FDLI; Hogan Lovells JDSupra) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Hogan Lovells, 2024](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-device-qmsr-includes-significant-5641492/)).

- Value differentiation must exceed consumer-grade lookalikes. FDA clarified the boundary between general wellness and regulated devices; medtech firms compete against lower-cost wearables by emphasizing clinical validation, accuracy under diverse and non-ideal conditions, and integration into clinical workflows. Segmenting portfolios across wellness and medical can build on-ramps to clinical adoption (Medical Design & Outsourcing) ([Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/fda-consumer-wearables-guidance-medical-devices/)).

- Demand vectors for flexible electrodes are strong. Market analysis projects flexible implantable electrode revenues growing to approximately $4.45 billion by 2035, fueled by high-density microelectrode arrays, biocompatible materials, and minimally invasive techniques—core to neurostimulation and BCI (The Business Research Company) ([TBRC, 2026](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/flexible-implantable-electrode-market-report)).

- Pricing anchors bifurcate. Commodity screen-printed electrodes on PET/PI substrates for research and early-stage prototypes sell for $0.06–$4+ per piece on marketplaces; in contrast, medical-grade, ISO 13485–controlled thin-film arrays and implantable assemblies are bespoke, with significant non-recurring engineering (NRE) and compliance costs (Alibaba; Allumcorp; Cirtec; Creanova) ([Alibaba, 2026a](https://www.alibaba.com/showroom/silk-screen-printed-electrodes.html); [Allumcorp, 2026](https://www.allumcorp.com/products/flexible-screen-printed-disposable-electrodes-flexible-biosensors-wearable-device-electrodes/); [Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Creanova, 2021](https://creanova.com/2021/07/29/what-you-should-know-about-non-recurring-engineering-costs-nre-for-your-medical-device-pcb/)).

- BCI electrode densities and integration are breaking thresholds. Subdural and cortical systems moving to tens of thousands of electrodes and 1,024 channels on flexible substrates demand 10 µm-class photolithography, thin-film processes, and high-reliability interconnects—precisely where LGIT’s CoF/R2R could be advantaged (Nature Biomedical Engineering; Bioengineer.org) ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w); [Bioengineer.org, 2025](https://bioengineer.org/revolutionary-65536-electrode-wireless-brain-computer-interface/)).

- The path to monetization for LGIT: lead as a component/CDMO partner, not a system OEM. Regulatory and clinical evidence barriers, plus entrenched OEMs in CGM and neurostimulation, argue for entering as an ISO 13485-certified component and co-development CDMO focused on high-density flexible electrode substrates and interposers, partnering with system integrators and medtech startups to embed capabilities early in design—while building biocompatibility and supplier-audit readiness per QMSR (Resonetics; Cirtec; FDA; Hogan Lovells) ([Resonetics, 2026](https://resonetics.com/services-capabilities/capabilities-products/sensor-technology/wearable-biosensors/); [Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [FDA, n.d.-a](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/basics-biocompatibility-information-needed-assessment-fda); [Hogan Lovells, 2024](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-device-qmsr-includes-significant-5641492/)).

The remainder of this report details the buying process and DMU, budget structure and commercial reality, and use cases, pricing, and selection dynamics for flexible substrate and electrode components across CGM, neurostimulation, and BCI in North America/Europe (with a view to global expansion).

A. Buying Process & Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

A1. Typical buying cycle length by segment

- Advanced OEM programs: two to three years from evaluations to mass production. In complex, safety-critical electronics procurement, proof-of-concept evaluations (EVTs) lead to RFQs, then 24–36 months of development/validation before mass production—an adjacent-industry benchmark that maps well to medtech’s development cadence (e.g., Quanergy’s SEC filing for automotive sensors) ([SEC, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794621/000119312522003213/d192398d424b3.htm)).

- Regulated medical devices elongate timelines via QMS and regulatory gates:
  - FDA PMA devices undergo pre-approval inspections, require valid scientific evidence, and now face the possibility of FDA inspecting supplier audit reports, increasing time and documentation required to qualify and onboard component suppliers (FDLI QMSR guidance) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/)).
  - For combination products, QMSR harmonization with ISO 13485 adds explicit, risk-based expectations, reshaping how FDA will inspect complaint handling, risk management, and supplier audits—factors that can stall decisions until suppliers meet these standards (Hogan Lovells JDSupra) ([Hogan Lovells, 2024](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-device-qmsr-includes-significant-5641492/)).

- Clinical and study timelines reinforce long arcs: CMS listings of approved IDE studies (across neuromodulation, cardiac, and neuro trials) illustrate multi-year clinical evaluation windows that, while not directly “buying cycles,” define when and how components can be locked and scaled; suppliers engaged pre-clinical can remain locked-in for the pivotal and commercialization phases (CMS IDE index) ([CMS, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coverage/investigational-device-exemption-ide-studies/approved)).

- Wearables vs medical devices: FDA’s clarified boundary between wellness and regulated devices means wellness-scope devices (e.g., Abbott Lingo) can move faster, while regulated CGM and neuromodulation devices must follow full device pathways, lengthening evaluation and supplier qualification cycles (Medical Design & Outsourcing) ([Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/fda-consumer-wearables-guidance-medical-devices/)).

A2. DMU size and role composition

A typical DMU for flexible electrode and substrate components in regulated medical devices includes:
- R&D/Engineering: Device design leads, sensor/electrode scientists, and systems engineers typically steward performance tradeoffs and design-in component technologies (Cirtec’s “design-through-manufacturing”; Nature BME thin-film arrays) ([Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w)).
- Quality & Supplier Quality: Assess QMS alignment (ISO 13485), complaint handling, supplier audits, and process validation; under the QMSR, FDA may inspect supplier audit reports, making Quality a powerful gatekeeper (FDLI) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/)).
- Regulatory Affairs: Ensures device- and material-level compliance (ISO 10993 biocompatibility plans, BEP/BER documentation), identifies additional endpoints for novel materials (Emergo UL; FDA biocompatibility center) ([Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-a](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/basics-biocompatibility-information-needed-assessment-fda)).
- Procurement/Sourcing: Manages AVL (approved vendor lists), RFQ, target-cost negotiation (tiered volume pricing), and cost transparency requirements (VentureOutsource RFQ guidelines; RFP best practices) ([VentureOutsource, n.d.-a](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/helpful-rfq-guidelines-before-negotiating-ems-manufacturer-contract-service-pricing-terms/); [VentureOutsource, n.d.-c](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/oem-rfp-best-practices-contract-electronic-manufacturing/)).
- Manufacturing/Operations: Ensures DFM (design for manufacturability), scalability, and transfer to production (I-Connect007 on vertical integration; Resonetics CDMO capabilities) ([I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex); [Resonetics, 2026](https://resonetics.com/services-capabilities/capabilities-products/sensor-technology/wearable-biosensors/)).
- Clinical/Medical Affairs (for implantables/BCI): Ensures clinical endpoints and risk profiles align with electrode performance and biocompatibility plans (FDA biocompatibility heuristics; ISO 10993 expectations) ([FDA, n.d.-b](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/biocompatibility-evaluation-endpoints-contact-duration-periods)).
- Executive sponsors: VP of R&D, VP/Head of Operations/Manufacturing, and CFO/GM for program-level capex/NRE approvals (Ivalua vendor selection process emphasizes executive scoring and RFP-to-contract flow) ([Ivalua, 2026](https://www.ivalua.com/blog/vendor-selection-process)).

A3. Typical initiator / champion titles

- Director/Senior Manager of R&D (sensors, electrodes, signal processing) and Principal Engineer/Scientist for CGM electrodes or neural interfaces commonly initiate evaluations, often spurred by performance gaps (density, SNR, biocompatibility) or integration constraints (CoF on flexible substrates) (Cirtec solution overview; Nature BME high-density arrays) ([Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w)).
- Product leaders for new platforms that straddle wellness-medical continuums (e.g., Abbott’s stratified portfolio including Lingo and Libre) can champion differentiated components to keep regulated devices ahead of consumer encroachment (Medical Design & Outsourcing) ([Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/fda-consumer-wearables-guidance-medical-devices/)).

A4. Typical evaluator / blocker titles

- Supplier Quality Engineering and Quality Assurance (QA): Under QMSR, supplier audit rigor increases; QA frequently blocks suppliers lacking ISO 13485 certification, inadequate CAPA/complaint systems, or insufficient process validation (FDLI; Hogan Lovells JDSupra) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Hogan Lovells, 2024](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-device-qmsr-includes-significant-5641492/)).
- Regulatory Affairs: Can halt selection if biocompatibility plans (ISO 10993-1) or test evidence are inadequate—particularly with novel materials or manufacturing (Emergo UL; FDA biocompatibility endpoints) ([Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-b](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/biocompatibility-evaluation-endpoints-contact-duration-periods)).
- Procurement/Supply Chain: Blocks when cost targets miss RFQ expectations or when BOM transparency, COO data, or tiered pricing are insufficient (VentureOutsource RFQ guidelines) ([VentureOutsource, n.d.-a](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/helpful-rfq-guidelines-before-negotiating-ems-manufacturer-contract-service-pricing-terms/)).

A5. Budget-holder and executive-approver titles

- Budget holders: Program General Managers; Directors of R&D (for NRE/prototyping) and VPs of Operations/Manufacturing (for scale-up and recurring component spend) typically control budgets, coordinated with Procurement (Ivalua vendor process; Creanova NRE categories for PCB and firmware/tooling) ([Ivalua, 2026](https://www.ivalua.com/blog/vendor-selection-process); [Creanova, 2021](https://creanova.com/2021/07/29/what-you-should-know-about-non-recurring-engineering-costs-nre-for-your-medical-device-pcb/)).
- Executive approvers: VP/Head of Quality/Regulatory (to greenlight risk/compliance exposure), CFO/COO or Business Unit head (for significant commitments; supplier changes can imply years-long commitments akin to adjacent automotive “design wins”) (FDLI; Luminar 10-K commentary on multi-year commitments for OEM programs) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Luminar, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1758057/000162828025015432/lazr-20241231.htm)).

A6. Approval chain stages and common failure points

Typical stages:
1) Technical discovery and feasibility samples (engineering/R&D) (Cirtec; Resonetics) ([Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Resonetics, 2026](https://resonetics.com/services-capabilities/capabilities-products/sensor-technology/wearable-biosensors/)).
2) Supplier qualification and QMS review (Quality, Supplier Quality): ISO 13485 verification; audit-readiness per QMSR (FDLI; Hogan Lovells JDSupra) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Hogan Lovells, 2024](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-device-qmsr-includes-significant-5641492/)).
3) Biocompatibility risk assessment and test planning (Regulatory) under ISO 10993, with added endpoints for novel materials (Emergo; FDA) ([Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-b](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/biocompatibility-evaluation-endpoints-contact-duration-periods)).
4) RFQ and cost-target negotiations (Procurement) with tiered volume pricing and BOM transparency expectations (VentureOutsource) ([VentureOutsource, n.d.-a](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/helpful-rfq-guidelines-before-negotiating-ems-manufacturer-contract-service-pricing-terms/)).
5) EVT/DVT builds and process capability proof (Manufacturing), including DFM, SPC, and vertical integration for adhesives/hydrogels and lamination for wearable patches (I-Connect007) ([I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex)).
6) Device-level clinical and regulatory submissions (Clinical/RA), where supplier processes may be cross-referenced; supplier changes late in cycle carry high risk (FDLI; CMS IDEs) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [CMS, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coverage/investigational-device-exemption-ide-studies/approved)).

Common failure points:
- QMSR/ISO 13485 gaps and non-auditability; FDA’s ability to inspect supplier audit reports raises stakes (FDLI) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/)).
- Inadequate biocompatibility dossiers for body-contacting materials or novel flexible substrates (Emergo UL; FDA) ([Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-a](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/basics-biocompatibility-information-needed-assessment-fda)).
- Cost or scale-risk misalignment versus RFQ targets and tiered pricing expectations (VentureOutsource) ([VentureOutsource, n.d.-a](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/helpful-rfq-guidelines-before-negotiating-ems-manufacturer-contract-service-pricing-terms/)).
- Inability to integrate vertically (adhesives, hydrogels, converting) for wearable patches, causing comfort/usability or yield issues (I-Connect007) ([I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex)).

B. Budget Structure & Commercial Reality

B7. Budget structure norms (OPEX vs CAPEX; recurring vs project-based)

- Recurring consumables and project-based NRE: CGM sensors are consumable wearables (e.g., press releases highlight integrated primary display wearables and modular reusable applicators, implying recurring sensor/adherent refills), while development of new sensor substrates involves NRE for electronics architecture, firmware, tooling, and machine programming (Trinity Biotech CGM+; Creanova) ([Trinity Biotech, 2025](https://trinitybiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Trinity-Biotech-Unveils-CGM-An-AI-Native-Platform-Targeting-the-260-Billion-AI-Wearable-Market.pdf); [Creanova, 2021](https://creanova.com/2021/07/29/what-you-should-know-about-non-recurring-engineering-costs-nre-for-your-medical-device-pcb/)).

- Supplier transparency and tiered volume pricing: Procurement expects BOM transparency, quarterly updates, and negotiated pricing by tiered volumes—indicating a mix of recurring unit cost, with structured pricing ladders, and upfront project-based costs (VentureOutsource) ([VentureOutsource, n.d.-a](https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/helpful-rfq-guidelines-before-negotiating-ems-manufacturer-contract-service-pricing-terms/)).

B8. Budget ownership norms by function

- NRE typically budgeted by R&D and program management; recurring per-piece costs controlled by Procurement/Operations with executive oversight. Vendor selection and RFP scoring follow a formal process across functions, with final negotiation and contract management under Procurement (Ivalua; Creanova) ([Ivalua, 2026](https://www.ivalua.com/blog/vendor-selection-process); [Creanova, 2021](https://creanova.com/2021/07/29/what-you-should-know-about-non-recurring-engineering-costs-nre-for-your-medical-device-pcb/)).

C. Use Cases, Pricing, and Selection Dynamics

C16. Dominant jobs-to-be-done by segment

- CGM electrode substrates:
  - Deliver accurate, continuous electrochemical glucose sensing using minimally invasive microneedles or skin-contact biosensors with stable batch-to-batch performance; integrate into compact patches with adhesives/hydrogels and often an integrated display (Biolinq; Allumcorp; I-Connect007) ([Biolinq, 2025](https://www.biolinq.com/news/biolinq-raises-58m-for-glucose-microsensor-pivotal-trial); [Allumcorp, 2026](https://www.allumcorp.com/products/flexible-screen-printed-disposable-electrodes-flexible-biosensors-wearable-device-electrodes/); [I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex)).
  - Support differentiation beyond consumer-grade “wellness” devices via clinical validation, accuracy in diverse/high-risk populations, and robust performance under non-ideal conditions (Medical Design & Outsourcing) ([Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/fda-consumer-wearables-guidance-medical-devices/)).

- Neurostimulation electrodes/interconnects:
  - Provide chronic, biocompatible interfaces with precise stimulation and long-term stability; hermetic packaging and feedthrough reliability are essential for IPGs and leads (Cirtec) ([Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci)).

- BCI thin-film electrode arrays:
  - Achieve high-density, minimally invasive cortical surface interfaces for multimodal neural decoding and stimulation; improve scalability in channel count and implantation speed while minimizing tissue damage—demanding micron-scale features and flexible thin films (Nature Biomedical Engineering) ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w)).
  - Integrate electrodes with CMOS processing on ultra-thin substrates (e.g., 50 μm) for high-bandwidth, wireless subdural systems—implying tight integration between electrode patterning, interconnect density, and packaging (Bioengineer.org overview of a Nature Electronics article) ([Bioengineer.org, 2025](https://bioengineer.org/revolutionary-65536-electrode-wireless-brain-computer-interface/)).

C17. Core vs nice-to-have use case split

- Core (must-have):
  - Compliance and safety: ISO 13485 QMS, biocompatibility per ISO 10993, supplier audit-readiness per QMSR (FDLI; Emergo; FDA) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-a](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/basics-biocompatibility-information-needed-assessment-fda)).
  - Performance reproducibility at scale: low batch-to-batch variability (<5% cited by specialty manufacturers) and robustness after bending for wearable electrodes (Allumcorp) ([Allumcorp, 2026](https://www.allumcorp.com/products/flexible-screen-printed-disposable-electrodes-flexible-biosensors-wearable-device-electrodes/)).
  - Manufacturing integration: verticalized converting (lamination, hydrogel placement), SMT to non-patient side, and scalable roll-to-roll printing to hit cost/yield (I-Connect007) ([I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex)).

- Nice-to-have (differentiators):
  - Multimodal sensor fusion and AI-native analytics in wearables (Trinity Biotech CGM+) and integration with primary displays (Biolinq) provide system-level differentiation but may not be component-level must-haves for substrate suppliers (Trinity Biotech; Biolinq) ([Trinity Biotech, 2025](https://trinitybiotech.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Trinity-Biotech-Unveils-CGM-An-AI-Native-Platform-Targeting-the-260-Billion-AI-Wearable-Market.pdf); [Biolinq, 2025](https://www.biolinq.com/news/biolinq-raises-58m-for-glucose-microsensor-pivotal-trial)).

Strategic recommendation: Enter as a component/CDMO hybrid, not a system OEM

Given the regulatory, clinical-evidence, and commercialization barriers in CGM and neuromodulation—and the nascency yet complexity of BCI—LGIT’s most defensible, monetizable path is to enter as a specialized, ISO 13485-certified component supplier with CDMO-style co-development services. The thesis:

- Where LGIT’s differentiation lands:
  - Fine-feature photolithography (~10 µm) and CoF on flexible substrates directly support high-density thin-film ECoG/BCI arrays and compact CGM electrode geometries, which are beyond commodity screen printing and increasingly central to clinical performance and miniaturization (Nature BME; Bioengineer.org; I-Connect007) ([Nature Biomedical Engineering, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01501-w); [Bioengineer.org, 2025](https://bioengineer.org/revolutionary-65536-electrode-wireless-brain-computer-interface/); [I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex)).
  - R2R capabilities align with CGM scale economics if electrode performance can be validated with low batch-to-batch variation; integrating converting operations (adhesives, hydrogels) or partnering with converters will be crucial for patch comfort and yield (I-Connect007; Allumcorp) ([I-Connect007, 2026](https://iconnect007.com/article/137147/designing-and-manufacturing-wearable-biosensors/137150/flex); [Allumcorp, 2026](https://www.allumcorp.com/products/flexible-screen-printed-disposable-electrodes-flexible-biosensors-wearable-device-electrodes/)).

  - Achieve ISO 13485 certification; build a biocompatible material library and a standardized ISO 10993 biological evaluation planning playbook; and prepare for FDA-inspectable supplier audits under QMSR—these unlock DMU blockers (FDLI; Emergo UL; FDA) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [Emergo by UL, 2025](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-updates-final-guidance-iso-10993-medical-device-biocompatibility); [FDA, n.d.-a](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/biocompatibility-assessment-resource-center/basics-biocompatibility-information-needed-assessment-fda)).

Comparative view: Component vs module vs integrated system entry

- Component (electrodes/substrates/interposers): Highest alignment to LGIT core; fastest path to revenue with CDMO-like support; defensible via fine-features/R2R and quality/regulatory posture. Competitive field is fragmented (CDMOs, specialty printers), but medical-grade thin-film density is a premium niche (Cirtec; Resonetics) ([Cirtec Medical, 2026](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Resonetics, 2026](https://resonetics.com/services-capabilities/capabilities-products/sensor-technology/wearable-biosensors/)).

- Module (sensor subassembly): In CGM, module-level is increasingly owned by OEMs due to IP in chemistry and algorithms; in BCI, arrays plus interposers may be a viable module offering in collaboration with system integrators. Regulatory liabilities and validation burden rise with module scope (Biolinq; Medical Design & Outsourcing) ([Biolinq, 2025](https://www.biolinq.com/news/biolinq-raises-58m-for-glucose-microsensor-pivotal-trial); [Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/fda-consumer-wearables-guidance-medical-devices/)).

- Integrated system (CGM/BCI device): Not recommended initially. This would thrust LGIT into clinical evidence generation, payer/reimbursement, and software/algorithm IP competition—domains where incumbents and focused startups are far ahead, and where FDA Class II/III or De Novo pathways would dramatically extend time-to-market and capital needs (FDLI; CMS IDEs) ([FDLI, 2025](https://www.fdli.org/2025/12/the-qmsr-transition-new-fda-guidance-and-their-impacts-on-active-implantable-medical-devices/); [CMS, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coverage/investigational-device-exemption-ide-studies/approved)).

Conclusion

LGIT’s core competencies—10 µm-class photolithography, CoF on flexible substrates, and R2R production—map directly to the most demanding and fastest-growing needs in flexible electrodes for medical devices: high-density thin-film arrays for BCI/neuromodulation and miniaturized, reliable CGM sensor substrates. The buying process is long and multidisciplinary; regulatory expectations (QMSR, ISO 13485/10993) raise the bar on supplier auditability and material safety; and procurement demands transparency and scalability. Entering as a specialized, ISO 13485-certified component supplier with CDMO-style co-development—rather than attempting integrated systems—offers the best balance of defensibility, time-to-revenue, and strategic leverage, with partnerships to complete vertical capabilities where needed. By aligning early with R&D champions, satisfying QA/RA auditors, and equipping Procurement with transparent RFQ responses, LGIT can turn substrate-level strengths into clinical-grade differentiation and durable supplier positions.
