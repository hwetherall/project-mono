Title: External Forces Creating Urgency for LG Innotek’s Entry into Medical-Grade Flexible Substrates for CGM, Neurostimulation, and BCI

Executive summary
LG Innotek (LGIT) has 10 µm‑class chip‑on‑film (CoF) and flexible substrate capabilities already proven at scale in IT/automotive. Multiple hard and soft external forces in MedTech are converging to make thin, flexible, high‑density electrode substrates and miniaturized sensor modules mission‑critical inputs for Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and emerging brain–computer interface (BCI) systems. The most urgent forcing functions are:

- EU MDR transitional deadlines and “significant change” constraints that prohibit material/sterilization shifts on legacy devices without MDR certification, pushing OEMs to secure stable, medical‑grade component supply without triggering design changes (hard deadline through 2027–2028; strongest in Europe) ([Casus Consulting, MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1; Specculo; Johner Institute](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/); [https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/)).

- A 2025 overhaul of ISO 10993‑1 biocompatibility (risk‑based, exposure‑driven, stronger justification) raising bar for implants and long‑term contacting wearables—driving material re‑justifications and new supplier qualifications (strong pressure from 2025 onward) ([Advisera 10993‑1:2025; Accuprec](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

- Intensifying competitive cycles in CGM (highly concentrated market, rapid iteration, quality scrutiny) and acceleration in neuro/BCI (first-in-human implants; Medtronic–Precision partnership) requiring miniaturization and advanced flexible electrodes (strong competitive pressure 2026–2031) ([Mordor Intelligence; Yahoo Finance; CNBC; Precision–Medtronic](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market); [https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html); [https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/02/neuralink-paradromics-human-implant.html](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/02/neuralink-paradromics-human-implant.html)).

- Heightened EU MDR supplier controls (NB surveillance and unannounced audits to supplier sites) increasing OEM urgency to on‑board demonstrably controlled, auditable component manufacturers (strong, ongoing) ([AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit)).

These forces create a window in the next 12–36 months for LGIT to position as a medical‑grade component/module supplier to large OEMs (primary target: Abbott; secondary: Medtronic, Dexcom, Senseonics, neuro/BCI innovators). The sharpest urgency peaks for EU‑market devices facing Article 120 transitions and for implants/long‑term contacting devices navigating ISO 10993‑1:2025 re‑evidence expectations. Given LGIT lacks medical certifications, biosignal IP, and finished‑device experience, the defensible play is Tier‑2/Tier‑1 component and subassembly supply (flex substrates, thin‑film electrode arrays, CoF interconnects, rigid‑flex HDI modules) under OEM technical files, backed by an MDR‑aligned QMS (ISO 13485) and material/biocompatibility documentation.

Scope and method
This report synthesizes regulatory guidance (EU MDR transitional provisions and MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1), standards updates (ISO 10993‑1:2025; EU harmonized standards movement), supplier control expectations (MDR audits), and market/competitive signals in CGM and neuro/BCI. It integrates multiple sources across regulatory, market, and manufacturing domains with a focus on North America and Europe (global extension). All statements are referenced via in‑text links; a consolidated references list appears at the end.

Market context: Why flexible, high‑density substrates are central now
- CGM: Rapid growth with concentrated competition (Abbott, Dexcom, Medtronic covering ~98% by revenue), with next‑gen platforms emphasizing thinner sensors, longer wear, factory calibration, multi‑analyte capability (e.g., glucose+ketone) and tight integration with pumps/apps. These require ultra‑miniaturized, reliable, high‑I/O flexible circuits and CoF interconnects to maintain skin comfort and accuracy at scale ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- Neurostimulation and BCI: Acceleration in clinical activity (e.g., Paradromics first human implant; Medtronic–Precision partnership pairing cortical electrode arrays with StealthStation navigation) is increasing demand for thin‑film, high‑channel‑count electrode arrays and dense implant interconnects, all of which depend on medical‑grade flexible substrates with biocompatible materials and hermetic interfaces ([CNBC; Yahoo Finance; Precision–Medtronic post](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/02/neuralink-paradromics-human-implant.html); [https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

- Manufacturing/technology: HDI flex and rigid‑flex processes (sub‑50 µm lines, stacked micro‑vias, mSAP) are becoming enabling infrastructure for miniaturized wearables/implants. Specialized vendors document fine‑line, dynamic‑flex, via‑in‑pad and micro‑via copper‑fill capabilities that map well to medical packaging constraints, suggesting supply can translate if qualified under medical QMS and material regimes ([PICA MFG](https://picamfg.com/expertise/fineline-circuit-hdi); [Hemeixin](https://www.hemeixinpcb.com/company/news/what-is-high-density-interconnect-flexible-circuits.html)).

2) Trend urgency: Is the problem getting worse?
- Regulatory tightening over time:
  - EU MDR transition extensions split by risk class now culminate in 2027/2028, but rely on stricter conditions (MDR‑compliant QMS, NB agreements) and removal of “sell‑off” deadlines—meaning continued surveillance pressure rather than relief. The “no significant change” barrier persists throughout, constraining legacy devices and increasing the cost of supplier/material change management over time ([Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/)).

  - ISO 10993‑1:2025 elevates expectations for exposure‑based justifications and tight linkage to risk management (ISO 14971). Notified Bodies increasingly flag generic or non‑traceable biocompatibility rationales, leading to delays, repeat testing, or redesign. This makes each materials change riskier and more expensive year‑over‑year as the new paradigm takes hold ([Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [Accuprec](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

  - Harmonized standards get refreshed (sterilization, clinical investigation, blood–device interaction), ratcheting up technical baselines in EU submissions; while legacy devices might defer, any significant change or MDR‑bound product family must align, increasing compliance uplift costs over 2026–2028 ([LinkedIn summary of Decision (EU) 2026/193)](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bioexcelife_eu-update-2026-activity-7426611438040875008-WG6d)).

- Competitive intensity and quality scrutiny:
  - CGM: Growth >15% CAGR (2026–2031) alongside concentrated market power accelerates miniaturization and multi‑sensor capability, with OEMs likely to refresh hardware every 18–36 months to defend share and pricing. FDA enforcement (e.g., Dexcom warning letter Feb 2024) creates acute remediation windows during which OEMs may re‑evaluate critical suppliers and processes to meet updated quality expectations ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

  - Neuro/BCI: More early‑clinical demonstrations translate to OEM development programs maturing from feasibility to scale‑up, prioritizing suppliers who can deliver chronic‑implant‑ready electrode arrays, dense interconnects, and sterile, hermetic packaging that survives fatigue and fluid exposure—raising the bar year‑by‑year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/02/neuralink-paradromics-human-implant.html); [Yahoo Finance–Medtronic/Precision](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

- Supplier audit pressure:
  - EU MDR surveillance and unannounced audits of critical suppliers are expected to expand in volume during the extended transition. Manufacturers are compelled to show real control, not just paper compliance; weak supplier systems raise the risk of NB findings. This elevates urgency to qualify suppliers with mature, auditable processes (ISO 13485 alignment, controlled changes, traceability) ([AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit)).

Overall, the trajectory points to growing difficulty and cost in making any material/sterilization change, coupled with rising expectations for miniaturization and chronic reliability in neuro/BCI and long‑wear CGM—an urgency trend that intensifies through 2028.

3) Trigger events causing organizations to seek solutions
Observable triggers that initiate urgent supplier searches or redesigns include:

- “Significant change” determinations under MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1
  - What: A planned change is classified significant (e.g., implant material change; sterilization method change), ending legacy status and forcing an MDR route.
  - Why action: OEMs must either avoid the change, or accelerate MDR submissions with updated technical documentation—often requiring new or better‑qualified material/component suppliers to meet current standards (10993‑1:2025, updated sterilization norms).
  - Predictability: Medium; depends on internal change pipeline; once scoped, the flowcharts can predict outcome early.
  - Frequency: Increasing as products age and supply chains evolve through 2027/2028 ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

- ISO 10993‑1:2025 biocompatibility gap findings by NBs
  - What: NB reviews flag insufficient exposure‑based justification, chemistry, or toxicological risk assessment for materials in long‑term contacting devices.
  - Why action: Delays/deficiencies force OEMs to augment chemistry/toxicology datasets or substitute materials, often initiating supplier requalification for flexible substrates, adhesives, or encapsulants.
  - Predictability: Medium; triggered during MDR submissions or surveillance when files are refreshed.
  - Frequency: Growing as new standard expectations diffuse through reviews ([Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [Accuprec](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

- Quality enforcement events (e.g., warning letters, field actions)
  - What: FDA warning letters or NB nonconformities focused on manufacturing controls or supplier oversight (e.g., Dexcom in 2024).
  - Why action: OEMs enter remediation programs that often include supplier audits, process revalidations, and—at times—supplier diversification or replacement for critical components like flex circuits and sensor substrates.
  - Predictability: Low; outcomes of inspections and surveillance.
  - Frequency: Episodic but impactful; rate increases with overall regulatory scrutiny ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- Partnership/roadmap acceleration
  - What: Strategic partnerships (e.g., Medtronic–Precision) or competitive launches compel OEMs to accelerate development of high‑density electrode arrays and fine‑pitch interconnects.
  - Why action: Compressed timelines require suppliers with production‑ready HDI flex, CoF, and thin‑film electrode manufacturing capacity that can pass medical audits.
  - Predictability: Medium; visible via press/analyst coverage and R&D pipelines.
  - Frequency: Increasing with neuro/BCI commercialization signals ([Yahoo Finance–Medtronic/Precision](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

- Sterilization supply shifts or regulatory drivers
  - What: Changes in sterilization availability or standard updates (e.g., revalidating moist heat per ISO 17665:2024) prompt method reassessments.
  - Why action: Under MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1, a terminal sterilization change is significant, often forcing MDR and supplier reassessment for material compatibility (e.g., radiation tolerance).
  - Predictability: Medium; observable through CMO or standardization announcements.
  - Frequency: Moderate; increases with standards refresh cycles ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/); [LinkedIn standards update](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bioexcelife_eu-update-2026-activity-7426611438040875008-WG6d)).

- Notified Body capacity and change of NB
  - What: OEMs must ensure continued NB surveillance responsibility (Article 120(3e)) and sometimes change NBs; incoming NBs may reinterpret supplier control sufficiency.
  - Why action: New NB may require tighter supplier qualification; OEMs may seek suppliers with stronger audit readiness (ISO 13485, design transfer discipline).
  - Predictability: Medium; linked to NB designation status and commercial choices.
  - Frequency: Episodic through the transition window ([Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/)).

4) Budget and procurement timing
- Fiscal cycles and planning windows
  - Large OEMs like Medtronic report by fiscal years offset from calendar (e.g., “fiscal 2025” Q2 referenced in Nov 2024 updates), implying annual planning/budgeting cycles that may close in late spring for the next fiscal year; operational adjustments (e.g., remediation, supplier qualification) are executed throughout the year as needed ([Medtronic press releases timeline](https://news.medtronic.com/index.php?s=20322&query=medtronic+supplies&submit=Search&l=100)).

  - For EU MDR‑impacted portfolios, procurement related to MDR submissions (V&V testing, supplier qualifications) is gated by NB dossier schedules and program gates. Material/supplier changes that would be “significant” tend to be deferred unless part of an MDR update plan—concentrating procurement around MDR project timelines (now through 2028) ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/); [Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/)).

- Supplier qualification and audit cadence
  - MDR surveillance and potential unannounced audits extend to critical suppliers; OEMs typically perform risk‑based supplier audits annually or biennially. Procurement awards for critical components (e.g., sensor flex, electrode arrays) often require pre‑award quality audits and pilot PPAP‑like validations that add 6–12 months lead time before full production—schedules that align with clinical build windows and design verification phases ([AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit)).

- Event‑driven spend
  - Remediation (post‑finding) and “significant change” determinations create off‑cycle budgets authorized at executive levels due to regulatory risk. These events compress qualification schedules and favor suppliers who can demonstrate audit‑ready systems and prior medical device experience (or partner with established medical EMS/CMOs) ([AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit); [Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

5) Urgency synthesis
- Overall urgency: High in EU portfolios and in implant/long‑term contacting devices globally; moderate–high in U.S.‑only CGM portfolios due to competitive and quality pressures.

- Strongest forcing function: EU MDR Article 120 transitional pathway combined with MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1 “significant change” constraints. These create a paradox: OEMs must advance to MDR, but cannot safely make certain supplier/material changes on legacy devices without triggering MDR; hence, new suppliers must be on‑boarded in the context of MDR submissions or for new platforms—raising the premium on medical‑grade, audit‑ready component partners now ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/); [Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/)).

- When urgency peaks: 2026–2028 as legacy device families approach EU MDR endpoints and as ISO 10993‑1:2025 expectations fully infuse NB reviews for implants and long‑term contacting devices. Competitive urgency in CGM remains elevated 2026–2031 due to concentrated rivalry and iterative launches ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- Segments most acutely affected:
  - EU‑market neurostimulation and BCI implants (material/sterilization changes are often “significant”; ISO 10993‑1:2025 is consequential).
  - EU‑market CGM sensors with long wear and skin contact (biocompatibility expectations rising; MDR transition management).
  - OEMs with active quality remediation (supplier controls in spotlight).

Regulatory deadlines and enforcement reality check
- EU MDR (hard dates and conditions)
  - Legacy device transition extended: Class III/IIb implantables (non‑WET) to 31 Dec 2027; IIb/IIa/Is/m and devices newly requiring NB involvement to 31 Dec 2028; contingent on MDR‑compliant QMS by 26 May 2024, NB application by 26 May 2024, contract by 26 Sept 2024; “sell‑off” removal allows continued availability but not relief from surveillance ([Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [Clin R](https://clin-r.com/new-eu-mdr-deadlines/); [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/)).

  - MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1: Flowcharts operationalize “significant change,” and cited examples underscore that implant materials (>30 days) and sterilization method changes are significant. A “significant” outcome ends legacy‑device allowance under Article 120 (hard effect) ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

- ISO 13485 (no external cut‑off date, but MDR‑QMS expectation):
  - No global “deadline” to be ISO 13485 certified; however, MDR expects manufacturers to operate an MDR‑compliant QMS (Article 10(9)), and extended transition explicitly required MDR‑compliant QMS by 26 May 2024—driving OEMs to impose ISO 13485‑like controls on critical suppliers and to anticipate NB audits that can include supplier sites ([Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit)).

- FDA 510(k)/PMA deadlines
  - No fixed “enforcement date” by which devices must obtain a 510(k) or PMA—timelines are per product submission. However, FDA has moved toward eSTAR electronic submissions and ongoing modernization; the 2026 environment emphasizes quality and completeness of documentation rather than calendar deadlines ([MedTech Intelligence](https://medtechintelligence.com/feature_article/the-510k-pathway-in-2026-navigating-a-shifting-regulatory-and-political-landscape-for-medical-devices/)).

- ISO 10993‑1:2025
  - Standard revision in 2025 does not set a hard legal deadline, but regulators/NBs increasingly expect alignment for new MDR submissions and significant changes; consequently, OEMs will pressure suppliers for enhanced material/chemistry data and risk justifications to meet this expectation ([Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [Accuprec](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

Implications for LG Innotek’s entry wedge
Given the urgency landscape and LGIT’s constraints (no medical certifications yet, no biosignal IP, no finished device history), the defensible positions in the supply chain are:

- Tier‑2/Tier‑1 medical‑grade component and subassembly supplier
  - What: Supply flexible circuits (dynamic‑flex HDI; rigid‑flex), CoF interconnects, and thin‑film electrode arrays for CGM transmitters/patches, sensor interposers, implant leads/interposers, and BCI cortical arrays under OEM design control (OEM technical files).
  - Why viable now: MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1 clarifies that manufacturing/organizational changes are not generally “significant,” allowing OEMs to qualify new manufacturing sources so long as materials and sterilization claims are controlled; however, for implants/long‑term devices, LGIT must commit to material stacks and process controls that fit the OEM’s existing biocompatibility and sterilization envelopes to avoid triggering a “significant change” ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

- Fast‑track medical QMS and audit readiness
  - Action: Implement ISO 13485:2016+A11 QMS aligned to MDR supplier expectations, with robust purchasing controls, change control, DFM/DFX for medical assemblies, and supplier notification processes. Expect NB‑linked audits and unannounced visits via OEMs ([AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13485-audit); [Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/)).

- Material evidence packages aligned to ISO 10993‑1:2025
  - Action: Build exposure‑based biological evaluation rationales for substrate layers, adhesives, coverlays, and metallization likely to contact tissue/skin or to be part of implants. Provide chemistry characterization and toxicological risk assessments that OEMs can incorporate directly into BEP/BER—with traceability to ISO 14971 risk controls ([Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [Accuprec](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

- Partner with medical EMS/implant packaging specialists
  - Action: For implantable subassemblies (e.g., BCI/neurostim electrodes and interconnects), partner with established medical EMS/ODMs offering hermetic packaging, feedthroughs, and sterilization validation. This reduces time to pass OEM/NB scrutiny and positions LGIT as a manufacturable component platform rather than a regulatory bottleneck ([Precision–Medtronic partnership context](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

- Prioritize programs where urgency is highest
  - EU‑bound implant portfolios mid‑transition (2026–2028) and CGM OEMs in remediation/rapid iteration cycles are most likely to fund supplier transitions if audit‑ready capacity and material evidence are available. Abbott (Diabetes; Neuro; Cardiac), Medtronic (Diabetes; Neuroscience), Dexcom (quality remediation context), and emerging BCI players (Paradromics, Precision Neuroscience partners) are near‑term targets ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/02/neuralink-paradromics-human-implant.html); [Yahoo Finance–Medtronic/Precision](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

Regulatory deadlines summary (requested items)

- ISO 10993‑1:2025
  - No “deadline,” but revised in 2025. Regulators and NBs already expect alignment in new MDR/510(k) dossiers and in significant changes, emphasizing risk‑based, exposure‑driven justifications, chemical characterization, and toxicological risk assessment ([Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-10993-1-2025-what-has-changed/); [Accuprec](https://accuprec.com/fda-eu-mdr-biocompatibility-expectations-global-cro/)).

- ISO 13485
  - No fixed enforcement date. However, EU MDR extended transition requires manufacturers to operate an MDR‑compliant QMS (Article 10(9)) by 26 May 2024 to benefit from extensions; NBs audit supplier controls and may visit supplier sites, making ISO 13485 certification de facto a strong requirement for critical component vendors ([Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [AMREP](https://amrepinspect.com/blog/mdr-iso-13585-audit)).

- FDA 510(k) and PMA
  - No external “deadline”; timelines are submission‑specific. FDA’s eSTAR program and modernized expectations increase documentation rigor, but do not set calendar cut‑offs ([MedTech Intelligence](https://medtechintelligence.com/feature_article/the-510k-pathway-in-2026-navigating-a-shifting-regulatory-and-political-landscape-for-medical-devices/)).

- EU MDR
  - Legacy device deadlines: 31 Dec 2027 (Class III/IIb implantables non‑WET) and 31 Dec 2028 (other classes), contingent on MDR‑compliant QMS by 26 May 2024, NB application by 26 May 2024, and contract by 26 Sept 2024; “no significant change” remains binding. Surveillance responsibilities clarified under Article 120(3e). Sell‑off period removed ([Specculo](https://specculo.com/eu-mdr-transitional-amendments/); [Clin R](https://clin-r.com/new-eu-mdr-deadlines/); [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/transition-periods-mdr/); [Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

Concrete opinion
The external environment is creating real, time‑bounded urgency for OEMs to (1) stabilize EU legacy portfolios under Article 120 constraints while (2) readying MDR filings and (3) meeting elevated biocompatibility and supplier‑control expectations. At the same time, CGM and neuro/BCI competition is compressing technology cycles toward ultra‑miniaturized, high‑density sensor/electrode platforms. This combination favors medical‑grade component suppliers who can:

- Provide fine‑line HDI flex/rigid‑flex and CoF with audit‑ready QMS and robust change control;
- Deliver pre‑built material evidence packages aligned to ISO 10993‑1:2025 to minimize OEM test burden;
- Fit within OEM technical files without forcing “significant change”—or, where MDR filings are already planned, accelerate time‑to‑compliance with ready‑to‑audit manufacturing.

LGIT’s capabilities are directly relevant. The gating factor is not technology, but regulatory readiness and integration into OEM quality and regulatory workflows. If LGIT can stand up ISO 13485 rapidly, lock biocompatible material stacks, and partner for implant packaging/sterilization where needed, the near‑term addressable opportunity is substantial—particularly with Abbott (Diabetes CGM; Neuro/Cardiac leads and arrays) and with Medtronic and emerging BCI players engaged in active development. The urgency window is strongest in Europe through 2028 and globally for implants/long‑term contacting devices adapting to ISO 10993‑1:2025.

Appendix: Additional structured insights

A. Representative “significant change” examples impacting component choices (per MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1)
- New sensor working principle (significant)
- Change of terminal sterilization method (e.g., EtO → Gamma; significant)
- Material or substance change for implants (>30‑day contact) or absorbed surgically invasive device (significant)
- Removal of design/development input (can be significant depending on flowcharts)
- New user interface modality (e.g., keyboard → touchscreen; can be significant depending on context)
These examples reinforce that substrate/material/sterilization choices for implants and long‑term wearables are locked unless part of an MDR program, elevating the value of suppliers who can deliver within existing validated envelopes or who can support MDR updates with complete evidence ([Casus/MDCG 2020‑3 Rev.1](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2020-3-rev-1-signifcant-changes-mdr/)).

B. Why miniaturization suppliers matter now
- CGM systems are converging toward smaller, longer‑wear patches with integrated analytics and multi‑analyte sensing—requiring sub‑50 µm traces, stacked micro‑vias, and reliable dynamic flex for comfort and adherence ([PICA MFG](https://picamfg.com/expertise/fineline-circuit-hdi)).
- Neuro/BCI requires high‑channel‑count thin‑film arrays and dense interconnects that can survive chronic implantation and fatigue—an area where flexible electrode substrate manufacturing prowess is critical, particularly when paired with hermetic packaging partners ([Yahoo Finance–Medtronic/Precision](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/medtronic-partners-precision-neuroscience-develop-180239671.html)).

C. Practical supplier entry sequencing for LGIT
- Phase 1 (0–12 months): ISO 13485 implementation; pilot builds for non‑implant CGM modules (transmitter flexible circuits, interposers) under OEM audits; material evidence packages for skin‑contact class; supplier change‑notification governance aligned to OEM SOPs.
- Phase 2 (12–24 months): Long‑wear CGM sensor interposers and flex tails with skin/subdermal contact; expanded biocompatibility dossiers; co‑validation with OEM labs to satisfy 10993‑1:2025 expectations.
- Phase 3 (24–36 months): Neuro/BCI thin‑film electrode arrays and implant interposers in partnership with medical EMS/hermetic packaging specialists; sterilization compatibility statements; chronic reliability data; alignment with MDR submission schedules for EU products.

By aligning investments to these external forcing functions and timing windows, LGIT can convert urgency into design wins consistent with the venture’s 3‑year ramp horizon—conditional on rapid QMS maturation and targeted partnerships.
