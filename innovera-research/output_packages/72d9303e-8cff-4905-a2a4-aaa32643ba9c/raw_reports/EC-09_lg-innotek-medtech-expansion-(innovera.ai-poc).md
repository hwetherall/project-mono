Advanced Medical Device Substrates, Flexible Circuit Components, and Electrode Modules for Wearable and Implantable Medical Devices: Analyst Coverage, Market Narrative, and Strategic Entry Options for LG Innotek (LGIT)

Executive summary

LG Innotek (LGIT) brings proven 10 µm–class chip‑on‑film (CoF) and fine‑pattern flexible PCB (FPCB) manufacturing used in IT and automotive to a medtech opportunity where thin, flexible, high‑density electrode substrates are the core enablers of next‑gen continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), neurostimulation, and brain‑computer interface (BCI) systems. Over the last 24 months, industry coverage has been strongest for end‑markets (CGM, BCI) and enabling stacks (thin‑film electrodes, HDI/rigid‑flex PCBs), with less direct coverage of “advanced medical device substrate modules” as a distinct analyst segment. That absence suggests a component‑level opportunity not yet carved out by major analyst taxonomies—but validated by converging demand signals across CGM scale‑up, neuromodulation/BCI R&D-to-commercial transitions, and flexible electronics supply chains.

Key findings

- Analyst consensus: CGM is a high‑growth, fast‑maturing market (low‑teens to high‑teens CAGRs, top‑five vendors ~78% share), increasingly patch‑based and integrated with AI/closed‑loop insulin delivery, which structurally expands demand for flexible substrates, printed electrodes, and smart patch modules. BCI and neurostimulation show accelerating clinical and commercialization momentum but remain earlier in maturity, with growing use of thin‑film, high‑density, biocompatible electrode arrays and rigid‑flex interconnects as critical bottlenecks and differentiators ([Congruence Market Insights, 2026–2033](https://www.congruencemarketinsights.com/report/continuous-glucose-monitor-(cgm)-market); [Mordor Intelligence, 2025–2031](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market); [Mordor Intelligence, 2025–2031 BCI](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/brain-computer-interface-market)).

- Enabling technologies: Thin‑film electrodes are forecast to approach ~$0.97B by 2030 (11.3% CAGR), with healthcare and flexible electronics as growth engines; achieving long‑term stability and biocompatibility is called out as the chief challenge. HDI/rigid‑flex PCB markets (including medical) grow high‑single/low‑double digits on miniaturization and 5G/AI device complexity—supportive for LGIT’s substrate heritage and capacity to meet fine‑line, high‑density requirements ([Research & Markets via Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/970-mn-thin-film-electrode-090700630.html); [Research & Markets HDI, 2026](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5939868/high-density-interconnect-market-report)).

- Conferences and ecosystem: Diabetes and sensors conferences (ADA, ATTD, DTC, Sensors Converge, HIMSS, MEDICA, Arab Health) increasingly feature CGM, AID, and wearable sensor platforms; exhibitors include Abbott, Dexcom, Medtronic, Insulet, Senseonics—validating continued scale‑up in patches, adhesives, and sensor interconnects. BCI/neuro events and regulatory milestones (e.g., Synchron, INBRAIN, Precision Neuroscience) signal rising demand for high‑density flexible arrays and implantable interconnects ([ADA DTC 2024 exhibitors](https://dtc2024.eventscribe.net/SearchByExpoCompany.asp?pfp=BrowseByCompany); [Sensors Converge 2026 exhibitors](https://sensors26.smallworldlabs.com/exhibitors); [Fortune Business Insights BCI, 2023](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/brain-computer-interface-market-105811)).

- Market gaps suited to LGIT: Near‑term opportunities exist in non‑implantable, medical‑grade skin‑patch interconnects and electrode modules for CGM and neuromodulation accessories (lower certification hurdles); medium‑term in high‑density surface ECoG/epidermal arrays and rigid‑flex harnesses as subcomponents to implant OEMs; long‑term in co‑developed implantable thin‑film electrode arrays—provided LGIT invests in ISO 13485 QMS, biocompatible stacks, and clinical‑grade traceability.

- Strategic thesis: LGIT can defensibly enter as a tier‑1/2 component and module supplier—leveraging its CoF/fine‑line manufacturing, HDI/rigid‑flex design for reliability, and automated yield to deliver medicalized FPCs, electrode laminates, and smart‑patch interposers—without owning biosignal algorithms or finished devices. Partnerships with ISO 13485 converters, adhesive specialists, and electrode IP holders can fill gaps while LGIT builds a staged regulatory posture.

Introduction and scope

LG Innotek’s (LGIT) core assets—10 µm‑class CoF, fine‑pattern photolithography, and flexible printed circuit manufacturing—map naturally to the performance and form‑factor constraints of modern wearables and implantables. In CGM, thin, flexible interconnects, electrode laminates, and skin‑conformable modules underpin accuracy and wear duration. In neurostimulation and BCI, high‑density thin‑film electrode arrays on polyimide/silicone with biocompatible metallization and long‑term stability are gating components. The challenge is to define where LGIT can enter the value chain—given initial absence of medical certifications, biosignal IP, and finished‑device experience—while exploiting component‑level scale and quality advantages.

This report synthesizes coverage from the last 24 months across analyst houses (where available), market research, peer‑reviewed reviews, conference listings, manufacturing capability notes, and company news to frame the opportunity landscape and recommend a staged entry path.

2) Market narrative: Consensus, divergences, and maturity

Consensus points

- CGM scaling and diversification: Analysts converge on strong CGM growth (CAGR ~15–19%), propelled by sensor miniaturization, extended wear, AI analytics, integration with Automated Insulin Delivery (AID), and expanding reimbursement/OTC access. This widens the TAM beyond insulin‑intensive users into preventive/athletic use, structurally increasing demand for wear‑durable, skin‑friendly, flexible substrates, and electrode modules. Top 5 hold ~78% share, but the ecosystem around adhesives, patches, interconnects, and accessory modules is broadening ([HTFMarketInsights, 2025](https://www.htfmarketinsights.com/report/4362839-continuous-glucose-monitors-cgms-market); [Congruence, 2025–26](https://www.congruencemarketinsights.com/report/continuous-glucose-monitor-(cgm)-market); [Mordor, 2025–31](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- Enabling component momentum: Thin‑film electrode markets (including biosensors) grow ~11% CAGR with healthcare and flexible electronics demand; core technical risks remain electrode stability, biofouling, and long‑term biocompatibility—precisely where high‑quality thin‑film processing and materials stacks differentiate suppliers ([Research & Markets via Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/970-mn-thin-film-electrode-090700630.html)).

- BCI/neurostimulation trajectory: BCI is advancing from R&D toward clinical adoption, with regulatory milestones (e.g., Synchron Apple Vision Pro control; INBRAIN FDA BDD; Precision Neuroscience FDA clearance) and increased clinical trial activity. Non‑invasive dominates near‑term shipments; implantables (cortical and depth) are scaling through trials, emphasizing high‑density, flexible, biocompatible electrode arrays and robust interconnects ([Mordor BCI, 2025–31](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/brain-computer-interface-market); [Fortune BCI, 2023](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/brain-computer-interface-market-105811); [OpenPR US market note, 2025](https://www.openpr.com/news/4191994/united-states-brain-computer-interface-market-2025-growth)).

- Manufacturing and supply chain realities: Scalable production of wearable biosensor patches and flexible circuits requires integrated printing/converting of conductive inks on flexible substrates, lamination of medical‑grade adhesives/hydrogels, cleanroom assembly, and automated packaging/traceability—capabilities concentrated in specialized ISO 13485 converters, but adjacent to advanced electronics substrate manufacturers’ strengths ([EWMFG, 2025](https://ewmfg.com/post/achieving-manufacturing-excellence-in-wearable-biosensors); [Innovize](https://www.innovize.com/who-we-serve/wearable-sensors/)).

Points of divergence

- CGM market sizing variation: Different firms present materially different 2025 baselines and 2030–33 forecasts—ranging from ~$10.9B (HTF) to ~$13.3B (Mordor), and 2031–33 projections from ~$31B (Mordor) to ~$47B (HTF). Differences stem from scope (inclusion of SMBG/connected pumps), methodology (top‑down vs. componentized bottom‑up), and pace assumptions for OTC/preventive adoption. For a component supplier, the directional consistency (double‑digit growth, expanding non‑insulin use) is more relevant than absolute totals ([HTF, 2025](https://www.htfmarketinsights.com/report/4362839-continuous-glucose-monitors-cgms-market); [Mordor CGM, 2025–31](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market)).

- BCI maturity/pace: Some reports emphasize rapid CAGR and proliferating pilots; others stress regulatory and ethical headwinds, with earlier adoption in research/assistive communication vs. mass consumer. Net effect: component opportunities in implantables are real but staged over multi‑year horizons with high technical/regulatory barriers ([Emergen Neural‑Interfacing, 2025–35](https://www.emergenresearch.com/industry-report/neural-interfacing-flexible-circuits-market)).

- Analyst taxonomy: Major generalist firms seldom name “advanced medical device substrates/electrode modules” as a category; coverage fragments across device markets (CGM/BCI), flexible electronics, and HDI/rigid‑flex. This fragmentation implies a white‑space positioning opportunity for LGIT to define a “medicalized flexible substrate and electrode module” value proposition that aligns with buyers’ device roadmaps while fitting procurement taxonomies (interconnects/electrodes) ([IMARC HDI, 2025](https://www.imarcgroup.com/high-density-interconnect-pcb-market); [R&M Thin‑Film Electrode, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/970-mn-thin-film-electrode-090700630.html)).

Market maturity assessment

- CGM: Late growth/early maturity; consolidated OEMs; strong component demand elasticity for longer wear, accuracy, comfort; procurement favors qualified, ISO 13485 component suppliers able to scale reliably.

- Neurostimulation: Mixed—established segments (spinal cord, vagus nerve) are mature but continuously innovating on leads/electrodes; emerging indications and wearables add new demand for conformal electrodes and skin‑patch modules; procurement varies by therapy and geography.

- BCI: Early growth; high technical risk; procurement mainly in research/clinical trials with select neurotech OEMs; component suppliers must meet implant biocompatibility standards and often co‑develop to specification; timelines are longer but strategic positioning can yield defensible moat.

Implications for LG Innotek (LGIT)

Supply chain positioning map

- Tier‑2/1 component supplier: Medicalized FPC/rigid‑flex interconnects; chip‑on‑film interposers for sensor/transmitter modules; high‑density thin‑film electrode laminates on PI/silicone; integrated electrode‑flex assemblies for skin‑patches and surface ECoG grids.

- Module partner (non‑implantable): Smart patch subassemblies combining flexible circuits, electrode layers, hydrogels/adhesives, and mechanical retention—delivered to CGM/neurostimulation OEMs or to contract device assemblers (CMOs).

- Co‑development for implantables: High‑density cortical/peripheral leads and arrays as per‑spec subcomponents with neurotech OEMs; LGIT supplies thin‑film stacks and flex harnesses; OEMs own clinical validation and system algorithms.

Where LGIT can be defensible now

- H1 (0–18 months): Skin‑worn medical components and modules
  - Products: Medicalized FPC harnesses for CGM transmitters/patch pumps; CoF interposers for compact transmitter‑sensor interconnects; flexible electrode laminates for CGM patch covers and neurostimulation wearables (e.g., TENS/vagal ear clips); ECG/EMG patch electrode arrays for remote monitoring.
  - Why defensible: Low implantability barrier; leverages fine‑line/CoF yield; procurement values reliability, thinness, and comfort. Partnerships with ISO 13485 converters add immediate credibility for Class II accessories and components.
  - Partners/customers: CGM leaders’ accessory/component supply; ISO 13485 converters (Innovize, ZenPro) for co‑manufacturing; adhesive/hydrogel suppliers; diabetes DME distributors for private‑label accessories. Evidence of ecosystem maturity is strong (ZenPro, Accio supplier network), enabling rapid entry while LGIT builds internal certifications ([ZenPro](https://zenpromedical.com/cgm-patches.html); [Accio guides](https://www.accio.com/supplier/cgm-patch-cover-manufacturer)).

- H2 (12–36 months): High‑density flexible electrode arrays and rigid‑flex interconnects for clinical research and early implantable systems
  - Products: Thin‑film surface ECoG arrays (polyimide/silicone with noble metals or carbon‑based stacks); high‑channel flex tails and connectors; sterilization‑ready assemblies; test coupons and validation kits.
  - Why defensible: Addresses an explicit scaling bottleneck—hand‑assembled, macro‑ECoG arrays can be supplanted by lithography‑grade fine features and repeatability; LGIT’s microfabrication and HDI flex expertise fit directly. Must pair with biocompatibility materials science and sterilization validation to meet clinical use.
  - Partners/customers: Neurotech OEMs (Blackrock Neurotech, Synchron, Neuralink, Precision Neuroscience, INBRAIN); neurosurgical research centers. Regulatory engagement and QMS evidence are prerequisites ([Fortune BCI](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/brain-computer-interface-market-105811); [OpenPR US BCI](https://www.openpr.com/news/4191994/united-states-brain-computer-interface-market-2025-growth)).

- H3 (24–60 months): Co‑developed implantable electrode systems (subcomponents) for chronic neuromodulation/BCI and next‑gen CGM microneedles
  - Products: Long‑term implant‑grade electrode arrays (e.g., high‑density cortical, peripheral cuffs, spinal paddles) with validated materials stacks; microneedle‑integrated flexible interposers for CGM; integrated sensing leads.
  - Why defensible: High regulatory/clinical barrier becomes moat; success requires deep materials/biocompatibility R&D, GLP testing, and OEM codevelopment; LGIT moves from commodity interconnects to high‑value medical IP in process stacks and reliability.

Required capability build and risk mitigation

- Quality and regulatory
  - Achieve ISO 13485 certification for medical manufacturing sites; implement FDA QSR/21 CFR 820‑aligned QMS; build device history record (DHR) and full lot traceability to component level; establish sterilization validation partnerships (EtO, gamma, e‑beam depending on materials) for subassemblies ([Innovize’s positioning as ISO‑13485 manufacturer underscores bar](https://www.innovize.com/who-we-serve/wearable-sensors/)).
  - For implantable subcomponents, build biocompatibility testing (ISO 10993 series) and materials characterization protocols; establish supplier‑quality agreements and design transfer interfaces with OEMs.

- Materials science and process
  - Electrode stacks: Optimize noble metals (Pt, Au, IrOx) and emerging carbon/graphene for adhesion, impedance, and corrosion resistance; consider boron‑doped diamond (BDD) where suitable; develop encapsulation chemistries for long‑term stability under flexural stress ([Research & Markets Thin‑Film Electrode](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/970-mn-thin-film-electrode-090700630.html)).
  - Skin interface: Co‑develop hypoallergenic adhesives (silicone, hydrocolloid), breathable carrier films, and hydrogels to minimize dermatitis—addressing known adhesive‑induced allergy patterns in CGM wearers ([JFPE cites contact allergy concerns](https://www.e-jfpe.org/archive/view_article?doi=10.56767/jfpe.2024.3.1.47)).

- Manufacturing integration
  - Add converting/lamination lines or partner to integrate conductive printing on flexible substrates, hydrogel dispensing, and rotary laser structuring of microfluidic channels within a Class 8 cleanroom pipeline ([EWMFG](https://ewmfg.com/post/achieving-manufacturing-excellence-in-wearable-biosensors); [Innovize](https://www.innovize.com/who-we-serve/wearable-sensors/)).
  - Formalize UHDI/HDI medical design DFM rulesets (bend radii, dynamic flex cycles, controlled impedance) and validate accelerated life performance under sweat, temperature, and motion.

- Commercial and partnership
  - Pursue JV or strategic supply with ISO 13485 converters and electrode specialists; license or co‑develop electrode IP where needed; engage CGM leaders on second‑source/dual‑source strategies for risk diversification.
  - Build clinician/Key Opinion Leader (KOL) advisory panel for ECoG and peripheral nerve electrode design input.

Risk analysis and mitigations

- Regulatory and certification lead‑time: ISO 13485 site certification and QMS maturation typically require 9–18 months. Mitigation: parallel path with contract converters for near‑term products; hire medical regulatory leadership early ([Innovize underscores ISO expectations](https://www.innovize.com/who-we-serve/wearable-sensors/)).

- Biocompatibility and long‑term stability: Especially critical for implantables; electrode delamination/corrosion risk must be engineered out. Mitigation: invest in materials stack R&D; adopt noble metals, IrOx, or BDD where appropriate; extensive soak and accelerated life testing ([R&M Thin‑Film Electrode challenge](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/970-mn-thin-film-electrode-090700630.html)).

- Skin compatibility: Adhesive dermatitis can erode brand trust. Mitigation: co‑develop silicone/hydrocolloid adhesives; barrier underlays; test across skin types; align with evidence of allergy pattern shifts ([JFPE cites adhesive allergy pattern changes](https://www.e-jfpe.org/archive/view_article?doi=10.56767/jfpe.2024.3.1.47)).

- IP/know‑how gaps: Biosignal algorithms and application‑specific electrode designs sit with OEMs. Mitigation: adopt a co‑development and manufacturing‑as‑a‑service model; focus on process IP, reliability, and manufacturability advantages.

- Supply chain reliability: Medical OEMs require higher transparency and traceability than consumer electronics. Mitigation: deploy component traceability systems; dual‑source critical materials; implement incoming quality and supplier audits consistent with medical norms ([Electronics traceability best practices](https://hawkerrichardson.com.au/solution/traceability-for-electronics-manufacturing/)).

Concrete recommendations for LGIT

1) Establish a Medical Business Unit (MBU) with dedicated QMS and applications engineering
- Charter: ISO 13485 certification for at least one fab; medical DFM practice; materials science lab for electrode stacks and adhesives; regulatory and clinical interface.

2) Launch a “Medicalized Flex & Electrode Platform” portfolio
- Offer pre‑validated stackups for: (a) skin‑contact electrode laminates; (b) sterilization‑capable flex assemblies; (c) high‑density ECoG arrays (prototype kits with varying pitch/materials). Provide DFM guides, sample kits, and validation reports to accelerate OEM adoption.

3) Partner to fill gaps and accelerate market entry
- Execute MOUs/JVs with ISO 13485 converters (e.g., Innovize‑like partners) for near‑term patch/module builds; engage electrode specialists for process transfer/licensing; co‑present at ATTD/ADA/DTC to build credibility.

4) Targeted BD into CGM and neuromodulation/BCI accounts
- Propose dual‑source programs for CGM interposers/flex harnesses; pilot neuro arrays with 1–2 leading neurotech firms under CDA for clinical evaluation; pursue private‑label accessory contracts (patch covers with integrated interposers).

5) Invest in validation infrastructure
- Build a biocompatibility testing pathway (outsourced labs plus in‑house pre‑screening); sterilization partner network; accelerated aging and mechanical fatigue rigs replicating real‑world flex/temperature/sweat conditions; skin‑patch usability testing cohorts.

6) Develop sustainability and regionalization angles
- Leverage APAC manufacturing strength and potential US/EU final assembly options for supply regionalization demanded by OEMs; invest in sustainable materials where feasible to align with OEM corporate sustainability goals referenced in flexible electronics reports ([OpenPR Thin‑Film notes sustainability angles](https://www.openpr.com/news/4408090/thin-film-electrode-market-outlook-2026-2030-emerging)).

Connections across research branches

- Device growth (CGM, BCI) directly pulls on enabling stacks (thin‑film electrodes, HDI/rigid‑flex), while manufacturing reviews clarify that the last‑mile integration (adhesives/hydrogels/cleanroom converting) is as determinative of success as the lithography itself. LGIT’s best path is to unify these branches: take electronics‑grade fine‑line quality and integrate it with medicalized materials and processes through partnerships and targeted capability build.

- Conference and exhibitor data verifies that market leaders are investing in patches and integrated ecosystems—bolstering the thesis that a medicalized substrate/electrode module supplier with scale can win multi‑year, multi‑program slots.

- Expert literature spotlights unresolved pain points (adhesive dermatitis; long‑term electrode stability; stretchability vs. electrochemistry rigidity) that a novel materials/process stack can address—shaping LGIT’s R&D roadmap.

Conclusion

The analyst ecosystem over the past two years paints a clear—if fragmented—picture: CGM is scaling rapidly into longer‑wear, AI‑integrated, and OTC channels; neurostimulation and BCI are converting R&D into regulated products, with implantables incrementally de‑risked. Across these markets, thin, flexible, high‑density electrode substrates and medicalized interconnects are immediate and compounding needs. While Gartner/Forrester/IDC lack a named category for “advanced medical device substrates/electrode modules,” adjacent coverage in CGM/BCI/thin‑film electrodes/HDI PCBs and conference agendas confirm both the demand and the gaps.

LGIT can enter now as a defensible tier‑1/2 component and module supplier by:

- Converting CoF/HDI strengths into medicalized flex/electrode offerings for skin‑worn devices (CGM, neurostim wearables), leveraging ISO 13485 partners to bridge certification gaps.

- Co‑developing high‑density thin‑film electrode arrays and flex harnesses with neurotech OEMs for clinical trials, progressively validating biocompatibility and sterilization.

- Investing in materials/process IP around electrode stability, skin compatibility, and stretchable interconnects to create durable competitive advantage.

A staged roadmap—H1 skin‑worn modules, H2 clinical‑grade arrays, H3 implantable subcomponents—balances near‑term revenue with long‑term moat building. The opportunity is real, growing, and insufficiently served by electronics‑grade, high‑yield suppliers who can meet medical quality and human‑factors demands. LGIT is well‑positioned to be that supplier—if it commits now to the medical quality journey and partners strategically to shorten the learning curve.
