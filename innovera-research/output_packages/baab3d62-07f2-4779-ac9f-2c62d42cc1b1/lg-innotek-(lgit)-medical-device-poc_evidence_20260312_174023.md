A2. Substitutes and Workarounds
- Rigid‑flex PCB assemblies and cable harnesses: In some medical systems (e.g., pump controllers, larger wearables), designers may prefer rigid‑flex or micro‑cables with high‑reliability connectors instead of pure flex arrays when bend radii and density allow. This can trade off thickness/weight for easier assembly or robustness ([Harwin blog, 2025](https://www.harwin.com/blog/flex-circuit-or-cable)).
- Conventional PCB + wired connectors in implants: For some implants, discrete wired feedthroughs and PCBs are used with minimal flex, relying on hermetic ceramic/metal packaging; newer BCIs and high‑channel neuro arrays increasingly require flexible thin‑film to minimize tissue response ([Frontiers review, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
- Low‑cost screen‑printed electrodes: For disposable patch biosensors, screen‑printed PI electrodes can substitute for higher‑end thin‑film flex where performance requirements are moderate (e.g., single‑use enzymatic sensors), trading precision for cost ([Allum, 2026](https://www.allumcorp.com/products/flexible-screen-printed-disposable-electrodes-flexible-biosensors-wearable-device-electrodes/)).
- Outsourcing to generalist EMS: Some OEMs use non‑specialist EMS for early builds, but migration to ISO 13485‑certified medtech CDMOs is typical as risk and regulatory needs escalate (design controls, traceability, post‑market surveillance) ([Arshon, 2026](https://arshon.com/blog/contract-manufacturing-for-medical-devices-a-strategic-guide-for-medtech-oems); [SimplerQMS, 2026](https://simplerqms.com/iso-13485-quality-management-system/)).

Regulatory and Quality Barriers (Gating Requirements)
- ISO 13485 QMS: Core requirement for medical device manufacturing and supplier qualification; FDA’s 2024 QMSR harmonization further aligns U.S. expectations with ISO 13485. Suppliers, CDMOs, and even raw material vendors are increasingly expected to operate within or align to ISO 13485 principles and documentation rigor ([SimplerQMS, 2026](https://simplerqms.com/iso-13485-quality-management-system/); [NQA, 2026](https://www.nqa.com/en-us/certification/standards/iso-13485); [ISO, 2016](https://www.iso.org/iso-13485-medical-devices.html)).
- Materials/biocompatibility: Substrate materials (polyimide vs PET), coatings (parylene C), and barrier stacks must be qualified to ISO 10993 biocompatibility and withstand sterilization regimens with chronic stability; electrode surfaces must maintain signal and corrosion resistance across lifetime ([Frontiers, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
- Design controls and traceability: CGM recall underscores supplier traceability needs (UDI linkage, lot/serial‑level process data). Medical PCBA/flex processes require AOI/X‑ray, MES data linking, and controlled adhesives/inks to support safety and post‑market CAPA ([SCSPCBA, 2026](https://scspcba.com/how-iso-compliant-medical-electronics-pcba-ensures-safety-traceability-and-long-term-reliability); [FDA recall PDF, 2026](https://ha2.edelivery-view.com/Public/CCX1000/Class%20I%20Recall%20-%20Abbott%20FreeStyle%20Libre%203%20%20FreeStyle%20Libre%203%20Plus%20Sensors.pdf)).
- Hermeticity for implants: Chronic implants (DBS, BCI) require robust corrosion barriers, hermetic enclosures, and reliable feedthroughs; substrate and metallization choices must reflect long‑term in vivo stability ([Frontiers citations, 2019/2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).

Strategic Implications and Recommendations for LG Innotek (LGIT)

Is 10 μm fine‑pattern substrate capability defensible and monetizable?
- Opinion: Yes—provided LGIT integrates this capability into a med‑grade offering. OEMs and CDMOs prize:
  - High interconnect density for multi‑channel electrodes (ECoG/SEEG/DBS/BCI) and compact CGM stacks (sensor + antenna + interposer).
  - Ultra‑thin, conformal flex that survives bend/fatigue and sterilization while maintaining conductivity and barrier performance.
  - Repeatable, high‑yield processes at scale with full traceability and ISO 13485 QMS documentation.
- Fine‑patterning alone is necessary but not sufficient. The defensibility emerges from:
  - Med‑compliant materials and validated stack‑ups (e.g., PI + parylene barrier + noble metal traces; nickel‑free finishes where relevant; skin‑safe adhesives interfaces).
  - Biocompatibility and sterilization validation packages ready for OEM design‑in (e.g., ISO 10993 evaluations, ETO/gamma/plasma compatibility).
  - Process capability indices (Cp/Cpk), longitudinal yield, accelerated aging, moisture ingress tests; test automation and MES data logging.
  - Alignment with implant packaging partners (hermetic feedthrough design rules; wirebond vs flip‑chip on flex; laser‑weldable terminations) ([Frontiers, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full); [SCSPCBA, 2026](https://scspcba.com/how-iso-compliant-medical-electronics-pcba-ensures-safety-traceability-and-long-term-reliability)).

Where to play in the value chain for superior margins
- Near‑term (0–18 months): Component + submodule supplier through accredited CDMOs.
  - Sell fine‑pitch, med‑ready flex substrates and electrode stacks to Linxens (wearable biosensor patches), Cirtec (thin‑film neural arrays), Resonetics/Integer (leads/interconnects), and select EMS (Flex/Jabil) for CGM/patch assemblies.
  - Co‑develop DfM/DfX rules and validation plans within their ISO 13485 frameworks to accelerate OEM design‑in and reduce LGIT’s regulatory burden initially ([MarketsandMarkets, 2026](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/medical-device-contract-manufacturing-market-170622851.html); [Medical Design & Outsourcing, 2025](None); [Resonetics PR, 2024](https://resonetics.com/news/resonetics-expands-bioelectronics-implant-offering-with-acquisition-of-manufacturing-assets-and-process-ip-from-med-ally/)).
- Mid‑term (18–36 months): Offer application‑specific modules under LGIT’s own ISO 13485 QMS.
  - CGM substrate stacks (electrode + interposer + antenna + connector pad arrays) as standard SKUs with configurable geometries.
  - Neural array substrates (ECoG/SEEG thin‑film arrays) with customizable site counts and routing density; incorporate CoF sensor/ASIC interconnect options for early BCI customers ([Cirtec BCI](https://www.cirtecmed.com/solutions/brain-computer-interface-bci); [Frontiers, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medical-technology/articles/10.3389/fmedt.2021.675744/full)).
- Long‑term (>36 months): Selective CDMO moves around high‑margin subassemblies.
  - Build in‑house micro‑assembly lines for flexible electrode stacks, thin‑film to feedthrough transitions, and conformal encapsulation processes; maintain alliances for hermetic case assembly and battery integration.

Conclusion
The medical flexible substrate opportunity in CGM, neuromodulation, and BCI is attractive, growing, and increasingly demanding of ultra‑thin, high‑density, biocompatible interconnects and electrode arrays. Market power sits with a few OEMs and their trusted, ISO 13485 CDMO ecosystems. LGIT’s 10 μm fine‑patterning can be a strong competitive advantage, but only as part of a medical‑grade value proposition: validated materials/barriers, sterilization‑proof stack‑ups, design controls, and production traceability.

A dual‑track strategy—entering via established CDMOs for near‑term design‑ins while building LGIT’s own ISO 13485 module capability—offers the best path to superior margins. Focus first on:
- CGM substrate stacks enabling thinner, more integrated patches at scale (with CDMO partners like Flex/Jabil and platform partners like Linxens/Presto).
- High‑density neural array substrates for ECoG/SEEG and early BCI, via Cirtec/Resonetics/Integer alliances.

Execute with rigorous QMS, compelling reference designs, and rapid, well‑documented prototyping. This approach transforms LGIT’s fine‑pitch prowess into durable, monetizable differentiation in regulated medtech supply chains.
