1. Context
LGIT possesses advanced PCB and CoF (Chip-on-Film) substrate manufacturing capabilities, including fine-pattern photolithography and plating technologies capable of approximately 10µm-level circuit formation on rigid and flexible substrates.
These capabilities enable:
•	Ultra-thin, flexible circuit formation
•	High-density, fine-pattern electrode structures
•	Multi-channel implementation on compact films
•	Enhanced device miniaturization and design flexibility
LGIT’s primary businesses today focus on packaging, optical, and mobility solution technologies serving the IT and automotive markets.
The company is exploring entry into high-value medical device markets, specifically:
•	Continuous Glucose Monitoring Systems (CGMS)
•	Nerve stimulation devices
•	Brain–Computer Interface (BCI) systems
The strategic rationale historically discussed includes:
•	Structural shifts toward personalized, preventive, AI-enabled healthcare
•	Growth in wearable monitoring demand
•	Increasing prevalence of neurological and cardiovascular diseases
•	Rising need for thin, flexible, high-density electrode substrates in implantable and wearable devices
LGIT is not currently operating as a certified medical device manufacturer. However, it has established partial regulatory and technical groundwork:
•	ISO 13485:2016 certification held from December 2018 to December 2021
•	Completion of four ISO 10993 biocompatibility evaluation items:
o	Cytotoxicity
o	Implantation
o	Intracutaneous reactivity
o	Systemic toxicity
Beyond fine-pattern fabrication, LGIT possesses additional capabilities relevant to medical electrode and sensor production:
•	Biocompatible films and materials including LCP, PET films, Au, Ag/AgCl, and Carbon
•	Roll-to-Roll (R2R) mass-production infrastructure
•	Low-temperature chip bonding technology designed to minimize surface damage on PET films with low heat resistance
•	De-noising technology under development to correct signal distortion caused by noise and unwanted biosignals
LGIT is conducting a joint research project with KAIST in the BCI domain. This collaboration includes:
•	Co-development of electrode substrates
•	Development of communication modules
•	Development of operating modules
•	Animal toxicity evaluation of developed operating modules
•	Planning for EEG data acquisition and utilization
LGIT does not own proprietary CGM sensor technology and does not have a development history of biosignal monitoring sensors for consumer wearable devices. However:
•	The company is currently developing CGM sensor substrates and modules in collaboration with a major CGM company
•	It is in discussion with a leading neurostimulation company regarding joint development of neurostimulation electrodes
•	It has more than five years of technical development experience in neurostimulation electrode technology through collaboration with a startup
All three counterparties across CGM, neurostimulation, and BCI applications have signed NDAs with LGIT.
Prior to entering formal PoC contracts, LGIT is currently conducting feasibility study projects with three separate companies to review and confirm its technical capabilities in each respective application area.
These feasibility studies are positioned as structured pre-PoC validation steps. Upon successful completion, the expected next phase would be formal PoC agreements. These activities represent technical validation efforts and do not yet constitute commercial-scale engagement.
The company’s strategic focus is not on finished end devices. It is evaluating participation at the level of:
•	Core components and modules for small wearable sensors
•	Key components and modules for implantable devices such as neurostimulation and BCI systems
The company is not pursuing an OEM finished-device business model. Instead, it is considering:
•	Component supply
•	Module-level participation
•	Potential CDMO-type participation
The strategic direction under evaluation includes expansion from substrate manufacturing into higher-value participation within the medical device value chain.
M&A is permitted as part of the entry strategy. The company is considering the acquisition of early-stage startups with system-solution capabilities, such as Echo. While no specific investment amount has been set, LGIT recently made an investment of approximately USD 35 million, providing a reference scale for potential transactions.
Echo partnership is currently in the exploration phase. LGIT has entered into an NDA with Echo and is exploring collaboration at the development stage. The intention is to assess Echo alongside other potential candidates to identify the most suitable strategic partner.
Regulatory preparation is recognized as a central requirement for entry into the medical device sector. This includes:
•	Medical-grade material certifications (e.g., ISO 10993)
•	Manufacturing facility certifications (e.g., ISO 13485)
•	Identification of additional regulatory requirements not yet fully mapped
•	Assessment of business implications associated with compliance
The financial modeling horizon for this initiative considers:

Technology Background

The company possesses the technological capabilities required for PCB and CoF substrate manufacturing processes, and its substrate technology expertise includes plating and photolithography technologies that enable fine patterning on both rigid and flexible substrates. These technologies are intended to be applied to nerve stimulation devices, BCI components, and CGMS patches.

In particular, the company’s CoF technology enables the formation of fine circuits on both sides of thin film-type substrates. Based on micro-circuit formation technology at the 10 µm level, the company can implement functions on smaller films as circuit line spacing becomes narrower.

Since circuits can be formed on ultra-thin films, this technology can be applied to flexible products, enhancing the freedom of device design and usability.

Purpose

Applying precision-patterned and flexible electrode technologies to achieve accurate neural stimulation or brain-signal interpretation, enabling signal acquisition from diverse locations in the brain.

Enhancing signal accuracy and enabling miniaturization for glucose monitoring through electrochemical electrode technology and precision chip-bonding processes.

If the strategy and hypotheses prove valid, the objective is to analyze the company’s existing capabilities to identify areas that can be leveraged and those that can be expanded, and to propose the direction for capabilities that need to be supplemented (from a Build / Buy / Borrow perspective).

The company aims to enter high value–added markets in the medical device sector based on its substrate technologies, focusing on nerve stimulation and BCI technologies that enable continuous monitoring, as well as wearable-type CGMS for glucose monitoring. The company seeks to advance and validate these initiatives through a strategy that expands its capabilities from substrates to modules and system-level solutions.
