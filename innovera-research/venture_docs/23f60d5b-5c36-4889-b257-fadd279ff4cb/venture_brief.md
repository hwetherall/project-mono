Technology Background

The company possesses the technological capabilities required for PCB and CoF substrate manufacturing processes, and its substrate technology expertise includes plating and photolithography technologies that enable fine patterning on both rigid and flexible substrates. These technologies are intended to be applied to nerve stimulation devices, BCI components, and CGMS patches.

In particular, the company’s CoF technology enables the formation of fine circuits on both sides of thin film-type substrates. Based on micro-circuit formation technology at the 10 µm level, the company can implement functions on smaller films as circuit line spacing becomes narrower.

Since circuits can be formed on ultra-thin films, this technology can be applied to flexible products, enhancing the freedom of device design and usability.

Purpose

Applying precision-patterned and flexible electrode technologies to achieve accurate neural stimulation or brain-signal interpretation, enabling signal acquisition from diverse locations in the brain.

Enhancing signal accuracy and enabling miniaturization for glucose monitoring through electrochemical electrode technology and precision chip-bonding processes.

If the strategy and hypotheses prove valid, the objective is to analyze the company’s existing capabilities to identify areas that can be leveraged and those that can be expanded, and to propose the direction for capabilities that need to be supplemented (from a Build / Buy / Borrow perspective).

The company aims to enter high value–added markets in the medical device sector based on its substrate technologies, focusing on nerve stimulation and BCI technologies that enable continuous monitoring, as well as wearable-type CGMS for glucose monitoring. The company seeks to advance and validate these initiatives through a strategy that expands its capabilities from substrates to modules and system-level solutions.

① Trend / Market
Verifying the Validity of Approaching the CGMS and BCI Markets with CoF Substrate Technology
Background

The company intends to enter the medical device market by leveraging its CoF substrate technology and is reviewing potential application areas such as:

Thin-film wearable CGMS (continuous glucose monitoring systems)

Nerve stimulation

BCI

The company believes that the wearable CGMS market continues to expand due to growing demand for daily health monitoring, and that the need for nerve stimulation and BCI technologies is also increasing as the number of patients with cardiovascular and neurological disorders continues to rise.

From a technical perspective:

CGMS devices are attached to the skin in patch form

Thin substrates and fine-patterning technologies enable accurate measurements while minimizing user discomfort

For nerve stimulation and BCI technologies:

Devices must precisely read and transmit brain signals

Ultra-thin substrates and fine-pattern technologies are essential to avoid irritation or discomfort on the brain surface

Across CGMS, nerve stimulation, and BCI applications, the following are core technological competencies:

Fine-patterning flexible substrate technologies

High-density electrodes

Multi-channel implementation capabilities

These elements are expected to serve as key differentiators in the market.

Hypothesis

The CGM and neurostimulation markets are expected to experience explosive growth, and LGIT can achieve differentiation by leveraging its substrate technology capabilities.

② Identifying Market Inflection Points, Customer Pain Points, and Value Delivery
Background

Based on the historical evolution of the medical device industry, the market has transitioned from a diagnosis-and-treatment-centric paradigm to a preventive, AI-enabled healthcare model.

The key trigger driving market transformation is believed to be the shift toward personalized, preventive medical devices.

As the market moves toward personalized devices, the company’s substrate technology enables the development of thin, fine-patterned structures capable of delivering accurate information without discomfort or a sense of foreignness.

This capability addresses customer pain points, particularly:

Need for enhanced portability

Reduced inconvenience in device use

By alleviating these pain points, the technology can deliver:

A more comfortable user experience

Reduced psychological resistance for implantable devices

Greater user reassurance

This is especially relevant for nerve-stimulation and BCI systems.
